Orb Combat EX: a match between your two PCs

What this is: one fight between your two PCs, with one pad on each, or two fights at once, one on each PC, against two computer fighters: as a team ("2v2 together") or apart. This game opens a network link between the two PCs, over your home network only. It's a private test and it has rough edges.

WHO PLAYS: Only Orb and Orb 2 play and watch, one on each PC, with nobody else in either room. This build has a flashing-effects warning; each of you reads it first. Don't copy, record, share or post this folder, the screens or the main PC's address. Two fights share one globe, so craters and damage from the other fight can appear in the world. Fighters in the other fight can hit you, and your partner's fight isn't shown on your screen.

BEFORE YOU START: ONE WINDOWS QUESTION, ONCE
This is the only approval it needs, and only on the main PC. Windows blocks programs from being reached over the network unless you say so. So you're allowing this one game, on one network port, from addresses on your home network only (not ZeroTier). It's only for this test: step 8 takes it away, so don't skip that.

Both PCs must be on the same home network: no guest or shared Wi-Fi, no ZeroTier or other member network, no port forwarding, no tunnel.

ON THE MAIN PC
1. Double-click "Allow through the firewall (main PC, once)". Windows asks "Do you want to allow this app to make changes?". Click Yes. A window says Done.
2. Double-click "Host (main PC)".

ON THE OLD PC
3. Copy this whole folder to the old PC first (your own USB stick or home share is fine). It stays on your two PCs.
4. Double-click "Join (old PC)". If Windows says it protected your PC because the program isn't recognised, click More info, then Run anyway. That's because the build isn't signed.

ON BOTH PCs
5. The game shows its flashing-effects warning on each PC. Each of you reads it on your own PC first: don't click through it for the other. Either of you may stop at any moment. Flashing stays reduced in this build, and you can't turn that off.
6. Choose a controller on the start menu and press Start. Do this on each PC, one after the other, in any order.
7. Look at the top of the window. It says "Waiting for the other PC", then "Connected, input wait 2 ticks". The PC that started first just waits, however long you take. Then fight. Your fighter is yours on each PC: in a 1v1 the main PC plays the first fighter and the old PC the second; in 2v2 each PC plays the hero in its own fight.

TWO OF YOU AGAINST TWO: "2v2 together"
Close both, then use "Host 2v2 together (main PC)" in step 2 and "Join 2v2 together (old PC)" in step 4. You and your partner are a team against two computer fighters, one fight on each PC, on the same planet. The two screens look alike: both show the same hero against the same rival, and each PC shows only its own fight. A hit from the pair you can't see says "Hit from the other fight". Hits cross between the fights: shots and beams can reach an enemy in the other fight, and near one you can land a single blow on his opening, as his fighters can on yours. Your partner's fight isn't shown on your screen. A knocked-out fighter is out, and the match ends when a team has nobody standing. Both PCs must use the "together" shortcuts.

TWO FIGHTS AT ONCE, APART
Close both, then use "Host 2v2 (main PC)" in step 2 and "Join 2v2 (old PC)" in step 4. Each PC now has its own fight against a computer fighter, on the same planet. The main PC's fight starts where the 1v1 does, and the old PC's half a world away. In this one nothing crosses between the fights, but their craters and broken buildings stay where they fall. If the two PCs use different shortcuts, the top of the window says "Couldn't start".

TO COMPARE TWO FEELS
Close both, then use "Host with a longer wait (main PC)" in step 2. The top of the window says "input wait 3 ticks". Your button presses land about 17 ms later, and the two PCs have less to correct when one of them stutters. Tell the EP which you'd rather play.

IF A WINDOWS FIREWALL BOX APPEARS ANYWAY
We haven't been able to test whether step 1 stops it. If "Windows Security Alert" appears on the main PC, click "Allow access", and tell the EP it appeared. Don't click Cancel: that blocks the game until the rule's removed. Step 8 takes away what that box adds as well.

WHAT DOESN'T WORK YET
- One match only. When MATCH OVER shows, or the top of the window says "The match is over", close both windows, and start again for another. MATCH OVER may show on one PC only: close both anyway. In 2v2, if your fighter is knocked out while your partner still stands, the top says "You're out" and your screen follows your partner's fight.
- A pause holds both PCs. The other PC's window says "Paused by the other player" and waits until you come back.
- If one PC goes (closed, crashed, or its network dropped), the other says THE OTHER PC HAS GONE on the screen and the computer plays the gone PC's fighter, so the match goes on. Tell the EP afterwards.
- If the top of the window says "OUT OF STEP", or it stays on "Waiting" for over a minute, close both and tell the EP.
- If a PC's address has changed from {HOST_IP}, "Couldn't connect" shows at the top. Tell the EP.

AFTERWARDS
8. On the main PC, double-click "Remove the firewall rule (main PC, afterwards)". Click Yes. Do this every time.
9. If you choose, give the EP the numbers in the files named lan-log-... on each PC: the ping, the step counts and the time.
10. Your matches are saved on each PC, in the folder "saved" beside the game: at the end, at every pause, and when you close the window. Press F9 on the keyboard, or View and RB together on the pad, to mark a moment you want looked at: that saves a report there too, and the screen says REPORT SAVED. Bring the "saved" folder from both PCs, with the lan-log files.
