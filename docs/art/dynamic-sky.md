# The dynamic sky

Owner: Art Director. 2026-10-06. Direction, not code: nothing in `render/` is changed by this file. Concept art and data, working labels, **pending Legal review** (the flash limit, the red horizon glow). Orb (`docs/ep/vision.md`, 2026-10-06): "I do want the sky to be dynamic and color changing", driven by **where you are on the planet**, **the fight's mood and damage** and **time passing** (not the match's acts), without changing the game's look.

**Sheets** (`art/concepts/sky/`): `sky-keys.svg` (the keys, the blend round the planet, the drift, the rate limit), `sky-mood.svg` (mood and damage), `sky-stills.svg` (the two fighters over each key), `sky-contrast.svg` (the contrast with the lane colours). **Data:** `data/art/sky.json` (Tools adds the schema; today `node tools/validate.js` warns "no schema"). Everything is generated by `art/concepts/sky/gen.mjs` from `keys.mjs` (the single source), so the sheets and the data cannot drift apart.

## The rule: extend the sunset, do not restyle it
The game's sky is four bands, top to horizon (`render/core/look.gd` `SKY`: `#111a3e`, `#4b4483`, `#d9776b`, `#f4b87a`; anchored to the horizon at 0, 0.12, 0.55 and 1.45 half-screens). That is the key **sunset**, it is exactly the current look, and it is the sky at the start of a match. Every other key has the same four bands in the same positions, so Rendering changes four colours and nothing else: no new shader, no new shapes.

## 1. By place: a lap round the planet is one day
The world wraps, so the sky runs one full cycle per lap. The sky tells you where you are (pillar 1).

| Key | Lap | top | upper | lower | horizon | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| noon | 0.00 | `#14307a` | `#005999` | `#0084c5` | `#e8e2c8` | a deep cerulean with a cream haze, not a pale blue (a pale blue would swallow the Protagonist's pale blue) |
| golden | 0.18 | `#1a2f6e` | `#0066a8` | `#e0a468` | `#f8dba0` | late afternoon: amber rises from the horizon |
| **sunset** | **0.32** | `#111a3e` | `#4b4483` | `#d9776b` | `#f4b87a` | **the current look**; a match starts here |
| night | 0.58 | `#05081a` | `#0a1636` | `#12294f` | `#243e66` | blue-black, no violet (a violet night would swallow the rival), stars at full |
| dawn | 0.84 | `#16264e` | `#00618b` | `#d98f86` | `#f6c9a0` | teal into rose into apricot, cooler and greener than the sunset |

The phase of a place is `fract(start_phase + (camera_x - spawn_x) / lap_length)`. In split view each pane uses its own camera's place (two panes can show two skies); the time drift and the mood are shared. The match starts at the sunset phase at the spawn.

**Blend:** each key holds its colours for a little (`hold`, 0.03 to 0.06 of a lap) so the keys are really seen, then blends to the next with a smoothstep ease in OKLab, band by band. Each band takes its own path per transition: straight in OKLab (`lab`) or round the hue wheel one way (`lch+`) or the other (`lch-`), chosen by search so no blend passes through a grey or a violet that would sit on a lane colour (a straight blend dipped to a colour distance of 11 from the rival; with the chosen paths the lowest is 20.3 and 23.1 round the whole lap). The paths are in `data/art/sky.json` `blend.transitions`.

## 2. By time: a slow drift
A drift of **0.0125 of a cycle a minute** (a full cycle in 80 minutes) is added to the place phase. A fight that stays in one place goes from the sunset to deep dusk by minute 8 (about a tenth of a cycle) and to night by about minute 20. The luminance changes by at most about 0.001 a second: no one sees the change, only a different sky when they look again. It is shared by both panes.

## 3. By mood and damage
Three drivers, each eased and each applied to the key's bands (`keys.mjs` `moodSky`):
- **Frenzy** (the fight's mood, 0 to 1; Encounter and Combat supply the number): the top, upper and lower bands deepen (18%, 25%, 10%), the horizon takes 10% ember (`#d9603c`). At least **30 s** from 0 to 1.
- **Ruin** (the share of the world destroyed, 0 to 1): the horizon and lower bands go toward dark brown smoke (`#2e221c`) by 60%, the upper by 40%, the top toward ash (`#2b2724`) by 25%, the stars thinning. It darkens and warms; it never greys toward the rival's violet. At least **120 s** from 0 to 1 (the world is wrecked over minutes anyway).
- **A burning town** (a glow per town in view, with its direction): the horizon band takes up to 35% ember in a wide low glow (about 0.22 of the screen across, a third of the horizon band tall), nothing above the lower band. At least **50 s** from 0 to 1; several towns add, capped at 1.
- Smoke and ash as shapes are VFX particles (low contrast, never brighter than the sky behind, under 20 px a second), not the sky's job.

## 4. Nothing fast: the fastest allowed rate
Measured in relative luminance (WCAG, 0 to 1). **All drivers together: no band changes faster than 0.05 a second and the weighted mean (horizon 0.18, lower 0.30, upper 0.30, top 0.22) no faster than 0.02 a second.** Place and time get 0.035 and 0.015 of that, the mood drivers 0.015 and 0.005. A general flash (`docs/legal/photosensitivity-note.md`, WCAG 2.3.1) needs an opposing pair of 0.10 or more inside a second, so the sky cannot make one at these rates, with margin for the area and gamma readings.

How it is kept: the shown phase follows the target phase through `slewStep` (`keys.mjs`): each frame it moves as far as it can without breaking the budget, so the sky catches up and never jumps. The mood drivers are eased at the minimum durations above. Measured: running once round the whole cycle at the limit takes **49 seconds**; a stress test (the target phase flying round at 0.25 of a lap a second and reversing every 3 seconds, 3 minutes) never changed the mean by more than 0.0148 or any band by more than 0.035 in any second.

**The sky must never:** change fast; pulse, flicker or follow the beat; react to a single hit (that is the VFX flashes' job, under the flash limit); go white or flash like lightning; or sit on a lane colour. Reduced motion: the slew limit applies as it is; with reduced motion on, hold the mood drivers and the drift and keep only the place blend (it is already slow).

## 5. Readable with the fighters
The aura colours are `#8fd6ff` (Protagonist) and `#9a80d8` (rival), drawn with a dark keyline (`#0a0d14`). Measured (CIEDE2000, `sky-contrast.svg`): at every key and every mood extreme, **24.6** (Protagonist) and **25.1** (rival) at the worst; round the whole lap, **20.3** and **23.1**. The rule: **24 or more at the keys and the mood extremes, 20 or more between them.** A pale blue cannot reach 3 to 1 contrast against the peach, cream or apricot horizons (it is already 1.1 to 1 against today's sunset), so the keyline carries it there and the bands keep clear of its hue. The keys were shaped for this: noon is a deep cerulean, night is blue-black, ruin goes to brown.

## What Rendering needs
`data/art/sky.json` (the keys as data, the blend paths, the start, the drift, the rate limits and the mood numbers), `keys.mjs` (the blend and `slewStep` as working reference code: GDScript is a port), and `sky-stills.svg` (the two fighters over each key and the mood extremes). **Legal:** the horizon ember glow (slow, never a flash; the red-flash test is about saturated red changing fast, and this changes over 50 s) and ruin's slow darkening, against their earlier screen that nothing in the sky's reaction darkens or flashes.

## 6. The world takes the key (2026-10-07)
Rendering's still (`docs/rendering/img/sky-keys.png`) showed what the first direction missed: only the sky's bands changed, so at the night key a daylit ground and daylit buildings stood under a black sky. That is not wanted. **Sheets:** `art/concepts/sky/world-light.svg` (the world at every key, as built against directed), `world-light-numbers.svg` (the tints, the checks, the rate). **Data:** `data/art/sky.json` `_world_light`.

**How:** inside the existing look (flat colours with a keyline, no new shading model), a multiply tint per layer, in linear light, and for the night a small additive lift so the dark ground is cool, not brown. Layers: ground, buildings, trees, water, civilians, bodies. The tints blend with the same phase, hold and smoothstep as the sky. A multiply (with the small night lift) is enough: no light direction, no per-pixel emissive.

| Key | ground (mul R G B) | bodies (mul) | windows lit |
|---|---|---|---|
| noon | 1 1 1 | 1 1 1 | 10% |
| golden | 1.0 0.84 0.62 (warms the sand) | 1.0 0.92 0.8 | 18% |
| sunset | 0.95 0.74 0.58 | 0.98 0.86 0.76 | 30% (as built) |
| night | 0.07 0.10 0.22, lift 0 0.006 0.034 | 0.5 0.58 0.82, lift 0 0.008 0.03 | 62% |
| dawn | 0.58 0.52 0.62 | 0.84 0.8 0.88 | 40% |

The other layers are in the data. **The bodies dim about half as much as the ground** (a luminance factor of 0.55 against 0.11 at night); **the aura rings, the lane colours, the lit windows' colour, the HUD, flashes, particles and the keyline never dim.** Windows come on with the dark (the lit share rises with the same phase, so they come on a few at a time).
**The one change to the start of a match:** the sunset world takes a gentle warm tint (about a fifth darker, a little more red than blue) so the first screen is lit as an evening; Orb should see it once. A strength knob that scales every tint toward 1 keeps the start exactly as it was if he prefers.
**The mood on the world:** frenzy dims the ground, buildings and trees a little (0.94 0.90 0.90); ruin takes the ground to 0.80 0.74 0.70, buildings 0.82 0.76 0.72, water 0.85 0.82 0.80 (soot); the bodies are never touched, so a fighter stays readable in a wrecked world. A burning town gives the ground, buildings and trees within 1800 body units a warm multiply (1.0 0.86 0.68), up to 35% at glow 1, falling off linearly.
**Readable:** at every key and mood, the bodies stay at CIEDE2000 **15.1 or more** (the game's body colours against the tinted ground: Protagonist #3d8fdc, rival #a52a2a) and the auras **20.8 or more**, which is no worse than today's noon (the game unchanged there).
**The rate:** the same hard limit, with the world in it: no sky band and no world layer changes in relative luminance faster than **0.035 a second** (place and time; 0.05 with the mood), the screen's mean (the sky weighted 0.45, each layer by its share of the screen) no faster than **0.015** (0.02 with the mood). The shown phase follows the target through `slewAll` (`world.mjs`). Measured: a lap at the limit takes **48 s** (the world adds none: the sky's horizon band is the slowest part), and the stress test never moved the screen mean by more than **0.0150** or any band or layer by more than **0.0350** in any second.

## 7. The mood's amounts are data
`data/art/sky.json` `mood._amounts` holds every amount as a number (frenzy, ruin, glow: which band mixes toward which colour by how much, the stars' thinning, the glow's reach per key), written from `keys.mjs` `MOOD_AMOUNTS`, so Rendering reads numbers and copies nothing. The prose strings stay as the explanation. (Tools: `_amounts` is an underscore key today, which the schema passes; promote it to a proper property when you next touch the schema.)

## 8. The town glow, and what "burning" should mean
**Sheet:** `art/concepts/sky/sky-glow.svg`. **Data:** `_town_glow` and `_burning`.
- **Larger and higher, not left as a far-off cue.** It was a third of the horizon band tall, which a town's own buildings hide from inside it. Now about **0.30 of the screen wide** (gaussian) rising to **0.55 half screens** above the horizon; the horizon band takes 45% ember, the lower 22% and the upper 8% at its centre (the last two scaled by the key's `glow_reach`: none at noon, half at golden, full from the sunset on, because ember mixed into noon's blue turns it slate, near the rival's violet). A far town is the same glow smaller by distance (down to 0.12 of the screen). From inside a town the towers can still hide it, so the town also takes **firelight** (above), and smoke and ash are VFX columns, not the sky.
- **It should die down.** There is no burning state in the sim today, so the glow follows damage and never falls. What the picture needs: a building **burns** when it reaches damage stage 2 or worse from **energy** (a beam, an explosion, a mine), not a plain blow. Its **fire** goes from 0 to 1 over 10 s, holds while it burns (60 s at most, and 90 s after the last energy damage to it), then falls to 0 over 30 s. A town glows by the **share of its buildings on fire** (half of them is a full glow); the glow eases at the mood rate (at least 50 s), so it lags the fire by design. When the fire is out the town stays wrecked: ruin keeps its smoke tint and the glow is gone. World and Simulation own the state; this is what it should mean.
