# First month on Steam: a realistic projection

Written by the EP on 2026-11-03 at Orb's request. This is market research, not financial advice, and nothing has been posted or bought. It updates section 9 of `go-to-market-plan.md` (2026-10-05) with 2025 and 2026 data. Every figure is either a cited benchmark or a labelled estimate.

## The short answer

**The most likely first month is about 120 to 200 copies, which is roughly $600 to $1,000 to you.** That assumes a paid release at $9.99 with a skeleton budget of about $500 and no publisher. A good run (about 1 in 3) is around 450 to 750 copies, or $2,300 to $3,800. A breakout (about 1 in 10) is around 1,800 to 3,000 copies, $9,000 to $15,000. That needs a clip of the planet-scale destruction going viral, which is this game's best shot.

The two biggest things that will move the number are things you control: **how many wishlists you have on launch day**, and **whether online versus is in at launch**.

What that means for us:
- **Wishlists still predict sales,** but each one is worth about half what it was a few years ago ([Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/27273/steam-wishlist-inflation-value-of-1-wishlist-cut-in-half-but-predictive-power-remains-unchanged)).
- **Your AI disclosure is a real cost, and you're paying it on purpose.** Expect roughly half the reviews and visibility a comparable undisclosed game would get. That's already folded into the wishlist assumptions below, not added on top.
- **Fighting is a crowded niche dominated by licensed giants.** I found no indie fighting-game benchmark, so this projection uses general indie numbers. An original anime brawler has no licence pulling people in, but it also has nothing stopping clips of its destruction from spreading.

## Where the project stands, and what that means for launch

The projection assumes the realistic course, not today's build.

- **Content:** two fighters are playable today, against a plan of four at launch and twelve eventually. Four is the minimum for a paid store page.
- **Online:** planned for after launch. Players of paid fighting games usually expect it at launch, and a missing online mode tends to show up in the first reviews. That costs reviews, which in turn costs visibility.
- **Blockers before any Steam page:**
    - The title clash with the OrbCombat project.
    - The licence choice.
    - Final AI-disclosure wording, read by Legal.
- **A realistic launch** is a late 2027 Steam release. That's after a free browser and itch.io phase, a Steam page live for 6 to 12 months to gather wishlists, and one Next Fest. My estimate, not a promise.
- **The free browser build** is the biggest advantage here. It's how strangers try the game with no risk.
- **Price:** $9.99, with a 10 to 15% launch discount. Above $10, first-week conversion drops, according to GameDiscoverCo.

## The skeleton marketing budget (about $500)

| Item | Cost | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Steam app fee | $100 | Required. Returned after $1,000 gross |
| Capsule and header art, by a hired human artist | $200 to $300 | The capsule is the most-seen asset on Steam. A human-made capsule also softens the AI-disclosure first impression |
| Trailer | $0 | Your own captures, edited by you |
| Next Fest and the demo | $0 | Free, and the best wishlist event on offer |
| Keys for small content creators | $0 | Free. Aim for creators who cover destruction and anime fighting |
| Small paid boosts on one platform | $50 to $100 | Optional. At this scale, ads rarely pay for themselves, so treat it as a test, not a plan |

## Three scenarios, first month after launch

**Method.** First-month copies = wishlists at launch × first-month sales per wishlist (0.15 to 0.25, around Zukowski's 0.22). Money to you = copies × $8.49 (the launch price after a 15% discount) × 0.70 (Steam's cut) × about 0.85 (refunds, regional pricing and tax). These figures are rounded.

| | **Likely (about 55%)** | **Good (about 35%)** | **Breakout (about 10%)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wishlists at launch | about 800 | about 3,000 | about 12,000 |
| What gets you there | The browser phase and one Next Fest at the median (806). Steady but small | Next Fest well above the median, plus a few clips that land with creators | A destruction clip spreading widely (a city block falling, a beam struggle). Rare, and not something money can buy |
| First-month copies | 120 to 200 | 450 to 750 | 1,800 to 3,000 |
| Gross at the launch price | $1,000 to $1,700 | $3,800 to $6,400 | $15,000 to $25,000 |
| **To you after Steam's cut and refunds** | **$600 to $1,000** | **$2,300 to $3,800** | **$9,000 to $15,000** |
| Budget back? | Yes, barely | Yes | Yes |
| $100 fee back (over $1,000 gross)? | Probably | Yes | Yes |

**The floor.** If wishlists end under 300, as happens to a large share of first-time games, the first month is under 60 copies and under $300 to you. Two in five Steam games don't reach $100, so this outcome is common, not a disaster.

**After the first month.** For a typical game, year one is about 2.6 to 4.5 times the first week. Updates that add fighters, or online versus, can bring a second wave.

## Where the number moves most

1. **Wishlists before launch.** Each extra 1,000 is worth about 200 more first-month copies. Keep the Steam page up as long as you can, and run one Next Fest with a strong demo.
2. **Online versus at launch.** It's the most common complaint about paid fighting games. Shipping LAN only, or no online, will likely cost reviews and visibility in the first month.
3. **Clips.** The game's hook is visual: craters, cities coming down, beam struggles. Short vertical clips cost nothing, and they're the only realistic route to the Breakout column.
4. **Price.** $9.99 is the sweet spot. Going under $5 sells more copies but earns less, and going over $10 lowers conversion.
5. **Free versus paid.** If you keep the whole game free and open source, Steam sales are $0. The income would then come from tips or an optional supporter pack, which usually brings in far less than this table.

## Open choices that change this projection

- **Paid or free on Steam** (tied to the licence choice).
- **Launch date,** and whether online is in at launch.
- **The title,** resolving the OrbCombat clash, before any page goes up.
- **Who makes the capsule art:** a hired human (my recommendation) or AI, disclosed.
