## Window 1: climate (`sim/world/climate.gd`, `data/biomes/climate.json`)

**What it replaces.** The stored planet's biome on ordinary land was two noise fields and a few thresholds (`_plainBiome`: wet below 0.42 and warm is desert, wet above 0.55 is forest, else plains; warmth was latitude only). Gen 2 makes it a **climate**:

- **Temperature** `T = eq * cos(lat)^pow - lapse * height / hScale + noise`, with the latitude wobbled by a slow noise (`jit`) so the belts are wavy and not ruled lines. Cos of the latitude is `sqrt(1 - y^2)`: no trig.
- **Moisture** is the sea's reach carried downwind. A coarse grid (192 columns of longitude by 96 rows uniform in the sine of the latitude, one planet's grid made once, about 170 ms native and read by a `PlaceJob`-style step if a host wants it in pieces) is scanned along each row **both ways, twice round** (so the carry crosses the seam): the sea recharges the wind (`evap`), the land rains it out (`rain0` a cell, plus `rainOro` for every `oroH` units of climb), so **a range casts a rain shadow**. The two directions are blended by the latitude's wind (`westerly(y) = 0.5 - 0.5 sin 6 phi`: trade winds near the equator and the poles, westerlies between: polynomials in y, no trig) and blurred a little between rows and columns. At a point the moisture is that carried value (bilinear), the **latitude bands** (`0.5 + 0.5 cos 6 phi`: wet equator, dry near 30 degrees, wet near 60), a noise and a bias, weighted by data.
- **The table.** Temperature in five bands (cold, cool, temperate, warm, hot) by moisture in five (arid to soaked) gives a **fine biome**; overrides first: mountains above the stored planet's `mountainAbove`, **volcanic** ground where a slow noise is high on high land, **highland** (rocky) from 2,300 units, **beach** below 70 units, **wetland** on wet warm lowland.
- **Fine biomes** (15; 0 to 6 are the stored planet's): ocean, plains, forest, desert, mountains, ice sheet, sea ice, **jungle, savanna, tundra, taiga, wetland, beach, highland, volcanic**. Each has an `alias`, the stored biome a consumer that knows only those sees: the renderer, the director and the picker go on working unchanged and read gen 2's land as plains, forest, desert and mountains until Rendering and Art learn the new ones. A slice carries both: `biomes` (stored) and `fine`.
- **Smaller polar caps in gen 2** (`polarY` 0.84 against 0.72): there is room for tundra and taiga on the cold edge of the land. The heights are the stored planet's function, so **window 1 changes the biomes, and the land only in the band between the two polar edges (latitude 0.72 to 0.84, where gen 1 drops to the sea and gen 2 keeps its land); the places are the same** (checked: the same seed makes the same places in either generation). Window 2's strips show it on a ring that reaches the band (seed 7 and seed 25).

**Cost.** `WorldClimate.gridOf(P)`: 140 to 200 ms once a planet (the same order as the picker's whole read); a column then costs about 1.3 times a gen 1 column (24 to 29 us against 18 to 22 on a busy PC: four more noise lookups and the table); on random points 1.07 times. A host that cannot spend 170 ms at once makes the grid in steps (`WorldClimate.Grid.step(n)`).

**Proof** (`climate_check.gd`, 9 checks): data clean, default gen 1; **gen 1 bit for bit** (12 slice hashes recorded from main before gen 2 existed); gen 2 pure (the same twice with a new planet object, differs between seeds and from gen 1); every column's stored biome is its fine biome's alias, and 4 Whole rings show 13 of the 15 fine biomes; temperature falls with latitude and height; **a range casts a rain shadow** (over 44 climbs of the westerlies the carried moisture 4 cells downwind is 0.01 against 0.61 on the windward side); **the coast is wetter than the interior** (0.71 within 2 cells of the sea, 0.13 14 or more cells inland); the cost against gen 1; the same places in either generation.

**Maps** (`stills/gen2-w1-maps.png`, from `planet_map.gd`, four seeds, columns: gen 1, gen 2 as the renderer sees it, gen 2 fine; no browser needed: the tool draws the planet function into a PNG).

**Not in a match yet.** A match reads `WorldPlanet.make(seed)` at many places, so a match's generation has to be set like its arena (`S.planetGen`, kept by `newMatch` from `setup.planetGen`, re-asserted by `step`): two lines in `sim/core/sim.gd` and one in `state.gd` (Simulation's). Until then gen 2 is for tools, maps and scratch stills.

## Window 2: ground profiles, trees, cover, props (`sim/world/ground.gd`, `data/biomes/ground.json`)

A fine biome now means something to a fight. `ground.json` gives each of the 15 a **profile**, read only by a match on a gen 2 planet (`WorldBiomes.fineCols` set); a gen 1 match never reaches it and is bit for bit what it was (six recorded match hashes: planets 7, 3, 25 and 11 at three ring sizes, the hand-made world and a gen 1 turn).

- **What it does to a body** (multipliers on contact.json's own numbers, per column, built when the world is): `grip` on the brake (mud 1.5, ice 0.45: over the same flat ground a fast skid runs 2,398 units on ice, 1,790 on plains, 1,598 in a wetland), `absorb` on the bounce's vertical keep (wetland 0.55, beach 0.7: a steep launch goes 219 over a wetland, 256 on plains), `rough` (at least 1: extra braking from stones, roots and rubble, so a skid on rough ground is shortened and never lengthened, and never becomes a tumble: Game Design's ruling 11, window 2b), `furrow` on the trench and berm (soft ground cuts deeper, rock and ice shallower). `surface` stays one of contact's three (soil, sand, rock) because the renderer's effects and Audio know those; ice and sea ice are rock with a low grip.
- **What grows.** A planet match laid no trees (`S.trees` was cleared), so forest cover never worked on a planet. A gen 2 ring now grows trees: each treed column draws a keyed chance (COL over the biome's mean gap) for a tree at a keyed place and height (`SimRng.keyed`, so a column's tree is the same whatever else changed; the seed is the ring's slice hash). Forest as genWorld's (16 to 40 apart, 46 to 110 tall), jungle denser and taller, taiga dense and conifer-tall, savanna sparse, wetland and beach few and low (reeds' trees, palms), none on tundra, desert, rock, ice.
- **Where a fighter hides** comes from the profile: canopy (jungle, taiga, forest: a tree near and low among it), ridge (mountains, highland, volcanic), and the sea as before. Savanna, tundra, wetland, beach and ice give none. The cover finder (`WorldCover.nearestCover`) reads the same profile so the AI never heads for cover that is not there.
- **Props** (rocks, ferns, cacti, reeds, driftwood, ice spires, vents and the like, per 1,000 units, a size range): `WorldGround.propsIn(fine, stored, base, seed, i0, i1, trees, lakes)` lays them from the columns by keyed draws, a pure function; the sim never reads them, the renderer may. W5's provisional bergs and lone trees, and `WorldGround.propsNear` for PROP SMASH: `docs/world/props-w5.md`.
- **The turn** (calm-turn): the fine columns blend with the same weight as the stored ones, S.planetRing carries them (`fine`, unhashed, so a state carried into another process rebuilds the same swap), and the trees are the old ring's where the ground is kept and the new ring's where it is new, with the new ring's laid in the preparation (the assembly step), so the swap pays nothing.
- **A match chooses its generation**: `setup.planetGen` (1 or 2; absent is the data's `gen`, 1). `WorldPlanetWorld.gen` sets it and resets the fine columns. With two sims alive in one process the statics follow whichever built last (the same as before for planetCols; Simulation's step re-assert would add `WorldClimate.curGen` and `fineCols` to the block that restores `planetCols`; optional, listed in the report).

**Looks.** `stills/gen2-w2-strips.png` (from `ground_strip.gd`): for seeds 7, 3, 11 and 25, the ring a match plays on, gen 1 above gen 2: height coloured by biome, trees as dark ticks, props as dots. Gen 2 reads: forest and jungle (the darker green) with trees standing on the slopes, savanna and tundra bands, cream beach strips at the coasts, highland on the heights; gen 1 the same ring with four biomes, bare. Rings that reach the polar band (seeds 7 and 25) also differ in height there (window 1's smaller caps). The renderer still draws every tree as one cone and every biome as its alias until Rendering and Art learn the new looks (a tree's kind is readable from its column in `WorldBiomes.fineCols`).

### Window 2b: Game Design's rulings and Combat's join (`scratch-build/gen2-w2b/`)

Game Design ruled on window 2 (`docs/design/rulings-2026-10-23b.md` items 11 and 12) and Combat's biome park (`docs/combat/pending/README.md`) asked for three things of it; 2b is those, on top of window 2:

- **Rough never tumbles** (item 11). `rough` is now a multiplier, at least 1, on the braking of stones, roots and rubble: a skid on rough ground is shortened (control returns sooner), never lengthened, and never becomes a tumble; the tumble band is contact.json's again. The data's smooth values (ice, wetland, savanna, tundra, beach 0.6 to 0.95) became 1; slipperiness is `grip`'s. Checked: on every fine biome the same launch runs no farther than with rough set to 1, and a slow launch's first contact is a skid on all 15.
- **`look`** on every profile: the look class (the five built, paving, rock, soil, sand and rubble, and Combat's nine: ice, snow, frost, mud, turf, litter, ash, crust, shingle) a biome reads as, by Combat's §4.1 table: sea ice ice, ice sheet and taiga snow, tundra frost, wetland mud, savanna and highland turf, jungle litter, volcanic crust, beach shingle. `WorldContact.lookAt(S, x)` returns it (paving and a rubble heap read as themselves; any other world reads as its surface). The sim still brakes by soil, sand and rock. **Ash** is not laid yet (Combat: "volcanic, where World lays it"): a later strip beside vents.
- **`WorldBiomes.fineNameAt(x)`**, the director's fine-name lookup: a gen 2 planet's fine biome name, any other world's biome name, and a town's or a city's ground as `village` or `city` (the stored codes 7 and 8 are not the fine ids 7 and 8).

Item 12 (control returns at the reference ground's stop; low grip is a skate after) is **window 6** (`scratch-build/gen2-w6/`): `WorldContact.journeyControl(S, body)`, the pure lookahead beside `journey`, runs the real journey, copies the body where its skid begins, runs the copy on reference ground (the surface's own braking, grip and rough 1) and returns the control tick, the reference stop and the skate's length. On flat ground a fast skid has control at tick 80 on plains, at the reference stop (tick 101) on ice sheet and sea ice with the body skating on to 141 and 145 (116 and 140 units after control), and at its own earlier stop on wetland and jungle (66 and 63). Not built: the fighter's hand-over at that tick (control begins while the body skates on) needs a skate state on the fighter, which is Simulation's (state.gd, fighter.gd, hash.gd, behind a switch): Encounter's planner can read `journeyControl` now.

## Window 3: landforms (`sim/world/land.gd`, `data/biomes/land.json`)

**What it adds.** Gen 2's heights are the stored continents (noise blobs) plus landforms with a cause:

- **Plates and ranges.** 14 seeded plates (best-candidate centres, each with a drift along the ground). At a point the plates near the nearest make pairs; a pair's two drifts say whether it **closes** or **parts**, and its dot-product difference, wobbled by a noise so the line is not straight, is the distance to the edge. Where plates close, a **ridged range** rises along the edge: up to 6,500 units of added height times the closing rate times a ridged noise that breaks the line into peaks and saddles (over deep sea only a fifth of that, so island arcs, not new continents). Where they part, a **rift valley** on land (1,500 deep) and a ridge on the sea floor (500). The stored planet's own range blobs stay at 55% (`keep`).
- **Continuity.** A pair's term depends on the pair and the point alone, and a smooth gate fades it where a third plate is nearer than both, so the ground is continuous across every edge **and every triple junction** (a first version summed only the nearest pair and jumped by 2,700 units at junctions; the check found it). Every bump has compact support, so a term cut off at its edge leaves no step. Across the plate edges the largest step between adjacent columns is 32 units; the steepest adjacent-column slope in 12,000 pairs is 3.5 (gen 1: 1.7), the 99.9th percentile 2.4 (gen 1: 1.3).
- **Coasts.** A noise moves the shoreline, full at sea level and zero past 650 units of height from it: **bays and capes** at two scales (5,200 units, up to 330 high; 1,300, up to 120). Inland and deep sea are untouched by it.
- **Plateaus and canyons.** On high dry ground (above 450, fading in over 220) a mask turns slopes into **flat tops with steep sides**: terraces 300 apart, the riser 65% of a step, pulled onto 80%. Ridged-noise lines cut **canyons** (480 deep) into the plateaus. 12% of adjacent high-ground columns are level in gen 2, 5% in gen 1.
- **The poles.** Gen 1's (and window 1's) polar zone began on a ruled latitude and cut continents along a straight line (Research's note). In gen 2 the edge moves with a noise (about -0.04 to 0.05 in the sine of the latitude, amp 0.07), so a continent ends raggedly.

**Places moved.** The places are sited on the ground, so a gen 2 planet has places of its own (53 against 42 on seed 7); window 1's "same places" check became "its own places". Every place stands between placeHMin and placeHMax.

**Rivers and lakes: can the slice carry them?** Lakes yes, rivers not yet. `S.water` holds a depth per column and the renderer draws any column with water, so a lake in a valley of the ring's profile (the profile filled to its spill level, off the settlements) is a few lines at match start; the water module's flow rules leave inland water alone unless a crater opens a hole below sea level. Rivers need a drainage network, which no per-column function gives: Research's erosion prototype (C) builds a per-planet grid (an estimated 2 to 7 s at 600-unit cells in GDScript) and would be a time-sliced job at the run's start; its channel would be a column or two of water along the ring where it crosses. Lakes are window 5, below; rivers are not built.

**Cost** (random points on a quiet machine, the whole of gen 2, the climate grid made): 15.8 µs a point in gen 1, 37 in gen 2 (2.3 to 2.4 times; the grid is 370 to 420 ms once a planet, the plates microseconds).

**Proof** (`land_check.gd`, 15 checks; chain on the window 3 tree): gen 1 untouched (the 12 embedded gen 1 slice hashes of `climate_check` still match, the six match hashes of `ground_check`); plates are a pure function of the seed; a gen 2 point is a pure function; where plates close, on the edge, the ground stands 1,106 units above gen 1's on average, where they part on land 844 below; no jump across an edge; slopes; the shoreline term; plateaus; the polar edge; places in range; cost. Maps: `stills/gen2-w3-maps.png` (seeds 7, 3, 11 and 25: gen 1 heights, gen 2 heights, gen 2 fine biomes).

## Window 4: placement and transitions (`sim/world/sites.gd`, `data/biomes/sites.json`)

- **Sites.** A candidate place's score in a gen 2 planet is the stored planet's own (spacing, height) plus a climate and coast term, so a city goes where a city would: **a harbour** (1 to 5 of the 8 points on a ring 4,500 units round it in the sea: some sea, not an islet) and a fertile biome (plains, forest, savanna), **nothing in the sea, on ice, on volcanic ground or on a range**, desert, jungle, tundra, wetland and highland marked down. Towns are scored more lightly (reach 2,500, a smaller bonus). The weights are per fine biome in `sites.json`, scaled by 6 against the stored score. Measured on 6 planets (323 places): none on ice, in the sea, on volcanic ground or on a range; **86% of the cities have a harbour against 38% in gen 1**; 15% of the places stand on a marked-down biome (26% before the weights were scaled); at least 11 cities and 52 places a planet; the ring picker still finds a ring on every try.
- **Transitions.** A ring's fine biomes meet in a **ragged band** and not a ruled line: runs of one biome are found on the circle; a column within 80 columns of a different biome of the set (plains, forest, desert, jungle, savanna, tundra, taiga, wetland, highland; the sea, ice, beach, mountains and volcanic stay crisp) takes it with a chance that falls from 0.7 at the border to 0 at 80, by two keyed draws averaged (one to each 8 columns, one to each 32), so patches cluster. The stored biome follows by alias. It is the last step of every read, whole or stepped, before anything hashes the biomes. Along 12 rings the biome changes rose 3.04 times; no biome's share of a ring moved by more than 3.1%.
- **Cost** (`gen_cost.gd`, on a busy machine, so read the ratios: gen 1 against gen 2 on four planets): a point 32 to 37 us against 72 to 77 (2.1 times), a ring pick 2 ms against 5, a slice read of 4,800 columns 57 to 67 ms against 148 to 169 (2.5 times), planet set-up (the climate grid, the plates, the places by scoring) 0.2 to 0.4 s against 1.1 to 1.4 s once a planet; the blend about 3 ms a ring.

## Window 5: lakes (`sim/world/lakes.gd`, `data/biomes/lakes.json`)

A gen 2 match's ring is a line of heights, and a valley in it that is walled on both sides holds a lake: from each local minimum the ground is climbed to a crest on each side, the lake stands at the lower crest, and the water is the depth from the floor to that level, a flat surface. It counts when it is at least 50 deep and 16 columns wide, its floor is above the sea (5), its level under 3,500, no town or city is within 10 columns of it, and its floor is not desert, volcanic, ice or mountain ground (a lake on a range is a tarn in highland, not on a peak). Trees do not grow in water. The water goes in `S.water` at the match's start (after `WorldWater.init`), where a crater's lake would be; the water module's flow rules leave inland water alone unless a crater opens a hole below sea level, so a lake stands until the ground under it is dug out. Hiding is out of the base game (pillar 6; Game Design's 23b row 13): `WorldCover.coverAt` reports "submerged" for a lake as for the sea, but only a `canHide` fighter (the future stealth fighter) reads it, and a base fighter's lock-break is by line of sight over the ground (and a canopy), which water does not change, so a lake gives no hiding and no lock-on loss. Window 7 makes a lake deep enough to hide in sea to `WorldTerrain.seaAt` (gen 2 only), so every rule that asks follows the sea's: a landing slams, a launch skims or sinks (`moveAir`, which reads any water surface). The calm turn plans the new ring's lakes in its preparation (on the blended ground) and applies them after the swap's water reset.

On 18 rings (6 planets, 3 touches) 13 hold a lake, about 3 a ring, 16 to 150 columns wide and 50 to 900 deep, at levels from the lowlands to 3,500 (`stills/gen2-w5-strips.png`: the lighter blue). **Rivers are not built**: they need a drainage network (Research's erosion grid: a per-planet, time-sliced job), see window 3.

## Window 7: the review's findings (`scratch-build/gen2-w7/`)

The EP's 10-agent review of the stack (each finding adversarially verified) found, and window 7 fixes:

- **The ring-slice memo had no generation in its key** (`_ringKey`): in one process a gen 2 match on an explicit ring, or a gen 2 turn's `memoRing`, cached a slice a later gen 1 match got back. The key now carries `dataHash()` (which carries the generation).
- **The per-match statics** (`WorldClimate.curGen`, `WorldBiomes.fineCols`) were not restored by `SimCore.step`. Simulation's grant, approved by the EP on Orb's word: `S.planetGen` and `S.fineCols` in `state.gd` (2 lines, not hashed), set in `newMatch` (after the world is built; the statics are reset before it) and restored in `step` beside `planetCols` (`sim.gd`, 7 lines); the turn keeps `S.fineCols`. Two sims alive, a gen 1 and a gen 2, stepped in turns for 120 ticks, are each the match they are alone, in both orders.
- **Lakes**: a dug bed or a berm keeps the lake's surface flat (`WorldWater.touched` refills a lake like the sea's reservoir columns, from `S.planetRing["lakes"]`); the calm turn carries the lakes of the kept core (their water as the fight left it) and plants only lakes wholly on ground the new ring takes (a lake that reached into the blend zone is not planted); a body landing on the bed of a lake deep enough to hide in slams, as in the sea (gen 2 only).
- **Cost**: the turn's assembly is spread over the preparation ticks (the trees and the lakes are their own steps; the dearest warm tick after the first fell from 7.7 ms to 1.9 ms); the trees are laid only where the new ring's ground is taken, with their keyed draws hashed once; placement skips candidates whose stored score plus the most the climate term can add cannot beat the best so far (identical places), and the sites' coast reads are skipped when the biome weight alone cannot win. **`WorldPlanet.makeJob(seed)`** makes a planet in steps (the climate grid, then the places a try-set at a time; `step(budget)`), and `make` is the same job run to the end (a gen 1 planet is the same by construction, a gen 2 planet equals make's); a host that cannot spend a second at once (the run start, the far land) can step it. Nothing calls it yet: those hosts are SimRun and Rendering's.
- The doc's rough text now says ruling 11 (this edit).
- **Lakes follow the sea's rules** (Game Design's 23b row 13): a lake deep enough to hide in is `seaAt`, so the rules keyed on it follow. The rules that also test `y < 0` (the sea's surface at absolute zero) do not yet see a lake at altitude: fighter.gd's sea speed damping (line 521), director/launch.gd's water landing flag, beam.gd's sea splash (y < 30) and the surfacing within 30 ticks; they need `y < WorldWater.surfaceAt(...)` in their owners' hands (Simulation, Encounter, Combat). For later rivers: crossings closer than 640 units merge into one span, with at most one span in any 3,000 units (GD's row 13).

Not changed (refuted by the review): the canopy tree scan, the epoch slice hash's fine bytes, load-only rules, absorb up to 3, polar.amp, journeyControl's reference start, biome multipliers under the tumble band.
