## Window 1: climate (`sim/world/climate.gd`, `data/biomes/climate.json`)

**What it replaces.** The stored planet's biome on ordinary land was two noise fields and a few thresholds (`_plainBiome`: wet below 0.42 and warm is desert, wet above 0.55 is forest, else plains; warmth was latitude only). Gen 2 makes it a **climate**:

- **Temperature** `T = eq * cos(lat)^pow - lapse * height / hScale + noise`, with the latitude wobbled by a slow noise (`jit`) so the belts are wavy and not ruled lines. Cos of the latitude is `sqrt(1 - y^2)`: no trig.
- **Moisture** is the sea's reach carried downwind. A coarse grid (192 columns of longitude by 96 rows uniform in the sine of the latitude, one planet's grid made once, about 170 ms native and read by a `PlaceJob`-style step if a host wants it in pieces) is scanned along each row **both ways, twice round** (so the carry crosses the seam): the sea recharges the wind (`evap`), the land rains it out (`rain0` a cell, plus `rainOro` for every `oroH` units of climb), so **a range casts a rain shadow**. The two directions are blended by the latitude's wind (`westerly(y) = 0.5 - 0.5 sin 6 phi`: trade winds near the equator and the poles, westerlies between: polynomials in y, no trig) and blurred a little between rows and columns. At a point the moisture is that carried value (bilinear), the **latitude bands** (`0.5 + 0.5 cos 6 phi`: wet equator, dry near 30 degrees, wet near 60), a noise and a bias, weighted by data.
- **The table.** Temperature in five bands (cold, cool, temperate, warm, hot) by moisture in five (arid to soaked) gives a **fine biome**; overrides first: mountains above the stored planet's `mountainAbove`, **volcanic** ground where a slow noise is high on high land, **highland** (rocky) from 2,300 units, **beach** below 70 units, **wetland** on wet warm lowland.
- **Fine biomes** (15; 0 to 6 are the stored planet's): ocean, plains, forest, desert, mountains, ice sheet, sea ice, **jungle, savanna, tundra, taiga, wetland, beach, highland, volcanic**. Each has an `alias`, the stored biome a consumer that knows only those sees: the renderer, the director and the picker go on working unchanged and read gen 2's land as plains, forest, desert and mountains until Rendering and Art learn the new ones. A slice carries both: `biomes` (stored) and `fine`.
- **Smaller polar caps in gen 2** (`polarY` 0.84 against 0.72): there is room for tundra and taiga on the cold edge of the land. The heights are the stored planet's function, so **window 1 changes the biomes, and the land only in the band between the two polar edges (latitude 0.72 to 0.84, where gen 1 drops to the sea and gen 2 keeps its land); the places are the same** (checked: the same seed makes the same places in either generation). Window 2's strips show it on a ring that reaches the band (seed 7 and seed 25).

**Cost.** `WorldClimate.gridOf(P)`: 140 to 200 ms once a planet (the same order as the picker's whole read); a column then costs about 1.3 times a gen 1 column (24 to 29 us against 18 to 22 on a busy PC: four more noise lookups and the table); on random points 1.07 times. A host that cannot spend 170 ms at once makes the grid in steps (`WorldClimate.Grid.step(n)`).

**Proof** (`climate_check.gd`, 9 checks): data clean, default gen 1; **gen 1 bit for bit** (12 slice hashes recorded from main before gen 2 existed); gen 2 pure (the same twice with a new planet object, differs between seeds and from gen 1); every column's stored biome is its fine biome's alias, and 4 Whole rings show 13 of the 15 fine biomes; temperature falls with latitude and height; **a range casts a rain shadow** (over 44 climbs of the westerlies the carried moisture 4 cells downwind is 0.01 against 0.61 on the windward side); **the coast is wetter than the interior** (0.71 within 2 cells of the sea, 0.13 14 or more cells inland); the cost against gen 1; the same places in either generation.

**Maps** (`stills/gen2-w1-maps.png`, from `planet_map.gd`, four seeds, columns: gen 1, gen 2 as the renderer sees it, gen 2 fine; no browser needed: the tool draws the planet function into a PNG).

**Not in a match yet.** A match reads `WorldPlanet.make(seed)` at many places, so a match's generation has to be set like its arena (`S.planetGen`, kept by `newMatch` from `setup.planetGen`, re-asserted by `step`): two lines in `sim/core/sim.gd` and one in `state.gd` (Simulation's). Until then gen 2 is for tools, maps and scratch stills.

## Window 2: ground profiles, trees, cover, props (`sim/world/ground.gd`, `data/biomes/ground.json`)

A fine biome now means something to a fight. `ground.json` gives each of the 15 a **profile**, read only by a match on a gen 2 planet (`WorldBiomes.fineCols` set); a gen 1 match never reaches it and is bit for bit what it was (six recorded match hashes: planets 7, 3, 25 and 11 at three ring sizes, the hand-made world and a gen 1 turn).

- **What it does to a body** (multipliers on contact.json's own numbers, per column, built when the world is): `grip` on the brake (mud 1.5, ice 0.45: over the same flat ground a fast skid runs 2,398 units on ice, 1,790 on plains, 1,598 in a wetland), `absorb` on the bounce's vertical keep (wetland 0.55, beach 0.7: a steep launch goes 219 over a wetland, 256 on plains), `rough` on the tumble band (rough ground, jungle 1.3 and volcanic 1.5, tumbles a body that skids on plains), `furrow` on the trench and berm (soft ground cuts deeper, rock and ice shallower). `surface` stays one of contact's three (soil, sand, rock) because the renderer's effects and Audio know those; ice and sea ice are rock with a low grip.
- **What grows.** A planet match laid no trees (`S.trees` was cleared), so forest cover never worked on a planet. A gen 2 ring now grows trees: each treed column draws a keyed chance (COL over the biome's mean gap) for a tree at a keyed place and height (`SimRng.keyed`, so a column's tree is the same whatever else changed; the seed is the ring's slice hash). Forest as genWorld's (16 to 40 apart, 46 to 110 tall), jungle denser and taller, taiga dense and conifer-tall, savanna sparse, wetland and beach few and low (reeds' trees, palms), none on tundra, desert, rock, ice.
- **Where a fighter hides** comes from the profile: canopy (jungle, taiga, forest: a tree near and low among it), ridge (mountains, highland, volcanic), and the sea as before. Savanna, tundra, wetland, beach and ice give none. The cover finder (`WorldCover.nearestCover`) reads the same profile so the AI never heads for cover that is not there.
- **Props** (rocks, ferns, cacti, reeds, driftwood, ice spires, vents and the like, per 1,000 units, a size range): `WorldGround.propsIn(fine, stored, base, seed, i0, i1)` lays them from the columns by keyed draws, a pure function; the sim never reads them, the renderer may.
- **The turn** (calm-turn): the fine columns blend with the same weight as the stored ones, S.planetRing carries them (`fine`, unhashed, so a state carried into another process rebuilds the same swap), and the trees are the old ring's where the ground is kept and the new ring's where it is new, with the new ring's laid in the preparation (the assembly step), so the swap pays nothing.
- **A match chooses its generation**: `setup.planetGen` (1 or 2; absent is the data's `gen`, 1). `WorldPlanetWorld.gen` sets it and resets the fine columns. With two sims alive in one process the statics follow whichever built last (the same as before for planetCols; Simulation's step re-assert would add `WorldClimate.curGen` and `fineCols` to the block that restores `planetCols`; optional, listed in the report).

**Looks.** `stills/gen2-w2-strips.png` (from `ground_strip.gd`): for seeds 7, 3, 11 and 25, the ring a match plays on, gen 1 above gen 2: height coloured by biome, trees as dark ticks, props as dots. Gen 2 reads: forest and jungle (the darker green) with trees standing on the slopes, savanna and tundra bands, cream beach strips at the coasts, highland on the heights; gen 1 the same ring with four biomes, bare. Rings that reach the polar band (seeds 7 and 25) also differ in height there (window 1's smaller caps). The renderer still draws every tree as one cone and every biome as its alias until Rendering and Art learn the new looks (a tree's kind is readable from its column in `WorldBiomes.fineCols`).
