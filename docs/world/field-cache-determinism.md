# The field cache's read-back is the fresh bake: the fault, the fix, the check (World, 2026-11-14; EP URGENT, from Simulation's fork_check, planet 12)

**Fault (Simulation's finding, reproduced).** With `field.json plans.everywhere` on, a world read back from `user://field-cache/` differed from a fresh bake in the last bits after a swing re-laid part of the planet: 72 ground columns by about 1e-13 and 5 or more building positions by about 3e-11; landings grew it until the state hash parted. Two PCs or two CI runs with different cache states played the same match differently.

**Cause (found by bisecting the blobs, not the data).** Not a stored value that loses bits and not a summation order. The two cache files are the planet (150 KB) and the **places** (2.8 MB, `plan.gd placesKey`). With only the planet blob the match is identical to a cold one; with only the places blob it differs. The places blob is lossless (written and read back in one process: equal). What differs is the layout a fresh lay makes against the one the blob holds, in `v` and `dir` by one unit in the last place, in every building of the place. In one process, `geometry()` called again after `WorldPlan._memo.clear()` gives different `v` and `dir` from what it gave before, though it is deterministic when its first call is the only one. The cause is the memo key: `int(round(shift / 8.0))`. `geometry(P, id, shift)` lays a place with its exact `shift` (the first ring's aligned shift, `lay`, a tiny non-zero number) or with 0.0 (`layFree`, a free place), and the key rounded both to the same bucket: **the layout made for whichever call came first answered the other**. A cold match laid a place first by `lay` (aligned, shift s) and later by `layFree` from that same memo entry, and stored that layout in the blob; a warm match restored the place from the blob and so never ran `lay` first in that bucket: later calls got a layout made for another shift. History decided the last bits.

**Fix.** The key carries the shift's exact bits (`PackedFloat64Array([shift]).to_byte_array().decode_s64(0)`), so the layout is a pure function of the shift it is made for, and a memo hit is what a fresh make would give. `PLACES_REV` goes 1 to 2: blobs written by the old key hold the history-dependent layouts and must not be read. The cache key, the planet blob and every other value are unchanged.

**Cost.** The geometry memo holds one layout per (planet, place, exact shift), cleared whole at 192: the first ring's aligned shift differs from ring to ring, so a first-ring place is laid once per ring, as it was in practice (the old bucket seldom matched across rings: it needed shifts within 4 units); a free place always has shift 0.0 and is cached exactly. Not timed (shared machine).

**What moves.** Nothing in the golden (it is unchanged on the fixed tree). A match on the aligned plan path whose first ring's shift fell in a bucket already used by another caller now lays the place for its own shift: those matches' building positions move by the difference in the last bits (155 lines of the 23,000 numbers of planet 12's swing). It is a determinism fix, so any recorded replay of an everywhere match in that case is superseded.

**The check.** `sim/world/tools/cache_warm_check.gd` (`CACHEWARM`, in CI): one match twice (planet 12, a rematch first, ten ticks, a 40 degree swing), the second from the cache the first filled, with `plans.everywhere` on; it compares every building's x, z, h, w, d and every ground column of the strip (23,326 numbers), bit for bit: 0 differ. Negative control (`--negative-control` puts the old key back, `WorldPlan.shiftBucketOverride`): **157 numbers differ, the largest 2.9e-11**, which is Simulation's finding; the check passes when it does.
