# The gen 2 ring read and the planet's load in steps (World, window 20, 2026-10-26)

On main (landed with its grant; the later add-on, the B plates in steps and the calm turn's read, too). Everything here gives the same planet, the same ring and the same match as before, to the last bit, and runs behind no switch: the golden does not move.

## 1. The ring read (Performance: gen 2 130 us a column at x1, gen 1 about 50; target under 60)

**What the cost is.** A land column of a gen 2 ring runs about 28 octaves of the lattice noise (continents and relief, the plate edges' ridges and shoreline, the polar edge, temperature, moisture, the jitter, the volcanic field, the surface detail): about 25 of its 51 native microseconds. The rest is the plate pairs (7), the trig (3), and the climate's and biome's arithmetic. Native, this PC: gen 1 16.5 us a column, gen 2 46.6 (a mixed bench ring) and 51 to 57 (the calm turn's own land rings).

**What was cut** (`mk_p20a.py`, `mk_p20b.py`, `mk_p20c.py`): the noise written out in the octave loop (no call an octave); the climate data's numbers kept as typed statics instead of dictionary reads a column (`WorldClimate._cache`); `readColumns` reading the planet's numbers once a call and taking the ordinary-ground path of `surfAt` inline; the plate pairs of `WorldLand._terms` over the plates that can pair (and a ball inside which it is known to add nothing). All the same arithmetic in the same order.

**What it bought.** The bench ring (`ring_bench`): 46.6 to 35.2-36.9 us a column (-21 to -24%; gen 1 16.5 to 14.4). The calm turn's real rings (`warm_ring_prof`, `epoch_prof`, 97% land): 57 to 54 us a column, the whole warm 295 to 285 ms for the 4,800 columns. **That is nowhere near the 60 us web target** (which needs about 22 us native): the 28 octaves at 0.9 us each are the cost, and a bit-exact octave cannot be made much cheaper in GDScript (an integer-floor variant measured no faster; the corner cache already hits 99% of octaves). The remaining levers are structural and not mine: fewer columns a tick in the state's `k` (a longer warm), reading a ring's columns only as the swap needs them, or a coarser gen 2 read (a different planet).

Proof: `read_digest.gd` (float64 heights, biomes and fine biomes of 24,000 points on four planets, both generations) and `ring_bench` hashes identical to the base for all of them; `planet_digest.gd` (places, grid, flow field and two touch-down slices, both generations, rivers on and off) identical to HEAD plus planet-load.

## 2. The planet's load in steps (EP's add-on)

Each piece goes on where it left off, bounded by the units a read gets (`SimRun.planetUnitsPerRead`, 250: about 5 ms; a unit is about 20 us).

| Piece | One call before | After |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| The wind's carry (ends the grid) | 17 to 26 ms | `Grid.step`: ten units a row of the wind, one a row of each blend |
| The flow field (rivers on) | 136 to 149 ms | `Flow.new(G, false)` and `step(ticks)`: setup, seeds, the flood (a pop is 15 ticks), drainage, runoff, count; a tick is 0.4 us; `PlanetJob.FLOW_CELLS` ticks a unit; `FLOW_COST` is now 7,400 units |
| A place's try-set | 72 to 80 ms (cities); the replacement sets up to 117 | `_tryRun` resumes a set at its next try (a try is 3, 6 or 14 units by how far it gets: measured 53, 124, 284 us), draw for draw |
| A touch-down's ring | one `sliceFor` call, 80 to 90 ms | `WorldPlanet.SliceJob`: the pick a circle a step (one circle is 96 surface reads, 4 to 6 ms), the columns a slice, the held places; memoised where `sliceFor` finds it |

Measured at a budget of 250 on this PC (the machine 35 to 40% busy): the planet job's longest call 148.8 to **7.7 ms**; the longest touch-down step 7.9 ms; a touch-down's match after the host's load is the cold one to the bit and starts in 5 ms. (7 to 8 ms is the 5 ms budget plus one try, one circle or a row of the wind.)

`WorldPlanet._planets` and `_slices` evict the oldest entry, not all of them: an eighth planet drops the first only.

**Not done here (done in section 3).** Simulation's `planet_load.gd` wants two lines (`grant_sim_load.py`): `spent += u` for the job's steps (the try-set floor of `PLACE_COST` units is gone) and `SliceJob` for a touch-down's ring.

Checks: `step_check.gd` (stepped equals whole at budgets 250, 37 and the whole, with and without rivers and for gen 1; `SliceJob` equals `sliceFor` for three touch-downs; the host's load then the match is the cold match; the eviction), `rivers_check.gd` (the stepped flow field equals the whole one at chunks of 1, 7, 1,000 and 20,000). Tools: `job_prof` (the job's calls), `try_prof`, `flow_prof`, `touch_prof`, `step_prof`, `warm_ring_prof`, `ring_prof`, `epoch_prof`.

## 3. Research's B plates in steps, and the calm turn's worst tick (the EP's second add-on)

**The B plates** (`WorldPlateB._make`, 46 ms one call; 49 to 55 on Performance's PC). 39 ms of it is the 2,000-point area sample (a nearest-cell search and a noise read each) and 5 ms a comparator sort of 2,000 pairs for one order statistic. `_make` is now `_makeBegin` (cells and drifts, 0.6 ms), `_makeArea` (a few points a call, about 20 us a point) and `_makeEnd` (the rest, about 1 ms); the land share's order statistic is read from a native sort of the values (the same value whatever the order of ties). `WorldPlateB.step(P, units)` makes a planet's plates a slice at a time (state in `Planet.platesBJob`); `PlanetJob.step` does it before the grid when B is on (the grid's first ground read would make them in one piece); `stateOf` is the three run to the end. Proof: the State's arrays and `lift` equal the whole make's to the bit (`step_check`, and `planet_digest` with B on: places, plates, lift, grid, flow and two slices identical to the base).

**With B on, a cell and a try are dearer** (ground by the plates' pairs): a grid cell costs 3 units (65 us) and a try 6, 10 or 32 units (119, 200, 649 us), set from `try_prof --b` and `job_prof --b`. At a budget of 250 with B and rivers on the planet job's longest call is 7.5 ms (it was 17 ms for the grid slice and up to 126 ms for a try-set).

**The calm turn's worst tick** (Simulation: about 10 ms, "probably `_prep`"). Profiled stage by stage (`epoch_prof` with every step timed; the timing was scratch only). At `k` 32 it is the two rings' reads: a ring's step is 2 to 3.3 ms and two meet in a tick. The preparation's own steps are small (over the 80 warm ticks: 1.2 ms for the weights, 4 for the hash, 0.9 for the first stage, 2.7 for the layouts, 1.4 for the assembly, 1.0 for the trees, 0.6 for the lakes; none over 1.3 ms). **At the lower read rate it is a different tick:** with `k` 12 shared (`warmTotal`, section 4) the mean tick is 0.8 ms and the worst, 8 ms, is **the tick the two rings complete together**: each ring's last columns, the fine biomes' blend and the held places (1.9 ms), the slice hash's biomes (0.4) and the preparation's first stage (0.5), twice. Section 4 moves the blend off that tick.

## 4. The calm turn's read for Simulation's K (`docs/architecture/calm-turn-read.md`; the EP's add-on)

All behind nothing new that changes a state at the data as shipped: with `warmTotal` absent or 0 the turn is the turn it was, to the bit (`calm_digest.gd`, 12 scenarios of a calm turn to its swap, gen 1 and gen 2, k 32 and 12, one and two rings, the gameplay hash every 100 ticks: identical to the base).

1. **`k` shared among the rings.** The turn data's `warmTotal` (`data/biomes/rings.json`, `turn`; read with a default, so the key is Simulation's to add with its schema line): at 1, `_warm` reads `k / rings` of each warm ring a tick (`WorldEpoch._share`); at 0 or absent, `k` of each, as always. A launch pick's two rings then cost a tick what a form pick's one does. With `k` 12: 6 columns of each a tick; a Half ring in 400 ticks.
2. **A ring is read TO its counted columns.** `_warm` reads each ring up to `w.cols + n` (`RingJob.readTo`), not "n more", and a ring counts as read by the state's count (never by how far the job has got), so the warm and the swap are on the same ticks whatever a host does. `_prep` hashes to the counted columns.
3. **`WorldEpoch.readAhead(S, units)`** steps the warm rings' jobs on past the count (the ring nearest to done first; `units` of about 20 us; a gen 2 column is about 3), for a host's idle time (Rendering's `SimHost`, between sim ticks). It changes no state: a whole turn to its swap with a host reading ahead 300 units a tick is the same state on the same tick (`calm_read_check.gd`; the job was up to 2,184 columns ahead of the count). `WorldEpoch.warmPlan(S)` returns `[columns read, ring length, columns counted]` a ring for a host that wants to see it.
4. **The ring's last step off the tick it completes on.** A warm ring's job is `lazy`: it reads the columns, and its blend of the fine biomes and its held places (`WorldPlanet.heldBegin` / `heldStep`, `WorldSites.blendBegin` / `blendStep`: the same arithmetic, a run of one biome at a time) are done in the preparation's first two ticks (`HELD_TICKS`: half the blend, then the rest and the places; the ring is memoised then), and the preparation proper has the other `prepTicks - 2` ticks. The ring is counted read on the same tick and the swap is allowed on the same tick (`prepTicks` after); the hash of the biomes waits for the blend. The state after the swap is the same (`calm_digest` above, and with `warmTotal` 1 the same as before this step). At `k` 12 shared, native, the worst tick goes **8.1 to 4.4 ms** (the layout of a fresh place, 2.3 ms, and the swap itself, 2.5 ms, are what is left).
5. **Skipping the carry zone's columns: not done, and not worth it.** The weights (`_w`) are 0 only inside `carryUnits / 4` = 3,000 units of the pivot (94 columns each way, **188 of a Half ring's 2,400: 8%, not 31%**); beyond that, out to 12,000, the new ring is blended in at a weight above 0 and its columns are read. And the swap's first stage reads the new ring's heights across every held place's span (`WorldPlanetWorld.chordsOf`), the places' totals (`wsum`) follow, and the slice hash covers the whole ring: a skipped column would change what the swap does. 8% of the warm for that risk: no.
6. **Not changed: `prepTicks` (6).** With the blend moved, no preparation step is over 2.5 ms; the 6 ticks stay. A larger `prepTicks` would spread the layouts thinner (a data change for Encounter and Game Design, and a later swap).
