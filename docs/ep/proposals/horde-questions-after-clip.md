## About the clip

There is one short clip, about six seconds: a second player joins a run and fires an energy blast that passes the first player and hits a weak fighter.

**It does not show a crowd.** The camera that can keep several fighters in the picture is not built yet, so the clip cannot show how the weak fighters arrive, how many are on you, or how busy the fight feels. Two questions below wait for a proper clip for that reason, and nothing on this page asks you to judge those things from the six seconds.

**Also not shown anywhere yet:** a weak fighter sent through a building (the trial build cannot do it), a player going down and getting up, and the result screen.

## What the trial found

The trial played 49 scripted runs alone and with two players, on your 9 November rules with top flying speed 3,500.

- **A run of eight waves lasts about seven and a half minutes alone** (7:29 to 7:43) and costs a careful player about a third of their deep health. That player never went down.
- **With two players it lasts about the same** (7:25 to 7:35), and each loses 15 to 21% of their deep health.
- **A swing starts at you a little more than once a second,** and each one is small. Two or three weak fighters are in your fight nearly all the time (91 to 98% of each wave alone).
- **Twelve of the most basic weak fighters fall in four to six seconds.** They are meant to fall in a blow or two.
- **Mashing X is the fastest and safest way through today.** A pad that only mashes X finishes in 4:38 and loses a tenth of its health, against seven and a half minutes and a third for the careful pad. Game Design has ruled that this is acceptable for the first version only: the tougher weak fighters get their own answers to mashing in the next step (one guards its front against light blows, one sidesteps the first), and guarding has to pay in Horde too.

## Settled since the first draft, so no answer is needed

| What | How it was settled |
| :--- | :--- |
| How hard and how often the weak fighters swing | Two versions were measured. They cost a player the same, and one is twice as busy with swings half as heavy. Game Design chose the busy one. You can still overrule it in question 9, once there is a clip that shows it |
| Giving your strength back between waves | Tried and dropped: it broke the rule that deep health never refills in a match, and one run lost almost half anyway |
| Two players had too little to fight | True in the first runs: each player had nobody in reach for up to 38% of a wave. Game Design made the weak fighters half again as tough and their swings a little closer together when two play, and a little fewer of them. Nobody is now without a rival for more than 28% of a wave |
| One player left standing could be swamped | When only one of two players is up, the weak fighters go back to the pace and toughness made for one, at once, and the rest of the wave is shortened. In ten test runs the player left up cleared the wave ten times, in 20 to 26 seconds |
| An energy blast could never knock a weak fighter out | True in the trial build: a blast lowers their health but never fells them. Game Design has ruled the fix for the real mode: a blast counts like a blow |

## The questions

One at a time. The first two are already on your sprint report and are worded the same here; answer them in either place.

### Already asked

**1. What is a Horde run?**
- **Eight waves you can beat** (about eight minutes). Clear the eighth and you have won.
- **Endless** until you fall. The result is how long you lasted.
- I think you would want eight waves first. It has an end to reach, the trial's runs came out at about seven and a half minutes, and an endless row can be added later from the same pieces.

**2. With two players, when one goes down:**
- **Up again when your partner finishes the wave.** In the trial that wait was 20 to 26 seconds from the middle of a wave.
- **Out for the rest of the run.**
- **Your partner gets you up** by staying beside you for a moment. New work, for later.
- I think you would want the first for now, with the third workshopped for later.

### Can be answered without a clip

**3. When a hit lands in Horde, what stops for a beat?** The trial plays the first option.
- **Only the fighter who was hit.** Everything else keeps moving.
- **The whole screen, as in a one-on-one fight.** Every hit has the weight you know, but with two players your fighter would stop each time your partner lands a hit.
- I think you would want only the fighter who was hit, because nothing should stop your fighter that you did not do.

**4. With a friend, should more than three weak fighters be on the field at once?**
- **Keep three,** tougher and busier when two play. This is what the trial measured, and it fits what the game can do today.
- **Four or five when two are playing.** It means lifting a limit in the game's engine: more work for four of the teams, and the frame rate on a Steam Deck becomes the thing to watch.
- I think you would want three for now. You said "GO" on three, the numbers with two players now hold, and it is easy to ask again once you have played it with a friend.

**5. Throwing weak fighters into each other: straight after the first version, or after Alpha?** This is what you described: send one into another and both go down, grab one and fly with them and throw them into the rest, hit one again while it is still in the air. Game Design has agreed it is the next thing built after the first version. None of it is in the trial or the clip.
- **Straight after the first version plays well,** before Alpha if it fits.
- **After Alpha.**
- I think you would want it straight after. You named these as what makes being outnumbered rewarding.

**6. Does knocking a weak fighter out give you a little energy?**
- **Yes, a little,** more for a tougher one.
- **No.** Landing hits already gives your stamina back in the new rules.
- I think you would want yes, a little. Game Design recommends it too.

**7. Does the result screen show how many you knocked out?**
- **Yes.** A number to beat next time, beside your time.
- **No.** Only the waves you cleared, your time, and what the fight did to the town.
- I think you would want yes, because a run needs something to beat. The word on screen would be "knocked out", and Legal reads the wording first.

### Waiting for a proper clip (do not answer yet)

These two are about how the fight looks and feels with a crowd on screen. They will be asked when a clip can show it, which needs the camera that keeps several fighters in the picture. That camera comes with the Horde's own milestone.

**8. How should a weak fighter arrive?** In the trial it streaks in from off the screen at about three times top flying speed, for a quarter of a second, and after that it always flies slower than you. The other ways would be from closer at normal flying speed (it would appear inside the picture, from nowhere), or from behind the fight, flying toward the screen (not tried).

**9. Is the fight the right amount of busy?** The trial's version has a swing start at you a little more than once a second, each one small. A calmer version (half as many swings, each twice as heavy) measured the same run length and the same cost in health.
