Every press, a different blow

A plan to make movesets varied, dynamic, emergent, and responsive. It covers what's there today, what's already decided, what's new, pseudocode, increments with goals and how each is measured, and what each costs.

The short version
  1. Your main attack input is the thinnest part of the moveset. The right stick has one quick and one charged blow per direction, so a quick flick down is the same blow every time. A fair test player using your scheme lost every match, easy included, mostly because its flicks were thrown away while it couldn't act. In your own play, though, the silence sat in heavy, signature, dodge and guard presses, not the stick.
  2. Two big pieces are already decided today, on your play.
    • The slam: a stick strike held to its top stage now sends, and down on a fighter in the air slams.
    • Never thrown away: a flick that can't start yet is held and becomes your first blow out. A planned exchange folds it in. Every refusal gets a word.
    This plan builds on both rather than re-deciding them.
  3. What's new here is composing each blow. Variety today is rotation: pick from a list, avoid the last two. Your press keeps deciding what a blow is: its weight, worth and direction. The game decides which blow of that kind you see, by scoring every candidate against the moment (his body, the world, the string and what you've just seen), then fitting it with a small bend. It's the "grammar, not a move list" answer I gave you on November 4, built.
  4. Six increments, responsiveness first, each with a number that says it worked and the tool that measures it. A scope table says plainly what's left for later: signatures and beams, forms and tiers, the other four stances, and 2v2.
  5. About 48M tokens for the six increments, a bit under half a week, spread over several builds. Four questions for you are at the end, one at a time.

1. Your four words, as goals you can measure

ResponsiveEvery press shows on the body within 3 ticks, and no press vanishes without a word. Measured in real presses, each counted once, with the computer's pace held fixed, so a slower computer can't pass it. Game Design's target for your-scheme test player: at least 70% on easy once its guard is fixed, read at 40 matches a cell. Your own silent presses, roughly 16 to 19% today, fall by press kind.
VariedThe same press in the same kind of moment shows at least 4 different blows over a few matches, the stick included. A blow's family repeats one of your last 3 under 10% of the time. The existing bands hold: the same three-blow series in under 10% of strings, and you can still name which cell a blow came from 80% of the time.
DynamicBlows visibly use the moment: a lean on a slope, a pin on the wall behind him, a send-off that matches the launch's direction. In staged pairs (a wall behind him or not, crouched or not), the fitting family wins most of the time, and the body or world term changes the pick in at least a quarter of blows. A launch reads at camera distance.
EmergentThe world joins the fight on its own, inside Game Design's bands: ricochets 2 to 8 a match, wall kicks 1 to 4, plus new wall pins and slope sweeps inside the brawl, and phrase finishes you can actually see. None of it is scripted. It happens because the moment offered it and your press took it.

3. What's new: compose the blow, don't just pick it

Your press keeps deciding what the blow is worth. The composer only chooses among blows of that cell, which share its timing and result, and never adds force, damage or reach.

How it answers the four words.

  • Varied, because freshness is scored on the blow's family (its key set and its silhouette), not its name. A re-aimed or hand-swapped copy counts as the same blow, as Animation found players read it.
  • Dynamic, because his body and the world are in the score. A crouched foe draws a rising blow, a foe on a wall draws a pin, and on a slope the sweep goes downhill.
  • Emergent, because nobody wrote "wall plus heavy equals pin". The world term made the pin the best fit at that moment, and your press asked for a heavy.
  • Responsive, because it runs on the press tick and never delays your press or swaps it for a different kind of blow.

The worth stays fixed, and here's how that holds in the code. In the brawl, timing belongs to the cell today: a light makes contact 2 ticks after the press, whichever piece plays. The generator's per-path beat points are unused. Two things do read the piece and could move worth: launch-anywhere's power reads the target region, and its ender reads the blow's family and path. Both are off today. Before they go on, either they read the cell's class instead of the piece, or the composer only swaps within the same target region and family. That's Game Design's call, and it's in section 9. Also kept: your rule that each press is one blow, the hard last-two and gut rules, the keyed randomness so replays and LAN stay exact, and your identity split: the Protagonist's palms, blade hands and arcs, and the Rival's fists, plates and straight lines.

Try it: same press, different moment

A toy with made-up scores, to show the idea. "today" pieces exist now, "move lane" pieces exist in another lane, and "new" pieces don't exist yet. His last two blows are never allowed again; beyond that, freshness lowers recent ones. The quick-down lists are the ground cell: a down flick thrown from the air is a different cell (question 2).

6. The stick: depth, the slam, and when the blow is chosen

Names are illustrative except where they already exist: the Rival's hammer, dropping elbow and plate drop are in his move lane today. Every blow in a direction keeps that direction's job: up floats him, down drives him down, toward pushes him back, away turns him past you.
  • The press decides the direction and its job, as now. The composer decides which blow of that family you see. On a crouched foe, down becomes the hammer. Against a wall, toward becomes a shove into it. On a slope, the sweep goes downhill.
  • When the blow is chosen.
    • Quick blows are chosen on the press tick. The pre-read crossfades into the chosen blow's own 7-tick wind-up, so a quick family can mix limbs and paths. A held flick is chosen on his first free tick, since he shows no chamber while he can't act.
    • Charged blows share their direction's charge stance. A charge holds its pose for up to 68 ticks, so the held layer draws the direction's charge stance (a render change, Animation's), and every charged candidate starts from that stance. The composer picks among them at release, using the moment as it is then.
  • The slam is Game Design's ruling: a full hold sends, down in the air slams, and down on the ground pounds him in. Its looks start from the down charge stance (the arm cocked beside the head) in each fighter's school: a blade drive or a two-hand palm drive for the Protagonist, a hammer or plate drop for the Rival. Encounter's parked quick-flick slam goes against the ruling and should be re-cut, not landed.
  • The computer uses the stick too. Today it never throws stick strikes.
  • The content, honestly sized. Spins and charged pieces are hand-drawn. First, Animation's own advice: read a pool built from the existing library with you, and count reskins, before drawing new. Then fill to 6 quick and 2 to 3 charged per direction. The Rival's move-lane pieces (hammer, dropping elbow, plate drop) need re-timing to the stick's 7-tick wind-up. His "away" has few legal shapes in his school, so his count may be lower. That's about 4 to 5 Animation sessions for the pair, inside the grammar's cap of about 175 to 180 shapes a fighter.

7. The composer, in pseudocode

Choosing the blow (sim, deterministic, explainable) 
# DirCompose (new). Called inside brawl.gd's _cellPiece, and stick.gd's piece choice at release,
# behind alchemy.json "compose": {"enabled": false}. With it off, today's keyed walk runs unchanged.
# On main: DirRecipe.pickWhere, SimRng.keyed, SimEvents.feed, SimWrap.sdx, DirBrawl._g(f, BLOWS), the roster helpers.
# New: DirCompose and its helpers, DirRecipe.tags, cfg weights in alchemy.json, and the family ring in dirI slots
# past DirBrawl's END, sized only when compose is on, so with it off the hash and the golden don't move.
# It chooses only among pieces of the cell (same timing and result). It never reads or writes worth.

static func compose(S, A, D, cell: String, pool: Array, chamber: int) -> String:
    var ctx = _context(S, A, D)                        # read-only facts, float64, his own fight only
    var best = ""
    var bestS = -INF
    var parts = {}
    for i in range(pool.size()):
        var p = pool[i]
        var t = DirRecipe.tags(p)                      # (new) limb, path, target, wallUse, school, family, chamber
        if not _allowed(S, A, t, ctx, chamber): continue   # HARD: not his last two pieces, not the gut twice,
                                                       # downAir, a broken limb moves the blow, a charged blow
                                                       # shares the direction's charge stance, same target/family
                                                       # where launch anywhere reads them (section 9), and the
                                                       # stick's send first: t.sends.has(want) while any piece
                                                       # has it, then '' (today's fallback order, brawl.gd:1105)
        var b = _fitBody(t, ctx)                       # crouch, guard side, stagger, in the air above
        var w = _fitWorld(t, ctx)                      # wall or building behind him, slope, water, edge
        var g = _fitString(t, ctx)                     # opener, builder, ender; flow unlocks showpieces
        var r = _fresh(A, t)                           # family memory, below
        var y = _school(A, t)                          # his school; mood, injury, form later
        var s = b + w + g + r + y
        var key = S.dirS.exN * 4096 + DirBrawl._g(A, DirBrawl.BLOWS) * 16 + _slot(S, A)
        s += cfg.noise * SimRng.keyed(S.game.seed, "compose", key * 64 + i)   # per candidate, per fighter
        if s > bestS:
            bestS = s
            best = p
            parts = {"body": b, "world": w, "string": g, "fresh": r, "school": y}
    if best == "":
        return ""                                      # caller falls back to today's _cellPiece, unchanged
    _remember(A, DirRecipe.tags(best).family)          # undone wherever PIECE_1 and GUT are undone today
    SimEvents.feed(S, "COMPOSE", "%s %s %s" % [A.name, best, parts])
    return best

# Freshness by FAMILY: a key set and its re-aimed or hand-swapped variants share one family,
# because players read the silhouette and the path, not the hand or the height.
static func _fresh(A, t) -> float:
    var s = 0.0
    for k in range(FAMILY_RING):                       # his last 6 families
        if _ring(A, k) == t.family:
            s -= cfg.familyW * (1.0 - float(k) / FAMILY_RING)
    return s

static func _fitWorld(t, ctx) -> float:
    var s = 0.0
    if ctx.wallBehindFoe and t.wallUse == WALL_PIN: s += cfg.pinW
    if ctx.slopeToward < -0.15 and (t.path == "arc_in" or t.path == "line") and t.target == LOW:
        s += cfg.slopeW                                # a low line or arc goes downhill
    if ctx.water and t.splash: s += cfg.waterW
    if ctx.edgeBehindFoe and t.sends.has("across"): s += cfg.edgeW
    return s
