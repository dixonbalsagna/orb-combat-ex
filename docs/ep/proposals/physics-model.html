Fights by physics

A first sketch of Orb's idea, 2026-11-02. These are first numbers only: nothing is built, and nothing has been played. They use the energy model the team already has (power-scale.md, the reach Simulation measured), with in-world units in place of newtons and grams.

Your idea, as I understand it

  • Every attack has two base numbers: damage, which takes health, and drive, which is how hard it pushes the body. Drive grows with the attacker's power and form.
  • Every fighter has a weight. The same hit sends a lighter fighter faster and further, while a heavy one needs more drive to move at all.
  • Outcomes come from thresholds, not scripts. Below the target's footing, a hit only staggers. Above it, the hit knocks them back. Further up, it launches. More drive means a longer flight, a bigger landing crater, and a longer, wider trench.
  • The meteor isn't a special move. It's an ordinary launch that clears the top threshold, so the body goes far and fast enough to arc into the upper sky. Only a max-form fighter at full power can deliver that much drive.
  • The aim is emergence. Fights come out of the numbers, so the same rules give a brawl, a knock-back, a building smash or a meteor, depending on who hit whom and how hard.

In-world units

Heft (hf)
A fighter's mass. Both test fighters, Grist and Loupe, weigh 100.00 hf. A lighter fighter might be 70 hf, a heavy one 150.
Drive (dv)
The kinetic energy a hit puts into a body. 1 dv is a 100 hf body moving at 900 units a second, which is the unit the crater code already uses. A fighter's drive is their power's energy times the attack's share.
Launch speed
900 × √(drive × 100 / heft) units a second, which is just kinetic energy against weight.
Crater
Radius 160 × √(drive at impact) units, as in today's energy model. The trench runs along the skid, and as a first guess its length is a quarter of the carry.
Thresholds
Measured in dv per 100 hf, so a heavier body needs proportionally more:
Footing 0.75: below it, a hit staggers only (it would move the body under 780 a second).
Liftoff 2.00: at this drive (1,273 a second at 100 hf) the hit becomes a launch.
Skybreak 52: at this drive (6,490 a second at 100 hf) a launch becomes the meteor arc.

One curve decides every outcome

The dots mark a fully charged B at each power level. Only max form at full power crosses Skybreak, so the meteor emerges from the same launch rule as everything else.

Weight matters

The same full B landing on fighters of different heft. A 60 hf fighter hit by a max-form B goes 9,000 a second. A 180 hf fighter hit by the same blow goes 5,200, under Skybreak, so a heavy fighter can't be meteored even at max. A design question for later: should Skybreak scale with heft, as here, or stay a fixed speed?

What it tells us so far

  • Lights and flicks never launch. A light staggers until max form, and a flick until tier 3; after that they knock back, so the brawl stays a brawl with no tuning needed.
  • At base, a tapped B or a Y staggers. A B held past the first flash knocks back. A lit launcher or a full B launches, the full B at 2,000 a second, carrying about 3,000 units into the next block. This matches the press rules you have now, with physics deciding instead of a hidden rule.
  • One change this needs: a knock-back and a launch differ only by drive here, so a B held past the first flash needs a smaller share (0.3) than the lit launcher (0.5).
  • By tier 3, a lit launcher and a half-charged B launch at 3,000 to 3,600 a second. The fight starts spilling across districts.
  • At max form, only full-strength attacks cross Skybreak and become meteors: a full B, the signature, or a won beam struggle. A lit launcher at max launches about 11,000 units at 4,950 a second, but stays a launch.
  • Two existing pieces fit straight in: Combat's power shares become each attack's drive share, and World's energy craters are already this model.

Open questions for you

  1. Fighter heft: what range do you picture across the twelve? These pages assume 100 for both test fighters.
  2. Skybreak: should it be fixed in drive per heft, as here, so heavy fighters are harder to meteor, or a fixed speed for everyone?
  3. Should damage scale with drive too (a harder hit hurts more), or stay a separate number per attack?
  4. The unit names: Heft and Drive are placeholders, so rename them freely.
