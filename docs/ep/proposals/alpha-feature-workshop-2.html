Workshop, round two: the loop

From Orb's stand-in, 13 November 2026. It follows the first workshop page. The stand-in is an estimate of Orb and holds none of Orb's approvals. Everything here is a proposal: nothing is briefed or built.

The short version

You answered the three questions and added one idea. All four are now in the loop:

  • A new form eases injuries: yes. The hurt fighter's form meter fills faster: yes. Both were already in the first toy and stay.
  • Plain attacks refill stamina. Fists are the respite between the big moves, as you said. Well-timed blows refill it fastest; mashing barely does.
  • The core is the most important part, and separate from the body. The core is now the body's gate: while it holds, little gets through; once it's broken, everything does.

The toy below is updated with both.

1. Fists are the respite

"I think base attacks should help fill stamina, so attacking with normal face-button attacks should be a respite between special or signature attacks."

I read this as a rhythm: the big moves spend stamina, and plain blows that land earn it back. So a fight breathes: fireworks, then fists, then fireworks.

What the numbers do

After a signature and a special, stamina is at 55. To get it back:
Well-timed strings of three blows3 strings
Ordinary strings4 strings
Mashed strings11 strings
Backing off and restingabout 3 seconds out of the fight

Over about four and a half seconds: brawling with good timing returns 83 stamina, resting returns 65, and mashing returns 19.

Why I like where this lands:

  • It rewards staying in the fight. The fastest way to recover is to keep landing clean blows, not to fly away.
  • It gives timing a real edge over mashing, which you asked for in October, and which the game needs: today a button-masher does as well as a careful player against the easier computer.
  • It chains into your other rules. Clean fists refill stamina, high stamina speeds energy, energy pays for the next big move. Fists fuel the fireworks.

The risk to watch: the fighter landing blows gets stronger while the one being hit gets worse injuries at low stamina. That could run away with a fight. The ways back are still there: stamina returns when you break away, a new form refills it, and the hurt fighter reaches a form sooner. QA should measure how often the fighter who falls behind first still wins before this is switched on. The first lever, if it runs away, is what a blocked blow gives back.

One reading of mine to check: I took "respite between special or signature attacks" to mean specials and signatures cost stamina as well as energy. If you meant only that fists give it back, the costs come out and the rest stands.

2. The core guards the body

"core region should be the most important but perhaps separate."

So there are four parts that get injured, head, arms, legs and core, and one deeper number, the body, which is what a fighter finally runs out of. The core is the gate between them.

  • A hit on the head, arms or legs reaches the body only if that part is already injured, and how much depends on the core. With a sound core, about half as much gets through as before. With a broken core, twice as much.
  • A hit on the core itself always reaches the body a little, and more as the core fails: 5% through a sound core, rising to 80% through a broken one.
  • So the core is the part worth guarding and worth attacking. Break it and every other blow counts double.

How you read it with no bars: the core shows on the torso and in how a fighter holds themselves: an arm wrapped across the ribs, a hunch, a flinch when they turn. The body shows in how slowly they get up and how their voice sounds.

What it gives each fighter later: the gate numbers are rows in a fighter's data. A tough fighter has a core that holds longer; a fast one has a core that fails sooner but a body that lasts.

Things to try:

  • Charge to full energy, throw a signature and a special, then get your stamina back three ways: well-timed blows, mashing, and resting.
  • Take five heavies to the arms. Start again, take six to the core first, then the same five to the arms.
  • Get beaten down, then charge and transform, and watch what a new form gives back and what it doesn't.
For the team: what changed from round one, as pseudocode 
# data/fight/vitals.json (all starting values; per-fighter overrides allowed)
#   refill {light 3, medium 5, heavy 8}, timedMul 1.5, mashMul 0.35, blockedMul 0.5
#   specialCost 15, signatureCost 30, staminaRegen 12
#   spill [0, 0.12, 0.28, 0.5]   coreSpill [0.05, 0.25, 0.5, 0.8]   coreGate [0.6, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0]

static func on_plain_blow_landed(S, A, weight: String, read: int, blocked: bool) -> void:
    # a plain face-button blow, no stance held; "read" is the press grade Controls already gives
    var g = C.refill[weight]
    g *= C.timedMul if read >= READ_GOOD else (C.mashMul if read == READ_MASH else 1.0)
    if blocked: g *= C.blockedMul
    A.stamina = minf(A.staminaMax, A.stamina + g)          # a miss never calls this

static func body_share(D, region: int) -> float:
    var st = SimWounds.stage(D, region)
    if region == SimWounds.CORE: return C.coreSpill[st]
    return minf(0.9, C.spill[st] * C.coreGate[SimWounds.stage(D, SimWounds.CORE)])

Open for Game Design: whether the knockout rule moves from "core broken" to "body empty", which this implies; what a grab, a stick strike and an energy blow give back, since they are not plain face-button blows; and whether a blocked blow should give anything at all.
