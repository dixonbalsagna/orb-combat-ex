Twenty ideas for the fight

A brainstorm for the Alpha, from Orb's stand-in. 9 November 2026. The stand-in is an estimate of Orb and holds none of Orb's approvals: nothing here is decided, briefed or built. Every idea is original to this project.

Answered by Orb on 13 November. Orb's answers to the five questions and to each idea are recorded in the project's vision record, and worked through in the workshop page. In short: The Grudge is out; Take It Standing is replaced by Orb's own absorb idea; Weights Off, The Long Lap and What He Wants are for the campaign; A Miss Still Lands and The World Flinches First are for late-game fights. The cards below are as first written.

The two-minute version

You asked for about twenty features that could realistically be built around the Alpha, to bring out the feeling of an over-the-top martial arts anime fight. Here is how I got them, and what I would do with them.

  • Mapped the game first. Five readers went through the combat, the fighters' energy and physics, the world, the camera and sound, and the campaign groundwork, so every idea stands on something that exists.
  • Brainstormed from six angles. That gave 48 candidates. Each brainstormer had your standing rules and a list of everything you have already planned.
  • Picked twenty and checked each against the real code. One checker per idea wrote how it would be built.
  • Had three reviewers attack the set: one for your rules, one for overlap and originality, one for cost.

The honest headline: all twenty together would cost about 118 million tokens, and the reviewers found that several were already yours in another form. So this packet does not ask you to build twenty things. It recommends eight, tells you what the review changed about each, and puts five questions to you.

What the review taught me

1. "Shortly after Alpha" does not exist. Your Alpha plan freezes new features after Alpha. So every idea here carries one of three honest labels: Alpha needs this anyway; extra, needs your yes to join the Alpha plan; or logged for after launch.

2. With no bars, the game needs one language of signals. This is the most useful thing the brainstorm found, and I would treat it as a feature in its own right. Ten of these ideas each want to tell you something through the same few channels: the aura, the ground under the fighters, a rising sound, the pad. Turned on together, a flickering aura would mean hurt, or out of energy, or giving energy away, and you could not tell which. Before any of them is built, the team should write one table: which signal means what, with one owner each. A new idea that cannot get its own signal waits.

3. The pad is nearly full. RT held is already the charge, three special moves and both signatures. Several ideas wanted it too, and LB had four new timings proposed. The team should write one table for each before adding anything.

4. An even rhythm of light presses was claimed four ways. One idea paid you for it, one let your rival catch it, one stored it. Game Design has to say once what a steady rhythm means.

5. Many of the systems these ideas read are still switched off by default. Your builds turn them on; the project's main copy does not. Making the energy model and the weighted hit-freezes the default comes first.

6. Several ideas were already yours. The dive, the payback crater, the domino, the quake and the falling debris all exist on your lists in some form, and two more collide with moves you gave Loupe. I have said so on each card. That is a good sign about your instincts, and a reason to trust the existing plans.

Five questions for you

Each has what I think you would say. Your word replaces mine.

1. Take It Standing: for every fighter, or Loupe's alone?

You gave Loupe a move like it, and ruled it his alone. I think: everyone gets the timed version, and Loupe keeps his own held version as the stronger one. It is too good a moment to give one fighter.

2. Does greater power draw the weaker fighter in, or hold him off?

The gravity well as planned draws him in. A new idea here does the opposite. I think: keep the well. Being pulled toward someone stronger is the more dramatic of the two, and it is already your picture.

3. May a right-stick flick send a dashing attacker flying, using his own speed?

Today only B sends anyone flying. I think yes, as the one exception: it is his speed, not your blow, and it is exactly the "I did that" moment.

4. The henchmen domino: capped at 3 and 5 bodies, or until the speed runs out?

I think: until the speed runs out. You have said you prefer physics to caps, and a slower send makes a shorter chain by itself.

5. "Fight weakened, then unleash": Loupe's own thing, or everyone's?

I think: keep it Loupe's. For everyone else, plain handicaps in custom setup with no surge are cheap and still fun between friends.

1Stamina, Energy, Body first pitched as "Breath, Energy, Body"

Alpha needs this anywayabout 6.5M for the core; about 4.5M more for the later pieces

Three reserves stacked on each other: stamina for moving, energy for power, and the body itself. When one runs dry the next one down pays, so a spent fighter can still act, and you can see and hear what it costs.

The moment. Two fighters blast apart and hang there, shoulders heaving, auras thin. One is empty, throws the big one anyway, and you watch it cost him.

You press

Nothing new. Dashing, flying flat out, zip strikes, mashing and guarding under blows spend stamina. Stop spending for a third of a second and it floods back, fastest with your feet on the ground. Energy moves cost energy, as now.

You see and feel

No bars anywhere. Stamina reads on the body: heaving shoulders, a sagging posture, slower flat-out flight, panting, and a slow heartbeat in your own pad when you are low. Energy reads on the aura.

The rule. Stamina runs 0 to 100 and refills in seconds. Energy refills in tens of seconds. The body barely refills. Stamina you lack is paid from energy; out of stamina you are winded: blows at 0.8 strength and slower flight until you recover. Nothing you press is ever refused for stamina.

What the review changed

  • Renamed to "stamina", your own word. The game already uses "breath" for something else.
  • Split in two. Before Alpha: stamina, the fall-through to energy, being winded, the body and aura reads, and the bars removed. Later: the "second wind" for fighting through exhaustion, and burning your own body when energy is gone.
  • Burning your body needs its own deliberate press. As pitched it sat on a full squeeze of RT, which you already hold for charging and for both signatures, so it would have hurt you by accident.
  • The fall-through gets its own sound and pad cue, so flying hard never silently eats the energy you were saving for a beam.
  • Dropped "fresh bruises" (new damage that shakes off if you break away). The game already mends damage after a quiet spell, and that rule is already giving back more than its target.
  • The reviewers counted 321 places in the code that touch energy, 51 of them fixed numbers. Moving those into data is the first step, and it is real work.

The rule as first specified. Breath is a new reserve, 0 to 100, beside the existing energy (f.ki, 0 to 100, refilling 5 a second) and the existing body (wear on head, core, arms and legs; core broken at 90 is the brink). Starting prices in breath: flat-out flight 6 a second, an LT step 8, a mashed light 2, a single light 1, and a guarded blow costs breath at 0.08 per point of damage blocked (this replaces today's energy drain on guard, it is not added to it); the LT zip strike keeps its existing price but, if the owner agrees, pays it from breath. Breath refills at 22 a second after 20 ticks without spending, at half rate inside the rival's reach, 1.5 times with feet on the ground, and 0.85 times with battered legs or core. Fall-through: breath you lack is paid from energy at 2 energy per breath point, automatically; energy you lack is paid from the body at 1 point of core wear per 4 energy, but only while RT is squeezed to the stop, and never past one step short of a broken core, so nobody can put themselves on the brink. Without the squeeze an unaffordable press gets today's answer (a B becomes the strongest blow you can afford; a zip gives the 'no energy' knock), and motion is never refused: flat-out flight just falls to 0.75 speed and steps come out short. At zero breath the fighter is winded (blows at 0.8 strength) until breath is back over 30; while winded every blow thrown or taken adds one to a wall count, and at 10 the wall breaks: breath refills at once, its ceiling is 130 and it returns 1.25 times faster until the fight's act next changes, and each later break needs 1.6 times the count, so there is a rising price and no cap. Fresh bruises: 40 percent of each hit's wear is fresh; it counts at once toward stages and the brink, but mends at 4 points a second while the fighter is out of reach and has not been hit for 90 ticks; the next hit on that region sets it for good, and any launch-weight blow sets all four regions. You press

For the team: how it was first specified against the code

Written by the checker before the review. Where the review changed something, the list above wins.

The rule as first specified. One number s (0 to 1) is taken from the launched fighter's own speed at the send, on a log scale between the slowest send the power model can make (a quarter-knock at base power, about 707 units a second) and the fastest (everything at top power, 7,000); both ends are computed from data/fight/power.json, so the scale follows the power model whether its switch is on or off. Below a floor of s = 0.25 nothing moves; above it s is rescaled to 0..1. Width: the shared view's zoom is multiplied by 1 - 0.35 x s, eased in over 10 ticks after the hit-stop, held until the flight ends (at most 90 ticks), eased out over 36; the existing floors (no fighter under 3.2% of screen height, never more than 0.9 of the ring) are untouched, and a bigger event replaces a smaller one rather than stacking. Linger: on sends fast enough to trigger the launch follow (4,000 units a second today), the chase or split start is delayed 4 + 14 x s ticks, and ends at once if the body comes within 12% of a frame edge, if the split opens by its own rule, or 2 ticks before the air brake's start window when the brake is on. Height: the whole frame eases toward a low angle of up to 3 degrees as the gap between the two fighters' power levels goes from 0.15 to 0.6, moving at most 0.05 degrees a tick, limited by the existing keep-above-the-ground rule, and taking the lower of itself and any shot's own low angle (never the sum). Reduced Motion: no linger, no low angle, half the widening. All numbers live in a new data/camera/lens.json; the camera only reads sim state and events.

What the checker corrected from the first pitch. 1) Events do not carry the power model's energy: the launch event carries speed and tier (sim/core/fx.gd:702), contact events carry speed, and crater and embed carry World's impact scalar in a different unit. Changed: s comes from the sent body's own speed, which is the same log scale (speed is unit x root of energy x share), and needs no new sim field. This also removes the pitch's dependency on the power switch being on. 2) The hold as pitched (the body leaves the frame and is found by its trail) is the old launch hold, which was switched off on 2026-10-19 after the owner's playtest note about losing sight of the launched opponent, and it would hide a player's own fighter. Changed to a linger in which hitter and body stay in one widening view; it ends the moment the body nears a frame edge, when the split opens, or before the brake window. 3) Camera angle is one value for the whole frame (split_frame.gd:37), not per pane, so 'low under the stronger fighter' and 'each pane applies it separately' are not possible today. Every blow traded in a close brawl adds to a shared store of force, and whoever lands the launching blow sends the other flying with all of it. A long wild flurry ends in a much bigger launch, and both players know it.

The moment. Two fighters hammer each other in place while the ground under them cracks and sags, the hits climbing in pitch, until one heavy lands and the loser leaves like a cannon shot.

You press

Trade X and Y as now; each blow feeds the pot. Cash it with any blow that already launches: B on a staggered rival, or B held to its flash. To steal it: a perfect block with LB, then B.

You see and feel

Nothing is drawn. Near the ground the earth cracks, then sinks into a bowl that deepens. Anywhere, the hit sounds step up in pitch and the pad's thump gets deeper. The cashing blow holds a longer freeze.

The rule. Each blow adds by its weight; well-timed presses add more; blocked blows add half. An abandoned pot leaks away. Whoever sends the other out of the brawl takes all of their own share and three quarters of the rival's.

  • A full pot must still help a fully charged B. As pitched it added nothing on top of a full charge, which broke the promise. It should add energy, with your power level as the only ceiling.
  • The idea "Late-Landing Blows" is folded in as a look (see below): the same stored force, shown arriving late when you cash it by breaking away.

The rule as first specified. Each fighter in a brawl has a stake; the pot is the two stakes added. A blow adds to its thrower's stake by its weight (starting values: mashed light 0.015, tapped light 0.03, medium 0.06, heavy 0.10), times 1.25 for a press graded good and 1.5 for perfect, times 0.5 if it was blocked; a blow turned by a perfect block adds nothing. Hold your guard, drop it a split second before a punch lands, and your fighter takes the full hit without budging. The attacker is left stuck on his own fist while you move first.

The moment. A heavy punch lands square on a fighter who has just lowered his arms. The air blows out behind him, the ground splits under his feet, and when the dust thins he has not moved an inch.

You press

The moment. Two streaks cross in mid-air with a crack. The fighters land where the other stood, backs turned, perfectly still while the clouds keep drifting. A heartbeat later one of them folds.

You press

You see and feel

The rule. Each side's result is the strength of its blow times its power, times how close to the beat it let go. Within ten percent, both blows land. Otherwise the stronger lands harder and the other is cancelled.

The rule as first specified. A crossing starts when a fighter presses LT + X or Y while a rival's zip is aimed at him and has not arrived (its tell or way in), at least 2 ticks before arrival, and he can pay the zip's own price (20 ki strike, 30 ki heavy); otherwise the press is what it is today. Each side's edge = its blow's energy share (strike 0.25, heavy 0.5) x SimPower.energy x a release factor: 0.6 for a tap let go before landing, 0.8 early or late or still held, 1.0 good, 1.3 on the beat (the existing press-read window, 4 ticks either side). The winning blow goes through the normal hit, then a knock-back, or a full launch if it was a heavy or the edge ratio is 1.5 or more. Seeing Through It

Extra: bigger than it looksabout 8M in full; about 2M for a first step

Repeat yourself too often and the opponent visibly figures you out and catches it. But a computer that has "read" you is making a bet you can bait and punish.

The moment. A flurry of fists hammers a guard, the defender's posture goes loose and bored, and then the next punch simply stops dead in an open palm.

You press

Nothing new for the attacker: vary how you open, and break your rhythm when you feel him settle. As the defender against a steady flurry, tap guard in time with the fists; the second tap in time catches one.

You see and feel

The reader's stance changes: Loupe straightens and his lenses catch the light, Grist grins and shifts his feet. A short line at the screen edge, and a click in the pad.

The rule. The game counts each fighter's last eight openings. When one kind is at least three of them, the rival holds a read on it: an easier block against it and a much bigger payoff. Inside a brawl, a steady rhythm of light blows becomes predictable and can be caught.

What the review changed

  • Bigger than it was priced: two new counts per fighter, a new timed guard read, new poses, lines and a look, across seven teams.
  • It rests on a balance problem that is already failing. A button-masher wins 60% against the medium computer against a target of 35 to 50%. That gets fixed first.
  • The catch should use an input that already exists and does nothing: guard plus a flick, which was built for exactly "answer his flurry" and never wired in.
  • A cheap first step: the game already weakens a repeated move. Making that visible, with a tell and a sound, costs about 2M and adds no new rules.
  • Each fighter's tell is a row in that fighter's data, so a third fighter needs no new code. The line must be about this rival and this habit, in our own words.

The rule as first specified. Two books are kept per fighter in hashed director state, both built only from presses already made. Opener book: the last 8 exchange openers by family (light rush, heavy rush, zip, energy light, energy heavy; rows in data) inside 45 seconds; when one family is at least 3 entries and at least half the book, the rival holds a read on it. The holder's perfect-block window against that family is 4 ticks wider (this replaces, never stacks with, the existing 'stale' widening), and a perfect block made on the read is a read counter: 'decisive' freeze, 10 extra stagger ticks, and the riposte always launches; the read is then spent. Tempo book: inside a brawl, when a fighter's last 4 light presses have 3 gaps within 3 ticks of each other, he has a beat and his next contact is predictable; a defender's fresh LB tap within 2 ticks of that predicted contact (plus 1 tick of grace) is a read tap, and the second read tap in a row marks the blow as a perfect block, which today is a near-blind 2-tick guess against a flurry. A read cannot pay off until 30 ticks after its tell has played. Humans who hold a read get only the upside; the computer, which holds reads at thresholds set per level and per fighter (easy never reads openers and needs 8 steady presses, medium 4 openers and 5 presses, hard 3 and 4; Loupe one fewer, Grist one more), commits before your next press: right family or on-beat blow and it blocks perfectly for certain, anything else and it cannot perfect-block, reverse or step out of that exchange and you get a 24-tick opening, and its book on you clears. Breaking tempo simply dissolves the beat; the attacker's own price for uneven tapping is the blur string's existing cadence bonus.

The Domino, with no cap first pitched as "Skittle Sends"

Part of the horde mode's own planpriced inside the horde mode

In the horde mode, an enemy you send flying knocks the next enemy flying too, and that one the next, each slower than the last, until the speed runs out.

The moment. One heavy blow lands on the front thug of a mob strung across a plaza and the whole line peels away in turn, the last one tumbling through a shopfront.

You press

Nothing new. Land any blow that launches and hold the left stick the way you want the body to go. In co-op, one player bunches the swarm into a line and the other sends the front body through it.

You see and feel

Each body it meets is thrown along the line, slower every time. A big enemy stops the body dead. Each meeting has its own thud, lower and softer as the speed drops.

The rule. Each body passes on a share of its speed by weight and keeps the rest, so a chain dies out by itself. Player fighters are never struck this way.

What the review changed

  • This is "the domino", which is already designed for the henchmen, with caps of 3 bodies from a medium blow and 5 from a heavy. The new idea is to drop the caps and let the speed run out (question 4 below).
  • It cannot come before the horde mode exists. There is no horde code yet, and the game holds five bodies at most today.
  • The speed split comes from weight, on the one weight scale you approved, not a fixed number.

This needs your answer to question 4.

The rule as first specified. Every fighter definition may carry a crowd grade: light (can be bowled), heavy (stops a body), or none (never struck; Grist, Loupe and any player body have none, so friendly fire cannot happen). While a body is in launched flight at or above the line speed (start 1500 units a second, measured with the launch's traversal factor divided out, the same way the ricochet measures), its path this tick is swept against every standing body that is an enemy of whoever sent it and is in the same depth lane (contact radius start 120 units, two body radii). A light body it meets is sent along the line between the two centres at 0.6 of the striker's speed and takes damage in proportion to that speed (start 0.004 a unit); the striker keeps 0.4 of its speed and flies on. A heavy body takes the existing short knock-back away from the meeting point and the striker keeps only 0.2. A struck body is now a normal sent body, so it can strike the next one; a body already in flight is not struck again. Because each link has 0.6 of the last one's speed, a send dies under the line by itself (a 6000 send bowls about three bodies, a 3000 send one), so there is no count cap and no cooldown. The launch planner gains one scored candidate, INTO THE CROWD, aimed at the nearest light enemy within 12 body heights on the far side of the target, scored 16 plus 9 for each light body near that line up to the number the send's energy can reach; the existing stick cone still filters it, and a stick held toward it adds 30 so the player's lean wins. Bystanders near a chain only scatter, as now, and nothing scores from them.

Height Is Power

Fold into planned workno separate cost

The rule as first specified. While a fighter in free flight holds LT with the left stick at least 0.7 down, a separate "fall" number grows by 2,000 units a second squared, up to 0.6 of that fighter's flat-out speed for their level (about 3,000 units a second at base, reached in about 1.5 seconds and roughly 115 body heights of sky). The fall is added to how far the fighter moves each tick; it is not written into their velocity, so it cannot leak into the kick's kept top speed. When the stick leaves the dive, the fall bleeds away at 6,000 units a second squared, so pull-out height grows with the square of the fall. Sideways steering still follows the stick but covers up to 60% less ground at full fall. A B press while at least 3 body heights above the rival, with a banked fall of 0.15 or more (the peak of the last 30 ticks, because the director's approach replaces the fighter's own motion once B is pressed), plays the ordinary heavy against the defender's stance; if it lands, its launch is the existing straight-down CRATER SLAM with force from a new 'dive' share row (0.25 at the least, 1.0 at a full dive) and hit-stop weight 'launch', or 'launch_full' at a full dive. Landing the blow spends the fall. There is no energy cost and no cooldown: a diver who meets the ground with 0.3 of full fall or more digs their own crater, takes landing damage at half the usual rate, and is embedded for up to 30 ticks. Guard and dodge answers are the director's existing heavy-versus-stance outcomes; a dodged diver keeps their fall and has to pull out or crash. The dive runs only with the one energy model and the any-range LT dash switched on, because without them flight near a rival is the slow melee dash.

Late-Landing Blows

Fold into The Brawl Potno separate cost

Fast blows that seem to do nothing, then all arrive at once a moment later.

Why it is here

  • An originality problem. Land many quick blows that seem to do nothing, go still, then the victim jolts once per blow: that staging is one series' famous signature, and the pitch kept exactly the recognisable parts.
  • It is also the same loop as The Brawl Pot, built twice: store the light blows, cash them with a heavy, lose them to a well-timed guard.
  • My suggestion: keep one store, the pot. When you cash it by breaking away at speed, the force visibly catches up along your trail as one impact. No stand-still beat, no ripple. Legal and the creative rulebook see the staging on paper first.
For the team: how it was first specified against the code

Written by the checker before the review. Where the review changed something, the list above wins.

The rule as first specified. A brawl light is held back when its press continues an even cadence: the last two gaps between light taps are within 2 ticks of each other and each is 8 to 12 ticks long, so a mash at 6 ticks never qualifies and the first two taps land normally and show the rhythm. A zip strike's one blow is held back when Controls reads the zip as 'tech' (a press within the beat window of the arrival). A blocked, armoured or perfect-blocked blow is never held back. A held-back blow deals nothing yet: no damage, no freeze, no reel and no gain to the attacker's run, so the defender can swing straight back, and each blow the defender lands multiplies the stored total and count by 0.5. The store comes due when the attacker has a neutral stick and no button for 12 live ticks after their rhythm lapses (any stick or press before that cancels the settle and keeps the store), when the attacker lands a B heavy, or 120 live ticks after the first stored blow. Cashed by choice it lands as one hit worth the sum times (1 + 0.15 per extra blow, growing up to 8 blows); 1 to 2 blows reel, 3 to 5 knock back, 6 or more launch with a share of the attacker's power that rises with the count, and the freeze uses the existing light, heavy and launch rows. If the 120-tick fuse runs out it lands as the plain sum with a reel only, so nothing is lost but nothing is gained. A fresh LB press by the defender inside the 12-tick settle (and not in the anti-mash lockout) clears the store and staggers the attacker 12 ticks; a held guard simply takes the hit at the normal guard rate. All numbers are starting values in a new lateBlows block in data, off by default.

What the checker corrected from the first pitch. 1) The pitch's 'perfect timing read' does not exist for brawl lights: DirAlchemy.log forces timed false in a brawl and the blow's grade is 'none' (alchemy.gd:211-213, brawl.gd:930). The rule as first specified. A fighter who is in the existing charging state, with a partner within 3 body-heights (225 units, and in depth) and the left stick held at least half-way toward that partner, pours instead of charging: no energy refill and no power-ladder gain that tick, still slowed, still taking 1.35x damage, and any hit still ends it. Every 10 ticks of pouring the giver pays 5 energy (30 a second) and 4 of it (80 percent) is sent to the partner through the 2v2 cross mail as a new 'lend' kind, landing on the partner's fight's next live tick. What lands tops the partner's energy up to 100; the rest goes into a separate surplus pool (cap 50) that drains 10 a second, so the 100 cap in the existing code is never touched. While the surplus is 15 or more, a right-stick beam hold can reach one stage higher than the fighter's tier allows (re-read during the hold, so the beam grows as the pour arrives), prices are paid surplus-first, and at the top of the ladder the beam instead gets 1.15x damage and life. A fighter holding surplus cannot pour, and nobody can pour into a partner who is pouring, so energy cannot be ping-ponged. Second step, own sub-switch: in a beam struggle the surplus counts toward the opening head start, and the receiver's own on-beat surge is worth 1.5x if a packet landed within the last pulse (20 ticks); the giver never pushes the point directly. The pour stops with a named reason: let go, hit, empty (under 5 energy) or too far.

The Long Lap

Logged for after launcha new mode: more than 6M

A race once round the planet with all the fighting left on. Being punched forwards counts as progress; being punched backwards costs it.

Why it is here

  • It is a new mode priced as a feature. It needs a computer racer that does not exist and its own finish, and it is the one idea here that is a race, not a fight.
  • With the stamina rule above, every racer would be winded before half way, which may be the real race, but nobody has designed it.
  • My suggestion: keep it for after launch, as a campaign episode.
For the team: how it was first specified against the code

Written by the checker before the review. Where the review changed something, the list above wins.

The rule as first specified. With the lap switch on, each of the two principal fighters has a progress number that every live tick adds the signed shortest-arc distance they moved along the race direction: flying, being launched, sliding and zipping all count, and vertical movement counts for nothing. The first to reach laps x the arena's length wins (1 lap of 153,600 units on the full ring: about 31 seconds flat out at base power, about 22 at the top); a finisher KO still wins the match the normal way. Progress has no floor and no cap, and nothing gives a bonus for being hit: at base power a full send travels about 3,000 units a second along the ground against 5,000 for flat-out flight, so any hit costs time, a forward hit costs least, a vertical one is dead time and a backward one costs double; near top power a full send (about 10,500) outruns flight (7,000), so a blow from behind becomes a real slingshot. Because the gravity well is not built, the race carries its own slipstream until it is: a racer behind the rival on the course, both moving the right way, flies up to 1.2 times top speed, full inside 6,000 units and fading to nothing at 30,000. The lap match also turns on the energy model, LT flat-out at any range, the air brake and the "launch goes the way of the blow, only the stick picks the far side" rule, so the direction of every send is the attacker's choice. All progress lives in one optional block of match state that exists, and is hashed, only when the lap is on.

What the checker corrected from the first pitch. 1) The gravity well does not exist in code (no sim/core/well.gd, no SimWell anywhere), so "the well already acts as a slipstream" is false; with equal speeds a leader could never be caught. I added a small slipstream rule in the lap's own data, to be removed when the well lands. 2) SimFx.decisive means "a decisive exchange was won" and ends nothing; a lap win instead sets S.game.ko the way a KO does (so SimCore.ended, the slow-down and the camera's hold on the loser all work) and sends a new lap event. 3) The zip does not go to any side of the rival: it strikes from the side it started on. The real way to choose a send's direction is a full heavy with the stick held away, plus the existing sideFromBlowOnly switch so the planner never picks a direction for scenery. 4) Top speeds are not equal: one fighter's data speed is 0.95 against 1.0, and flat-out speed grows with power tier. I left this standing as character and as a pit-stop choice rather than forcing it equal. 5) Flat-out speed at close range only applies with the energy model and the LT dash on (otherwise two racers side by side fly at about a tenth of traversal speed), so the lap setup must turn on power, dash and brake, all off on main. 6) "Being punched forwards is progress" is literally true but at low power a send is slower than flying, so it is the least bad hit, not a gain; the turn-your-back slingshot only becomes real near top power. 7) The "hidden 6x traversal boost" is not a problem with the energy model on: the factor is then the data's travel value (1.25 to 2.0) and progress counts the distance the body really covers. 8) Progress cannot reuse run state (no host calls it); it is its own optional block, hashed only when on, which keeps versus hashes untouched as the pitch wanted. 9) The campaign 'training_day' family is only an id with no host, so the first home is a LAN setup variant; the campaign episode waits for the mode shell.

Risks the checker listed:

  • Runaway leader: with the slipstream tuned too weak the first clean hit decides the race; too strong and leading is a punishment. The 1.2 share and 6,000 to 30,000 wake are guesses that need a QA batch.
  • A lap is short: about 31 seconds flat out at base power on the full ring. If it feels thin, laps is a data value, but three laps of one course may repeat scenery.
  • Powering up may dominate: top tier flies at 7,000 against 5,000, so the best line could be 'stop and charge first', which is dull to watch. Needs measuring.
  • LT does two jobs in this mode (flat out while flying, brake while sent). A racer holding LT who is hit forwards brakes without meaning to. It is learnable, but it must be felt on a pad before landing.
  • Reusing the KO end path means the end card, the KO pose and the KO sting will say 'knocked out' until UI, Animation and Audio read the new finish event; shipping the sim half alone would look wrong.
  • On field worlds the fight line can swing or the arena can turn at a calm moment (S.epoch, SimSwing). I did not verify that x stays continuous across such a turn; the lap setup should pin swing off and World must confirm, or progress could jump.

Ten more from the brainstorm, not yet checked

These were among the 48. Nobody has checked them against the code or your earlier picks, so treat them as sparks. Say which you like and I will have them checked.

The Standoff
Both players let go of everything and face each other. The fight goes quiet, a stake builds, and the first to move throws a single crossing blow the other can answer. Never forced on anyone.
Call Your Shot
During a taunt, point the right stick at a tower, a mountain or the sky. Put your rival through it within twenty seconds and it lands with everything behind it. Fail, and they cash in your boast.
The Wind-Up Throw
After a grab lands, circle the right stick to whirl your rival round and round. Let the stick go and they fly the way they were pointing. The longer you wind, the further they go and the more chances they get to tear loose.
What He Wants
Each computer opponent enters the match wanting two or three things: Grist wants to see your strongest form; Loupe wants his lenses untouched. They bend their choices to get them, so you beat a personality, not a difficulty number.
The Onlookers
People sheltering nearby comment from a small panel at the screen edge, and every line is true: "his legs are going", "he's still holding something back". The astonished witness, doing the job of the health bar the game refuses to draw.
The Band Follows
The music is cut into layers that switch on the beat from what the fight is doing. The brink strips it to bass and drums; a transformation hands the lead to that fighter's theme. You learn to hear who is winning. It fits the audio toolset you have queued.
Steam and Spray
A beam fired into the sea bursts upward as a geyser under whoever is there. A body slammed into the water raises a wall of spray that eats shots. Fights over the ocean get their own rules.
Old Scars Steer
A body knocked into an old beam trench is funnelled down its length like a ball in a gutter. Glassed ground is fast and rubble is slow. By the last minute the fight is steered by its own history.
The Boss's Home Tour
A boss whose fight changes chapter every time you truly hurt them: they drag it to a new part of their planet and change how they fight. The shape of a multi-episode fight, with no health bar and no script.
Stand in the Way
A boss fires giant beams at a town instead of at you, and the town's only shield is your body on the line. Every answer to a beam you already have is the mission.
