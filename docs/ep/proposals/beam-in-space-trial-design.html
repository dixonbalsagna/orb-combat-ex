The beam in space: the design for the first trial

From Orb's stand-in, 2026-11-14, for the EP, then Encounter, who builds the trial (the path's "spike S1": a throwaway build on a scratch copy). It turns Game Design's option A (docs/design/beam-physics.md, picked by Orb on 9 November: "A sounds good") into something a builder can follow. Design only: nothing here is built or decided, and every number is a first value in data. Code references are to main at def3445a; the beam code there is byte-for-byte the code of the 9 November build Orb played, and only data switches differ. The tables come from a small model, not from a Godot run. An independent review found faults in the first draft; what it changed is in section 18.

1. In plain words

"opponent fired a beam at me, I didn't even see it on my screen, seemed like it was way under me and it still counted and hurt me ... lets get a big overhaul to the beam physics" (Orb, 2026-11-08, docs/ep/vision.md:1675)
"beams should not be instant rays cast in a direction, they should appear to the player to have a velocity and project out from the fighter" (Orb, 2026-11-01, vision.md:1601)
  • A beam becomes a real thing in the world. It leaves your hand along the line your right stick pointed, and its front travels at a speed you can see.
  • It hits only what it touches. If it passes under you, it digs the ground under you and you are fine.
  • You get out of its way by moving. A beam never holds you in place and never moves you until it touches you. The timed dodge, the guard and the powerful block all still work, at the moment it reaches you. With the first numbers, getting out means already being on the move, or dashing: the beam is too fast to walk away from once it has left.
  • A bigger charge is a wider, faster, longer beam, so it is harder to get out of. A small beam at long range mostly makes someone move.
  • Firing plants you. You stand still while you pour the beam out, a third of a second for the smallest and a second and a half for a full one, so a miss is an opening for your rival.
  • Two beams that meet head-on start the struggle where they met. Fire back late and it starts closer to you, though never so close that it is over before you can push.
  • The computer plays by the same rule: it has to aim, it can miss, and it moves off your line when it sees you charging.

What the trial itself will show: the first five. The struggle from two beams meeting is in it only if time allows, and the computer gets its aim and a first sidestep on one level. The trial does not yet fix "I didn't even see it on my screen": that needs UI's edge-of-screen line switched on, which is behind the look gate (section 10).

2. What a beam is today, and why it felt arbitrary

  • A beam is an exchange with a named target, not a thing in space. Once it starts, both fighters are held (sim/director/exchange.gd:363-364). The hit is a beat scheduled for a fixed tick after the fire, and it runs wherever the defender is (beamplay.gd:292, opReach at 398).
  • No code tests the beam's line against a fighter in a one-on-one fight. The drawn front is paced to arrive on that tick (beamplay.gd:308-313), from 36 to 60 ticks after the fire at any distance: "so it always arrives" (beamplay.gd:55-58).
  • The defender cannot move between the fire and the hit. Their stick moves them only during the 6-tick tell, by about half a body height. What decides it is a press: a dodge tap in the last 18 ticks, a fresh guard press in the last 12, or firing back.
  • That is Orb's Sunday: five of the computer's seven beams hit, four of them from 2,400 to 6,600 units with up to 2,850 units of height between the fighters, each about a second after the fire whatever the range (docs/qa/runs/1931a0f8/README.md:20-30).
  • What is already there to build on: the beam object is a fixed line with a front (SimState.Beam: origin, direction, length, front share p; sim/core/state.gd:757-779), the renderer already draws that front, and a line-against-body test exists for 2v2, where a beam of one fight hits the first enemy of the other fight on its line and stops there (sim/core/cross.gd:273-323).

3. The rule

Game Design's three questionsTodayIn the trial

Escapable: can you get out by moving?NoYes: be off the line by the beam's width when the front gets there

The agency side of it, since Orb's latest word on control is "PLAYER AGENCY MUST BE RESPECTED": a beam is something its shooter chose, so it may plant the shooter. It is not something the target chose, so it may not hold or move the target until it touches them. That one sentence is what takes the beam out of the exchange system.

5. Aim

  • The line is the right stick's direction at the release, as the beam controls already read it: 32 directions, 11.25 degrees apart, steadied over the last 3 ticks before the release (sim/input/strike_read.gd; DirBeamAim.thrown).
  • A small assist: if that direction is within the assist angle of the line to the rival, the beam is aimed exactly at where the rival is on the tick it fires. Outside it, the beam goes where you pointed. No leading, and no steering after it leaves.
  • The assist angle is a number to settle. The page Orb picked option A from said 15 degrees. The data has 35 (front.aim.assistDeg). With 32 stick directions, 15 is enough that pointing at someone always finds them; 35 forgives a stick pointed two directions off. The trial tries both, and it is Game Design's and Controls' number.
  • Up and down are real. A beam aimed up goes up. One aimed down digs the ground where it lands, as today.
  • It fires when you let go. Today a released beam waits up to 90 ticks for the target to have been free for 18, and then fires into whatever you are doing (row A-COMBAT-05, the late beam at tick 5220 of Orb's last match). A beam in space does not hold its target, so the wait goes: it fires 6 ticks after the release, or it is refused at once with the cue that says why (no energy, or you were hit).
  • The rule behind that wait stays, in another form. Game Design ruled after Orb's 11-06 play that "a beam should never come ... at a target who can't act" (docs/design/beams-third-play.md:47-51). So a beam in space passes through a body that cannot act: one that is launched, down or stunned, or has been free for less than 18 ticks. That keeps the rule without the wait, and it stops a beam being repeated on a body it has just launched. Game Design should rule on it; the other choice is to refuse the fire while the target cannot act.

  • Standing still gets you hit, at any range the beam reaches.
  • A small beam at range is for making someone move. Anyone already moving across it is safe from about 25 body heights out. Flying up or down is a little slower than flying sideways (0.85), so those margins are thinner.
  • Inside about 20 body heights no sidestep that starts after the fire gets out, at any stage. The path's test for Alpha asks for a beam dodged by moving "at near, middle and far range" (X-16). With these numbers the near range passes only for a defender who is already moving. Either the values slow down or the EP agrees that wording.
  • A full beam catches a fighter who is only drifting, at any range it reaches, and one who dashes a quarter second late out to 60 body heights.
  • Reacting to the fire with a walk never works; the front is too fast. You react to the charge, or you dash.
  • Up close the answers decide it: inside about 10 body heights nobody leaves the line of a beam they were standing on.
  • Game Design's bands to read the trial against: hit rate on a moving target beyond 3,000 units, 10 to 30%; inside 1,500, 40 to 70%; beams that hit someone they did not visibly touch, 0; beams whose source was not on the target's own screen and gave no cue at its edge, 0.

7. Contact: what happens when it touches you

The answers Orb asked for on 11-01 ("a brief window where the second player can dodge, powerful block, or fire a beam of their own") all stay as inputs. They are read on the tick the beam touches the body, not on a scheduled tick. But the window shrinks up close: the dodge and the fresh guard count in the 12 ticks before contact, and inside about 20 body heights the whole time from release to contact is 9 to 18 ticks. So up close an answer has to be pressed on the charge, not on the release.

  • The launch follows the beam's line, not the old line between the two fighters at the fire.
  • The point-blank shove is a switch, filmed both ways. Today, firing from inside 9 body heights throws the other fighter back to 9 with no damage (beamplay.gd:163-200). Game Design's reason was reaction time: the travel never takes less than 0.6 s, "what lets a person react", and the shove makes room for that (power-scale.md:329); option A's own page says "the point-blank throw-back stands". Against it: a shove is the game moving a body that nothing touched. Without it, nobody inside about 20 body heights can answer after the release. A third way is a longer tell when a rival is that close. This is Game Design's ruling to make before the clip, and the trial's data has all three (shove, closeTell).
  • The dodge no longer lifts you 300 units (beam.gd:184-192). You stay where you are and the beam goes through.
  • One touch per beam per body, on the tick the front crosses it.

The shooter

  • While charging: as built. Free, at a tenth of flying speed after 12 ticks, no dash, armoured against lights, no exchange and nobody held.
  • From the release: planted. No movement and no other action for the 6-tick tell plus the stage's "stays lit" time: 0.43 s for a stage 1, 1.6 s for a stage 6. (The beam itself lives a little longer, while its front finishes its reach.) The shooter stays in the ordinary free state and keeps the hold's armour, so the rival's attacks start normally against them; a blow that armour does not take, or a launch or a stun, cuts the beam off where its front is.
  • That makes a miss an opening, and a bigger beam a bigger bet. With the gravity well's snap, a rival who sidesteps a stage 6 can be on the shooter before they can move again.

8. What the struggle becomes

Today no beam ever meets another beam. Every struggle starts from the defender pressing an answer inside a window (in the tell, late while the beam "travels", or RB toward to fire back), and "where the fronts meet" is a share of the travel time, at most 0.3 past the middle (beamplay.gd:219-236).

In space: two beams struggle when each would have hit the other's shooter, and their fronts meet.
  • Each tick, for a beam of yours and a beam of your rival's: if your rival is on your beam's line, you are on theirs, and the two fronts between you have met, the two beams are replaced by the struggle.
  • It starts at the point where they met, as a share of the way between your hands. Fire at the same moment with equal beams and it starts in the middle. Fire back late and it starts nearer you (table below). The head start from power (tier, energy) is added as today.
  • The start is capped, as a late answer's is today: never more than 0.3 past the middle, and never within two pushes of either end. Without the cap a late stage 1 against a stage 6 would start at nine tenths and be over on the first pulse. Orb, 10-16: "it felt like the winner of the beam struggle was determined as soon as the beams were fired".
  • From there it is the built struggle, unchanged: 15 pulses, timed pushes, nobody gains by mashing, let go on the pulse to slip out (beam.gd:253-606). Both fighters are held for it. Both chose to fire, so that is a set piece each of them picked.
  • Beams that cross at an angle do not struggle. Each carries on and hits what it touches.

  • The answer "in the tell" and the "late answer" stop being separate rules. They are both just firing your own beam.
  • Two beams struggle only if each reaches the other's shooter. A stage 1 cannot struggle with anything from beyond its own 56 body heights; the bigger beam simply arrives.
  • The world: each beam carves as its front passes until they meet, then both stop. That is already Game Design's ruling ("the beams carve as normal until their fronts meet, then stop"; power-scale.md), and with real beams it finally has beams to stop. World's meeting-point crater code (struggleMeet, off) can then find them.

9. What the computer's beam play becomes

Today the computer's beam always arrives, so it never needed to aim, to judge a shot, or to move out of the way. It needs all three now, and the audit already asks that it use only inputs a player has (A-AI-07).

10. Seen

  • The charge is already drawn (the hand's glow grows with the hold). The stage steps are not drawn today; they go only to sound and, when switched on, rumble. Game Design's page asks for "stage pulses"; that needs VFX and the look gate, and it is not in the trial.
  • The front is already drawn from the beam's own front position, so once the sim moves it at a real speed the picture follows with no renderer change. One thing does need VFX: a beam's fade is timed from the fire, and a long beam would start fading before it arrives. The trial sets the beam's life to its travel time plus its "stays lit" time and reports how the tail looks.
  • On your screen. Game Design's page says a beam fired at you "brings its source into your frame, or shows Camera's steady edge cue". Two corrections from the records: the camera does not frame a shooter today, and the edge line is UI's, built and switched off (docs/ui/beam-answer-alert.md; feature answer_alert), with Legal's terms on it (RL-227: a steady thin line, never over the fighters). It listens for a cue that only the old scheduled beam sends.
  • So the trial sends two cues. When a charge reaches its first stage, every enemy inside that beam's reach gets beam_charge: that gives the edge line, the sound and the rumble the whole hold to work with, which is where the warning now lives. At the fire, anyone on the line gets beam_warn (the cue 2v2 beams already send) with the ticks until the front arrives; with these speeds that is only 3 to 30 ticks.
  • The trial does not by itself fix "I didn't even see it on my screen". A cue is an event, not a picture. For a shooter off your screen, what you would see is UI's edge line, and turning that on is UI's work behind the look gate. Until it is on, a beam from off screen gives less warning than today's, because it arrives sooner. The trial measures this (reading 6) so the gap is on the record.
  • Nothing is drawn on, over or between the fighters.

  • It needs the build's three beam switches on as well (front, front.ownMove, front.aim), all off in main's data today. Director switches have no setup key; a page build flips the data.

  • Any edit to interrupts.json changes the data key of every saved match, so this lands in a golden window, as the path already plans (GW2).

13. Pseudocode and where it hooks

GDScript-shaped. Float64, SimWrap.sdx(a, b) is b minus a, SimDetMath and SimMathx for math, no new random draws in the beam itself (the computer's choices use keyed draws, as the struggle's do).

The one structural fact. The game runs one exchange at a time (S.dirS.ex), and while one exists every other request waits (exchange.gd:257). So a beam that leaves the target free cannot be an exchange at all: a free target who could not press anything until the beam ended would be worse than today. The beam in space is fired like a shot (sim/core/shots.gd): no exchange, nobody held. Only the struggle, which both fighters chose, goes back into an exchange. 
# ---- sim/core/state.gd, class Beam (757-779): three new fields. Hash them only when set, as cross, first and
# stopAt already are (hash.gd:299-301), so a match with the switch off hashes as today. Snapshots copy them unasked.
var sv: float = 0.0      # the front's speed, units a tick; 0 = not a beam in space (today's schedule)
var hw: float = 0.0      # half the width that hits
var stage: int = 0

# ---- the switch: director switches have no setup key; a page build flips the data through tools/preview/switches.mjs
static func spaceOn() -> bool:   # needs the build's three beam switches too (front, ownMove, aim: all off in main's data)
	return DirBeamMove.on() and DirBeamAim.on() and bool(cfg().get("enabled", false))

# ---- sim/director/stick.gd (798-806) and beam_move.gd: the release, the tell, the fire
# Today the release calls released() and tryFire() on the same tick, and the 6-tick tell is the exchange's first beat.
# In space the tell is a count on the stick's own state:
#   released() sets M_PEND and M_PAT as today (beam_move.gd:247-248) and sets the plant: PLANT = now + releaseTell + lit ticks.
#   holdMove (stick.gd:248-253) returns 0.0 while spaceOn() and (pending or planted): no movement from the release on.
#     (Today it returns 1.0 while pending.)
#   ST_ON stays 3 until the plant ends, so armoured() (232-238) keeps the hold's armour through the pour.
#   _holdLost and holdHit (256-266) already fire when the shooter is launched, downed, stunned or hit through the armour:
#     in space they also cut the beam (stopAt = its front) and end the plant.

# ---- sim/director/beam_space.gd (new): class_name DirBeamSpace
static func _len(st: int) -> float:
	return float(cfg().length.base) + float(cfg().length.perStage) * (st - 1)      # 4,200 at stage 1, as the other two

static func fire(S: SimState, A, st: int) -> void:
	var c: Dictionary = cfg()                                      # interrupts.json beamPlays.space
	var D = DirBrawl.rivalOf(S, A)
	var ox: float = A.x
	var oy: float = A.y + 38.0
	var dir: PackedFloat64Array = _line(S, A, D, ox, oy)           # the thrown stick direction (B_THROW, cleared here), snapped onto
	                                                               #   D's chest when within space.assistDeg. DirBeamAim.wide is not used
	DirBeam.fireBeam(S, A, ox, oy, dir[0], dir[1], _len(st), "")   # beam.gd:658-671: returns nothing; it appends to S.beams
	var b = S.beams.back()
	b.sv = (float(c.frontSpeed.base) + float(c.frontSpeed.perStage) * (st - 1)) * SimConst.DT
	b.hw = float(c.hitHalfWidth.base) + float(c.hitHalfWidth.perStage) * (st - 1)

static func touch(S: SimState, b, s0: float, s1: float) -> void:

17. Risks, unknowns, and what I could not verify

  • The first speeds may be too fast to read. See question 2. The fix is data.
  • The ground does not stop a beam. Today a beam aimed down keeps going underground for its whole length and grooves every column above it, the same depth however deep it is (beam.gd:680-706). "It hits only what it touches" is then not true of the ground, and a fighter standing behind a hill can be hit through it. Orb agreed on 11-01 that a beam goes through a tower. Whether it goes through a mountain is World's and Game Design's to settle, and the trial should film one beam fired into a slope.
  • Depth. A beam carries no depth today (its depth fields are never set), so in a match with fighters in depth the line test reads depth zero. Depth is off in Orb's builds. It must be set before depth is turned on.
  • The planted shooter is new pressure on the shooter. 1.6 seconds planted for a full beam may be too much once the gravity well's snap can reach them. It is one number to turn (a share of the lit time).
  • A juggled fighter. As written, a beam passes through a launched body, by the "can't act" rule. That gives up beams as part of a juggle. Orb's own test question of 10-06 was "do energy attacks look cool as part of close-range combos?", so Game Design may want a beam to touch a launched body after all, once per launch. The trial should film both.
  • The turntable. The planet's turning waits while any beam is alive (swing.gd:32). Beam lives are 0.9 to 2.1 seconds here against 0.9 to 2.5 today.
  • The computer's "always aims right" reading is from the code and one match record, not from a run. The trial's first probe should confirm it before anything is built on it.
  • A third beam bug the mapping found, not run: a stick release that fires no beam leaves its thrown aim set, and the next signature can use it and fire wide (beam_aim.gd:79-80; stick.gd:799-808). Worth a row in Encounter's list whether or not this design is built.
  • Not verified by me: that cross.gd's landing code can be called for a body in the shooter's own fight without change; how VFX's tail and fade behave on a long-lived beam; whether the clip tool can show two switch settings side by side.
