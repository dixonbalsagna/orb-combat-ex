1. The loop: body, stamina, energy, injuries and forms

You gave four rules. Here they are as one loop, with a fifth piece you asked for: a reason to reach new forms.

Your four rules, with starting numbers

Your ruleAs a numberWhat it does to a fight
"high body gives a bonus to stamina regeneration"Stamina comes back at 22 a second, times 0.6 at an empty body, rising to 1.2 at a full one.A healthy fighter recovers their wind about twice as fast as a wrecked one.
"high stamina keeps energy gain boosted"All energy gain is times 0.75 at empty stamina, rising to 1.25 at full.Charging while rested is two thirds faster than charging while winded. Catch your breath first, then power up.
"taking damage with low stamina increases severity of injuries"Each hit's injury is times 1.0 at full stamina, rising to 1.6 at empty.Getting caught winded is what really hurts. Stamina is your armour.
"taking damage with injuries starts affecting body"None of a hit reaches the body through a sound part. Through a bruised part, 15% does; battered, 35%; broken, 60%.The body is safe until something is injured. After that, protecting the injured part matters.

The fifth piece: why you reach for a new form

  • A new form is the reset. Stamina refills at once and its ceiling rises by 15. Every injury eases by a step. The body does not heal: a form gets you back in the fight, it doesn't undo it.
  • The hurt fighter gets there sooner. Taking a hit fills your form meter faster the lower your body is, up to 1.8 times. Being beaten down is what pushes a fighter past their limit.
  • The rested fighter gets there sooner too, by the energy rule above. So both the fighter who is winning cleanly and the one being broken have their own road to the next form, and they arrive in different states.

How you read it with no bars

The first packet's biggest finding was that every hidden number needs its own signal. For these four:

NumberYou read it from
StaminaBreathing and posture: heaving shoulders, a sagging stance, slower flat-out flight. A slow heartbeat in your own pad when low.
EnergyThe aura: how large and how steady.
InjuriesThe part itself: an arm that hangs, a leg that drags, torn clothing there. You picked all of these in October.
BodyHow the fighter stands back up and how their voice sounds: slower to rise, strained lines. Damage on the torso that stays through a transformation, as you ruled.

What keeps it fair

  • It must not become a slide you can't climb out of. Three ways back: a new form resets stamina; the hurt fighter's form meter fills faster; and stamina always comes back in seconds if you break away.
  • No press is ever refused for stamina. Out of stamina you are winded: weaker blows, slower flight, and stamina you lack is paid from energy, with its own sound and pad cue so it is never silent.
  • Guarding costs stamina. So holding the guard forever drains the very thing that protects you, which is the rule you set in September: the defensive stance is punished more the longer it is held.
For the team: the rules as pseudocode 
# sim/core/vitals.gd (new), switch data/fight/vitals.json "enabled": false. All numbers are data.
static func regen_rate(f) -> float:                      # rule 1: body feeds stamina
    var r = C.staminaRegen * (C.bodyRegenLow + C.bodyRegenSpan * f.body / 100.0)
    if SimWounds.battered(f, SimWounds.LEGS): r *= C.legsMul
    return r

static func energy_mul(f) -> float:                      # rule 2: stamina feeds energy
    return C.energyLow + C.energySpan * f.stamina / f.staminaMax

static func on_hit(S, A, D, region: int, dmg: float, guarded: bool) -> void:
    var wear = dmg * (1.0 + C.severityMax * (1.0 - D.stamina / D.staminaMax))   # rule 3
    if guarded:
        wear *= C.guardTakes
        pay(S, D, STAMINA, dmg * C.guardCost, "guard")
    var to_body = wear * C.spill[SimWounds.stage(D, region)]                    # rule 4
    SimWounds.addWear(S, D, region, wear - to_body)
    D.body = maxf(0.0, D.body - to_body)
    SimForms.gain(S, D, dmg * C.formTake * (1.0 + C.formHurtBoost * (1.0 - D.body / 100.0)))
    SimForms.gain(S, A, dmg * C.formDeal)

static func on_form(S, f) -> void:                       # the reset
    f.staminaMax += C.formStamina;  f.stamina = f.staminaMax
    for r in SimWounds.REGIONS: SimWounds.ease(S, f, r, C.formMend)   # the body is not healed

static func pay(S, f, pool: int, cost: float, why: String) -> void:
    var take = minf(f.stamina, cost);  f.stamina -= take
    if cost > take:                                       # nothing is refused: energy pays, with its cue
        f.ki = maxf(0.0, f.ki - (cost - take) * C.fallThrough)
        SimFx.cue(S, f, "stamina_fallthrough", "", why)

Open for Game Design: whether "body" is a new number or the existing core region renamed (the toy treats it as its own number); how this replaces the two mends the wounds system already has; and the first step the reviewers named, moving the 51 fixed energy numbers in the code into data.

Three small questions

  1. Does a new form ease injuries, or only refill stamina? I think ease them by a step, so a transformation visibly straightens a fighter up. The body stays hurt either way.
  2. Should the hurt fighter's form meter fill faster? I think yes. It is the comeback you asked for in September ("the trailing fighter gets help"), and it makes being beaten down the road to a new form.
  3. Is the body its own number, or the fighter's core? I think its own number: head, arms and legs get injured, and the body is what's left to lose. Game Design should confirm it fits the knockout rule.

2. The Absorb (your version of Take It Standing)

"holding the LB guard and then timing hitting the energy RB that could be a way of absorbing the kinetic and some of the damage if timed well"

  • You press: hold LB to guard, then tap RB as the blow lands.
  • If you time it: the blow's force is swallowed. You are not knocked back or launched, and you take about 60% of the damage. It costs energy in proportion to the force you absorbed.
  • If you miss: an ordinary guarded hit, and the energy is still spent.
  • What you see: the fighter's own aura snaps tight around them and the ground under their feet takes the force instead. The attacker feels a hard dead stop in their pad.
  • Why it fits: in October you confirmed LB plus RB as its own combination, "defensive ki". This is its timed move. Every fighter has it, as you ruled.
  • Where each fighter differs: the window, the share of damage and the cost are rows in each fighter's data. Loupe's "On the Chin" stays his own stronger version: he can hold it, and what he absorbs feeds his climb.
  • One idea to try later: the force you absorbed is carried into your next blow. That would tie it to The Brawl Pot.

4. Debris you can fling

"I do like the idea of flinging debris at the opponent"

Drop the random rain. Keep the part you liked, and hang it on something you already picked: at high power, rubble floats up around a fighter.

  • The floating rubble is ammunition. Near a piece: B bats it at your rival, A grabs it to throw, a right-stick blast breaks it into a spray.
  • Big craters and broken buildings make more of it, so where the fight has been decides what there is to throw.
  • It is real: each piece is a body that flies and lands by the same rules as a launched fighter, on the weight scale you approved. A thrown piece hits like its weight and speed.
  • It never takes a press meant for your rival: only when your stick points at the piece.
  • Late-game by nature, because rubble only floats from the third power level up.

5. The late-game pair

You asked for A Miss Still Lands and The World Flinches First to arrive in high-level, late-game fights. Both can be switched on by power level in data:

  • A Miss Still Lands from the third power level: below it, a miss is just a miss.
  • The World Flinches First grows with power: a faint stir at low power, the full reaction of ground and water at the top. I'll ask for a clip for you before anything is decided, as you asked to see it in action.

7. The rest of your "workshop this" list

  • Slipstream. To keep it fun and fair, try the narrow version first: you can only ride the wake of a fighter you have just launched. It makes flying after them feel earned and feeds the mid-air rallies you asked for, and nobody running away gets chased down by a rule.
  • The Planet Rings. As destruction physics more than an attack: a huge slam sends a shock through the ground that topples buildings already damaged, sloshes water out of craters and shakes loose rubble for the flinging above. Fighters on the ground get a bounce, not a punishment. Only the biggest slams go right round.
  • Height Is Power. The honest version is the long strike aimed down: dive flat out and your real speed goes into the blow and the crater. Miss, and you make the crater yourself.
  • Late-Landing Blows. No time tricks: pure speed. Blows too fast for the eye, where the sound and the impact catch up a moment later. Best kept for the speedster you listed for the roster, or as a finishing move.
  • The Wind-Up Throw. Sound as pitched: after a grab lands, circle the right stick to whirl him, let go to throw. It fits what you said in October about the right stick taking patterns, and your thumb is free because the grab has already landed.
  • The Onlookers. As an option in settings, sparse, and never during a clash, so it can be turned off if it grates.
  • The Band Follows. It fits the audio toolset you have queued. The first question is practical: whether your music tool can give each track as separate layers. I'll ask for that to be checked.
