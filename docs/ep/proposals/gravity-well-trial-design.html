The gravity well: the design for the first trial

From Orb's stand-in, 2026-11-14, for the EP, then Simulation and Encounter, who build the trial (the path's "spike S2": a throwaway build on a scratch copy). Design only: nothing here is built or decided. Every number is a first value, kept in one data file. Code references are to main at def3445a; the fight code there is the same as in the 9 November build Orb played, and only the switches differ. The timing tables come from a small model of the code's formulas, not from a Godot run. An independent review found faults in the first draft; what it changed is in section 15.

1. In plain words

"KEEP WORKSHOPPING, PLAYER AGENCY MUST BE RESPECTED" (Orb, 2026-11-13, on "up close, does your stick move only you?"; docs/ep/vision.md:1800)
  • Every fighter has a gravity well around them. It grows when they transform, and while they power up.
  • Inside your rival's well, X, Y, B or a flick of the right stick snaps you in to them and the blow follows. It is the lunge you already have: the same short crouch first, so it can be seen coming. What is new is how far it reaches (a bigger well reaches further) and that a stronger fighter's snap flies faster.
  • Outside it, the same press is the long-range kit you already have: the taunt, the charge that flies in, bolts and beams.
  • Let go of everything inside a well and you drift together, gently. Touch the stick and the drift is gone on that same instant. After you have been knocked away it stays off until you make a move of your own.
  • Up close there are two versions to try side by side. Today your stick moves the whole fight, both of you. In the new version your stick moves only you, and nobody's stick ever moves the other player. Your rival stays with you by attacking, because every blow steps its thrower in.
  • One test for all of it: nothing moves your fighter except your own hands, a blow that lands on you, and (once it arrives) a rival's attack starting the close fight.

The trial builds both versions of the close fight behind one switch, so the question in section 13 can be put to Orb with something to watch and, I hope, to play.

3. The agency rule: what may move your fighter

This is the test every part of the design is held to. It is built from Orb's words and recorded answers:

  • "the worst is how pressing a single attack key at any distance essentially locks both fighters from performing actions as the attacker flies in and performs the attack." (10-02)
  • "players should always feel like they are able to control their character, with few exceptions like during knockbacks" (10-05)
  • Recorded answer, 10-05: "After a knock-back the re-close is always the player's own move (no automatic pull)."
  • "no situation where they feel confused as to why their character just started turning on their own" (10-31)
  • "when I fire a beam, the character slowly drifts in a direction, then fires. I don't like this." (11-01)
  • "sometimes engaging in close-up fights it felt like we weren't in control of our fighter" (11-08)

What "magnetic" means under this rule. Orb asked on 10-04 for brawls that hold together so fighters "trade continuous strings of blows without interruption". The fight holds together because attacking closes distance: each blow steps its thrower in, and inside a well a blow from further off snaps them in. The magnet lives in the presses, not in a force on the bodies.

  • 12.5 at tier 1 is today's middle band edge (bands.midBh), where the lunge already reaches. 20 is the cap Game Design accepted.
  • Tier is f.tier, 1 to 4, hashed already. When forms or the physics packet's weight land, they replace the tier term; the data keeps one line for it.
  • Charge is new: a count that rises over 30 ticks while the fighter powers up (RT held, state "charging") or holds a beam (f.beamCharge), and falls over 15 ticks after. There is no standing charge value on a fighter today, so the well keeps its own.
  • A swollen well is a dare, and the page should say so. Your own reach is set by your rival's well, not yours. So growing your well while you power up lets your rival snap in from further (15.6 plus the edge band, against 12.5 today) while you stand charging, and a blow that arrives on a charging fighter is the built charge-interrupt exchange. That fits the workshop table Orb read ("power up at your peril" inside a well), but it is a cost, and chargeGrow can be set to 0 if the trial says it punishes too hard.
  • The edge moves only on things you can see: a transformation, or a power-up starting and stopping. It does not creep with the power meter.
  • Speeds assume flight 3,500 as the default (Orb's decision 5, docs/design/flight-3500.md): flat out is 3,500 × 2.305 to the power of the level. The snap takes that speed from the level directly (S.powerFlat), not from flightSpeed, so stance, distance and the dash's switches cannot change it.

Each fighter asks one thing: am I inside the rival's well? Wells differ in size, so the two fighters can have different answers at the same moment.

  • That asymmetry is my reading, not Orb's decision. It was the packet's question 3; Game Design supports it; Orb has not answered it. Orb's 13 November answer in full: "I need this as a underlying mechanic to sell the idea of the fighters stepping in and engaging each other. within the gravity well they are using their close range attacks, outside the well each fighters' abilities are altered. lets workshop this and find a way to complement our systems and keep combat engaging and fair-feeling." It can also be read as "when either of you is inside the other's well, you both fight close". The trial carries that second reading as a switch (zones.either), and counts how often the bigger fighter's press became a taunt they did not want (section 12, row 9).
  • The edge band (3 body heights past the well) exists so there is no cliff: a tap just outside still snaps in.
  • A tap on a taunting rival is still the meeting (both rush to the middle), as built. The well does not change that.
  • How it is stored. The close band stays DirBands' own (slot 26 of the director's integers, with its hysteresis). The well answers "middle or far" for a press, per fighter, from a zone written once a tick. Four places read the band on a press's path and must take the well's answer (section 11). The computer's choices (ai.gd:282-299), flight strikes, the chase tap and the grab keep today's edges on purpose.

6. The draw

  • When: you are inside the rival's well, outside the brawl, in free flight, and silent. Silent means the stick as you hold it is at rest (read before the game's own gates, so a stagger cannot fake it), and no guard, dash, attack button, right-stick hold, beam charge or approach is in hand. No draw while either fighter is staggered, launched, down, or after a knockout.
  • Never after a knock-back. When something other than your own hands has moved you (a knock-back's slide or drift, a launch, a stagger), the draw stays off for you until you are free again and make an input of your own, and it stays off for your rival too until you do. So the re-close after a knock-back is always a player's own move, for both of them, as Orb's 10-05 answer has it.
  • How strong: at most a quarter of your plain flying speed, about 107 units a second at tier 1 (flat-out flight is 3,500). Nothing at the well's edge, full by half its radius. It fades in over 12 ticks of silence.
  • How it ends: it is applied as its own small movement, not through your fighter's speed, so there is no leftover drift: on the tick your stick moves, the draw moves you by nothing.
  • Where it stops: it eases out over the last body height before 2.5, the mark where today's approach also stops. It never pushes you into the rival.
  • What you see: two fighters who both let go drift from 12 body heights to striking distance in about 7 seconds, and from 8 in about 2. The pulling-together Orb pictured is mostly the snap; this is the quiet part.
  • Three settings for the trial: off; in silence only (this page); and Game Design's capped draw (a quarter of flight speed, halved on a fighter holding away). I think Orb would want the silent one, but that is a judgement, and it should be felt before it is treated as the rule.

7. The snap

The snap is today's approach with its reach and speed set by the well. Today a press in the middle band crouches for 6 ticks (10 for a heavy), then flies the attacker in at 4,000 units a second to 2.5 body heights from the rival, where the close fight takes over (DirBands.begin, sim/director/bands.gd:194-236; bands.lunge). The defender is free and unmoved until it arrives.

Ticks from the press to the blow landing (60 ticks a second): the crouch, the flight, up to 4 ticks of step-in, 2 ticks for a light to land. B keeps its 28-tick wind-up counted from the press, so it lands about 32 ticks after the press from anywhere inside 15 body heights, as today.

An option for the trial: a snap you can outrun

Today's rush cannot be outrun: it speeds up to arrive on time, and the gamble is the pursuit exchange that follows. An alternative worth one arm of the trial, off by default: the snap flies at its own speed and no faster, and if it falls 6 ticks behind schedule it ends as "outrun", with the attacker handed back their own flight and nobody held. In the model, at equal tiers a defender who starts dashing away before the snap arrives nearly always gets away (flat-out flight is 3,500 against the snap's 4,000), a tier 4 attacker catches a tier 1 defender who reacts a tenth of a second late from 9 body heights, and a tier 1 attacker almost never catches a fleeing tier 4. That turns the escape into a race, which pillar 3 warns against, so it is here as a comparison, not as my recommendation.

8. The close fight: two modes behind one switch

Today: your stick moves the fight

  • This came from Orb's own October calls: each stick nudges the centre of the fight (10-05), and both sticks pushed apart walk out (10-06).
  • Both fighters are held and neither body reads its own stick. One shared speed is the sum of both sticks, at 0.4 of flying speed each (172 units a second), and both bodies get it (brawl.gd _move 2865-2998, carryOn 3014).
  • You can drag the fight where you want it, toward a building or away from a crowd, and it never drifts apart while anyone is attacking.
  • Push away from a rival and you carry them with you. Against the computer your stick always wins at least half its push.
  • Ways out: both sticks held apart for 13 ticks; the escape (LT and away, 15 energy); a flight strike away (the exit Orb used most on 8 November: 11 of 26 brawl endings); a knock-back or launch; or nobody pressing an attack for a second, after which the fight lets go and both are stopped.
  • Stepping back while the rival winds up a heavy already moves only you ("giving ground", 5 body heights a second, to a limit of 2.4 past striking distance).

Proposed: your stick moves you

  • Each fighter's stick moves their own body, at the same 0.4 of flying speed as today, so the test changes one thing only. Up and down work the same way.
  • Nobody's stick moves the other player. A body is solid: walk into your rival and you stop.
  • The magnet works only while both sticks are at rest.
  • Your rival stays on you by attacking: each blow steps its thrower in, exactly as today.
  • Walking away from a rival who is not attacking: free. The gap opens as you walk, and the fight lets go at 4.5 body heights or after a second without an attack, and you keep your speed.
  • Walking away from one who is attacking: it costs you the hits. Hold away and you break clear on the third blow that reaches you, landed or guarded (a first value). A heavy that staggers you, or a grab, stops the walk. The escape (LT and away, 15 energy) is still the fast, safe way out.
  • Giving ground stays; past its limit you keep walking at your own pace instead of being held.
  • You steer the fight by hitting (the right stick's strikes already carry a rival 1, 2 or 4 body heights) or by backing off and being followed.

What the new mode gives up, said plainly

  • Dragging the fight with one stick. On 10-05 Orb asked that "each player should be able to nudge the center of attraction". In the new mode the stick alone no longer moves the rival, in any direction. You steer by hitting or by being followed.
  • A fight that cannot drift apart. Two players who both stop attacking separate sooner. One press snaps them back together.
  • Holding someone with lights alone. Three lights no longer keep a rival who wants to leave. That is a real change to pressure, and the number is Game Design's.

What must change in the code for the new mode

  1. _move (2865-2998): one speed per fighter in place of the shared one. The sum of the sticks, the half-push floor (2925-2940) and the both-sticks cancel go. Keep, per fighter: the live gate (2870: no stick during a zip's ticks in reach or a double hit), the stagger branch (2881-2889), and the building and slope tests on that body's own position.
  2. The rush freeze (2946-2948) becomes per body: a fighter with a rush does not move by their stick; the other keeps theirs.
  3. carryOn (3014-3023; also called after every blow from exchange.gd:480): writes each fighter's own speed.
  4. The arrival drift (begin 288-312): each fighter keeps half of their own speed, fading over 20 ticks, in place of the summed pair drift. Nobody is stopped dead by the other's arrival.
  5. The magnet (3903-3928): rests unless both sticks are at rest. It already rests while someone may give ground (3911).
  6. The counted gap. A wound-up blow is judged against a gap counted from the start of its wind-up (GAPV), which only giving ground updates today (2576, 2637; read at 1313-1315). Every own-body movement must be counted the same way, or heavies land from too far.
  7. Giving ground (_giveStep 2542-2639): inside its leash, as built. Past it, the fighter keeps their own walking speed, where today the step is projected back and the body stands still (2612-2613).
  8. The walk-out counter (3029-3071, 3862-3870) is skipped. over zeroes both fighters' speed for every reason but "walk" (464-468); in the new mode "apart", "idle" (3929-3930) and "broke" join that exemption, each fighter keeping their own speed.
  9. The break-away: a count on the leaver of blows that reached them while they held away, reset by their own attack or by letting go. It is counted in contact, on the guarded branch (1421) and the landed path (1408, 1511). "Held away" is the brawl's own angle test (_away, 3066-3071) read from the stick as held (heldMx, heldMy: a heavy's stagger zeroes the gated stick) for a player, and AI_PART for the computer, whose movement stick is kept out of its intent (3042-3043). At leaveAfterBlows the brawl ends with a new reason, "broke". "Left" is already the paid escape's reason (interrupt.gd:459) and the two must be counted apart.
  10. A body is solid: in a brawl in the new mode, the push-apart below 45 units (melee.gd:129-134, sideways only, half each today) moves back only the body whose own stick is pushing in; with both pushing, both stop. If neither is pushing in (an overlap left by a blow's placement), it stays half each as today. No one is pushed into a building by a stick.
  11. While a grab holds either fighter (DirGrapple.holds; the grab is on in Orb's build), own movement is off, as the walk-out is today (3864).
  12. The own speeds live in the well's state, which the brawl's reset (2840-2841) does not touch: zero them in begin and over.
  13. The computer's stick in a brawl was written to steer the pair (_aiNudge 3143-3156). "Away while guarding" would now walk it out of its own fight. For the trial it holds still where it used to push away. Encounter retunes after.
  14. Depth (fighters in depth) is off in the trial; _moveZ and carryOn's depth branch stay pair-wide until it is needed.

What does not change: every blow's timing and worth, the step-in, the reels, the hit's pause, the escape, the burst, the right stick's carries, and the camera's close shot (it has its own gap guard, and the bodies stay within 4.5 body heights while the brawl lasts).

11. Pseudocode and where it hooks

GDScript-shaped. Float64 throughout, SimWrap.sdx(a, b) is b minus a, SimDetMath and SimMathx for math, no random draws anywhere in the well. The pattern to copy is the kick (sim/core/kick.gd): state that exists and is hashed only while the switch is on.

Two traps the review found, for the builders.
  • The core may not name the director. hash.gd will name SimWell, so well.gd is in the core's load, and any DirBands or DirInterrupt name inside it fails the load-split gate (sim/core/tools/load_split.gd, run by CI; rule at sim/core/dir.gd:7-9). So SimWell keeps its own 75.0, measures distance itself, and reaches the director only through SimDir. The press's band read lives in DirBands.
  • The two-fighter count has no slack (365 of 365) and it counts comments. Do not spell the roster's rival helper or a first-slot index anywhere in a shipped well file, not even in a comment.

	r *= 1.0 + float(c.chargeGrow) * float(f.wel.chg) / float(c.chargeRiseTicks)
	return SimMathx.jclamp(r, float(c.minBh) * BH, float(c.maxBh) * BH)

	_ensure()
	return _d

static func zoneNow(S: SimState, A, D) -> int:                    # pure: for a press before the first write
	return _zone(_dist(A, D), radius(D), -1)

static func _dist(a, b) -> float:                                 # as DirBands.dist (bands.gd:49-52), without naming it
	if a.z != b.z and (a.zT != 0.0 or b.zT != 0.0):
		return SimDetMath.hypot(SimDetMath.hypot(SimWrap.sdx(a.x, b.x), b.y - a.y), b.z - a.z)
	return SimDetMath.hypot(SimWrap.sdx(a.x, b.x), b.y - a.y)

static func _zone(d: float, rj: float, was: int) -> int:
	var h: float = float(_d.zones.hysteresisBh) * BH * 0.5 if was >= 0 else 0.0
	if d <= rj + (h if was == IN else -h):
		return IN
	return EDGE if d <= rj + float(_d.zones.edgeBh) * BH + (h if (was == IN or was == EDGE) else -h) else OUT

## The one writer. Called once a live tick from SimCore._fightTick, after the control loop and before the stepFighter
## loop (sim.gd, between 429 and 430), behind `if S.wellOn:`. It reads every body as the tick began and then writes
## them all, so the random slot order changes nothing. In 2v2 each fight is swapped into S, so it runs per fight.
## A face press is read inside the control loop, so it reads the zone written the tick before, as it reads last
## tick's band today. The zone is one movement step older than the band beside it (the band is written after the move).
static func tick(S: SimState) -> void:
	var n: int = S.fighters.size()
	var rs := PackedFloat64Array(); rs.resize(n)
	for k in n:
		var f = S.fighters[k]
		var up: bool = f.state == "charging" or f.beamCharge != null
		f.wel.chg = mini(int(_d.size.chargeRiseTicks), f.wel.chg + 1) if up \
			else maxi(0, f.wel.chg - ceili(float(_d.size.chargeRiseTicks) / float(_d.size.chargeFallTicks)))
		rs[k] = radius(f)
	var zs := PackedInt32Array(); zs.resize(n)
	var ds := PackedFloat64Array(); ds.resize(n * 2)
	for k in n:
		var f = S.fighters[k]
		var j: int = _other(S, k)                                  # the other body of this fight, found by a loop over the fight's bodies
		if j < 0:
			zs[k] = OUT; continue
		var o = S.fighters[j]
		var d: float = _dist(f, o)
		zs[k] = _zone(d, rs[j], f.wel.zone)
		if bool(_d.zones.either) and zs[k] != IN and _zone(d, rs[k], S.fighters[j].wel.zone) == IN:
			zs[k] = IN                                             # the second reading: either of you inside the other's well
		_draw(S, f, o, d, rs[j], ds, k)
	for k in n:
		var w = S.fighters[k].wel
		w.zone = zs[k]
		w.r = roundi(rs[k])
		w.dx = roundi(ds[k * 2] * 16.0)
		w.dy = roundi(ds[k * 2 + 1] * 16.0)

## The draw, only in silence. Writes the wanted movement toward the rival into ds.
static func _draw(S: SimState, f, o, d: float, r: float, ds: PackedFloat64Array, k: int) -> void:
	var i: SimIntent = f.input
	var moved: bool = f.state == "launched" or f.state == "down" or f.state == "dropped" or f.stunTicks > 0 \
		or (f.rush != null and f.rush.tgt == null)                 # a send's slide or drift is a rush with no target and no
	if moved:                                                      #   state change (launch.gd:1237-1265); an approach's rush has one
		f.wel.own = 0                                               # something else has the fighter: no draw until they act
	var rest: bool = f.heldMx == 0.0 and f.heldMy == 0.0          # the stick as held, before any gate (state.gd:1170):
	                                                               #   a stun zeroes i.mx and i.my (wounds.gd:279-281), not these
	var hands: bool = i.guard or i.dash or i.light or i.heavy or i.sig or i.lightHeld or i.heavyHeld or i.sigHeld \
		or i.rsHeld or i.rsStart or f.beamCharge != null
	if not moved and f.state == "free" and f.rush == null and (not rest or hands):
		f.wel.own = 1                                               # their own input, once free of it: the latch is theirs again
	var silent: bool = rest and not hands and f.state == "free" and f.rush == null and f.stunTicks == 0 \
		and S.game.ko == null and not SimDir.approaching(S, f) and f.wel.own == 1 \
		and o.wel.own == 1                                         # and the rival's too: nobody drifts after a fighter they sent
	# The director also calls SimWell.sent(f) (sets own = 0) where it knocks a fighter back without a rush:
	# DirRecoil.knocked (recoil.gd:74) and the old DirLaunch.knockBack (launch.gd:1381).
	f.wel.quiet = mini(int(_d.draw.quietTicks), f.wel.quiet + 1) if silent else 0
	if str(_d.draw.mode) == "off" or d > r:
		return
	var stop: float = float(_d.draw.stopBh) * BH
	if d <= stop:
		return
	var share: float = float(f.wel.quiet) / float(_d.draw.quietTicks)   # "rest": nothing unless silent
	if str(_d.draw.mode) == "capped":                              # Game Design's: always on, halved on a fighter holding away
		share = 0.5 if SimDir.heldAway(S, f) else 1.0
		if moved or f.state != "free" or f.rush != null:
			share = 0.0
	if share <= 0.0:
		return
	var depth: float = SimMathx.jclamp((1.0 - d / r) / (1.0 - float(_d.draw.fullAt)), 0.0, 1.0)
	var ease: float = SimMathx.jclamp((d - stop) / (float(_d.draw.stopEaseBh) * BH), 0.0, 1.0)
	var sp: float = float(_d.draw.cap) * SimFighter.flightSpeed(S, f, false) * depth * ease * share
	ds[k * 2] = SimWrap.sdx(f.x, o.x) / d * sp
	ds[k * 2 + 1] = (o.y - f.y) / d * sp

13. The question for Orb

A draft for the EP to put to Orb under the clips. One question.

Up close, when you push your stick, who should move?

A. The fight moves (today, from your October calls). Each stick nudges the whole fight, so you can drag it where you want and it never drifts apart. You carry your rival with you and they carry you. To leave on your own you escape for 15 energy, zip away, or wait for a second without attacks.

B. You move (what I think you would want). Your stick moves only your fighter, at the same pace as today. Nobody's stick ever moves the other player. Your rival stays on you by attacking, and you stay on them the same way. You can always walk away from someone who is not hitting you. Walk away from someone who is, and you take three blows before you break clear, unless a heavy or a grab stops you. The paid escape is still the fast way out.

Watch each pair of clips. If the two links to play are ready, fight the computer for a minute on each: push away, push into them, circle.

  • Why B, in my view: it is the only version where "we weren't in control of our fighter" cannot come from the other player's hands, and brawls still hold together because attacking closes distance.
  • What B costs: you can no longer drag the fight across the map with the stick alone, and three lights no longer hold someone who wants to go.
  • If the answer is "neither yet": the knobs are B's walking pace, how many blows it takes to break clear, and whether the magnet should hold while one stick is busy.

14. Risks, unknowns, and what I could not verify

  • The arrival still holds both fighters. A snap arriving starts the close fight; in today's version that stops the defender and holds both, now from up to 23 body heights away for a strong attacker, about a third of a second after one press. That is the shape of Orb's 10-02 complaint. The new close-fight mode softens it (the defender keeps their stick and half their speed). Row 5 measures it. The dodge is worse: a snap that arrives on a rival who tapped dodge starts the planned "dodge" exchange, which locks both; a snap on a charging fighter starts the charge-interrupt. Game Design's call 5 turns those into brawl answers; it is outside the recommended cut, and it is the largest remaining source of "locked".
  • The moving edge is still invisible. The same tap at the same distance snaps in or taunts depending on the rival's tier and whether they are powering up. The edge band softens it. A pulse on the pad and a low sound at the crossing are in the recommended cut and not in the trial. Showing it in the world is open: Game Design's reading said dust along the ground toward a big well, "never on fighters". Orb's 13 November answer on the marks ("PLACEHOLDERS FOR DIEGETIC AURAS, GLOWS, FLARES ETC. THAT SHOWCASE GAME MECHANICS WHERE NECESSARY. UI/UX ELEMENTS MUST BE NON-INTRUSIVE") may reopen whether the rival's own aura could show a swollen well. That is Orb's and Art's call, behind the look gate; the ground dust stays the first thing to try.
  • One snap at a time. While one approach is on, every other request waits (exchange.gd:259-261). The first press in a tick starts its snap; the rival's press waits and becomes their first blow when it arrives, as with today's lunge. The clash of two snaps, from the clash table Orb read, is later work.
  • The computer. Its closing rules use fixed distances (130, 700; ai.gd:209-215), its attack choice reads the old band (282-299), and it answers only approaches of 16 ticks or more. It will play the well without knowing it is there. Fine for a trial, not for Alpha.
  • 2v2. A 2v2 is two fights swapped into the state in turn, so the well as written works per pair and knows nothing of the other pair.
  • The LAN copy. The LAN setup forces only power, travel, dash and slip. A well switch would follow each PC's data file unless it is added there; the handshake would then refuse two PCs whose well data differ.
  • Flight strikes still reach a fixed 12.5 body heights. Game Design ruled their range should follow the defender's well. Left out of the trial.
  • The zone is a tick behind, and one movement step older than the band it sits beside. Near an edge at closing speeds the two can disagree for a tick. The trial should report any press where they did.
  • Not verified by me: the exact contact tick of a brawl blow after an approach (the tables use the scheduled lead from _throw, read but not run); whether the clip tool can show two settings of an arbitrary switch side by side (its flag today is for the hit's pause only); whether Orb's LAN copy carried the same setup words as the page.
  • A record to correct: "27 brawls, 0 walk-outs" in my 8 November packet and in Game Design's reading sits beside one brawl_end:left and one brawl_end:idle in the same file (docs/qa/runs/1931a0f8/slot0-cue-summary-orb-play-20261108-1726.txt). "Left" is the paid dodge out (interrupt.gd:459), so "no free walk-outs" is right. The same file has 5 more brawls that began as counters, so 32, not 27.
