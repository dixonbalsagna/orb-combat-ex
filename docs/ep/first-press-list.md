# First press: what stops the handover

The EP's list for Orb's first play of versus on the globe with the new pad (`docs/ep/vision.md`, the order of 2026-11-01). Written 2026-11-01. **Only what's on the first list lands from here until Orb has played.** Everything else waits, however good.

## Stops the handover

1. **Hidden fight.** With a scripted player who moves, 3,600 ticks: neither fighter stays behind a drawn building for over 3 s, and the stills agree.
2. **A planet you can read.** Same reel: the frame reads as a fight on a planet, up from down, with no towns hanging in the air behind the fight.
3. **Fighters in the ground.** Same reel: no bodies cut off by land they should be standing on or above.
4. **A clash a player can reach.** Hold RT and press B against the computer or a second pad; the clash shows, and the meeting point isn't behind a roof.
5. **A match that ends.** Every fight ends in a finisher, and Play again is one press from the next fight.
6. **The pad tells the truth.** Everything on the sheet (`docs/tools/pad-steps.md`) does what it says on the live page. No press is silent. No hint is shown for a press that does nothing. That includes the meteor: the full-power shortcut plus the sheet's steps fire it, with the camera following. If it's flat or broken, Orb hears that before they try it.
7. **It doesn't break.** Ten minutes of the sim natively, through the real input hub, on several seeds: no script errors, stuck states or hangs. Then a few minutes of the moving reel on the page, for what only the page can break.
8. **The frame.** On a quiet PC, one scripted fight with a clash and a collapse: median frame under 16.7 ms on this PC, and the slowest moments are named. A hitch Orb would stop over stops the handover.
8a. **Sound and rumble.** Sound plays in the browser after the first click, and the hits, launches and clash fire their cues, checked by a log of cues played and the output's level. The pad rumbles where the browser allows it. The handover names what's on and off, and asks Orb whether the hits sounded right, since nobody here can listen.
9. **The warning and the black box.** The warning shows first, and the match record saves, including from a match cut short.
10. **The right build.** Orb gets exactly the build that passed the quiet read: nothing running on the PC, no watch to swap it mid-match. That build is copied to a folder named by its commit, outside the repo, and kept, so Orb's recording can still be played back after anything lands.

**And one no test answers: is it fun to watch?** The EP looks through a full minute of the moving reel, frame by frame on a dense sheet, before handing over. If it's embarrassing, Orb hears that at the handover, even with all ten passing.

Rock bands, lake beds, low hills, scar fringe, the swing scored by the land, town-sea, the flight curve, melee crescents, beam alerts, ring restyle, the clinch's look, fighter select, match length tuning. If the flat grey is still bad once 2 is fixed, rock comes back up.

## What Orb is told at the handover

- Specials and transformations are off. RT does less at first.
- There's no teleport or vanish: that's a question for them.
- It's always Grist against Loupe, with no fighter select.
- The Driver seeks and has no look of its own yet.
- One meteor a match. A full-power shortcut reaches it without six minutes of play.
- A light takes a flick's stun, not the flick. That's new, and their hands judge it.
- Nobody has played any of this with a real pad before them.
