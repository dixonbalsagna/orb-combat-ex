# Roster of twelve: overview for Orb (2026-10-05)

Orb asked for the groundwork for a roster of twelve: the roles and subroles the first four establish, generic templates for niches not yet touched, and proposals for the rest. Two directors wrote it overnight. This page is the EP's merge; the detail is in the two documents.

- Mechanics: [docs/design/roster-twelve.md](../design/roster-twelve.md) (Game Design)
- Identity: [docs/narrative/roster-twelve-identity.md](../narrative/roster-twelve-identity.md) (Narrative)

Everything here is a template or an option. Names, lore and looks are Orb's. Nobody has touched the voice lab.

## The first four

| Fighter | Mechanical role (Game Design) | Story function (Narrative) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| The Protagonist | Duelist, "the redirector" | Heart: the guardian brawler |
| The rival | Duelist, "the closer" | Foil: the dark mirror |
| The Empress | Commander, "the court" | Spectacle: the ringmaster |
| The Cyborg | Devourer, "the scavenger" | Appetite: the parody horror |

Game Design describes a fighter on eight axes: role and subrole, where he wants the fight, his two best stances and his worst, his relation to the world, his meter, his forms, his wounds, and his part in the story. The Empress's and the Cyborg's stance leanings are read from their written kits, since neither is built.

Gaps the four leave: no fighter who wins by grabbing, by setting traps, by hiding, by sheer size or by pure speed; no fighter who protects a place by standing in it; no mentor, newcomer, turncoat or former villain.

The two halves were written in parallel. Game Design is lining its templates up with Narrative's, adding the juggernaut, and ending its document with one merged table of twelve.

## Three ways to fill twelve (Game Design)

- Set A: the eight classic niches (grappler, trapper, stalker, giant, speedster, puppeteer, shaper, wildcard). Three of them are high cost.
- Set B: swaps the giant and the puppeteer for the banker and the warden. One high-cost fighter instead of three.
- Set C: swaps the speedster and the wildcard for the banker and the warden, so every fighter has a relation to the world.

Narrative's suggested balance: 4 heroes, 2 rivals, 3 villains, 3 wildcards.

Build order advice for a zero budget: speedster, banker, warden, trapper, wildcard, grappler, shaper, stalker, puppeteer, giant.

## The "fourth season" feeling

- Narrative: name the event, show its effect, never explain it. A ledger of past events that Orb owns (blank table in its document). Eleven devices on systems we already have: taunts that refer to things never shown, old-grudge lines, a "previously" card, win poses and costumes from "earlier seasons", an episode guide by title only.
- Game Design: "threads". A pair with a shared past changes one thing when they meet: the intro, a revealed signature, a struggle's look, a line at the last stand.

## What Orb is asked

From Game Design:
1. Do the eight axes describe a fighter the way you think of one?
2. Are the first four's roles right: the redirector, the closer, the court, the scavenger?
3. Which eight templates, or which set?
4. The giant: a true giant late, an armoured heavy early, or one fighter who grows?
5. The wildcard: the mimic, the gambler or the mood rider?
6. Threads between fighters: wanted?

From Narrative:
1. Which eight of its nine identity templates; the Caretaker in place of the Colossus or the Turncoat?
2. Is the mentor right as a grappler, or should the teacher be someone unexpected?
3. How much of the fourth season should show: references only, or also a "previously" card, earlier-season costumes and an episode guide?
4. Is a fighter with no jokes (the Harbinger) welcome in a cast this funny?
5. The parked mask: on the Turncoat, or on someone you will invent?

## Risks both directors raise

- Scope: twelve fighters are 66 pairings to balance where four are 6, and the Empress and the Cyborg are not built yet.
- Costs are rough judgements, not estimates from the directors who would do the work.
- Several templates push on design pillars: the trapper and the stalker on "never out of range" and on downtime, the shaper on "no walls".
- Narrative's hooks are placeholders written before Orb's universe arrives.
