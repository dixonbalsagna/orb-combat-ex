# Roster of twelve: overview for Orb (2026-10-05)

Orb asked for the groundwork for a roster of twelve: the roles and subroles the first four establish, generic templates for niches not yet touched, and proposals for the rest. Two directors wrote it overnight and lined their halves up. This page is the EP's merge; the detail is in the two documents.

- Mechanics: [docs/design/roster-twelve.md](../design/roster-twelve.md) (Game Design; section 8 is one table of all twelve)
- Identity: [docs/narrative/roster-twelve-identity.md](../narrative/roster-twelve-identity.md) (Narrative)

Everything here is a template or an option. Names, lore and looks are Orb's. Nobody has touched the voice lab.

## The first four

| Fighter | Mechanical role (Game Design) | Story function (Narrative) | Relation to the world |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| The Protagonist | Duelist, "the redirector" | Heart: the guardian brawler | Tends it |
| The rival | Duelist, "the closer" | Foil: the dark mirror | Ignores it |
| The Empress | Commander, "the court" | Spectacle: the ringmaster | Annexes it |
| The Cyborg | Devourer, "the scavenger" | Appetite: the parody horror | Feeds on it |

Game Design describes a fighter on eight axes: role and subrole, where he wants the fight, his two best stances and his worst, his relation to the world, his meter, his forms, his wounds, and his part in the story. The Empress's and the Cyborg's stance leanings are read from their written kits, since neither is built.

Gaps the four leave: no fighter who wins by grabbing, by trapping or zoning, by hiding, by sheer size or by pure speed; no mentor, newcomer, turncoat or former villain.

## The nine templates

| Identity (Narrative) | Niche (Game Design) | What makes it play differently | Cost to build |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| The Veteran, the mentor who speaks in corrections | Grappler | He waits and catches; his grab beats a zip on arrival | Medium (needs the grab and hold state) |
| The Prodigy, the newcomer with a running commentary | Speedster | Cheapest zips and shortest tells at his first form; his forms add reads, not speed; a move repeated against him goes stale sooner | Low |
| The Turncoat; the natural home for the parked mask | Stalker (stealth) | Hides and strikes from cover; needs an answer to what each player sees | High |
| The Colossus | Giant | Sheer size; a cheaper armoured heavy at normal height exists | High (a second skeleton) |
| The Former Villain | Zoner | Wide slow beams that leave a hot lane for a few seconds | Low to medium |
| The Legacy | Puppeteer | Two helpers that act for themselves, ordered from the charging stance | Medium once the Empress's guard exists; high if the player steers one |
| The Showman | Wildcard | "The bill": nine specials in three seeded turns shown in advance, and an applause meter fed by variety | Medium; a lot of special moves |
| The Harbinger, the arc's real threat | Juggernaut | Marks one of the rival's body regions in plain sight; armoured against mashing; a meter that only rises | Low to medium |
| The Caretaker, the spare | Terrain shaper | Walls, pits and thrown ground; the least distinct niche, since every fighter already reshapes the world | Medium |

Game Design keeps its extra niches as alternatives: a banker (a charging-stance fighter), a warden (a counter-fighter who guards a town by standing in it), a mine trapper, a mimic and a mood rider.

## After Orb's answers (questionnaire 18, 2026-10-05)

Orb chose: stealth, heavy, speedster and terrain shaper as the niches to explore; a hero-heavy cast, "an ensemble of allies"; bodies close to all humanoid (2 of 5).

Both directors revised for that:

- Certain: the speedster (the Prodigy), the stealth fighter (the Turncoat), the heavy (the Colossus, now a big armoured human on the shared rig, up to about a fifth taller, not a giant) and the terrain shaper (the Caretaker, now a full slot).
- Four slots stay open, with five candidates: grappler (the Veteran), zoner (the Former Villain), puppeteer (the Legacy), wildcard (the Showman), juggernaut (the Harbinger).
- The two directors differ on which to leave out, and that is Orb's call:
  - Game Design leaves out the juggernaut: the heavy now carries its armour, and a hero-heavy cast has less room for another villain.
  - Narrative leaves out the Showman, keeping the Harbinger as the arc's real threat. That gives 7 allies and 5 threats; swapping the Harbinger for the Showman gives 8 and 4.
- Narrative's cast for 7 and 5. Allies: the Protagonist, the Veteran, the Prodigy, the Legacy, the Colossus, the Caretaker, and the Turncoat on probation. Threats: the rival, the Empress, the Cyborg, the Harbinger, the Former Villain.
- All twelve are humanoid, with size differences (the Prodigy small, the Colossus big, the Harbinger tall), the Empress's tail and the Cyborg's machine parts.

Build order advice for a zero budget: speedster, heavy, zoner, wildcard or juggernaut, grappler, shaper, puppeteer, stealth last (it is the one high-cost template, and needs an answer to what each player sees).

## The "fourth season" feeling

- Narrative: name the event, show its effect, never explain it. A ledger of past events that Orb owns (blank table in its document). Eleven devices on systems we already have: taunts that refer to things never shown, old-grudge lines, a "previously" card, win poses and costumes from "earlier seasons", an episode guide by title only.
- Game Design: "threads". A pair with a shared past changes one thing when they meet: the intro, a revealed signature, a struggle's look, a line at the last stand.

## What Orb is asked

From Game Design:
1. Do the eight axes describe a fighter the way you think of one? Is one missing?
2. Are the first four's roles right: the redirector, the closer, the court, the scavenger?
3. Which set: Narrative's twelve, or the spare in place of the stalker or the giant?
4. The stalker: what is hidden, and from whom? A screen each (online or split screen); or on a shared screen hide his tells but not his body; or both.
5. The puppeteer: helpers that act for themselves, or one the player steers?
6. The wildcard: the bill, the mimic or the mood rider?
7. Threads between fighters: wanted?

From Narrative:
1. Which eight of the nine identity templates?
2. Is the mentor right as a grappler, or should the teacher be someone unexpected?
3. How much of the fourth season should show: references only, or also a "previously" card, earlier-season costumes and an episode guide?
4. Is a fighter with no jokes (the Harbinger) welcome in a cast this funny?
5. The parked mask: on the Turncoat, or on someone you will invent?

## Risks both directors raise

- Scope: twelve fighters are 66 pairings and 12 mirrors to balance where four are 6, and the Empress and the Cyborg are not built yet.
- Costs are rough judgements, not estimates from the directors who would do the work.
- Two armoured fighters in one roster (the giant and the juggernaut) both shrug off mashing, which is hard on new players.
- The wildcard's nine specials are three fighters' worth of that content.
- Several templates push on design pillars: the zoner and the stalker on "never out of range" and on downtime, the shaper on "no walls".
- Narrative's hooks are placeholders written before Orb's universe arrives.
