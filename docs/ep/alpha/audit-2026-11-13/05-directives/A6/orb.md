# A6: Orb

Leads: none
Supports: none

This page is not a brief. It is the short list of what only you can do or decide in the last milestone. Each question has one recommendation; a plain "yes" takes it.

## What you do

1. **Play the candidate, exactly as your friends will get it.** One match at each difficulty, and say whether Easy, Medium and Hard feel like three different things. One run of each mode. Nothing in it is new: it is the last build with what the checks found fixed.
2. **Invite your two friends with Steam Decks.** The team hands you four things to send each of them: a download key, the tester guide, the one-page checklist and the tester note. You send them; the team does not contact your friends. This happens only after the team tells you every safety check is green on that exact build.
3. **Ask each friend for three things back**, posted to your private Discord server: the ticked checklist, the report file the game makes in one press, and the small file the five-minute speed test saves. If one of them is willing, ask them to leave the two-hour test running once on their Deck.
4. **Put the candidate on the itch.io page**, and any fixed build after it, with the command the team hands you. The account is yours.
5. **Say the word that starts the freeze**, on the day the team shows you one table with every line passing on one build.

## What you decide

1. **After the freeze, does every new idea wait unless you lift it in yourself as a scope change?** Recommendation: yes. New ideas go in the ideas log; fixes, tuning of existing numbers and polish carry on. (decision 23)
2. **If a check or a friend finds something that can only be fixed by changing the fight's numbers, do we go back one step, or leave it and name it?** Going back means the numbers reopen, the two-hour test and the difficulty readings are taken again, and saved replays stop playing back once more. Recommendation: go back only if it stops a newcomer from finishing a match or makes Easy unwinnable; otherwise write it down as the first job after Alpha.
3. **If one line of the table misses and it is not a safety check, which of three do you want: wait, ship without it and carry it forward by name, or cut something?** The team brings you each such line as its own question within a day, with its recommendation. Nothing ships short silently.
4. **Is the Steam Deck frame rate your friends actually got good enough?** Recommendation: 30 held is the bar and 40 is the aim; if their files show 30 held at the Deck's starting picture quality, yes.

## What you will be shown

- One table: every line of the Alpha definition, passing or not, on one build.
- Your friends' two checklists and a one-page summary of what stopped them, what confused them and what was only rough.
- Six sets of the same shots, one from each milestone, side by side.
