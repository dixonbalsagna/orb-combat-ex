Beam answer alert: mockups (second version)

Orb's ask: tell the player on the receiving end, without getting in the way, that there is a moment to fire their own beam back or dodge. This version follows Game Design's ruling: the beam travels, the chance opens when it leaves the hand and closes when it arrives. Stand-in shapes only.

Every mark here is steady: it appears and it goes, with no fade, no flash and no pulsing. Nothing is drawn over a fighter. The times are made up; the real ones are Game Design's (a beam takes about a tenth of a second to a full second to arrive, longer at range). Button words follow your layout.

The scene

You are on the left. The rival fires a beam at you. Press Show it: the beam travels from his hand to you, and the alerts you have switched on appear for the length of the chance.

What tells you

Seven things, and you choose how much of each. The first three are in the picture itself; the screen edge line and the caption are the only parts added on top, and they stay at the screen's edge.

The shooter's glint

A bright spark at his hand while the beam is gathered, in his own colour. It is part of the fight. Visual effects draw it.

The beam itself

It leaves the hand and travels. This is the game, and it is always there.

Air and light

The air ripples ahead of the beam, its colour sweeps along the ground. Off, subtle or strong.

Sound

A low rising note that climbs until the beam arrives, and comes from the side the shooter is on. Off or on, its own volume.

Rumble

A buzz on your controller that grows until the beam arrives.

Screen edge line

A thin steady line in the shooter's colour at the edge of the screen on his side, with a small arrow pointing in. It comes in over about a tenth of a second and never blinks. It is never white, gold or red (a shooter in one of those gets a violet line). Only for the player being fired at. By default it appears only when the shooter is off screen.

Caption

Small words at the edge: BEAM INCOMING, then your answers as your own buttons: FIRE BACK, and DODGE, which looks dim until the moment a dodge works. A bar runs down to arrival, with a notch where the dodge opens, and the glyphs are your own buttons. It sits on a solid plate so it reads on any sky. Off by default; for players who cannot hear the sound.

The chance: you can fire back at any time before it arrives (answering late means the beams meet closer to you), and a dodge only works in the last moments, so the caption shows the dodge as ready only then.

The settings page

Every row changes the scene as you press it. Each player sets their own, and each controller remembers its own.
