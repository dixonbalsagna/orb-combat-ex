# The HUD's text warm-up (UI, 2026-10-23)

Owner: UI & UX. Parked behind `features.json` `text_warm` (on in the patch): `docs/ui/pending/text-warm/text-warm.patch` (new `ui/core/ui_warm.gd` and its `.uid`, a call in `UiHud.setup`, one flag, `_text_warm_rules` with 3 checks).

**What it is.** VFX found the integrated-GPU hitches of the city beam scene at ticks 6, 36 and 82, and tick 36 is the first signature wind-up (the banner's text, the ring, the after-image). Rendering's warm-up (`render/core/warmup.gd`) draws every effect shader once at boot, behind the notice, but the HUD's first draws are not shaders: **UI has no shader and no material** (a grep of `ui/` finds none). What a HUD draws for the first time are **glyphs**: the first time the font is asked for a letter at a size (and, with `draw_string_outline`, at an outline width) the text server makes it in the font's atlas and the renderer uploads the texture. The banner is the biggest case (about 38 px at 720p, with a 4 px outline), and a bark, a card title or a hint reaching the screen for the first time does the same.

**The change.** At `setup()`, behind the start menu or notice, `UiWarm` draws one line of the common glyphs (A to Z, a to z, 0 to 9 and the punctuation the HUD uses; the font has all of them, a check) once at **every size and outline the HUD uses** (the design sizes its widgets pass to `UiText.px`, at the layout's scale, with a 2 px outline from 16 px up, and the banner's own fit with its 9% outline): 16 lines at 720p. It sits at 1% opacity for three frames and frees itself; it reads and writes nothing of the sim. It is not made in a headless run (no renderer). The switch `text_warm` turns it off.

**Rendering's warm-list lines: none needed.** The HUD adds no shader, so `render/core/warmup.gd` stays as it is.

**The numbers, honestly.** Performance's `frame-bench.mjs --scene signaturecity --igpu --ticks 150 --warm 0 --save-rtt` on a scratch export of main, three runs of main and three of main with the patch, interleaved. **Every run is INVALID by the load rule** (the machine was at 70 to 100% in all of them, with other sessions' jobs), so they are upper bounds, and **none of the six reproduces the 50 ms-plus hitches at ticks 6, 36 and 82**: the cleanest run (main, 70 to 84% load) has a worst frame of 12.3 ms, and the spikes in the others fall on different ticks from run to run (load, not the scene). Main with the patch: worst frames 23.1, 52.3 and 18.7 ms. So I cannot show a before and after; the effect is the expected one (a glyph is made once, at boot, and not in a fight) and costs about 1,300 glyphs behind the notice. **A quiet-window run by Performance (`audit-run.mjs --wait-quiet`, base against base plus this patch, three interleaved repeats) is what decides it.**
