# The wind-up read: the release in a freeze, `press_read.gd` on paper, the burst, and the nudge ramp

Controls, 2026-10-06, for Game Design's three strengths (`docs/design/brawl-second-pass.md` sections 2 to 12). Part 1 is checked and tested; Part 2 is on paper only (the Y and B reads wait for brief C2a); Part 3 is built.

## 1. Is "no `relWaited`" sound? Yes, with one rule for one rare case

**What reaches the sim when a level changes during a hit-stop.** The host builds an intent every tick (`sim_host.gd:tick`, `hub.intent(slot)`), `SimCore.step` returns `false` in a freeze **before** it advances `S.T`, reads an input or runs the director (`sim.gd:100` to `112`), and the host then does **not** call `consumed()` (`sim_host.gd:202`). So nothing in the sim runs between the freeze's first tick and its last, edges stay pending in the layout, and on the first live tick the sim reads **the level as it is then, plus any edges carried, with `waited` on the oldest**. A wind-up that counts live ticks (Game Design and Encounter confirm: `S.T`, not `S.tick`) has not moved. So **a release made in a freeze is read at the live point the freeze began, plus the first live tick: it can never read later than where the freeze stopped the clock.** That is why `relWaited` is not needed: the trap I raised assumed the wind-up's end was on the real clock.

**Checked on the pad (Arena), the keyboard (solo) and Full touch**, in `stance_test.gd` (`_freeze_release`, 15 checks, all three devices the same):
- **A release in the freeze:** pressed live and consumed, released in the middle of a 6-build freeze: the first live build has the level down, no edge, nothing carried. Read as a release at the point the freeze began.
- **A tap wholly in the freeze:** press and release both inside it: the first live build has the edge, the level on (the edge carries it) and `waited` of at least 3; the next build the level is down.
- **Debounce does not interfere:** debounce is per button and only for a re-press within 2 ticks of its own release, measured in host ticks.

**The one case that still reads a tap as a hold: a release and a re-press of the same button inside one freeze** (Encounter's case). The smallest example: Y pressed live and consumed; a 9-tick freeze begins; the player lets go at tick 2 and presses again at tick 6. The level is down in the middle and up at the end, so the sim sees the level on at its last live tick and on the first live tick: **the release is invisible; only the new press edge arrives** (tested: the edge arrives with the level on, `waited` of the re-press). A masher's Y is about 12 ticks apart and freezes run 4 to 9 on a heavier hit, so it can happen.

**Ruling on Encounter's rule: confirmed.** *A fresh press edge on a button the director believes is held means "released, then pressed"; the old wind-up resolves as a tap (or as a charge's release if it was past its wind-up and grace) at the first live tick, and the new press starts its own.* It is sound on the input side because:
- **The edge reliably arrives on all three devices** whenever the re-press is more than 2 host ticks after its release (the tested case uses 4; a human re-press is never under about 4). The layouts keep the edge pending through the freeze (no `consumed()`), and Full touch does the same (`req_*` and the layout's `_aedge` are kept until consumed).
- **Debounce does not contradict it.** A re-press within 2 ticks of the release is treated as contact bounce: the level never goes down and **no edge is sent**, so the director sees a hold with no edge, which is what it should read (no human can release and re-press in 33 ms; a bounce is not a tap). With the rule above that is consistent: no edge means no release.
- **Two edges in one freeze merge into one** (the layout sends one edge per button per build; the oldest `waited` stands). For the director that is a press and a release and a press, read as one fresh press: it loses the middle press, which is the existing limit I reported for lights and now applies to a 4-to-9-tick freeze with two re-presses. A player cannot press three times in 9 ticks on one button (debounce, finger speed), so it does not arise on a slow button.
- **Under RT or any layer, and on Simple** (the hold-attack control's light fires on release), the same holds for the layer's `special` and the Simple bridge's request: edges are kept until consumed. A latched charge (the setting) changes this: the latch makes **the next press the release** and sends no new edge, so under a latch the rule must not apply (the director gets a level falling, not an edge).
- **`waited` on that edge** says where in the freeze the re-press was, so the director can place the old wind-up's resolve at the first live tick and need not back-date anything.

**The KO's slow motion** (time scale 0.35) only stretches live ticks in real time, which is favourable to a tap (a physical tap is shorter in live ticks); nothing changes in the input layer. **Simple counts its hold lengths in real host ticks** (the layout's `holdStart` 12, and 28 for the heavy), so a Simple hold is a real duration, unaffected by freezes: right for a finger, and different from the live-tick wind-up the other layouts feed (see section 2).

## 2. `press_read.gd` on paper: wind-up lengths in place of hold points, and the burst

**Where a hold point is used today.** `hold_ticks_for(entry, p, opts)` returns the cell's hold (X 8, Y 16, B 20 or 45 in the charging stance, A 18); `classify` uses it for the style `hold` (a held button counts from that many real ticks) and `zip_read` uses it for the heavy reading. Both count the log's real ticks (`S.tick`).

**What changes, for the three strengths.** The decision is no longer "held for N ticks" but "still down when the wind-up and its grace have run, on the live clock":

1. **New data under `read` (Tools' schema first):** `windupMedium` 12 (Y), `windupHeavy` 28 (B), `windupContext` 12 (A's shove), `windupGrace` 4 (one for every device, as Game Design ruled). X has no wind-up. The old per-cell holds `holdHeavy`, `holdSig`, `holdSigCharging`, `holdContext` are retired; `holdLight` 8 stays as the burst's start (below).
2. **`hold_ticks_for` becomes `windup_for(entry, p)`**, returning the wind-up in live ticks for the cell, and `decision_for(entry, p)` returning wind-up plus grace (16 for Y, 32 for B, 16 for A).
3. **The clock.** The log's entries carry real ticks; the wind-up counts live ticks. So `push(log, kind, mode, tick, beat, size, stance, live)` and `release(log, kind, tick, live)` take an optional **live tick** (the director's `_now(S)`, default -1 meaning "same as `tick`" so every existing caller and test is unchanged), stored as `ldown` and `lup`.
4. **A new pure read `charge_read(entry, live_now, p)`** returning `"winding"` (still down, before the decision point), `"tap"` (released at or before wind-up plus grace live ticks after the press), or `"charge"` (still down at wind-up plus grace, its charge counted from there). It has no clock of its own: the caller passes the live tick, and a release in a freeze is stamped at the first live tick, which is the point the freeze began (section 1).
5. **`classify`'s style `hold`** keeps working for the alchemist's press styles but switches to `charge_read` for Y, B and A, and to the burst read for X. Its inputs stay the same.
6. **`zip_read`'s heavy** becomes "the starter is still down at its wind-up plus grace, on the live clock" (the starter's `ldown` against the zip's live tick); the speed and tech readings do not change.
7. **Simple.** It cannot start a wind-up on the press, because the layout does not know the weight until the hold is over: **under 12 held is the light (on release), from 12 the medium, from 28 the heavy.** Read literally the medium's latency is 12 (the hold) plus its 12-tick wind-up, and the heavy's 28 plus 28, so a Simple heavy lands 56 ticks after the press against 28 on the other layouts. **I recommend Simple starts the lowest wind-up on the press and lets the hold escalate it** (the medium wind-up starts at the press, a hold past 12 turns it into the heavy's, a release before 12 is a medium... which is Game Design's call, since it changes "under 12 is a light"). Left as Game Design's rule, it is a layout-level read: the `hold-attack` bridge gains a second threshold (`read.simpleHeavyStart` 28) and sends the weights at their thresholds.
8. **The two settings are layout-only, no intent change.** *Charges off:* the slow buttons never report held (the level goes down the tick after the press edge), so every press is a tap. *Latched charge:* a tap on a slow button holds its level on until the next press of that button, and that press is the release (a level falling, no new edge): in the latch the director gets a normal hold and a normal release. Both are per player (hub settings, like the one-shot).

## 3. The burst on a held X (from `lightHeld`)

Game Design: a held X is **a burst of eight lights that slows**, not a charge; X has no wind-up and every press is one attack. The input layer already supplies everything it needs: the press is an `light` edge on its tick (an instant light), and **`lightHeld` stays true while the button is down.** The read:

- **`burst_read(entry, live_now, p)`** (pure, in `press_read.gd`): for a LIGHT entry still down, returns `{burst: bool, lights: n, since: live ticks past the start}`: the burst starts at `read.holdLight` 8 live ticks after the press (data, kept), and ends when the button is released (the level falls) or the eighth light has been thrown. **The lights of a burst are the director's, not presses:** they are not pushed to the log, they are not graded for timing (a metronome must not earn skill strikes: the same rule as the hold-to-flurry note) and they do not count as mash presses; the player's real presses before and after still do.
- **What the classifier says while a burst runs:** the style is `"burst"` in place of `"hold"` for a LIGHT entry (a new value; the HUD and the alchemist treat it like a mash, not like a charge).
- **The slowing** (the eight gaps, e.g. 6, 6, 7, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12) is Game Design's data in the brawl, not the reader's; the reader only reports that the burst is on and how long it has run.
- **Freezes:** the burst's clock is live ticks like the wind-ups, so a freeze stops it and a release inside one is read at the first live tick, as in section 1; a re-press in the same freeze is the fresh press of the rule above (the old burst ends, the new press is an instant light).
- **Accessibility:** "charges off" does not switch the burst off (X has no charge); if a player cannot hold, the burst is simply never started. A latched X would make a burst run to its eighth light on a single tap, which is an unfair advantage and is not offered: **the latch applies to the slow buttons only.**

## 4. Built: `SimAim.nudge_ramp` (C1)

`SimAim.nudge_ramp(prev: int, raw: int, p: Dictionary = {}) -> int` and `SimAim.nudge_units(x: float) -> int` in `sim/input/aim.gd`, tested in `aim_test.gd` (the new `_nudge`, 38 checks). It is the slew limit specified in `q19-input.md`: letting go is 0 at once, a fresh push or a flip starts at `aim.nudgeStart` (0.4, which is 51 of 127), the same direction rises by `step` = ceil((127 - start) / `aim.nudgeTicks`) (7) a tick and follows the stick down at once. A keyboard reaches full on the 12th tick; a gradual analogue push passes through unchanged. Integers throughout, no float state, so the director can keep `prev` in its hashed integer state. **No change to the intent's shape.** Data: `aim.nudgeStart` 0.4 and `aim.nudgeTicks` 12 in `timing.json`; the schema change is `docs/controls/nudge-schema.patch` (applies at `-p1` on HEAD, and `tools/validate.js` passes with it).

## 5. Built for C2a and C2t (2026-10-06): `sim/input` and the settings

**Files:** `press_read.gd`, `layout.gd`, `touch.gd`, `hub.gd`, `data/input/timing.json`, tests `press_read_test.gd` (438), `charge_test.gd` (new, 84), and the updated `layout_test`, `touch_test`, `remap_test`, `agency_input_test`, `touch_glue_test`. **The intent's shape and version are unchanged** (nothing needed a gate). The schema, fixtures and cross-reference changes for the new and retired `read` keys are Tools' parked script `apply-input-c2a.cjs` (my two earlier patches are deleted as superseded).

**`press_read.gd` (pure).**
- `windup_for`, `decision_for` (Y 16, B 32, A 16; B only in the martial stance; Y keeps 12 in the energy stance), `held_ticks` (live clock when the entry carries `ldown`, else the real one), `charge_read` (winding, tap, charge, none), `tap_throw_at`.
- `push(..., stance, live, latched)` and `release(..., live)`: the optional live tick is stored as `ldown` and `lup` only when given (an unstamped entry is exactly what it was). **The re-press rule:** a fresh press on a button the log still has held releases the old one at that point, unless `latched`.
- `zip_read`'s heavy reading on the new rule (still down at wind-up plus grace, or let go after it; `opts.live_now` for the live clock); the `read.holdHeavy`, `holdSig`, `holdSigCharging` and `holdContext` keys are retired.
- `burst_read` (a held X: `{held, ready, since, stance}`), `pair_read` and `pair_commit` (the PAIR kind; the window 3 doubled by assist, the freshness 8, none under RT, a pressed-first light kept), `flurry_gap`, `mash_of` (per button), `alternation` (X, Y, B in any order, with `count_mode` for fist and bolt), `a_read` (the shove, the clinch and its taps, the coil with its share of the way to full).

**The burst's start is 16 live ticks** (Game Design's ruling, 2026-10-06: the same line as Y's and A's hold; `read.burstStart` 16). Until 16 a held X is one light and a 10-tick set; let go before and nothing more is thrown. **The classifier's and the zip's reading of a held X stay at the old 8** (`LEGACY_LIGHT_HOLD` in `press_read.gd`) until C2a, so nothing live moves; `burst_read` uses `burstStart`. Fold the constant into `burstStart` with C2a.

**The Simple layouts (pad and touch): the hold is the wind-up, and the bridge counts live ticks.** Yes, it can: `consumed()` is called only for steps the sim took (`sim_host.gd` calls it when `SimCore.step` returns true, never in a hit freeze), so the layout and `SimTouch` keep a `live` counter that `consumed()` advances, and the attack's hold is `live` at the release minus `live` at the press. A 9-build freeze adds nothing (tested on both). The KO's slow motion is the only place the sim's own clock (`S.T`) runs slower than the steps, and there the bridge reads steps; it is a moment when nothing is being decided. What the sim now receives from a Simple attack:
- **Held:** no edge. The level is the weight reached: `lightHeld` under 12 live ticks, `heavyHeld` from 12 (the medium), `sigHeld` from 28 (B's cell, the heavy).
- **Released:** one edge at that weight: `light`, `heavy` (the medium) or `signature` (B's cell). The edge carries `waited` as any edge does, and **its level is OFF on both pad and touch** (the hold was the wind-up and it is over). That is how the director tells it from a press of the same cell: Y's `signature` edge (Y is B's cell whole on Simple) always has its level **on** on the tick of its press, and its wind-up starts then. So a `signature` edge with `sigHeld` false is the Attack's already-wound heavy (lands 2 ticks after the release); with `sigHeld` true it is Y's press. **No intent bit is needed.** It also means the director's re-press rule (a fresh edge on a held button is release-then-press) applies only to an edge whose level is on. The director must treat a Simple `heavy` or `signature` edge as an already-wound blow (the hold was the wind-up): it lands 2 ticks after the release (about 14 and 30 after the press). The sim already sees `lightHeld` or `heavyHeld` rising before the edge, which is how the armour from tick 12 can be shown.
- **Ruled (Game Design, 2026-10-06):** Simple's cells are X Attack (light and medium by hold length), **Y Heavy, B's cell whole** (the binding does not change, only the label: Y stays `signature`), B guard, A dodge, RT power, **LB Context, A's cell**; no tap fires a signature on any layout. Tested in `charge_test.gd` (`_simple_cells`): LB is a `context` edge with `contextHeld` while held, which is what the director logs as the CONTEXT kind and `a_read` reads; RT + Y is the layer's `signature` with `stanceMask` 4 and `sigHeld`, the hold being the ladder. **On Simple touch:** there is no Y button, so **Y is a swipe up on Attack**: a `signature` edge at the swipe with its level on, and the finger staying down is the hold (`sigHeld`, the charged heavy); **RT + Y is Power held, then the same swipe, with the finger kept down**: the same edge in the charging stance (`stanceMask` 4), the hold being the signature's ladder. Its tell is the Attack button's ring, which `display_state()["attack"]` now describes with `swipe: true` and `hold_ticks` (live ticks since the swipe) for UI to mark at the ladder's 24 and 48. With Power held, a plain tap on Attack is the director's special, now decided on the release (it cannot be decided at the touch, because a swipe may follow). Touch Simple has no Context button (its fourth slot is Transform), so A's shove, clinch and tackle are the director's for a touch Simple player.
- **Was a question, now answered above:** on Simple, **Y is bound to `signature`** (a base press and a swipe on touch). With C2a a base `signature` edge means **B, the heavy**, so Simple's Y would throw a B heavy. The signature moves to RT + B (RT + Y on Simple). Simple's Y base cell needs a decision (the heavy's twin, or the signature on RT + Y only, with Y unbound); I have not changed it.
- `SimTouch.display_state()["attack"]` keeps `down` and `hold` (now live ticks over 12, full from the medium on) and adds **`hold_ticks`** (live ticks held), **`tier`** (0 light, 1 medium, 2 heavy), **`medium_at`** 12 and **`heavy_at`** 28 for the ring's two marks.

**The two settings** (layout only; per player; off by default): `SimLayout.set_latch_charge`, `set_charges_off`; `SimTouch` passes them to Full touch; the hub has `hub.set_latch_charge(on, slot = -1)`, `set_charges_off(on, slot = -1)`, `latch_charge_of(slot)`, `charges_off_of(slot)`, applied to the slot's device each tick (pad, keyboard, touch Full). They cover Y, B and A (base presses), never X, never the RT layer's signature (its ladder is the director's hold), and **never the Simple layouts** (their holds are weights). With both on, charges off wins. Under a latch a **second press lets the charge go with no new edge** and its release does nothing, so the director's re-press rule must not apply (`push(..., latched = true)`); a bounce within the 2-tick debounce does not let it go.

**Taps read as charges, by device.** `charge_test.gd` models tap lengths from the ranges in `q19-input.md` (pad 3 to 6 ticks, keyboard 4 to 8, touch 5 to 12, each with a small tail of lingering presses: 0.3%, 0.5% and 3%) and reads 20000 taps per button through `charge_read` (the wind-up plus the grace: Y 16, B 32, A 16). **Result: pad 0.00%, keyboard 0.00%, touch 1.86% on Y and 1.84% on A, 0.00% on B. QA's bands (under 2% pad and keyboard, 5% touch) hold.** These are **modelled, not measured**: the tap lengths are my assumptions, so this shows the numbers have room, not that real players meet the band. The touch band holds until about 8% of touch presses linger past 16 ticks. QA's measured tap lengths per device replace the model; the test names the assumption and its cut-off.

**Energy in reach (section 5b): what the stance and press reads need to chain with no gap.** Nothing new in the intent; four things for Encounter:
1. **Read the energy stance from `mode`, not `stanceMask`, for a press.** `mode` is the RB level on the build the edge arrives; `stanceMask` keeps a released stance button for `stance.releaseGrace` 2 ticks (so a flutter does not flicker the stance), which would turn a fist thrown 1 or 2 ticks after letting go of RB into a bolt. `mode` has no grace (and in the hybrid style it is the latch). An X and an RB pressed in the same build read as a bolt; an X a tick before RB is a fist.
2. **Keep the press's stance for its whole wind-up.** RB + Y is read as a medium (12 live ticks, `windup_for` gives 12 in the energy stance): if RB is let go during the wind-up the blast should still go as a blast. The log's `stance` field (the mask at the press) and `mode` are for that; the director should store them with the press, as `push(..., stance, live)` allows.
3. **The flurry does not care about the mode.** `mash_of` and the flurry's pace are by button, so punches and bolts thrown in turn are one X flurry (tested); the mix counts the bolts as `energy`. For a "two instruments" mix-up, `alternation(log, now, {count_mode: true})` counts a change between fist and bolt as a switch.
4. **A held X in the energy stance is the volley, not the burst:** `burst_read` reports `stance`, and the director decides. Pairs are read in the energy stance as in the others.
