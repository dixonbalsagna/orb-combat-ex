# Photosensitivity: what counts as adequate

Owner: Legal and IP Compliance. 2026-10-06. For the flash limit k05 and the energy-in-reach recording (`docs/vfx/reach.md`). **A screen, not legal advice and not a medical opinion.** Photosensitive seizures are a safety matter before they are a legal one. Read 2026-10-06; sources at the end.

## The standard to test against
**WCAG 2.2 success criterion 2.3.1, "three flashes or below threshold"** (W3C; primary). Content must not flash more than three times in any one second, **or** the flashes must be below the general-flash and red-flash thresholds.
- **A general flash** is a pair of opposing changes in relative luminance of 10% or more of the maximum, where the darker state is below 0.80 relative luminance.
- **The area test:** flashes together cover no more than about 25% of a 10-degree field of view; for a screen, a **341 x 256 pixel rectangle at 1024 x 768** is the stated estimate.
- **Red flash:** a stricter test for saturated red.
WCAG is written for web content, but it is the published, testable rule, and the Harding test used for broadcast and games follows the same science. Photosensitive seizures are usually triggered at about 5 to 25 flashes a second, some people at 3 to 60.

## What the recording shows (VFX, `docs/vfx/reach.md`)
Both fighters mashing at the design's rate: 9 full flashes in 3 seconds, never more than 3 in any 60 ticks; the largest frame-to-frame luminance change in a crop round the fighters 0.052, which is under the 10% threshold. The other landings give small sparks and a low-contrast rim at up to 20 landings a second, which is in the sensitive rate range but below the luminance threshold. **That is a good sign and not a pass:** the check is crude (one crop, not the standard's windows), staged at the design's rate rather than the sim's, and counts only this one effect.

## Addendum (2026-10-06): reading the standard for Tools' frame analyser
- **Area:** the **341 x 256 rectangle at 1024 x 768 is the estimate of the 10-degree field, and the threshold is 25% of that field** (about 0.006 steradians, roughly 21,800 pixels). The standard's words: "the combined area of flashes occurring concurrently occupies no more than a total of .006 steradians within any 10 degree visual field on the screen (25% of any 10 degree visual field)". The window is slid across the whole screen (any rectangle). Reading the rectangle as the threshold itself is four times too lenient.
- **General flash:** a pair of opposing changes in relative luminance of 10% or more of the maximum (1.0), the darker state below 0.80, with RGB gamma-decoded (sRGB) before the luminance is computed.
- **Red flash (WCAG 2.2):** a pair of opposing transitions where one state has R/(R+G+B) of 0.8 or more and the two states differ by more than 0.2 in the CIE 1976 u'v' chromaticity diagram. The older broadcast formula (BT.1702) uses (R-G-B) x 320 above 20 instead. Either is acceptable if stated. Counting every pixel that is not a saturated red as zero is not the standard's wording; it is plausibly conservative, but label it "conservative variant" in the doc and name the formula used.
- **Wording to use in public:** "An automated flash check based on WCAG 2.3.1 (general flash, red flash and area), run on recorded gameplay, found no failure." **Never** "safe", "seizure-safe", "tested for photosensitivity", "WCAG compliant" or "certified". Keep the in-game notice. The camera cut's whole-screen dip counts as a flash over the whole screen area: cap it before the public release.

## Addendum 4 (2026-10-06): the bounded memory, the gate, and the notice
- **Bounded one-second memory:** a fair and defensible reading, at the lenient end. The standard counts flashes in any one-second period, so a one-second memory is natural, and the defect it fixes (a count that depends on where the analysis starts) is real and must be fixed. But the established broadcast method (Harding, ITU-R BT.1702, my understanding, not re-read today) tracks each luminance transition from one direction reversal to the next with no time limit, so a slow swell over 1.2 s followed by a fast fall counts as a pair there and only the fall in Tools' reading. **Report both:** the primary reading (one-second memory) and a **stricter cross-check** (a two-second memory, or direction-reversal transitions without a time limit), and say which formula each uses in `flash-check.md`.
- **The gate:** the primary reading must show the worst second at **2.5 or below**, and **no sensitivity run may exceed 3** (the area threshold 15% lower, and the stricter-memory cross-check). A scene that is over 3 on any sensitivity run is a fail, not a pass with a note. Also report how many counted changes sit within 15% above the area threshold, as Tools proposes.
- **The notice:** I agree not to weaken the gate card or the warning until the whole required set passes at the gate. **But it must stay true today.** If the corrected recount finds nothing over 3 on the primary reading, "found scenes that flash more often than the recommended limit" is no longer what the check says. Then change line 2 to a statement true under every reading: "Our own automated check has found scenes that are at or near the recommended limit of three flashes a second, and we are reducing them." Keep "no independent photosensitivity analyser has been run", keep the click-through gate, and keep the advice for people with photosensitive epilepsy. Remove neither until the set passes.

## Addendum 6 (2026-10-12): a margin for a release pass, and the required set
- **A release pass is never "just under".** In addition to the combined rule (primary reading at most 2.5 in the worst second; no sensitivity run above 3): (1) **the area-minus-15% run and the stricter-memory run must both be at most 3**, and a clip whose worst second differs by more than 1.0 between runs is flagged **sensitive** and re-captured at another seed or start before it counts; (2) **no counted change may sit within 15% above the area threshold in the worst second unless the second totals 2.0 or less**; (3) **the pass is on the commit being published and on both modes** (normal and Reduce flashing), and a clip at exactly 3 is a fail of our gate. A pass at 101% of the area threshold on a busy clip is not one I would publish on.
- **The required set grows with the content.** Add now: the re-laid city (a flyby of all three streets and the avenues, at depth); fighters in depth in AI matches (the pair moving between streets); an open-floor building opening and closing in view; the mash at the foot of the towers; a signature and an explosion in the city; a zip chain and a launch across the city; the sky at its extremes with a town in view; camera travel at maximum speed over high-contrast scenery (striped facades, the crowd's shirts); a beam clash; the transformation; 20 fixed AI seeds (every pairing and signature). Re-run the whole set whenever a change touches the camera, lighting, scenery contrast, an effect or the crowd.
- **The HUD is in the frame.** The crown's flicker (k06) and any persistent HUD cue are tested with the rest.

## Addendum 7 (2026-10-12): answers to Tools' seven questions, and two facts
**Facts recorded.** (a) **A capture taken while the machine was starved can be wrong, not only slow** (one frame with an 87,000 px artefact changed a reading from 2 to 2.5): a clip captured on a starved machine is void, and the pass records that the capture was taken through the guarded launcher on a machine that was not starved. (b) **AI seeds are not stable across sim changes:** a seed that showed both signatures on one build may not on the next, so the required seeds are re-chosen and their coverage proven on the release commit.

1. **Per-change subset of 3-second windows after a fast-forward:** yes, as a development check, never the release pass. Its windows are the worst seconds of the previous full run plus the changed scene's own window, it is labelled "per change", it cannot be cited as a pass, and a failure stops the change.
2. **"20 fixed AI seeds":** 20 **in all** for a release, not 20 per pairing (66 pairings at 20 each is not capturable). Spread them so that every fighter appears in at least 5 matches and every signature at least twice, with **every pairing captured at least once over a rolling cycle** (track a coverage table across releases, uncaptured pairings first), and **any pairing new in a release (a new fighter) captured at least 3 times before that fighter ships**.
3. **Re-picking the 20 at each release by the scan:** yes, if the rule is deterministic and recorded and the picks are listed in the pass. Two safeguards so the picks cannot flatter the build: the **five worst seeds of the previous release stay in the set**, and **any seed that failed in an earlier candidate stays until the cause is fixed and recorded**.
4. **Guarded launcher on a machine that was not starved as a recorded condition:** yes. Record the launcher's CPU cap and time limit, the owner's tag and the machine's load before and after. For any clip whose worst second falls between 2.0 and 3.0 a **second independent capture** is required, and the **worse** of the two readings counts; two captures that differ by more than 0.5 are flagged and re-run.
5. **The margin rule:** confirmed as Tools reads it. "A counted change within 15% above the threshold" is each alternation event in the worst second whose area is between 1.0 and 1.15 times the threshold (the analyser's marginal count), and "totals 2.0 or less" is the primary reading of that second. A second with any marginal event must total 2.0 or less.
6. **"Another start" for a fixed scenario:** a start 600 ticks later counts as another start for the **steadiness of the measurement** (it tests start-dependence). For a clip flagged **sensitive**, the re-capture must be a different run of the scenario (a different start position, distance, direction or camera phase), not a later window of the same run.
7. **k06 in the pass:** yes, show the HUD's own rate (hud_check's result against the 3-a-second limit) as a separate line in the pass record, beside the general rule. It costs nothing.
- **The toll chip** (brightens for 2.5 seconds after a change, no sound): fine as it is. Keep it a brighten-and-hold with no re-pulse, so a burst of changes extends one brightening and never strobes.
