## The looks
- **Ledger hall** (stepped, pale stone, a dark arcade at the street): clear. A setback tower is generic. Keep it without an antenna mast or a lit crown in colour (w03), so it never reads as a famous New York tower.
- **Bell tower** (a tall pale shaft, a dark belfry block, a pyramid roof): clear; the rule of no clock face holds.
- **Crown tower** (a glass tower with a low gem between four corner points): from the side only two thin pale spires show, standing up on a dark mansard-like roof, and the silhouette reads as a tower with horns, which invites one particular comparison (a known building nicknamed for a masked vigilante). It is not an infringement by shape, but it is the one association I would not hand to a viewer. **Nudge:** make the four corner points short stubs (a third of the present height) in the tower's own stone or glass tone, not a pale accent, and let the low gem roof read between them.
- **Chimney stack, lighthouse, village hall, hill chapel:** clear in principle; **the lighthouse has no stripes and no beam** (w03), as Rendering already holds.
- **The skyline** (the bell tower and glass slabs with pyramid and flat roofs): clear. No red and white, no striping, no franchise silhouette.

## Open floors
A tall building opening its lowest floors on a few thin posts looks, by the still, like a tower that has lost its support, which is the one image Orb's sensitivity rule keeps out. It is acceptable only if it reads as design. Conditions (w01): **regular, sturdy columns** (a row across the front, not four corner posts and one in a bay; each at least about a twelfth of the front's width), **a deep beam or slab edge visible above**, **a lit lobby interior** (warm windows, not black), **one double-height floor rather than two** if the building is under about ten floors, **opens with a short fade and never pops**, **never opens or stays open on a damaged building or one in a break stage**, **closes when the fight leaves**, and **no still, clip or caption of an open-floor building appears in public with debris, a crater or a casualty count above zero**. The words "stilts", "hollow" and "gutted" stay out of every caption.

## The civilian count
World's flag is right: it bears on an age rating. **1,800 civilians and a HUD reading "CIVILIANS LOST n / 1800" with 200 to 350 lost in a typical match is a scored, displayed toll of non-combatants, and the villain is rewarded for it.** What I think:
- **Depiction drives the rating more than the number:** keep casualties non-graphic (no blood, no bodies, no close-ups, no children, no hospital or school staging), as already planned. Answer the questionnaires honestly: violence against non-combatants is present, tracked and, for one fighter, rewarded.
- **Expect the upper end of the range** I gave before (Teen or PEGI 12 to 16), and some boards treat a scored toll of innocents as an aggravating factor. Run the IARC and Steam questionnaires with this build and read the outcome before any store page.
- **What I would show:** in game, the count as it is (it is the game's point: anguish and menace). **In public: no picture, clip or caption shows a count above zero or the toll as a headline** (w02); screenshots use a moment with the HUD at 0 or hide it. Do not market the toll ("hundreds lost") in any copy.
- **Wording in the HUD:** "lost" is neutral and fine. Do not add celebration, a score, a bonus sound or a flourish to the count going up.
