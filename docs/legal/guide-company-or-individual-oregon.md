# Company or individual? Selling the game from Oregon

Owner: Legal and IP Compliance. 2026-10-16. Written for Orb, a beginner. **General information, not legal or tax advice.** I could not open Oregon's own fee pages (sos.oregon.gov) today, so **every dollar figure below is from memory or secondary sources and must be checked on the state's site before you pay**. Where a professional is worth paying, it says so.

## The short answer
1. **Before your first dollar of sales: form a single-member LLC in Oregon.** It is cheap (low hundreds of dollars a year), takes about a week, keeps the game's money, contracts and music subscriptions apart from your personal life, and puts a company name on the store, not yours.
2. **An LLC is not a magic shield.** It limits what a customer's or a copyright claimant's lawsuit can take to what the company holds, but only if you keep the money and records separate. It does **not** protect you from your own illegal acts, and it does not remove the need for the licence and AI rules in the other guides. Insurance is a separate (later) question.
3. **If you want to start even smaller:** a sole proprietor can sell on itch.io with no filing at all. That is fine for a free or pay-what-you-want release. Form the LLC **before** you take real money or open Steam (see the selling guide, stages 3 and 4).
4. Pay a business lawyer or an Oregon-friendly accountant for **one hour** to confirm this, because details (your city, your other income, other owners) can change the answer.

## Sole proprietor versus single-member LLC

| | **Sole proprietor** (you, doing business) | **Single-member LLC** (a company you own alone) |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Filing | None. You may use a different public name ("assumed business name") by registering it with the Oregon Secretary of State (a small fee; check) | File "Articles of Organization" with the Oregon Secretary of State online |
| Cost to start | About $0 (up to about $100 if you register a business name) | **About $100 filing fee** (check the state's page) |
| Yearly cost | None to the state | **An annual report, about $100** (check the state's page); you must keep it filed or the company is dissolved |
| Personal liability | **Unlimited:** you personally owe what the business owes | **Limited** to what the company has, if you keep it separate (the "veil" can be pierced by mixing money or ignoring the formalities) |
| Taxes | You report profit on your personal return (Schedule C) | **By default the same.** A single-member LLC is "disregarded" by the IRS; you still file Schedule C and pay self-employment tax. Oregon has a small minimum-tax rule for some entities; for a disregarded single-member LLC this does not normally apply (check with your accountant) |
| Looks to a store or a bank | Your own name and Social Security number | A company name and an EIN (you can use the EIN instead of your SSN on forms) |
| Privacy | Your legal name tied to the business | Your name is still on the state filing unless you use a registered agent address and a commercial mailing address; the state record is public |

## The Oregon steps (an LLC)
1. **Choose a name.** It must be distinguishable from every other Oregon company and include "LLC" (the state has a name-availability search). **Check the name against trademarks too:** the state does not do that. This is the same worry as the game's title; do the formal title search first.
2. **A registered agent.** Every Oregon LLC needs one: a person or company with an Oregon street address (not a PO box) open during business hours who accepts legal papers. You may be your own (your address is then public), or hire a service (about $50 to $150 a year). A hired agent gives you privacy and reliability if you move or work odd hours.
3. **File the Articles of Organization online** with the Secretary of State's Corporation Division. Approval is usually within days.
4. **An operating agreement.** Oregon does not require you to file it, but write one (a one-page single-member form is fine). Banks often ask for it, and it is evidence that the company is a separate thing.
5. **An EIN** (Employer Identification Number): free from the IRS, in minutes, on the IRS site. **Only use irs.gov.** Paid "EIN services" charge for what is free.
6. **A business bank account** in the LLC's name; deposit all game income there and pay all game costs from there. This is the step that actually makes the liability limit hold.
7. **Local licences.** Oregon has no general state business licence and **no sales tax.** But some cities and counties charge their own business taxes or licences. If you live in or near Portland, the city and Multnomah County have business income taxes and licences, and the Portland area has other local business taxes; other cities differ. Ask your city (and the county) what applies to a home-based software business.
8. **Oregon's Corporate Activity Tax** applies only above $1 million of Oregon commercial activity; you will not reach it at first, but if you ever might, it exists.
9. **The annual report** each year on the anniversary of the filing, with the fee, and keep the registered agent and address current.

## Store payouts and tax forms
- **Both Steam and itch.io ask for a tax interview** (a W-9 style form for a US person) and a bank account. If you have an LLC and an EIN, you enter the **LLC's** name, its EIN and its business bank account; a single-member LLC is "disregarded" so the form also asks for **your** name and a tax classification (the form's instructions say which box).
- **Steam:** the bank account must be in the **same name** as your legal identification (as Steam's own page says), so open the bank account first, in the exact name used on the form. Allow the 30-day waiting period.
- **itch.io:** payout to PayPal or Stripe is in the name on that account; to avoid mixed personal and business money, open them in the LLC's name.
- **Taxes:** the stores send you forms (or report to the IRS) once earnings pass a threshold; you still owe tax on every dollar regardless.

## The privacy question: using "Orb"
Orb's public name is a pseudonym. **A pseudonym is fine for a page, a title and a brand,** but the state filing, the bank, the stores' tax forms and any contract must carry your **legal** name (or the company's). A company name that is not your own name (and a hired registered agent) keeps your name off most public pages; the state's record will still show who the organiser or registered agent is. If keeping your name private matters a great deal, ask the lawyer about a registered agent service and a commercial mailing address.

## Costs, first year (rough; check them)
LLC filing about $100; registered agent $0 to $150; annual report about $100 from the second year; an accountant about $200 to $500; a lawyer's hour about $200 to $400. **About $300 to $1,000 the first year** with professional help, **about $100 to $250 without.**

## The ownership link
The licence, the music and the AI-content notices should name the **copyright holder.** If the LLC exists, it is cleaner for the LLC to hold the game's rights (you assign what you own to it in a short signed paper). A lawyer writes that paper in minutes. Do it before the first sale.

## My recommendation, in order
1. Free browser release on itch.io as a sole proprietor, if you wish: no money, no filing.
2. **Form the LLC, EIN, bank account and registered agent** before you take any payment or open Steam. About a week.
3. **One hour with an accountant** about the first-year tax calendar and the quarterly estimates.
4. **One hour with a lawyer** on the assignment of rights, the licence files and the platform agreements.
5. Insurance (general liability, and later cyber) once there are real sales; ask a broker.

## Ask a professional when
You have a co-owner, a publisher or investor offers money, you want to sell outside the US, you hire anyone (even a contractor: who owns what they make?), a customer or a rights holder sends a complaint, or you are unsure whether your city taxes the business.
