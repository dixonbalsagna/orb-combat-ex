Owner: Legal and IP Compliance. 2026-10-22. Screens `docs/narrative/planet-and-homes.md` (commit 06ef4e3d) and `docs/narrative/opponent-grounds.md`. A screen, not legal advice and not a trademark search. RL-163 to RL-168; new rows w07 and ln04.

## 1. The 18 planet names: **Pass as in-game names; two cautions**
Method: each name against GeoNames (every town and city over 15,000 people: no match for any), and a web search for a game, brand, trademark or fictional place with the same name (no hit for any; the search engine is weak on rare words, so this is a screen and not a register search). From general knowledge I looked for echoes of well-known fictional planets and franchises (none: none of the 18 resembles a named planet from the genre's famous series). None contains "orb".

| Style | Names | Verdict |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Plain compound | Brindle, Everhold, Wayhold, Tallowmere | **Pass.** Ordinary English parts. Brindle is also a small English village and a coat pattern: harmless |
| Short and invented | Tessane, Oarel, Kestrane, Ostrel, Marrowell | **Pass.** Coined; nothing found. Tessane sounds like a singer's first name; the spelling differs and a place is not a person, so no issue |
| Old and weathered | Carrowdeep, Harrowfell, Thornmarch, Sallowgate | **Pass.** Near the many "Harrow-" fantasy places (Harrowdeep, Harrowdale), but each is a different, ordinary compound; none is a mark |
| Title or lore-tied | The Round, **The Long Way Round**, Kindlewick, Ebbhold, Gazemere | **Pass except The Long Way Round:** that is the title of a well-known travel documentary series and book, a brand with sequels. Used only inside the game as a stage name it is low risk, but **do not use it as the planet's public name, a mode name or a marketing title.** The others pass |

**Caution 1: a name used as a public product name** (the planet's name on a store page, a mode title, a shirt) is a different question: it needs the same formal search as the game's own title. Inside the game it is fine.
**Caution 2:** "Brindle" and "Everhold" are the two most ordinary of the set, so most likely to be a small company's name somewhere; pick them freely for the game, search before they appear in marketing.

## 3. The Cyborg's breaking yard, claim and line: **Pass with conditions (ln04)**
Slagholt, "wrecks and cranes to throw people into", the desert, and the claim "wreckage, which is his fuel".
- **The yard itself:** wreckage and no people. **Pass.** It fits ln02: his hunger is aimed at fighters and wreckage, never at civilians. "Wrecks and cranes to throw people into" means throwing a fighter into scenery, which is ordinary.
- **The claim's weak point:** it also chooses "a damaged or fallen place from earlier in the run" (weight 3) and avoids an untouched town "so nothing about his ground puts a living person on his menu". A fallen place is one where, in the run, people were counted lost. Fighting there and feeding on its wreckage is not a depiction of harm, but it could read as profiting from a toll. **Conditions (ln04):**
  - The picker scores **wear state (damaged, fallen), never the casualty count**, and no line, caption or card mentions people there.
  - The Cyborg's line stays about wreckage and does not change with the toll.
  - No bodies or remains are drawn in a fallen place; the toll is still only a count (c08).
  - He does not gain a gameplay advantage from fighting on fallen ground (a home is colour only, as the file already says); a stat that grows with how many people died would breach c08.
- **The line:** "Lots of wreckage out here. Free fuel!" **Pass.** It is about objects; "fuel" is the machine half of him.
- **Other lines in the file:** Empress "Wide open ground, insect. Nowhere to hide!" passes (an insult at an individual fighter, ln01); the rival's "Up here, everyone can see you lose." passes; the Protagonist's "Keep it over the water. Nobody's roof breaks!" passes. The four heavy full-charge hints (option 1 to 4) are plain instructions: pass.
- **The home lines in `planet-and-homes.md`:** "Smells like old steel. My favourite kind of lunch!" (Cyborg) passes as a hunger joke on metal (ln02); "Our province, rat. Do mind the banners!" passes. In the same file, the Cyborg's ground under the Tide idea mentions "dead hulls": ships, fine.
