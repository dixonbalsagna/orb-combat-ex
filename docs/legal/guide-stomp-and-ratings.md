# The stomp: how rating boards judge a violent blow

Owner: Legal and IP Compliance. 2026-10-16. Written for Orb, who asked for more context before any ruling: a brutal curb stomp and an over-the-top crash from the sky "should be judged differently". They are. This is general information, not legal advice; board rules change and are applied by people, so the only real answer comes from filling in the questionnaires on the real build (free, see the selling guide). Board criteria below are from the boards' public descriptions as I know them, not re-read today.

## What rating boards actually look at
Boards do not count blows; they ask what a player sees and what the game does with it. The same move can land in different places on these questions:

1. **Who is the victim?** An armed opponent in a fight is the norm. A **defenceless** one (down, helpless, a civilian, someone begging) is treated as worse. PEGI's top category names violence against defenceless people.
2. **How real does it look?** A cartoon or fantasy figure in a world with energy beams is read very differently from a lifelike body. Stylised, over-the-top violence is the lowest violent tier ("fantasy violence").
3. **How much detail, and how long?** A brief impact is not a lingering close-up. Blood, bone, a deformed face, repeated blows and slow motion push it up.
4. **Is it rewarded or celebrated?** Points, an achievement, a cheering sound or a taunt after a defenceless kill count against you. A cost or a grim tone counts for you.
5. **Is it the point of the game, or optional?** A rare finisher the player chooses is different from every fight ending that way.
6. **The target body part.** Blows to the head, face, neck and groin of a downed person read as the most gratuitous, because they look intended to maim rather than to win.

The tiers, roughly: ESRB T (Teen: fantasy or some realistic violence, some blood), **M (Mature 17+: intense violence, blood and gore)**; PEGI 16 (realistic violence), **PEGI 18 (gross violence, or violence towards defenceless characters, motiveless killing)**; Germany's USK 16 and 18 (strict on realistic depictions of cruelty, and on anything that looks like glorifying it); Australia R18+; Japan's CERO Z. Orb's target is **Mature**, so most of this is within reach. The questions are about where you sit inside it, and what you must keep off GitHub, trailers and ads.

## Plain examples (no franchise names)

| The scene | How boards tend to read it | Our s09 verdict |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| A fighter stamps a **standing** opponent in the chest or leg and sends them sliding, a clean cartoon impact | Ordinary fantasy or combat violence | **Clear** |
| A fighter stamps a **downed opponent's torso or limbs**, once or twice, impact and reaction only | Standard fight-game violence at Teen and Mature; no gore | **Clear** |
| A fighter leaps high and **crashes down from the sky** onto a standing opponent or the ground beside them, a crater, a shockwave, the opponent flung | **Over the top and fantasy**: the force is physically impossible, the target is a fighter not a defenceless person, the result is a crater and not an injury | **Clear** (and the safest form) |
| A fighter crashes down on a **downed opponent's torso** with a crater and a cloud, no visible injury | Over-the-top; the downed target pushes it a little up, the spectacle pulls it down | **Clear** |
| A fighter **stands over a downed opponent and slowly stamps the head or face**, repeatedly, with a close-up, a sound, blood or a deformed face | **The textbook "brutal" version.** Defenceless victim, gratuitous detail, a target that reads as intended to kill. Likely the top tier in Europe and Germany, and may trigger a store review even at Mature | **Out of the base moveset** until a rating decision |
| The same stomp as **one rare finisher**, chosen by the player, filmed from far away, impact cut to a bright flash and a crater, no visible head | The detail and duration shrink, the reward is gone; still reads as a stomp on a downed face | **Possible, with conditions** |

## So is the over-the-top sky drop "judged differently"?
**Yes.** What moves a scene down the scale is: a standing or fighting victim, stylised results (a crater, not an injury), brevity, no reward, and an impossible force that signals fantasy. What moves it up is a **defenceless victim, a target on the head, face, neck or groin, repetition and close-ups.** The sky drop onto a downed body is in between; the curb stomp on a downed head is at the top.

## Our standing ruling (s09) and the question for you
**Clear:** a stomp on a standing opponent; a stomp or drop on a downed **torso or limbs**; any over-the-top sky drop whose result is a crater and not a visible injury.
**Held, until the rating decision:** a stamp or drop on a downed **head, face, neck or groin**, as a base move.
**If you want the brutal finisher in the game:** I would allow it only as a **rare, optional finisher** (not a fight-ending default), **never in a public trailer, page or GitHub-hosted image**, shown from a distance with the injury **not** depicted (impact flash, crater, cut away), **never rewarded** (no score, achievement or cheer), and **with the rating questionnaires (Steam content survey, IARC) answered from the real build before you commit to it.** If the board answers come back at a tier you do not want, the finisher is the first thing to change.
