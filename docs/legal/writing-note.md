# Writing the public copy plainly: what the law needs and what it does not

Owner: Legal and IP Compliance. 2026-10-12. For Marketing, Narrative and the EP, after Orb's note that the forward-facing writing reads as rushed and bizarre. Some of the stiffness came from my screens; this says which constraints are real and which were caution. A screen, not legal advice. Keep Orb's rule: Orb sees public text before it is used.

## What must stay (true facts; the wording is free)
1. **The AI statement, upfront.** The facts: AI tools wrote the code and drafted the art, animation data, dialogue, UI text and design documents; Orb directs it; the music is Suno-generated once music ships; no AI model runs while you play. It must be near the top of any page. It can be written in the game's own voice. A plain version: "Orb Combat EX is a one-person project. Orb directs it, and AI tools write the code and draft the art, animation, dialogue and design documents. Nothing in the game is generated by AI while you play: the fight director and the world are ordinary game code."
2. **The flash notice's facts** while true: what our own check found, that no independent analyser has been run, the reduce settings, and the advice for people with photosensitive epilepsy. Plainer: "Some scenes flash faster than the three-flashes-a-second guideline, so the build is offline while we fix them. We have checked it with our own tool, not an independent analyser."
3. **The privacy line** matching what the build does.
4. **The licence line:** "all rights reserved for now" (or whatever is chosen), and no "open source" unless true.
5. **Never-use words and claims** (`never-use-words.md`): no franchise names or comparisons, no "human-made", "no AI", "safe", "tested for photosensitivity", no implied endorsement.
6. **One sentence** that the game is in development and may change.

## What can go (hedges that were caution, not law)
- **"Planned:" on each sentence.** One line, "Everything here is in development and may change", covers a page where nothing is playable. State the game in plain present tense.
- **"is designed to", "are meant to", "we are working to".** Drop them. On a page that says the game is in development, "The fight director chains your light, medium and heavy strikes into combos as you fight" is an ordinary description of the game, not a promise of a feature on sale.
- **"What we are working on" and "What to expect when it comes back".** A heading can say "What the game is" and a closing line can say the build is offline and returns when it passes the flash checks.
- **The extra caution on destruction words.** Withdrawn with the sensitivity rule: describe buildings breaking, towers coming down and the world wrecked as the hook it is.
- **"Found scenes at or near..." once the checks pass.** Say plainly what is true when it is true.

## Still careful (a truthfulness ground, not a hedge)
- Do not say a feature is **in a build players can play now** if it is not. Until a build returns, write about the game, not "what you can try".
- Avoid absolutes that can be disproved ("never", "every building") where a plain "most" or "any" is true; puffery ("climactic", "no two fights alike") is fine.
- **Where it is hosted:** the GitHub-hosted pages keep graphic images and sales copy off (p01); the store pages and video platforms take them.
