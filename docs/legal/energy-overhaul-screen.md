## Verdict: **Pass, with conditions.**
The grammar (light rapid fire, medium lob, heavy blast or charged beam, grab, mines) is genre-generic, and the spec already holds my rules: no pause, nothing on or over the fighters, a thin ring or edge light at the hand and never over the body (E6), the beam struggle as built, c08 for the people. The conditions below are what the kits, looks and effects must also respect.

## 1. Shapes, poses, call-out patterns and names to avoid (e11)
The genre's best-known energy signatures, so nobody builds them by accident:
- **Shapes:** a ball, sphere or orb held in or above a hand (the spirit-bomb, death-ball and ball-in-palm shapes); **a flat spinning disc or hoop, especially one held above the head** (the cutting disc); a beam with a spiral, drill or helix; a beam shaped as lines from a hand-frame (hands forming a triangle or diamond around the target); a fireball thrown from two cupped palms; a lightning-wrapped hand; a finger-gun or two-finger beam.
- **Poses:** hands cupped at the hip then thrust; both palms together; arms raised overhead to gather; a crouch with fists at the sides and a scream; arms wide to take a blow; a fist-to-the-sky charge. (All already in the banned rows b, h and e; this restates them for the kits.)
- **Call-out patterns:** shouting the technique's own name at release; a two-part call (a long syllable chain while charging, then a shout at the release); a shouted multiplier or number; a countdown. **Our call-outs ("Here it comes! Don't blink!") pass; keep them taunts and instructions, never the move's name or a split chant.** Only a signature or finisher is shouted.
- **Names to avoid:** any name built on "wave", "flash" (as a technique), "bomb", "ball", "disc" or "cannon"; "Destructo", "Spirit", "Dragon" or "Final" as a lead word. Ours are place and tool words: keep it so.
- **Beam looks:** E96's "a lance drills" and "a twin beam braids" must **not** mean a rotating drill, a spiral or a helix. A lance is a thin straight beam; a twin beam is two parallel beams in lane colours, the tints alternating **along** the beam and never around it (e07, fx03). A split beam is a straight beam that forks, never a spiral. A wide beam never fills the screen white.
- **Charge looks:** the hand steps up in **brightness** at three levels ("a click and a rumble tick"), at the heel of the palm or a thin ring, **not a ball or sphere**, never above or between the hands, never a scream, an aura flare or a crouch (h05, e06, e10). A loaded hand is a steady light (no pulse over 1 Hz), and the other hand stays open and a shoulder width away, with no wrist grip.

## 2. The grab looks (e12): **Pass**
GD's note holds and I agree: **the ring binds and never cuts.** Conditions:
- **No disc.** The ring is a thin loop that closes, not a spinning flat disc and not an edge. It is never held, charged or raised above the head, never spins like a saw, never severs and throws no sparks. It flies facing forward or at hand height and snaps shut round the arms and torso.
- **The binds** (net, ring, lash, cage) take **arms, torso or body**, never the throat, face or hair (g01, g05). The held fighter is drawn **struggling or bracing** and never limp or hanging (g07), with no sexualised framing (no tight wrapping of the hips or chest).
- **The payoffs:** the yank, the bind (a shot lands on a held fighter, drawn as a normal hit), the slam (into the ground under him, a torso, s09) and the throw (a launch through the planner) are fine. No slow motion, zoom or taunt over a bound fighter, and no finisher on a bound fighter.
- **The cage of bars** is an energy cage in lane colour, not a prison or a cell with a person seated; keep it brief (at most the 40-tick catch).

## 3. Mines, traps and snares (e13): **Pass, with the landmine point**
- **Looks:** small, in the fighter's colour, dim while arming, a slow glow when armed (at or under 1 Hz), a bright flare for 6 ticks, a faint line or zone for a trap. All pass: in the world, nothing on the fighters.
- **"Mine" is a landmine's word.** Its real-world harm falls on civilians long after a fight, so the design must never read as a minefield. **A mine fizzles after 12 s (E76) and never survives its fight**, and is never a lasting hazard for the places or people in a run. No mine ever moves toward people or is laid on one (E79). Keep "the people it kills are on the layer's account" as a count only (c08): no score, no cheer, no achievement. The carer's mines holding while a person is in the burst is good and should stay.
- **Names pass:** Warm Patch, Hot Doorstep, Pressure Plate, Quiet Step, Carpet Snap, Red Carpet, Scrap Charge, Wired Heap. "Pressure Plate" is a plain term; "Red Carpet" is an idiom. Keep the player-facing word "mine" plain, with no joke about the real thing.
- **Flash:** a string of spark mines each flaring for 6 ticks is a rapid sequence: see the register below.

## 5. Flash conditions: what must go in the register (fx08)
The standard counts the **combined area** of luminance changes of 10% or more in any one screen region, so many small flashes in the same place add up. The stream, the barrage and the beam's own flares are the likeliest new sources. All of the following are **registered flash sources** (each counted at its drawn area at the framing in use; the cap of 3 a second holds over everything together, with the margin rule; red read 0):
1. **Bolts:** each muzzle flash and each hit spark. A mashed or held stream is up to 10 a second, so **a stream must not flash per bolt**: bolt bodies are small moving objects; the muzzle is a steady low light at the hand while the stream runs; the hits build one steady scorch glow on the target, not one spark per bolt. A per-bolt flash is allowed only when it is registered and refused beyond the cap.
2. **Lob bursts:** each burst (a barrage is one every 12 ticks, 5 a second, which is over the cap). **At most 3 counted bursts a second in any one screen window** (c04); a burst beyond the cap draws as smoke, dust and an ember, not a white flash. The level-2 forms: three bursts in one landing count as **one** event with their summed area; a clinging lob that goes off later is its own event; a rolling lob is fine.
3. **The heavy blast** (burst plus a column of flame): one event per blast, the flame amber-orange and slow (fx05).
4. **Beams:** the start flash, each level change if it is more than a hand light, the end or detonation, and the **pulse flares**. E50's pulses can be 10 a second: **a pulse is a swell in the beam body of under a 10% luminance step; its front flare is a registered source, refused beyond 3 a second**. Under Reduce flashing (the locked default) it is the swell only. A held B pulsing every 8 ticks (7.5 Hz) must be the swell only. The struggle's beat stays at or under 3 a second (fx04).
5. **Charge steps:** three brightness steps within 1.4 s at the hand: a small area, but each step rises over **at least 0.1 s, never a single-frame pop**, and counts in the register. The loaded glow is steady.
6. **Mines and traps:** every flare (6 ticks) and every detonation. A **line of spark mines** must not flare in a cascade faster than the cap: stagger the flares or let a chain count as one event. Arming glow is slow (at or under 1 Hz).
7. **Grabs:** the catch flash and the payoff flash (the yank, the bind's shot, the slam).
8. **The struggle** as built; level 3 beams and signatures count as already ruled.
9. **Clips before any public pin:** a held stream and a mash at close and far framing; a lob barrage; a beam at levels 1 to 3 with pulses mashed and held; a string of spark mines triggered in a run; both grabs; a mixed string (C9). In both modes, margin rule, red 0. The nightly seeds on the new commit will include energy once the switch is on; add these fixed clips.

**Nothing here reaches the live demo:** the work sits behind `energy.v2`; the page is the pinned, calm build.

### New row fx09: saturated red on the standard's own test
The standard counts a red flash when R/(R+G+B) on linear values is 0.8 or more and the chromaticity changes by over 0.2 over a large area. **Computed from the hex codes, the kits' colours all exceed 0.8:** the Empress's #d81f3c about 0.92, the Cyborg's #c8321a about 0.93 and #ff6a1a about 0.87 (the Protagonist's cyan and the Rival's violet are far from it). A steady colour is not a flash, and small slivers, rivets and needles are not near the area threshold. **The risk is the biggest events in these hues:** a level-3 beam's start and end, a crater's flame column, a heavy blast, a level-2 burst and the mantle's banners in the Empress's or the Cyborg's colour. So: (1) **the analyser, not the colour code, decides** and must read red flashes 0 on the clips; (2) **peak frames use the white-hot core, which lowers the ratio below 0.8**, and the saturated hue stays on the body of the effect; (3) Art's non-red lane for the Empress is welcome and removes the question for her; the Cyborg's ember-red to orange needs the same read.

### 1. Do the grab call-outs count as taunts over a bound fighter (e12)? **Not if they follow ln06**
e12 bans a taunt, zoom or slow motion that *holds* a scene over a bound fighter: a pose, pause or camera that dwells on the humiliation. A call-out said as the grab lands is part of the action, a short battle line (4 to 12 words), and passes if it follows ln06:
- **One call-out per grab,** played **on the throw (as the grab leaves his hand) or in the first 10 ticks of the catch**, inside the hit-stop and never extending it.
- **Nothing is said during the rest of the catch, over the payoff, or over a kneel or landing after a slam.** No second line, no repeat, no reply line from the grabber while the fighter is held.
- The line never holds the action, triggers a zoom, a camera cut, a pause or a held pose, and no finisher follows it.
- Insults in the line (rat, ranked, clown) follow ln01: at the fighter, never a slur. "Gotcha! Come in close!", "Arms down. You're being ranked.", "Gahaha! Down in our cage, rat!" and "Hook's out! Throwing you clear across!" all pass on this basis. The commands that name the move's act ("Arms down", "Hook's out") suit the throw; "Gotcha" suits the first ticks of the catch.
