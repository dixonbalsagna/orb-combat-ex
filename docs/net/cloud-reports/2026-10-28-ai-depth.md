# Cloud report: rollback under fighter AIs to the KO, with depth and gen 2 rings (Netcode and Online, 2026-10-28)

Brief: `docs/ep/cloud-briefs/netcode-ai-depth.md`. Branch: `cloud/netcode-online/ai-depth`. Design: `docs/net/rollback.md` section 12 (new), plus sections 6 and 10 updated.

## SUMMARY
The rollback check can now play the fighter AI on every slot to a KO, with `--ai`. Each peer runs the sim's own AI for its slot from its own predicted state. 36 sessions to a KO or a 60,000-tick cap all passed, on lan, p120 and far, with the default, depth and gen 2 ring setups: 3.40 M confirmed-tick digest comparisons, 343 k SimHash comparisons and 432 k rollbacks, with no mismatch. 29 of the 36 reached a KO.

As the sim calls it, the AI is not pure: `DirAI.aiInput` writes `S.rng`, `f.ai`, `f.act.dirI[AI_HOLD]`, `f.act.v2`, `f.input` and an fx. So the driver runs it on a snapshot copy, and the AI's memory belongs to the driver. On every lan run, the AI run straight gave the same inputs tick for tick as the AI on the peers. So it reads nothing the snapshot does not restore. The sim's own in-tick AI (`--ai=sim`) also holds under rollbacks of up to 17 ticks.

## DECISIONS
1. **The AI reads the peer's predicted present, as the brief asks, on a copy.** Running `DirAI.aiInput` on the live state would desync: the `impure` control is caught at tick 30 or 31, the AI's first random draw. The AI's memory lives in the driver, not the sim. An input once sent is final, so the memory needs no rollback.
2. **The straight run replays the confirmed inputs.** Under latency, each peer's AI sees predictions, so its inputs depend on the network, and no independent run could invent them. Purity is tested instead wherever no prediction was wrong: there, the AI run on the straight state must give the peers' inputs exactly.
3. **AI mode hashes every 60th tick and compares the digest every tick.** A full SimHash on every tick would take 1 to 2 hours a session. The every-tick SimHash set (291 k comparisons) shows it agrees.
4. **`S.fineCols` and `S.planetRing["fine"]` are read-only shared.** Both are given the slice's array whole (`core/sim.gd`, `world/planet_world.gd`), and the calm turn builds a new one (`world/epoch.gd`). Nothing writes either in place, the same case as the ring axis vectors.
5. **`--ai=sim` was added.** It tests the other model, where the AI is part of the sim and is re-run by every rollback. That is the model for one-versus-many bodies and co-op AI.

## RISKS
- Mutable statics outside the snapshot, read during a tick: `DirBrawl._frOn` and the other `_fr*`, `_inTick`, `_enderStep`, `holding`, `DirExchange.planEntry`, `DirRally._noDown`. Their comments say per-tick scratch (the depth frame opens and closes within a tick), and 2.5 M re-simulated ticks found no divergence, but no one has audited that each is set before it is read. A static carried across a restore would desync only under rollback.
- The desync digest is a subset of SimHash. Between full hashes, a divergence outside it is caught once it reaches the digest's fields or the 60-tick hash.
- No calm turn happened inside an AI match: the `calm` setup has not been played with `--ai`.
