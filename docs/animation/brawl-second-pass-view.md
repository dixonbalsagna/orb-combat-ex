# The brawl's second pass: Animation's view (2026-10-08)

Owner: Animation. Answers Game Design's `docs/design/brawl-second-pass.md` (sections 1 to 8, the build order of section 10) and the EP's brief. Docs only: nothing here is built, and nothing is built before Legal's verdicts on the new moves. Sizes are for Animation's side: small is under a day of a director's work with a lab and tests, medium a few days, large a week or more. Counts of poses are of new poses for the two launch fighters together.

What I have to start from: 454 and 703 baked poses (the protagonist's and the rival's waves), **19 light and 22 heavy pieces for the Protagonist, 15 and 20 for the rival** (every one playable at any wind-up length, because a wind-up is a blend time, not a pose); the press styles (speed, tech, heavy, push) as data rows with the beat fields; the contact solve (the hand reaches the defender's body on the contact tick, measured on 32,000 live ticks); the carry blend between blows; the energy presses (bolt, volley, spray, swat), the charged shot (charge, full, release poses) and the beam layer; 17 and 15 entries (spiral, pivot, arc dive, lane step, cartwheel step, fade, rising, skid ...); the zip's body with its **held layer** (the carried, lifted, tackle and throw poses, a cosmetic rise, 5 poses); the stagger, shove and absorb sequences of step 3; the buried and get-up poses; the riposte and the stagger fitted to the sim's ticks; lean into acceleration and banking; the flight blend of a moving fighter under any layer.

## 1. C1, control: a brawl that drifts. Size: small to medium

- **Footwork and air-strafe under strikes:** mostly there. The base layer blends the flight poses (dash forward, dash back, hover, climb, dive) from the fighter's velocity, and the strike layers go over the arms and torso only, so a fighter who is moving shows a moving lower body under his blows. **One gap:** if the brawl moves the pair by position (as a rush or a knock-back does) and leaves `f.vx` and `f.vy` unchanged, the base layer sees a standing fighter. I already measure a fighter's velocity from his position change for exactly that (the flight lead); feeding the base layer from the measured velocity while he is in a brawl is a few lines. If Simulation sets `vx` and `vy` to the drift, nothing is needed. Either way I need the drift to be smooth tick to tick: a step of more than about 0.3 bh in one tick reads as a pop whatever the pose.
- **The lean:** exists (lean into acceleration, up to 0.35 of the pose's lean, and banking into a change of direction), and a drifting pair uses it. The eight-tick ramp of the centre's speed (Game Design's) is what it needs: it leans into a smooth acceleration and has nothing to lean into on a jump.
- **Strikes landing on contact while the pair moves:** the contact solve reaches the defender's body on the contact tick from his live position, so a pair moving together (same drift) changes nothing; a pair moving **apart** (one holds toward, one away: a tug of war) changes the gap during a wind-up by up to about 0.3 to 0.8 bh over a 2 to 6 tick wind-up, and the lunge is fixed when the blow is announced. I will measure a staged drifting pair at 0.4 and 0.8 of flight speed, and a tug of war, for the contact error and the gap; the fix if it shows is to re-read the gap on the contact tick (a lunge top-up), which is small.
- **What it needs from the others:** the drift as velocity (or smooth position steps), Camera's frame for a pair that moves (not mine). **New: nothing to author.** A lab scene (a drifting pair) and a test (contact error and gap with a moving pair, no foot slide on the ground).

## 3. C4, A: the shove, the clinch, the trip, the throw, the tackle. Size: large (the clinch chain), small (the shove), small to medium (the tackle)

- **The shove (tapped A):** both hands, a push of 3 bh, no damage. The rival's reaction is the existing shove (`s3.shoved`, 12 ticks). New: the shoving pose, a two-hand push (a short wind-up and a drive): **2 poses a fighter, 4 in all**. Small.
- **The tackle (held A):** a zip's tackle already exists (the coil, the run, the shoulder-first tackle pose, the carry with the rival in the carried pose, 5 poses, the lift and throw too). New: the **crater end** (both end in a crater, the rival buried): the buried and get-up poses exist (`ag.embed`, the prone lie); the tackler's landing in the crater as a crouched stop: **1 pose a fighter, 2 in all**. Small to medium.
- **The clinch, the trip and the throw (mashed A): paired motion.** This is the large one. How I would build it:
  1. **Two bodies, one lock.** Each fighter stays his own `AnimFighter`; the pair is a **paired sequence**: two sequences on one timeline (the holder's and the held's, the same ticks, authored as entry sequences with a `pair` id), plus per phase an **anchor** (the holder's hand or forearm to a socket of the held body: chest, shoulder, waist, ankle).
  2. **The grip is solved, not authored:** the holder's hands reach the held body's sockets by the contact IK I already have (it reaches a point on the other body); the held body's pose is his side of the paired sequence and **the held body's position is the sim's**: Simulation moves the held fighter relative to the holder each tick during the clinch (the phase's offset), so the bodies cannot part, and the render never moves a position.
  3. **Phases and poses:** reach (10 ticks), grip, **the trip** (the holder turns the rival over: the held body's turn in the air), **the throw** (the holder's release and drive; the held body goes to the ragdoll tumble the sim launches): holder 4 poses and held 3 a fighter, so **14 poses**, plus the shove's 4 and the tackle's 2: **about 20 in all for C4**.
- **What it needs from Simulation:** the **held state**: `f.heldBy` (the holder's slot), `f.holdT`, the phase (reach, clinch, trip, throw) and the held fighter's offset from the holder each tick, and a launch at the throw. A read like the zip's (`DirGrab.read(S, f)`: kind, phase, ticks into it and its length, the partner's slot, the side). **From Combat's generator:** it makes single-body pieces today. For paired motion it needs **a `pair` kind of part**: two roles (`holder`, `held`) with equal tick lengths, the anchors (`hand_r to chest`, `forearm_l to shoulder_r`), and per phase the poses from the existing vocabulary (a reach, a grip, a push-up, a turn-over): the generator composes poses I already have and states the anchors; **the grip itself is the body's IK**, so the generator never has to compute a coordinate that depends on the other fighter's body.
- **Legal:** the clinch, trip and throw are holds on a body: the held poses need the same screen as the zip's carried and lifted poses (no embrace, hands where the held rules say), before any pose is authored.

- **Ki flurry and the third part of the mix-up:** the `spray` press (mashed) and `bolt`, alternated with X and Y blows through the carry blend. **0 poses.**
- **Blast (held 12 ticks, point-blank):** the charged shot's charge, full and release poses on a 12-tick hold. **0 poses.**
- **Art (24) and signature (48), live and held by the player:** the signature tells and held ends exist (the held-pose lint covers them); a beam held for as long as B is held is the beam layer's firing pose held for a variable time, which it supports. **New: a read for the held B** (the level, the tier flash) and the body holding the pose to the sim's tick: the same read pattern. Small code. A melee art as a string the player drives is a string of strike beats: nothing new.
- **While he charges B, armoured from the first flash:** the armour flag of C2 again.
- Total: **0 to 2 new poses** (a held-B charge pose if the bolt's press reads thin); the work is the read and the tests.

## 6. What to cut first, and the order I would build in

To keep Orb's two fighters playable soonest, **cut the paired chain first: the clinch, the trip, the throw and the seize** (about 18 poses, an IK grip layer, a new generator kind and a held state in the sim: the one slice that needs four directors). Keep the shove and the tackle (they are nearly built). Then, in order of cost per benefit:
1. **C1 control** (small to medium): no poses; Orb's first complaint.
2. **C2, the data half:** the `quick` style row, the heavy flurry and the mix-up (0 poses), then the **charges** (2 poses, the read from Encounter).
3. **C5, B** (0 to 2 poses): almost all reuse.
4. **C6, the lift, the slip and the breaker** (about 6 poses, only the breaker new).
5. **C4, the shove and the tackle's crater** (about 6 poses).
6. **Last: the clinch chain and the seize** (about 18 poses) and the sim's held state.
Z2, the chase and the zip away, are already in the plan of the zip (the rush functions exist); the fire-on-the-move hybrid (H1) I would cut to last.

Total new poses if everything is built: about **40** (against 454 and 703 baked today), and none is needed to play C1, C2's flurries, C5 or the slip.

## 7. Orb's questionnaire: how much flash the press styles show today

**An ordinary exchange of lights: where it sits on the body side.** The speed style is what an exchange of lights plays: a twist of 0.16 rad, an overshoot of 0.22 on a 4-tick follow-through, no squash, no stretch, 2 after-images (the data's `ghosts`), no hold: on the body side I would call it **about 4 of 10**, and its two ghosts are exactly "at most two ghosts". What I cannot judge is VFX's part (the sparks, the lines, the shake), which is theirs and which Game Design asks to be hit sparks and two ghosts with no shake and no speed lines. Of the three styles on the body: speed about 4, **tech about 5 to 6** (a 10-tick held beat on a perfect press and three after-images: the pause is the flash), **heavy about 7** (squash, stretch, a smear frame, three ghosts).

**If the exchange of lights reads above 4 when Orb plays it, turn down in this order (all are data in `press_styles.json`, no code):** (1) the speed row's `overshoot` 0.22 to 0.12 and `twist` 0.16 to 0.10; (2) its `ghosts` 2 to 1; (3) the `carry_ticks` (the retract blend) 5 to 4 so strings look less liquid. **For a timed string's payoff to be subtle:** the tech grade table `perfect` hold 10 and 3 ghosts to hold 8 and 2 ghosts (good 6 and 2 to 5 and 1), and keep the B2 pinned pose and return as designed (it is the cue, not the payoff). I would not change anything before Orb has watched the live build with the flip on (Shift+F7 is the A/B): the numbers above are my reading of the data, and the real judge is his eye.
