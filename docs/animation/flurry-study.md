# The flurry look study (2026-10-06): X, Y and B moving, view only

Owner: Animation. The EP's brief: "Orb wants to see three flurries moving (X, Y, B) before the sim builds them", from Orb: "X Light Hold should give a high speed burst of low damage attacks that quickly slows down on a curve; I'm excited to see Y and B flurries, I want to see how this looks visually." X is light, Y medium (today's heavies), B a new tier of super-heavy blows. **Every number is provisional** (Game Design is rewriting `docs/design/brawl-second-pass.md`), **every new move is provisional and listed for Legal in section 6**, and nothing here is read by a live match.

Headless, `godot --headless --path . res://render/anim/tools/flurry_study.tscn -- --measure` plays every scenario at full speed and prints the contact error, the joint violations that reach the screen (0) and NaN (0).

## 3. What was built (data and a little code; all render-only)

- **`data/anim/press_styles.json`: three provisional rows.** `burst` (the speed row plus `gap_amp`: a blow is mixed toward the guard it came from by `1 - amp`, `amp` from 0.5 at a 4-tick gap to 1 at 11, so the burst of 4, 4, 5, 6, 8, 11, 15 reads as short quick jabs that grow as it slows); `medium` (a wind-up of 8, a 0.55 squash, a small stretch, a smear frame, 2 ghosts, the carry blend); `super` (a wind-up of 18 on a linear ease so the raise reads, the full squash, a 0.34 lean back and 5 units of step back, a stretch of 0.34 with a lead of 9 units and two smear frames, a hold of 8, 2 ghosts). **The live `speed` row is untouched** and no live match names a new style: an ordinary mash is exactly as before (the anim_check mash test and the live-match probe say so).
- **`render/anim/anim_fighter.gd`:** `_gap_amp` and its use before the style body (one mix toward the base pose), and a beat field `windup` (ticks: the whole window is the wind-up, which is how a held blow coils for as long as it was held; the sim can send it the same way, as B0's `charge` is sent).
- **Two parked waves, `protag10` (prefix `pu`) and `rival7` (`ru`), two pieces each (12 poses),** generated by `wave_gen` like every wave. They are parked: neither fighter's `more` list names them, the study names them in its own scene only. `tip_path.mjs` gained their four rows.
- **`flurry_study.gd/.tscn`** (the scene), **`anim_check` `_test_flurry_tiers`** (below), and `docs/animation/handoff/flurry-study-schema.patch` (the schema delta for Tools: `styles.burst`, `styles.medium`, `styles.super` and `gap_amp`; **`validate.js` fails on the three style rows and `gap_amp` until it is applied**, and passes with it).

## 4. Do the three read apart, and does the burst slow

- **In silhouette, at fight distance** (`silhouettes.png`, the attacker dark): the X light is an upright body with an arm thrust; the Y medium is a bigger limb or a kick from a lunge; the B super-heavy is the **whole body thrown low and long**, a horizontal dive with the arms ahead, which is a different shape at a glance and not a bigger version of the Y. The honest caveat: a kick from the Y set (the roundhouse, the spinning heel) is also large; the super is distinguished by the body's line, not by the limb. The wind-up helps: at about 26 ticks before a B blow the fighter is already leaning back with an arm rising overhead (the clip `bmash`), and a Y or an X blow shows nothing like it.
- **The burst slows, and does not stutter:** measured (`anim_check`): at gaps 4, 4, 5 the arms stay about a quarter closer to their guard than the same blows in a plain mash (2.72 against 3.60 rad summed over the four arm bones for the Protagonist, 2.56 against 3.07 for the rival), a burst at gaps of 11 and more is identical to a plain mash, and an ordinary mash is not touched. The clip shows short quick jabs at chest height that lengthen as the rate falls. Pieces are chosen to Combat's grammar (a line, a rise, an arc, no raise and no turn; never a hook or backfist at the head, which sweeps over the dummy's head and reads as a flail).
- **The holds coil for the whole hold** (40 and 60 ticks): the checked wind-up runs 38 and 56 ticks or more; the squash deepens, the body leans back, the arm rises.

## 7. The size of Combat's heavy tier, and of a full super-heavy set per fighter

**Combat's tier (docs/combat/pending/movegen/three-strengths.md): ten heavies a fighter, eight to ship, so 16 to 20 key sets of 3 poses (chamber, contact, follow) = 48 to 60 new poses.** The four in this study are the pinned first four (`su_ram`, `su_heel`; `su_turn`, `su_drive`) and are 12 of the 48. The drives need little more than these four taught: a heave and a fall are drawn here, a turn is drawn, a step-through is a long lunge like the ram, an unwind and a full turn are variations of the turn; each new key set after the first of a drive is a few hours (sketch, `pose_lint`, the wind-up check, a clip), so **16 key sets are about 6 to 8 working days and 20 about 8 to 10**, with Legal's screen of the drawn frames in parallel (the long part), and `wave_gen` and the tests already in place. Every tier piece also has a **28-tick wind-up at shoulder height or lower** by h05 (the sink and the rise of the heave and fall are the wind-up), so the held-pose lint takes them as class `charge` when it learns h05 (built with the tier, not the study).

(Earlier estimate, kept: an open-ended super set of 8 pieces a fighter with 2 hold poses was 26 poses a fighter; Legal's rules make the hold poses unnecessary: the coil is the chamber at shoulder height and the squash deepening.)

## 7a. The size of a full super-heavy set per fighter (earlier estimate)

A B mash is a blow every 28 ticks, so a 3-second mash is six blows and must not repeat a piece. **A full set is 8 pieces a fighter: 8 keysets of 3 poses (chamber, contact, follow) = 24 poses, plus 2 hold poses for the B hold (a deeper coil and a full-charge tremble) = 26 poses a fighter, 52 for the two.** The two pieces each in the study were the work of a morning each (sketch, lint, review); eight is about a week with Legal's screen in the loop, and the screen is the long part. **The cheap first tier is already shown:** `bmash2`, today's 20 and 22 heavy pieces at the `super` style, **0 new poses**, a legitimate way to play B while the 52 are authored. What I would build next, if Orb likes the look: the super style on the existing heavies first (data only), then the four provisional pieces made final, then four more a fighter.

## 8. What the study does not show

The VFX looks (hit sparks, ghosts, smear lines, the full flash of a charge): the body exposes `ghosts` and `smear` on each style, but the pictures are the body alone. A real defender's flinch is the dummy's. The camera is fixed. The hold clips show the coil but not the glint at the full charge (VFX's). The Y and B holds are staged with the beat field `windup`; the sim's real charge read (Encounter's, planned) will feed the same field.
