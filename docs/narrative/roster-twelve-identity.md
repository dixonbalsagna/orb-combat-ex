# A roster of twelve: the identity half

Owner: Narrative and Fighter Identity. Version 1, 2026-10-05. The identity half of the roster document; Game Design's mechanical half is `docs/design/roster-twelve.md`, and the EP merges the two for Orb. **Everything here is an option for Orb.** The nine templates are *generic types*, not characters: Orb is writing an original universe and will fill them with their own names, histories and abilities. Names, titles and visuals here are placeholders or plain descriptions. No franchise names or recognisable characters are used, **masks stay parked for one future character** (section 4 says which template is the natural home), **the artefacts are never orbs**, and player-facing text treats the player as the fighter.

Orb's request, in short: classify the roles and subroles the first four set up, give generic templates for the niches not yet touched, give notes and proposals for fighters to fill the other eight, and aim for the feeling of **a game based on an imaginary anime's fourth season**: an implied history, backstories and earlier adventures. Orb hands back the taunt and quote lists on the evening of 2026-10-06, so everything below is built to take them.

Read with: `identity.md` and `voices/*.md` (the first four), `matchups.md` (their relationships), `fighter-sketches.md` (the axes), `world-abilities.md` (what each reshapes), `docs/design/future-stealth-fighter.md` (the parked hiding kit), and `dynamic-intros.md`, `taunt-system.md` (the slots this document extends).

## 1. The first four, classified

### 1.1 The map

Four axes already sit under the first four (and `fighter-sketches.md` proposed two more for the next ones). They are the coordinates the templates in section 4 use.

| Axis | The Protagonist | The Anti-hero | The Empress | The Cyborg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Story role** | The hero, an idealist with a flaw | The rival, a proud foil | The comic tyrant of a court | The recurring monster, parody horror |
| **Personality function** (what they do for the ensemble) | **The Heart.** Makes the audience care. | **The Foil.** Shows the hero's choices by refusing them. | **The Spectacle.** Makes the threat entertaining. | **The Appetite.** Makes the stakes literal and the scale absurd. |
| **Relationship to the world** (collateral) | **Tends** it, and breaks it by being himself | **Ignores** it: wrecks things to make a point | **Annexes** it: a planet is a filing | **Feeds** on it |
| **Relationship to an audience** | Wants none; a little embarrassed by one | **Needs a witness** (the rank, the stare) | **Plays to one**, her guard | Treats everyone as a customer |
| **Relationship to their power** | Afraid of what he will break | Proud of it; the pride is the price | Dreads the cost of changing form | Hungry for it |
| **Voice device** | Future tense, promises, "I'll fix it" | The past tense about his opponent, ranks; the present when he cracks | The royal "we" slipping to "I"; "petitioner"; revision numbers | A customer-service script over appetite; a stutter on the chip |
| **Comic register** | Gentle self-deprecation; awkward, sincere warmth | Deadpan contempt; pomp punctured when the facade cracks | Farce: bureaucracy as hubris, a rage that is also paperwork | Horror by politeness: the cheerful, atrocious script |
| **How they threaten** | Collateral from earnestness; a huge hand-to-hand repertoire | Dominance and delay; barrages; a finish by hand | Cutting beams from range behind a guard; a chain of revisions | Consumption: he grows by eating, then molts |
| **What they want** | A real fight, and for nobody to be hurt by it | To be first, and seen to be | The empire tidy, and her image kept | To be served: the supper, the five stars |

**Subroles.** Each of the first four is a *type plus a subtype*, which is the useful way to place the others:

- **Protagonist:** the idealist hero, subtype *the guardian brawler* (heroism expressed as a fighter who loves the fight).
- **The Anti-hero:** the proud rival, subtype *the dark mirror* (the hero's path, taken by someone who put ego first).
- **The Empress:** the comic villain, subtype *the ringmaster* (a leader who fights through followers).
- **The Cyborg:** the monster villain, subtype *the parody horror* (frightening in concept, played for laughs, and still a threat).

### 1.2 How the first four relate to each other

From `matchups.md`, the ten pairings reduce to six distinct relationships (the mirrors are comic variants):

| Pair | Stakes | The relationship in one line |
|---|---|---|
| Protagonist and Anti-hero | rivalry | Old rivals; the hero warm, the rival contemptuous, a respect he will not admit. |
| Protagonist and Empress | world at stake | A hero against a would-be world-ender who treats the planet as a filing; no banter on his side. |
| Protagonist and Cyborg | appetite | A hero against a machine that eats the people he protects; angry and plain against polite and cheerful. |
| Anti-hero and Empress | rivalry | A proud rival against a loud clown he despises; he ranks her below the goons. |
| Anti-hero and Cyborg | rivalry and appetite | A proud rival who hates being on the menu. |
| Empress and Cyborg | world at stake and appetite | Two bureaucracies, each sure the other belongs to its department. |

**What it shows:** the first four give us **one warm pair, one cold pair and four comic ones**, and only one pair (hero and Empress) is truly grim. A roster of twelve needs more *warm* relationships (a teacher, a friend, a younger fighter), more *grim* ones, and some that are *complicated* (betrayal, a debt, a former equal).

## 2. The story roles a cast of twelve still lacks

A cast needs the roles an ensemble story uses. The first four give us a hero, a rival, a flashy villain and a monster. Here is what is missing, with the template (section 4) that would fill it. A template may fill more than one.

| Role | What it adds to the cast | Filled by |
|---|---|---|
| **A mentor** | A warm, authoritative figure; the one who taught somebody; gives a "who learned from whom" web. | T1 The Veteran |
| **A newcomer** (the audience's stand-in) | Somebody outclassed who learns in front of us; a way in for a new player and a target for everyone. | T2 The Prodigy |
| **A turncoat** | A changed allegiance, and a fighter whose loyalty is part of each match. | T3 The Turncoat |
| **An outsider to the conflict** (and a guardian) | Someone with no stake in the rivalry; the fight is a trespass on their world. Gives scale and a neutral register. | T4 The Colossus (T9 The Caretaker as a spare) |
| **A former villain** | The old big threat, now diminished, complicated or reformed. Carries the fourth season's history on his back. | T5 The Former Villain |
| **A legacy character** | An heir to a famous fighter's name, gear or style; the burden of the mantle. | T6 The Legacy |
| **Comic relief with teeth** | Funny *and* dangerous; the joke is the way they fight. | T7 The Showman |
| **The arc's real threat** | A grim, sincere, frightening fighter, so the comic villains are not the highest stakes. | T8 The Harbinger |

## 3. What "an imaginary anime's fourth season" implies

### 3.1 What it means for the writing

A fourth season is a world with a past: the audience arrives late and is trusted to catch up. The feeling to build is **trust in a history we never show**. That asks five things of the roster and none of them is writing lore.

1. **Characters refer to events we never see.** A name, a place, a night, a bridge. The reference is never explained.
2. **Old grudges and old debts.** A pair does not meet for the first time; they meet *again*.
3. **Changed allegiances.** Someone is now on a side they used to oppose.
4. **A fighter who used to be the big threat.** The old threat is now one among many; the cast treats them with a history.
5. **Power levels that were once shocking.** The form that stunned the world in season one is, by now, an opening move. Veterans scoff at spectacle that newcomers find astonishing.

Add the show's own habits: **running jokes** that have been running for seasons, **recaps**, and a sense that **what we see is one episode in a long run**.

### 3.2 How a roster carries it without writing lore

A rule first, in three layers: **name the event, show its effect, never explain it.** A character can say "after the lighthouse" and the audience understands that the lighthouse mattered, from the way the other fighter reacts, and nobody says what happened there. The explanation is the part Orb's universe keeps.

**The tool: a canon ledger.** A short list Orb owns of the events the cast may mention (placeholders are in 3.4). Each entry is a name, a season, who was there, what changed, and who remembers it how (which can disagree: that is a source of jokes). The dialogue system only ever refers to an event from the ledger (the "callback never lies" rule from `dialogue-director.md` section 2.2, applied to history that happened off-screen).

### 3.3 The devices that carry it

A short list of things the game can do, each a small piece of work that hangs on the systems we already have.

| Device | What it is | Hangs on |
|---|---|---|
| **Reference taunts and barks** | A slot for a line that mentions a ledger event without explaining it ("This is not the lighthouse."). Two fighters who share an event can answer each other. | A new taunt slot `reference` (`taunt-system.md`); the facet `shared_event` from the ledger. |
| **Old-grudge lines** | A pair-specific taunt or answer that treats a past defeat or betrayal as known. | The matchup register `grudge` (section 5.4) and the taunt answers. |
| **Power-level lines** | A veteran mocks a form that would once have shocked, or a newcomer is awed. A fighter has a **canon peak** (the form they reached in earlier seasons) and a line fires when the opponent's current tier is below it. | A facet `peak_gap` in the taunt context; per-fighter `canon_peak`. |
| **Intro scenarios** | New templates for `dynamic-intros.md`: *The Reunion* (two who have not met since a ledger event), *The Old Guard Arrives* (the veteran lands slowly and the room changes), *The Newcomer's Entrance* (late and nervous), *The Recap* (the showman's address to a crowd). | The slots and the facts of the intro system; the rivalry record. |
| **A "previously" card** | Before a rematch, one short caption built from the rivalry record: who won last, how it ended. An anime habit; optional and skippable. | `user://rivalry.json` (`dynamic-intros.md` section 6). |
| **Win poses** | Each fighter has a current win pose, and an **earlier-season** one that returns when they reclaim a loss ("the old pose, done wistfully"). | Animation; the history record. |
| **Alt costumes as earlier seasons** | Cosmetic looks labelled "Season One", "Season Two" that show a fighter at an older power level, unlocked by play. A line can react to it ("You are wearing that again?"). | The unlocks (`docs/design/unlocks.md`); a taunt facet `costume_season`. |
| **Running jokes** | Bits with levels (`dialogue-director.md` section 2.4) that carry across matches in the local record, so a long-time player has *heard the joke before*. | The bits system; the seen memory. |
| **An episode guide** | A menu page listing earlier arcs by **title only**, with no summary: the implied history is the point. Orb fills the titles. | UI, a data list of titles. |
| **A "next time" sting** | After a match, one line of teaser in the style of a next-episode preview. A staple, not a signature. | UI and the narrator slot. |

## 4. Nine identity templates for the open slots

Each is a *generic type* for Orb to fill. Eight fill the open slots; the ninth is a spare (section 5). Each is distinct from the first four and from the others in role, voice device, silhouette, comic register and niche. Pronouns are open (they/them below). The **unseen-history hook** is a placeholder reference that ties the type to the canon ledger.

**On niches.** The mechanical niches are Game Design's (`roster-twelve.md`); the pairing here is where the fit is natural. Three cautions from the project's own notes: the **speedster** must not be the fighter that "gets too fast at the top form" (Orb's note), so its identity is *acceleration at the base tier*; the **stealth** kit needs split-screen or a similar solution (`docs/design/future-stealth-fighter.md`); and every fighter already reshapes the world once a match (`world-abilities.md`), so a **terrain shaper** is the least distinct niche, which is why T9 is the spare.

### T1. The Veteran (`veteran`)

- **Story role:** the mentor. A retired champion called back, or one who never quite left.
- **Temperament:** gruff, patient, dry; fond of people in the way a teacher is, which looks like criticism.
- **Voice device: the correction.** Speaks in terse corrections and instructions to the opponent, as if coaching ("Chin down. Wider. Again."). Imperatives, short, with a sigh. No boasts, no promises.
- **Relationships (hooks):** *Protagonist:* taught him once, and is proud and hard on him. *Anti-hero:* taught him too, and he never forgave the lesson (an echo of backstory option 9A). *Empress:* has seen her revisions come and go; unimpressed. *Cyborg:* a grim, practical disgust, and a fondness for a good sandwich.
- **Visual hook (silhouette):** broad, low, with a stoop; a heavy coat or sash; stands like a doorway. A square block with a stoop.
- **Funny:** weary exasperation, bad knees, a lesson delivered mid-throw. **A threat:** immovable, and once he has hold of you he does not let go.
- **Unseen-history hook:** "That is how you lost the second winter." (a ledger event)
- **Niche:** **grappler** (throws, holds, command grabs). Natural: the correction is a throw's rhythm.

### T2. The Prodigy (`prodigy`)

- **Story role:** the newcomer and the audience's stand-in. Outclassed, quick to learn, and everyone's target.
- **Temperament:** eager, cocky, anxious by turns; a bright young fighter who knows the rules and has never been hit this hard.
- **Voice device: the running commentary.** Narrates their own moves live, in the present continuous, and is surprised by the results ("Going left, going left, and, ha, that worked!"). Gets the commentary wrong when it matters.
- **Relationships:** *Protagonist:* an idol, and the thing that he is scared to disappoint. *Anti-hero:* treats the Prodigy as a nuisance, and that stings. *Empress:* wants to recruit them ("a very promising petitioner"). *Cyborg:* sees a snack.
- **Visual hook:** lanky, in oversized gear or loose sleeves that whip; a trailing scarf or cord. A kite: a small body with a long trailing line.
- **Funny:** confident mistakes; the commentary falling behind the fight. **A threat:** learns your pattern within a minute, and the second round is much harder than the first.
- **Unseen-history hook:** "I was in the crowd at the lighthouse." (and no one remembers the Prodigy there)
- **Niche:** **speedster**, with an *adaptive* spin (acceleration, and a counter to what you repeat). Mind the top-form speed caution above.

### T3. The Turncoat (`turncoat`)

- **Story role:** the turncoat. Changed sides once, perhaps twice; not sure they are done.
- **Temperament:** charming, evasive, guilty underneath; someone who always has an exit.
- **Voice device: the disclaimer.** Every line comes with a retraction or an alibi ("I didn't say that. Nobody heard that. I was elsewhere.").
- **Relationships:** *Protagonist:* forgives them too quickly, and they cannot stand it. *Anti-hero:* owes him something, and pays it in barbs. *Empress:* defected from her court (the bridge). *Cyborg:* the one who talks him out of a meal, and then back into it.
- **Visual hook:** lopsided: one sleeve long and one cut short; a coat worn so that two colours show. An asymmetric flap. **Not a mask**: masks are parked for one future character, and the Turncoat is the *natural home for it* if Orb wants the seed used, since a mask suits the type. If not, a hood.
- **Funny:** a terrible liar who is an excellent fighter; caught switching sides mid-line. **A threat:** the ambush; they are never where you looked.
- **Unseen-history hook:** "After the bridge, we agreed not to discuss the bridge."
- **Niche:** **stealth** (the parked hiding kit). Natural: concealment fits the disclaimer. Hidden information needs a screen solution.

### T4. The Colossus (`colossus`)

- **Story role:** an outsider to the conflict, and a guardian. A local force who wants to be left alone; the fight is a trespass.
- **Temperament:** gentle, slow, literal; terrifying only by accident.
- **Voice device: the landmark.** Measures time and distance in landmarks and in ages, in short, slow, literal sentences ("That hill is younger than I am."). Never rhetorical. Never in a hurry.
- **Relationships:** *Protagonist:* lives near a place he is trying to protect; an unlikely ally. *Anti-hero:* cannot be intimidated, which he finds insulting. *Empress:* wants him as a monument. *Cyborg:* sees a banquet and is told, kindly, no.
- **Visual hook:** several times the others' size; the silhouette of a hill with arms; moss, a footbridge across one forearm, a tiny head. The camera has to hold the whole of them.
- **Funny:** oblivious, apologises to the buildings he has stepped on. **A threat:** sheer scale, and terrain: where he stands, the ground answers.
- **Unseen-history hook:** "The last ones who woke me are now a lake."
- **Niche:** **giant** (a large hitbox, slow, a screen-filling presence; wrapped-planet camera and hit rules). Natural.

### T5. The Former Villain (`former_villain`)

- **Story role:** the former big threat: the season-one antagonist, now diminished, complicated or tentatively reformed.
- **Temperament:** weary, dignified, resentful when remembered, privately glad to be fighting again.
- **Voice device: the résumé.** Threatens by listing what they used to be able to do, in the past tense *about themselves* ("I once unmade a moon. Twice. Before lunch."). A bitter nostalgia: the threat is real and it is out of date. (This is *not* the Anti-hero's device, which is the past tense about the opponent. It may also reuse the prototype villain's elegiac tone, if Orb wants it to live on.)
- **Relationships:** *Protagonist:* he lost to the Protagonist once, and it is a mutual wound with a respect in it. *Anti-hero:* hates being compared to them. *Empress:* pretends not to remember them, loudly. *Cyborg:* the Cyborg ate their lieutenants.
- **Visual hook:** grand regalia worn thin: an oversized collar, a cape pinned at the shoulders, patched at the elbows. A throne without a throne: tall, an angular collar over a stoop. (Distinct from the Empress's train and the Anti-hero's polish.)
- **Funny:** bitter nostalgia, trying to menace and finding it out of date, careful with their back. **A threat:** the old tricks still work, and they have experience: heavy, telegraphed beams that land if you are careless.
- **Unseen-history hook:** "This was my coast. Ask anyone. Ask the lighthouse."
- **Niche:** **zoner** (beams, area denial). Natural: the old signature is a wide beam.

### T6. The Legacy (`legacy`)

- **Story role:** the legacy character: the heir to a famous fighter's name, gear or style, and its burden.
- **Temperament:** earnest, anxious and stubborn; trying to be worthy while refusing to copy. (Distinct from the Protagonist's warm ease: the Legacy is *tight*.)
- **Voice device: the quotation.** Quotes the predecessor ("As the old one used to say...") and then amends it, doubts it or breaks from it. Attribution and revision in every line.
- **Relationships:** *Protagonist:* the predecessor was the Protagonist's friend, or the Protagonist's equal, and he does not know how to tell them. *Anti-hero:* sees the predecessor in them and hates it. *Empress:* collects the Legacy's gear. *Cyborg:* has already tasted the predecessor's rig (it was not a good meal).
- **Visual hook:** a small figure in a large shape: a coat or armour too big, sleeves rolled, a belt notched to fit; the predecessor's rig fights alongside them. A small figure inside a bigger outline.
- **Funny:** the gap between the quotation and the result; a hero in someone else's boots. **A threat:** the inherited constructs fight alongside, and each one remembers a different fight.
- **Unseen-history hook:** "The old one was at the second winter. I was in the next room."
- **Niche:** **puppet or summon** (constructs that fight alongside, as AI-only helpers, like the Empress's guard). Natural: the inherited rig *is* the summon. (Legal's rule on summons concerns the artefacts: no wish, no summon from one. A fighter's own constructs are not covered.)

### T7. The Showman (`showman`)

- **Story role:** comic relief with teeth. Funny, and genuinely dangerous; the joke is the way they fight.
- **Temperament:** manic, delighted, always performing; a fighter who wants the crowd as much as the win.
- **Voice device: the recap.** Addresses an in-world crowd with a recap of "previously" and a cliffhanger sting ("Previously, on things I did to you..."). Gets the recap wrong in a way that is funnier than the truth. (The in-world crowd is the audience: the voice never addresses the player.)
- **Relationships:** *Protagonist:* a fan of his career, and a critic of his form. *Anti-hero:* the one person he cannot unsettle, and tries anyway. *Empress:* hired for a gala once and fired for stealing the show. *Cyborg:* the best audience he has had; the Cyborg always claps.
- **Visual hook:** top-heavy: a tall collar like a microphone stand, a wide sleeve for flourishes, patch panels in bright colours, a ribbon that trails. (Not a mask.)
- **Funny:** unreliable recaps; stopping mid-fight to take a bow. **A threat:** their moves change from round to round; the opponent never settles.
- **Unseen-history hook:** "In our last episode, you were *wonderful*. Do you remember the roof?"
- **Niche:** **wildcard** (a moveset that changes between rounds or exchanges, within bounds Game Design sets).

### T8. The Harbinger (`harbinger`)

- **Story role:** the arc's real threat. A grim, sincere fighter, so the comic villains are not the highest stakes of the season.
- **Temperament:** calm, quiet, unhurried, with no pleasure in it. The only fighter who does not seem to be performing.
- **Voice device: the forecast.** Short, flat predictions of what will happen to the opponent, stated as facts ("You lose the left side first."). Never a boast, never a threat, never a joke. It is funny only when it is wrong, and then it is *very* funny.
- **Relationships:** *Protagonist:* the first fighter he has not been able to like, or to reach. *Anti-hero:* the one who makes his pride go quiet. *Empress:* her guard does not salute. *Cyborg:* has nothing to say to him; even the service script stops.
- **Visual hook:** tall, narrow and still: a long coat that does not move in the wind, the one cloth on screen that is still. A vertical line. No regalia, no ornament.
- **Funny:** nothing, until the forecast is wrong, and then the others cannot stop. **A threat:** inevitability; each exchange seems to have been predicted.
- **Unseen-history hook:** "The second winter was a rehearsal."
- **Niche:** **juggernaut** (absorbs damage and escalates; a charger style), an extra niche to offer Game Design. Natural: the forecast is a clock.

### T9. The Caretaker (`caretaker`), the spare

- **Story role:** an outsider to the conflict: a local keeper of the land, who sees the fight as a trespass. (A spare for T4's slot or T3's.)
- **Temperament:** serene, patient, absurdly calm; a gardener at the end of the world.
- **Voice device: the aside to the land.** Addresses the ground, the plants and the weather, not the opponent ("Sorry, hill. This will hurt.").
- **Relationships:** *Protagonist:* helps him mend; they share an instinct. *Anti-hero:* regards him as an unkempt hedge. *Empress:* fines him for the garden. *Cyborg:* is a pest, and a harvest is a harvest.
- **Visual hook:** a wide-brimmed rain hat and a long staff, moss on the shoulders, bare feet. A cone over a vertical line.
- **Funny:** the apologies to the ground. **A threat:** the land itself: ridges, roots, ground that holds.
- **Unseen-history hook:** "I planted this before your first war."
- **Niche:** **terrain shaper.** The least distinct niche (everyone reshapes the world once a match), hence the spare.

### 5.3 Who talks to whom

Twelve fighters have 66 pairings, so the ten authored pairs in `matchups.md` cannot grow to 66. Two things keep it tractable.

**1. Clusters.** Fighters belong to a small set of circles, and a pair inside one circle is warm and a pair across circles is not.

| Circle | Who | The register inside it |
|---|---|---|
| **The Circle** (the heroes) | Protagonist, Veteran, Prodigy, Legacy | Warm: teasing, teaching, awe, family. |
| **The Court** (the threats) | Empress, Cyborg, Harbinger | Competing, condescending, or silent. |
| **The Old Guard** (those with a past) | Anti-hero, Former Villain, Veteran | Grudge, comparison, a respect that is hard to say. |
| **The Drifters** (no side) | Turncoat, Colossus, Showman | Amused, wary, or oblivious. |

**2.
