# Backstory skeleton: the questions the power system needs answered

Owner: Narrative and Fighter Identity. Version 1, 2026-10-04. **Everything here is an option for Orb. Nothing is decided.** Orb: "I'm thinking about a document defining the backstory so we can flesh out the power system a little more thoroughly." This is a skeleton for you to fill, not a finished story: ten questions the power system needs answered, two or three contrasting directions for each in a sentence or two, and what each choice would change in the mechanics we already have. Names are placeholders. Every direction is original; none uses a franchise's lore.

## How to use it

1. Read the **guardrails** (the lines no direction may cross).
2. For each question, read **what we have today** (so you see what a choice would touch), pick **A, B or C**, or write your own (the "Other" is always open).
3. The **bundles** at the end show how the choices hang together, in case you would rather pick a whole direction and adjust.
4. When you have picked, I (Narrative) write the one-page backstory from your choices, and each director gets a list of what changes. Pick the first three (the power questions) before the rest: they decide what the others can be.

**Suggested order.** The power: questions 1 to 3. The world: 4 and 7. The people: 5, 6 and 9. The style: 8. The wider cast: 10.

## Guardrails (Legal and Orb's rules, so no option crosses them)

- **The artefacts are never orbs.** No sphere, no pearl, no ball, in the fiction or the art. Shards, plates, stitches, tools and keepsakes are fine. (Orb wanted orbs; Legal found no safe way. Orb's decision stands: the collectibles are not orbs.)
- **No fixed count, no wish, no summon, no search.** Never seven; no complete-the-set; no wish granted; nothing summoned; no radar or sensor that finds them (`docs/legal/q3-screen.md`).
- **No column of light, no darkening sky, and only the fighters speak or appear** at the artefact moments.
- **Staples yes, signatures no** (Orb): the genre's beats, not any series' specific devices, names, catchphrases, transformations or hair.
- **Tone:** mature destruction, a humorous voice, a sincere homage, all at once.
- **Player-facing text treats the player as the fighter.** A backstory is told in the game as the fighters' own words, never as a narrator addressing "the player".

## 1. Where does the energy come from?

**What we have today.** Ki runs 0 to 100: it starts at 60, regenerates at 5 a second, rises by 30 a second while charging in the open (with an interrupt risk), and gains 4% of the damage dealt. Power is separate (0 to 100, never falls). A signature costs 45 ki. Nothing ties ki to the world; the planet is the stage. The fold's fragments are "the planet's bare bone" (a hint, not a rule).

| Option | The idea | What it would change |
|---|---|---|
| **A. The planet** | The energy is the world's own, drawn up through the ground. Fighters are conduits, and a world can be drained. | **Ki:** regeneration and charging depend on ground contact and biome (bare ground strongest, long flight slowly draining, a scarred field spent). **Signatures:** their biome variants (pillar 7) get an in-world reason. **Tiers and anguish:** wrecking the land is a cost to *yourself*, which gives menace and anguish a mechanical edge. **Alchemy:** none. **Planet destruction** ends everyone's supply. |
| **B. The fighter** | The energy is life-force: built by discipline and feeling, and owned by the one who holds it. | **Ki:** unchanged. **Ego meters (Respect, Pride, Wrath, Hunger)** become literal fuel and the forms' triggers keep their personal meters. The planet stays a stage. **Anguish and menace:** moral, not mechanical. |
| **C. The between** | A tide runs through every world and swells where there is conflict; fights call it up. | **Ki:** charging strength rises with the fight's intensity (the mood: calm, tense, frenzied), and tier-ups draw the weather. **Tiers:** "power never falls" gets a reason. **Space combat** (zero-g if the planet goes) fits. The planet matters less. |

## 2. What is a tier, and what is a transformation?

**What we have today.** Tiers 1 to 4 at power 25, 50 and 75. Each adds damage (+9%), speed (+10%) and launch force (+16%), and the world reacts from tier 3 (clouds part, rubble floats). A form maps to a tier. Forms are permanent for the match except drain states. The Protagonist's tracks advance with Respect; the rival's forms cost him guard; the Empress has numbered revisions; the Cyborg molts with Hunger.

| Option | The idea | What it would change |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Locked doors** | The body holds power back by habit or vow. Each tier opens a door, and a transformation is a release. | **Forms** can close again (the drain states and "re-lock") and a fighter can hold a form back by choice. **The Protagonist's restraint** becomes explicit: he fights holding back, and anguish can slam a door. **Tiers** read as release, not growth. |
| **B. Bargains** | Each form costs something for the match: a guard, a memory, a vow. | **Every fighter** gets a per-form cost, generalising what the rival (guard), the Empress (paperwork) and the Cyborg (Hunger) already have; the Protagonist's form cost becomes his anguish multiplier. **Tiers** read as price. |
| **C. The world answers** | Power is not inside; the planet responds to how loud you are. A tier is how much the world gives back. | **Tier effects** couple to the world (the world already reacts from tier 3 and that becomes the *cause*). **Forms** differ on the ground and in the air. **Alchemy:** loud, sustained play escalates; quiet play stays low. |

## 3. What does power cost?

**What we have today.** Anguish pressures the Protagonist as collateral rises; menace feeds the villain. Ego meters decay or are spent. Anguish is only a pressure: the reward for protecting is a composure bonus that collateral takes away, never a bonus for letting people die.

| Option | The idea | What it would change |
|---|---|---|
| **A. The people** | Every point of power spends something living. Collateral is the price. | **Anguish** scales with tier, and menace becomes literal feeding. **Interpose** (the protective block) gains weight. **Casualties** feed forms directly (more than today's small terms). |
| **B. The body** | Power burns the holder: heat, wear, wounds. | **Location-based damage, wear and heat** (already in) carry the cost; the moral meters become flavour. **Forms** end at a body limit. |
| **C. The time** | Power is borrowed against the end of the match: a finale, a clock. | **Acts and the time floor** matter more; each form shortens the clock, the match ends by the time cap with consequence. **Transformations** become a countdown. |

## 4. Why does the planet matter?

**What we have today.** A wrapped planet (9,600 units around) with procedural seeds, biomes and settlements; towers, villages and civilians; craters and scars that stay all match; an ending of knockout or the planet destroyed at top tiers; a folded proving-ground planetoid with no civilians.

| Option | The idea | What it would change |
|---|---|---|
| **A. It is home** | Real people, real towns, a living world. | **Collateral** weights and casualty counts matter; **evacuation** and bystander barks are central; **planet destroyed** is a defeat for the hero, a triumph for a villain. |
| **B. It is a proving ground** | An old arena used by generations: old scars, old ruins, a tradition. | **Procedural planets** carry old craters and ruins as set dressing; **a rivalry record** (the history hook) can show as visible old scars; less moral weight on collateral. |
| **C. It is the source** | If the energy comes from it (question 1A), the planet is a battery. | **Destroying it ends power for everyone**: a mutual deterrent that keeps top-tier destruction rare; the last-tier "planet's last breath" sequence has a reason. |

## 5. What does the Protagonist want?

**What we have today.** An earnest martial artist who powers up when a challenger gives everything; he fights to protect his loved ones, and his fixation on the fight itself causes massive collateral damage. He gathers fragments to move the fight somewhere no one is hurt, and his final form unlocks only there. His meter is Respect.

| Option | The idea | What it would change |
|---|---|---|
| **A. To protect, and be worthy of it** | Guilt drives him; the fixation on the fight is his flaw. | **Anguish** is heavier and shapes his lines ("I'll fix it"); the **fold** is a promise; **Respect** reads as relief. |
| **B. To find an equal** | He loves the contest and sees every strong challenger as a gift. Collateral is a blind spot. | **Respect-driven forms** lead; **taunts** are warm; **registers** stay playful; anguish is a lighter pressure and a surprise when it lands. |
| **C. To keep a promise** | A teacher's or a town's, of which this fight is part. | **A personal stake** across a season (rematch hooks); the **final form** is tied to the promise being kept; the **fold** is a keeper's duty. |

## 6. What does the rival want?

**What we have today.** A brooding rival whose sense of justice comes second to his ego and pride. He drags fights out to enforce his hierarchy, bombards with energy barrages until the foe submits, then finishes by hand. The player may accept a fusion item the Protagonist throws. His meter is Pride, which drains when he is humbled; he needs a witness.

| Option | The idea | What it would change |
|---|---|---|
| **A. To be first, and seen** | Rank is proof, and the proof needs the Protagonist watching. | **Pride** mechanics and the **witness behaviour**; the **rank gag** has a reason; accepting **fusion** is humiliation. |
| **B. To take back what was given** | Something the Protagonist received that he believes was his: a teacher's regard, an inheritance, a title. | **Fusion** is the ultimate surrender; **Pride drains** when he is helped; envy shows in **registers** and taunts. |
| **C. To end the old order** | He thinks the contest or its hierarchy is rotten and means to prove it by winning at the top. | **His collateral indifference** becomes an argument; **barrages** make a point; **fusion** can be a strategic choice and not only a defeat. |

## 7. What are the artefacts? (not orbs)

**What we have today.** Fragments: irregular pieces of mass 1 to 5, shed by planet damage and held only by the Protagonist; once the held mass reaches 12 (any mix, never a count) and the match has passed 4:00, he can fold the fight into a proving ground. The rival knocks them loose; anyone can grab one for a surge. Not orbs, never seven, no wish, no search.

| Option | The idea | What it would change |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Bone of the planet** (the working fiction) | Pieces of the world's bare stone. Held close, they let him draw lifeless ground over the fight. | **As built.** The shapes are shards; the fiction ties to question 1A and 4C. |
| **B. Stitches of the seam** | The wrapped planet's seam is held shut by plates; the pieces are the seam's own keys, and folding is closing the seam inward. | **Mechanics unchanged.** Shapes become flat plates and tools, not lumps. Fragments could cluster near the seam, a **place** to fight over. Links to the planet's wrapped geometry (pillar 1). |
| **C. Keepsakes** | Relics of earlier bouts: a worn glove, a broken blade, each with a memory. | **Mechanics unchanged.** Pieces differ by biome and by who left them. They could drive **unlocks** and the **history hook** (a piece from your own earlier fight). No power of their own. |

## 8. Where did the fighting styles come from? (the alchemy)

**What we have today.** The fight alchemist reads the last five presses of both fighters, compares each player's running mix of attack types, and composes a sequence the player would expect. Style labels (turtle, rusher, runner, charger, sniper, mixer) describe play. A signature adapts to biome, altitude and the defender's stance. A recipe display is a setting.

| Option | The idea | What it would change |
|---|---|---|
| **A. A school, or lineages** | Forms handed down: presses are the ingredients of a named discipline, and each fighter belongs to one. | **Recipes** get names (a school's forms); the **recipe display** reads as that school; **unlocks** are learning a form. |
| **B. Instinct** | Each fighter's style is their nature; the alchemist is the fight's own grammar. | **Names stay neutral**; the style label is personality. No lineage. |
| **C. The land teaches** | Styles answer the ground; the same press differs by biome. | **Pillar 7** (signatures adapt) deepens: recipes keyed to biome, with procedural planets producing new forms. |

## 9. What is the history between the Protagonist and the rival?

**What we have today.** "A bitter rivalry after a whole season of build-up." The rival respects the Protagonist and will not admit it. Their registers: the Protagonist warm, the rival contemptuous with a hidden envy. The intro can carry a rematch hook (`dynamic-intros.md`).

| Option | The idea | What it would change |
|---|---|---|
| **A. The same teacher** | They trained together; the rival believes he was passed over. | Pairs with 6B; **lines** and the **rematch hook** carry a shared past; **fusion** means more. |
| **B. Former allies** | They split over what protecting means: the rival would trade a town for a result, the Protagonist never would. | Pairs with 3A; **anguish and menace** are a quarrel, not only meters; **taunts** can name it. |
| **C. Strangers** | Each is the other's first true equal; the grudge forms over the season's bouts. | **The rivalry record** is the backstory: rematch counts and "who won last" are literal; light on lore, heavy on play. |

## 10. Where are the Empress and the Cyborg from?

**What we have today.** The Empress watches from behind her guard and treats a planet as a filing; the Cyborg eats the crowd and molts. The matchup matrix frames both as would-be world-enders with their own comic logic.

| Option | The idea | What it would change |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Off-world arrivals** | Both come from beyond; the planet is a prize. | They stay **outsiders**; arcade ladders end on them; no shared history with the Protagonist or the rival. |
| **B. Products of the same contest, gone wrong** | The Cyborg is what happens when someone feeds on the world instead of drawing from it (with 1A); the Empress is a former champion who annexed. | **Setting unifies**; their powers are **perversions** of the main one; **final forms** gain a reason. |
| **C. Called by the noise** | They are drawn by the rivalry's wake (with 1C). | **Arcade ladders** escalate: the noise brings them; no deeper tie. |

## Bundles (three coherent sets)

If you would rather pick a whole direction, here are three that hang together. Mix at will.

| Bundle | Picks | The headline |
|---|---|---|
| **The Living World** | 1A, 2C, 3A, 4A (or 4C), 7A or 7B, 8C | The planet is alive and the fight draws on it; wrecking it costs the fighters themselves. Mechanically the widest: ki, tiers and signatures all couple to the world. |
| **The Proving Ground** | 1B, 2A, 3B, 4B, 7C, 8A | An old tradition of single combat on a world kept for it. Mechanically the lightest: the planet is a stage with a history; the cost lives in the fighters' bodies. |
| **The Tide** | 1C, 2B, 3C, 4A, 7B, 8B | A current runs through every world and conflicts raise it; every form is a bargain against the clock. Mechanically the most dramatic: intensity and time drive power. |
