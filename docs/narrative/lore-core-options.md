Owner: Narrative and Fighter Identity. Version 1, 2026-10-18. Orb (homework 30 and the forms pick): "lets delve into the universe lore before we finalize anything on transformations" and "lets get lore of the universe settled so we can explain reasoning behind transformations". Orb also wants the auras to convey "each characters power foundation", the two fighters' auras to have "similar but inverted themes", and the training dummy's lit pads to form a "dummy aura" that "simulates a real fighter's aura using the latest technology". **These are options, not canon: Orb writes the universe.** This page builds on `backstory-skeleton.md` (the ten questions, still open) and answers its first three questions as one idea per option. Original, in the frame of an imaginary anime's fourth season. Names are placeholders.

## What every option has to do

1. **Say where power comes from,** why fighters transform, and what a form is, in one idea.
2. **Give the Protagonist and the Anti-hero one foundation each, as mirrors:** the same fuel used in opposite ways, so their auras are similar in kind and inverted in direction.
3. **Explain the slingshot:** why waiting while the opponent grows stronger pays off, and why only the Protagonist can do it.
4. **Explain the dummy:** a rig of lit pads that copies a real fighter's aura with the latest technology.
5. **Fit what is already in the game:** the Protagonist's five steps and his two roads (Witness and Trial), his blood heating from warm to boiling, steam and heat seams; the Anti-hero's Pride, his three forms (Regalia, Sovereign, Apex) and his violet aura that warms and then cools, with a blade-cut outline (Orb's pick, 70/30); the Empress's numbered revisions; the Cyborg's hunger and his limiter chip.
6. **Keep the guardrails:** no orbs, no fixed count, no wish or summon or radar for the artefacts, no column of light or darkening sky at the artefact moments, staples yes and signatures no, no number that measures a fighter's strength on a gadget, and the Cyborg's appetite aimed at fighters and wreckage only (Legal).

**How the fourth-season frame is kept.** None of this is explained to the player. It comes out as callbacks and as what people say: "That's the burn talking", "Mind the draw", "He's got their regard now". The player joins at season four, and everyone in the story already knows.

## Option A. The Furnace: power is inside you, and it is heat

**Where power comes from.** Every living body has a furnace. Effort, feeling and getting hit feed it. Ordinary people use it to heal and to lift; fighters learned to let it burn hot without burning out. The planet is the stage, not the source (so the world stays a place to protect, not a battery).

**Why fighters transform, and what a form is.** A form is a **setting**: the furnace opened one more notch. A fight pushes the furnace past the point where the old setting is enough, so the body steps up to the next one. That is why a form is an achievement and never a burden: the fighter chose to open the door. The cost is heat that has to go somewhere, which is the wear and the wounds the game already has.

**The Protagonist's foundation: the open hearth.** He burns wide and gives heat away; people near him are warmed, and he is warmed by what is thrown at him. He rises when he meets a higher fire (Trial). When he sees harm done in front of him the hearth goes still and banked: a cold quiet (Witness). **His aura** spreads outward, warm and loose, with steam and seams of heat; it fills the space around him.

**The Anti-hero's foundation: the sealed vault.** The same furnace, shut tight. He keeps pressure and no flame, and he is proud of how little he wastes. His power is control, and his rank is the proof of it (Pride, the need for a witness). **His aura** presses in against his body, cool violet, with its edge cut into blades; it warms only where the pressure leaks, then cools again. His frames fog when he lets it out.

**The mirror.** Open and sealed. Give and hold. Warm and cool. Wide and tight. Respect and Pride. Tested and ranked. One furnace, opposite doors. Their auras are the same material pointing in opposite directions: his rises out, the Anti-hero's presses in.

**Why the slingshot works.** A hearth can bank coals. While the opponent's fire grows, the air around them gets hotter, and a fighter who stays low and open soaks that heat in with every blow he takes. That is the coil: banked heat, scaled by how long he waited and how much was traded. When he finally opens the next door, all the banked coals flare at once, and he can leap several settings. The Anti-hero cannot: a sealed vault does not soak, it only holds until it breaks.

**The dummy's aura.** The rig is a furnace in miniature: a frame with heat-sensing plates that glow in a pattern **recorded from a real fighter's burn**, so the hall behaves as if one stood there. "It copies a real aura, plate by plate."

**The others.** The Empress's revisions are numbered settings stamped by her court (the paperwork). The Cyborg is a furnace with a demon's appetite bolted into a chassis, and his limiter chip is a damper.

**What it changes in the game:** very little. Heat, wear and wounds are already in. **Risk:** "inner energy" is the most familiar idea in the genre, so keep it ours by never measuring it with a number or a gadget and by calling the forms settings, not levels.

## Option B. The Tide: power is outside you, and it belongs to the world

**Where power comes from.** A tide runs through the planet and between worlds, and it swells where there is strain: fights, storms, crowds. A fighter is a **shoreline**, a body that lets the tide in. That is why the fights keep happening here: the planet sits on a strong seam. It is also why destroying the land matters: a broken shore changes how the tide comes in, and a big enough break lets it spill (the catastrophe tier is a spring tide).

**Why fighters transform, and what a form is.** A form is a **tide mark**: how high the water stands in them. They transform when the tide in the room rises to meet the tide in the opponent. The mark is read by everyone: the water is visible.

**The Protagonist's foundation: the open shore.** A gently sloping beach: the water comes up and goes out again, and it is shared with whoever stands near. He rises when the water around him rises (Trial); when someone does harm in front of him, the sea goes slack and still at low water: a cold quiet (Witness). **His aura** is a warm current, a surf that moves outward in swells with steam where it meets cold air.

**The Anti-hero's foundation: the sea wall.** A harbour wall, built to hold the tide back at a line. The water piles behind it, and his power is the pressure behind the wall. **His aura** is a tight line with a sharp crest: violet that lights at the top edge and cools beneath, the outline cut into blades.

**The mirror.** Shore and wall. Welcomed and held. Flowing and standing. A warm current and a cold depth. The same sea, taken in opposite ways. His aura moves; the Anti-hero's stands still.

**Why the slingshot works.** Before a big wave the sea draws back. A fighter who stands in the ebb and does not contest it lets the water leave the opponent's side and gather behind him. The longer the draw-back and the harder the trade, the bigger the wave when it returns, so he can rise several marks at once. A wall cannot ebb: it holds until it breaks.

**The dummy's aura.** A rig that makes a **small artificial tide**: a ring of plates that pulse in a swell pattern copied from a recorded fighter, so the training hall behaves as if one stood in it. "It makes a small tide. Stand in it."

**The others.** The Empress charts tides for an empire: her revisions are tide tables, reissued as the sea moves. The Cyborg is a drain that pulls the tide into a chassis, and his limiter chip is the sluice gate.

**What it changes in the game:** the planet becomes the source, so the biomes can touch power (strong surf near the sea, thin air on a peak, the pillar-7 variants). **Risk:** it asks Game Design and Art for the most (ki tied to the ground, a visible tide), but it is also the one that explains the wrapped planet and the catastrophe fights.

## Option C. The Regard: power is between people, and it is witnessed

**Where power comes from.** Power comes from being truly met: attention, challenge and recognition, which in the show is called **regard**. It is not fame. It is the strength a fighter gets from an opponent who is really looking at them, from a crowd that cares, and from a ranking that someone keeps. The League's ranks measure it. This is why the two branches are named as they are: a fighter can **witness** (the regard turns to what must be protected) or be **tested** (the regard of an equal).

**Why fighters transform, and what a form is.** A form is a **standing**: the place a fighter has in the world's regard. In a fight the opponent and the crowd re-estimate you, and when the estimate moves, the body becomes what they now expect. A transformation is the world re-ranking you in the middle of a fight.

**The Protagonist's foundation: he pays regard.** He gives his whole attention to whoever is in front of him, and he wants them at their best. He rises as they rise (Trial); he goes quiet when the regard must turn to the ones in danger (Witness). **His aura** flows outward and picks up the other fighter's colour at its edges: it shows who he is looking at.

**The Anti-hero's foundation: he collects regard.** He ranks everyone, so that he stands above them; he needs a witness, and he is stung by a fighter who will not look at him properly. **His aura** pulls inward, lines of violet converging on him, the blades slashing toward his centre; his lenses reflect the opponent.

**The mirror.** Pays and collects. Open palm and closed fist. Looks at you and looks at the room. Respect and Pride, at their roots. The same exchange, opposite way round, and the aura shows which way.

**Why the slingshot works.** Regard is owed. When he stands back and lets the opponent grow, he pays attention the whole time, and every blow he takes is attention paid. When he finally steps up, what he paid comes back: scaled by how long he waited and how much was traded. He gets the interest, so he can rise several standings at once. The Anti-hero cannot, because he collects and never pays.

**The dummy's aura.** The League's calibration rig is a ranked, hollow fighter. It simulates an aura **by borrowing the room's attention**: sensors and lights read how closely it is being watched, and its plates glow in proportion. It dims when nobody looks. "It only glows when you watch it."

**The others.** The Empress is the regard of a whole empire; her revisions are official changes of standing, stamped by her court (the paperwork). The Cyborg is the one nobody notices; his hunger is, funnily and sadly, a hunger to be seen (he must not eat civilians, and does not).

**What it changes in the game:** the meters already match (Respect, Pride, the witness); the League is a place to hang the run's rank and the titles. **Risk:** the most abstract to draw and to say aloud, and "power of belief" is a staple, so keep it about attention and not worship.

## The three, side by side

| | **A. Furnace** | **B. Tide** | **C. Regard** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Power is** | Inside you, heat | The world's, a tide | Between people, attention |
| **A form is** | A setting (a notch) | A tide mark | A standing |
| **The Protagonist** | Open hearth | Open shore | Pays regard |
| **The Anti-hero** | Sealed vault | Sea wall | Collects regard |
| **Their auras** | Spreading warm / pressed cool | Swelling surf / standing crest | Flows to the other / pulls to himself |
| **The slingshot** | Banked coals flare | The draw before the wave | Paid attention comes back |
| **The dummy** | Copies a recorded burn | Makes a small tide | Glows when watched |
| **Best at** | Needs nothing new | Explaining the planet and the catastrophes | Explaining the meters and the ranking |
| **Costs** | Nothing | Most (the ground feeds power) | Some (the League needs a place) |

A mix works too: any one idea can be the body (what power is) and another the forms (what a form is). For example, **Furnace for the body and Regard for the standing**.
