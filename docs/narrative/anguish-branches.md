# Anguish reworked: the Protagonist's two branches, in his voice

Owner: Narrative and Fighter Identity. Version 2, 2026-10-16 (adds the name pitches, section 4b; the flavour lines for the waiting game and the slingshot are in `waiting-game-lines.md`). Game Design writes the rules alongside this; this page is the **why** and the **voice** of each branch. Everything is an option for Orb, names included (the branch names below are plain labels, not titles). Lines follow Orb's writing rules (`writing-quality.md` section 0): four to twelve words, plain, a reaction to what just happened, not a description. No franchise echoes (section 6).

**Orb, 2026-10-16, verbatim:** "the anguish mechanic might need to be reworked somehow, lets keep working on this because building up anguish by intentionally killing civilians is a perverse incentive for the protagonist, and a fight against another 'good guy' once there are more fighters might be lame and boring if neither can power up because they're avoiding civilian deaths. we could solve this potentially with Protagonist having multiple branching forms, one branch achieved by witnessing civilian deaths at the hands of his opponent, perhaps one achieved by allowing his opponent to power up first and testing himself against a higher form"

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

He is a protector who loves a real fight, and he has two ways to get stronger that fit both halves of him. **He gets stronger when someone else does harm in front of him**, never from his own collateral (that is the perverse incentive, gone: the deaths that count are the ones his *opponent* causes). And **he gets stronger when he lets the other fighter get stronger first**, because a higher opponent is the point of the fight (that keeps a match between two good fighters from stalling: each can wait, and waiting is how he pays respect). Grief does not make him stronger. **What he does about it does.** One branch is serious and quiet; the other is delighted and loud.

## 2. Branch A: when someone else does harm in front of him

*(A plain label: "the witnessed branch".)*

**The why.** He lives to protect people, and he can't undo a death. What changes is that the playing stops. Until now the fight was a sparring match and he has been asking for the other fighter's best; now he is asking them to stop. It's the opposite of his usual self: no grin, no technique-talk, no compliments. This is *not* rage. He goes **quiet, plain and direct**, and that quiet is the tell that he has stopped enjoying it (his old bible has it: "anger makes him go quiet").

**What he feels.** Horror first, then a cold clarity. He notices every move; he stops talking about them. He is aware of who is behind him.

**What it looks like in lines.** Fewer words, shorter sentences, periods and not exclamation marks. He speaks to the opponent in the second person ("you"), and about the people only when they are in the way ("them"). He doesn't banter back. When it ends he checks on people before he checks on himself.

| Moment | The line |
|---|---|
| He sees civilians die at the opponent's hands | "You didn't have to do that." |
| More deaths, the same fight | "That's enough. We're done playing." |
| He lands a hit in the form | "Stay away from them." |
| He takes a hit in the form | "Doesn't matter. I'm still standing." |
| The opponent taunts him | "Not now. I'm not in the mood." |
| A launch would send the opponent toward the town | "Not toward them. Over here." |
| He wins in the form | "It's over. Stay down." |
| It fades, after the fight | "Okay. I'm back. Is everybody okay?" |

**What it is not.** It isn't a tantrum, tears or screaming. It isn't a colour change in his hair or eyes (Legal's rule on forms), and it isn't lightning or a roar at the sky. It doesn't come from his own broken buildings. The look (Art's) should read as *stillness*: less movement, more weight.

**Why a rival wants it.** The Anti-hero wants the Protagonist's full effort and takes anything less as an insult (his marks). He has no reason to care what the quiet cost.

| Moment | The rival's line |
|---|---|
| The Protagonist goes quiet | "There it is. That's the look I wanted." |
| The same, shorter | "Finally. Took you long enough." |

## 3. Branch B: when he lets the other fighter get stronger first

*(A plain label: "the tested branch".)*

**The why.** A fight is a test, and a higher opponent is a better one. He is fascinated by technique (Orb's mark), and a power-up is the most interesting technique in a fight. So when the other fighter starts to change, he **waits**, and when they arrive he meets them. That is respect, not carelessness. It also solves the good-fighters problem: two of them both want the other at their best, so each gets their form, and each is rewarded for letting the other go first.

**What he feels.** Delight, hunger, a little pride in the other fighter. He's grinning through it.

**What it looks like in lines.** Quick, warm and short. Exclamation marks are natural here. He narrates what he *sees* ("I see how you move"), never the dynamic. He asks for more.

| Moment | The line |
|---|---|
| The opponent starts powering up; he waits | "Go on. Power up. I'll wait." |
| The opponent's higher form arrives | "Higher! Yes! This is what I wanted!" |
| He takes a hit from the higher form | "Ha! Now that's a hit!" |
| He studies it | "Okay, I see how you move." |
| He steps up in answer | "My turn. Let's see what I've got." |
| In the form, he lands one | "Keep going! I'm just getting started!" |
| The opponent won't power up | "Come on, show me! Don't hold back!" |
| After a win | "That was amazing. Let's do it again." |

**Why a rival hates it.** The Anti-hero hates being pitied (his marks). A head start is pity. So the rival turns it down, which makes the Protagonist's patience *worth* something: he's offering, and the rival's pride is the cost.

| Moment | The rival's line |
|---|---|
| The Protagonist waits for him to power up | "Don't wait for me. I don't need it." |
| He powers up anyway | "Fine. Hope you're ready for this." |

**Two good fighters.** When neither will harm anyone, branch A never fires and branch B is how a match gets going. Each waits for the other: a small, funny standoff that ends in whoever blinks.

| Moment | The line |
|---|---|
| Both wait | "You first. I'm serious." |
| The other answers | "After you. I can wait all day." |

### 4b. Name pitches (version 2, 2026-10-16)

Orb: "please pitch these", and the witnessed branch stays a **cold quiet**. Six pairs, witnessed first. Each has a card (one or two words, in capitals, says what the player sees), a reason, and one sample line for each branch. The branch name is also the word a form carries (a form is "Hush, second step", and so on); that is for Game Design and Orb to settle once a pair is chosen. Every sample passes the lint. No colour, no hair, no scream.

| # | Witnessed / tested | Cards | Why it works | A sample line each |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Vigil** / **Trial** | VIGIL / TRIAL | A vigil is a quiet watch kept over something lost. Trial is Game Design's own working word, so the rules and the names agree. | "I saw what you did." / "Go on, power up. I'll wait!" |
| 2 | **Hush** / **Heat** | HUSH / HEAT | The nearest to "cold quiet": one word for the stillness and one for the grin. Short enough for a card, and the sounds contrast. | "That's enough. I'm done playing." / "Okay, now it's getting good!" |
| 3 | **Warden** / **Challenger** | WARDEN / CHALLENGER | Named for what he does for people (guards them) and for the other fighter (meets them). Reads as two jobs of one man. | "Nobody else gets hurt. Not today." / "Is that your best? Show me more!" |
| 4 | **Still** / **Stoked** | STILL / STOKED | The most plain and the most cheeky. "Still" is a cold quiet; "Stoked" is how he says he feels. Risk: slang that dates. | "Stay down. I mean it." / "You're stronger? Great! Come on!" |
| 5 | **Steady** / **Game On** | STEADY / GAME ON | A steady hand versus a ready grin. Easy to say aloud in the English dub. Risk: "Game On" is a common phrase and may read as generic. | "Not another one. I'm stopping this." / "Take your time. I want the full thing." |
| 6 | **Shield** / **Open Hand** | SHIELD / OPEN HAND | Shield is what he puts between people and harm; Open Hand is the head start he offers the other fighter. Risk: both are objects, not feelings. | "Behind me. All of you. Go." / "I'll wait. Give me your best." |

**My pick: pair 1, Vigil / Trial,** because it keeps the rule word and the card word the same, and Vigil is the coldest of the six without being a flavour of rage. **Second: pair 2, Hush / Heat,** for a card that reads in one glance across the room. **Not recommended: pair 4,** whose slang will age badly in a game with no text yet.

The cards above replace HE'S GONE QUIET / RISING TO IT only if Orb picks a pair. Those two stay as the plain-words fallback on the first screen that teaches the branch.

## 5. What this asks of the rules (for Game Design, not decided here)

- **A needs a witness rule:** deaths caused by the *opponent* count; his own collateral doesn't. Whether a fighter can *cause* the deaths on purpose to power a rival is the same question Orb raised, and it's Game Design's.
- **B needs a rule for who goes first** when both are good, and what counts as "letting them": a short hold, a named gesture, both fighters pausing. A tie must not stall a match.
- **Either branch should show up in the lines at once**, so a player knows which one fired before they read a card.

## 6. What I avoided (no echoes)

Colour-shifting hair or eyes (Legal's rule). A scream that shakes the sky. A named rage state. A chant, or a speech about justice. The classic line where a fighter says he'll never forgive the other. A tearful breakdown. A catchphrase for either branch. The Protagonist waits *because he's curious*, never to show off or to humiliate.

## 7. Questions for Orb

1. Should branch A change **how he talks** (the quiet) for the whole fight, or only while the form lasts?
2. In branch B, is it enough that the opponent has *begun* to power up, or must a higher form actually arrive?
3. Do you want the rival to ever **accept** the head start (a rare line, a rare cost to his pride)?
4. Names for the two branches and their cards.
5. Should branch A be possible if the Protagonist's own blow caused the deaths? (I think **no**: it keeps the old perverse incentive alive.)
