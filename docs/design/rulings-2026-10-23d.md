# Rulings, the 23c series' fourth file (2026-10-25): the daze, the sell and the rally

Owner: Game Design. **One decision per item, routed by owner.** Paper only; every number is a first value. It answers Orb's early-B question (homework 46, `rulings-2026-10-23c.md`), and builds on 23c without undoing any of its fixes. All of it sits behind one switch, `rally.enabled`.

**Orb's answer** (`docs/ep/vision.md`, "Orb's homework answers of 2026-10-25"): "find a balance between the two. I want moments where a fighter gets stunned, then sells some big powerful hits from the attacker, who then continues to bounce him around with elaborate flight attacks. if the knockbacks are varied and entertaining enough, there should be nothing stopping two players from knocking each other all around the planet. if it gets too repetetive, it will violete the rule of cool."

**The balance, in one paragraph.** An early B in a string still pops, so the brawl keeps its middle (23c). But a fighter who is truly stunned, **dazed,** takes the attacker's big hits, **the sell,** and then a full send that opens **the rally:** a chain of flight attacks, each one the attacker's own press, that bounces him around the planet. Nothing caps a rally. **Variety holds it together and repetition ends it:** a link of a kind just used is easy to escape. The one being bounced can turn it back, so two players can knock each other around the planet.

## 1. What starts it: the daze

| # | The decision | Owner |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | **The daze is a stun that is earned, not one every early B gets.** Six things daze: **a broken guard**; **a perfect block's reversal** of a heavy, a charged blow or a string's ender (the one who was perfect-blocked is dazed; amended in `rulings-2026-10-26.md` item 8); **a wall slam or a bury** (the world hits him); **a clean, fully charged heavy**; **a heavy's whiff, punished** by a tapped medium or heavy, or a perfect follow-up; and **a struggle lost by overwhelm,** when he lands. A flurry's close stays a plain stagger, as today, so mashing alone never dazes | Encounter |
| 2 | **The daze lasts 40 ticks.** He can't strike or guard. **His way out is his own:** each press he makes takes 3 ticks off, counted at most one every 6 ticks, so the fastest thumb cuts it by about a third, to about 27 ticks (`rulings-2026-10-26.md` item 17). RT's burst breaks it at once after its first 12 ticks, at the burst's price. It reads on the body (a sway, his guard dropped, his eyes unfocused) and in the rumble, never with a marker over him | Encounter, Animation |
| 3 | **A fighter just out of a rally is wary** (`rulings-2026-10-23.md` 10c, widened). For 2 s after a rally ends on him, a daze source on him is a plain stagger, so a rally can't be chained straight into the next. It is an answer window for him, and no button waits on it | Encounter |

## 2. The sell: big hits that read as big

| # | The decision | Owner |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 4 | **In the daze, the attacker's blows are sold.** Each landed medium (Y) takes the launch's hit-stop (12, not a heavy's 8), a camera push and a reaction a size larger: the head snaps, the body folds, he is driven back a step and pulled in again. Two or three sold blows fit in a daze. The damage is each blow's own, with no bonus: **the sell is the look, not the number** | Encounter, Animation, Camera |
| 5 | **B ends the sell with the send.** B at a dazed fighter is the sure launcher at full power, whatever the string: a send, with the journey, the decisive hit-stop (20), the planner's candidates and freshness. That send opens the rally. If the attacker never presses B, the daze runs out and they are back in the brawl | Encounter |

## 3. The rally: links of flight attacks

| # | The decision | Owner |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 6 | **A rally is a send followed by links,** each one the attacker's own press while the body is in flight. **Any full send can open one,** but only a send out of a daze comes with the sell. The links: | Encounter, Combat |
| | **The chase strike:** LT with X or Y. The zip catches him and the blow sends him on, nudged by the stick. It is the zip and pays its ki (20 or 30), as ruled | |
| | **The overtake:** the same chase, with the stick held back against his flight. The zip passes him and meets him head-on, sending him back the way he came: the ping-pong. LT with B stays the movement signature, so no new button is needed. The zip's price | |
| | **The spike:** B from above him: he is slammed straight down, with a crater. On hard ground under the plant speed, he glances back up (`fight-feel.md` §3) and the rally can go on. On soft ground it ends there | |
| | **The bank:** the chasing attacker kicks off a wall, a cliff or a building's face to change his line at the body, at a dodge tap within 4 ticks of the contact. It is free, and it is the attacker's mirror of the wall kick | |
| | **The energy link:** a lob where he will land, a beam that catches him (level 3 carries him along it, `energy-overhaul.md` E44), or the energy grab thrown at him in flight, whose payoff then plays in the air. Each at its energy price | |
| | **The world's link:** no press at all. The planner's candidate for the send carries him into a building, off a cliff, skipping across water, down a slope, into a vent's lift (Combat's biome candidates). Ricochets keep their rule of at most two in a row | |
| 7 | **No cap on links or on rallies.** What ends a rally is the defender's answers (§4), the attacker's ki, the ground, and repetition (item 8). Each link marks freshness by its kind, as every send does | Encounter |
| 8 | **Repetition loosens the grip, and this is the guard.** At each link's arrival the defender has his tech: a guard press timed to the arrival, as the chase already rules. **For a link of a kind not used yet in this rally, the window is 4 ticks. For a kind used earlier but not as the last link, 6 (item 17). For the same kind as the last link, it is 8. A third of one kind in a rally is teched by itself.** The planner's variety penalty and freshness by kind push the same way. So a varied rally holds, and a repeated one falls apart, which is Orb's rule of cool made into a rule | Encounter |

## 4. How it ends, and the turn

| # | The decision | Owner |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 9 | **The defender's answers, all his own presses:** the air tech at a link's arrival (item 8); **the wall kick** when he meets a face (`fight-feel.md` §3); **the recoil flip** when he lands (a dodge tap in its first 10 ticks); **the brake** while sent (LT held); and **the burst** after a link's first 12 ticks | Encounter |
| 10 | **The turn: a rally can change hands.** A teched link, a wall kick or a recoil flip gives him the counter-rush within 20 ticks (`fight-feel.md` §2). **If his counter-rush lands on the chaser in the air, it is a full send of the chaser,** and the rally is now his: the same links, the same rules, his ki. Two players who keep answering knock each other around the planet. The meet rules apply when both arrive together (a 3-tick window; the heavier goes through; the third meet in a row is the clash exchange) | Encounter, Animation, Camera |
| 11 | **It ends** when he lands and no link follows within 20 ticks, when either side runs out of ki for the next link, when a link is teched and not turned, or at a burst. **The landing pays by 9a** (`landPay` 0.5), on the rally's last landing only | Encounter |

## 5. What makes two rallies different

| # | The decision | Owner |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 12 | **Seven sources of variety, and none of them a script:** the player's choice of links; the planner's candidates for each send, with its variety penalty and freshness by kind; the biome and what stands in it (buildings, cliffs, water, ice, forest, vents); **each fighter's own links** (Combat: the Protagonist's chase strike, the Rival's plated ram, the Empress's mantle wall (item 18), the Cyborg's crane hook reeling him back mid-flight); the energy links; Animation's ragdolls and flails (Orb: out-of-plane joints on); and the camera's shot grammar for a chase | Combat, Encounter, Animation, Camera |
| 13 | **The choreographer scores variety in a rally.** A link whose kind and destination differ from the last two scores higher. The world's links that use the place (a building smash, a cliff, a sea skip, the parted sea) score highest. With a neutral stick, the planner picks for spectacle. The stick still wins (`stickWins`) | Encounter |

## 6. Keeping 23c's fixes

| # | The decision | Owner |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 14 | **The pop is unchanged** (23c items 1, 2, 9 and 12). Early B in a string pops, a popped fighter is free from the apex, and B at him is the spike. **A pop never opens a rally:** only a daze's send, or a full send, does. The lit-B newcomer's line (mash, B) makes pops and staggers, never a daze | Encounter |
| 15 | **No free juggle.** The daze is short and his to shorten. The sell is two or three blows. Every link gives him a tech, and repetition widens it. So no rally is free | Encounter |
| 16 | **Length stays in band,** and these are the levers in order if it drifts: the daze's sources (item 1), then the AI's chase rate, then the links' prices. Never a cap | Encounter, QA |

## 7. QA's rows (AI v AI with 1 to 3 in unless it says so)

| Row | Band |

## 8. Addendum (2026-10-25): each fighter's own links

Combat parked each fighter's own rally links (`docs/combat/pending/rally/`). These rule its four asks, plus one it put to Controls that is a design question too.

| # | The question | The decision | Owner |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 17 | **Do a fighter's own links count as their own kinds,** for item 8 and the scoring? | **Yes.** An own link is a different move, with its own look and destination, so it is its own kind for item 8's grip and for item 13's scoring. Otherwise the fighters with the most character would have the shortest rallies. **Item 8 gains its missing middle step,** for a kind used earlier in the rally but not as the last link: a 6-tick window. So the windows are 4 for a fresh kind, 6 for one used before, 8 for the same as the last, and a third of one kind is teched by itself. Alternating two kinds still wears out, a little more slowly | Encounter |
| 18 | **The Empress's wall link** (amended 2026-10-25). Combat redrew Guard's Wall as **Mantle Wall:** the body rebounds off her own braced mantle (heel planted, the mantle swung forward rigid as a door; it never wraps or grabs), with no guard involved | **Combat's Mantle Wall stands and replaces the guard version,** which is withdrawn. Her guards stay out of rallies altogether, so `empress-guards.md` §4 holds with nothing to argue. Mantle Wall is her own link in every form (item 17) and a ricochet under `fight-feel.md` §3: it counts toward the two-in-a-row limit, and the force and damage are hers. **It works both ways:** a sent fighter can wall-kick off her braced mantle as off any face. Item 12's "her guards taking a link" is answered by her own mantle instead | Encounter, Combat |
| 19 | **The Rival's Plated Ram:** the body is sent exactly along the stick's line, snapped to eight ways, with no bend from the planner | **Allowed: precision is his identity.** The stick already wins (`stickWins`). Here the planner adds nothing, so a ram along an empty line is his choice, and the choreographer scores it low for variety, which is his trade. Two things still hold: the tier's collateral budget caps the deaths on that line (with no borrowing at tiers 1 and 2, a place over budget evacuates), and a surface on the line is met by the ricochet rules. With the stick neutral, the line is straight on along the chase | Encounter |
| 20 | **The Cyborg's Wreck Toss:** the throwables' pick-up mid-rally | **Allowed, as a world link in two presses,** the pick-up (A) and the throw (Y), each one press and one action. The pick-up rule holds: he is free (not inside a zip), and the body is out of striking reach, which a rally gives. The wreck must lie within 2 bh and inside his tier's lift. **It must be empty, with nothing living near it** (Legal RL-183, ln04). The lift's tell takes its time while the body flies on, so the throw has to arrive while the body is still in flight or within the 20 ticks after it lands (item 11). Otherwise the rally has already ended. The wreck bursts into scrap, which damages structures by the tier factor. It carries no ki, as throwables don't | Encounter, Combat, World |
| 21 | **The Protagonist's Palm Turn** (the stick held across the flight) against the overtake (the stick held back) | **No overlap by design.** "Back" is within 45° of the reverse of the flight, and "across" is within 45° of either perpendicular, so the sectors touch only at their edges. Controls confirms the left stick's sector read at those edges (its four-way model reads about 99 in 100) and adds a few degrees of dead band between them if the capture shows misreads | Controls |
