# Rulings, 2026-10-23 (third file): launch anywhere's cause, the pop and the send

Owner: Game Design. **One decision per item, routed by owner.** Paper only; every number is a first value. It answers Encounter's `docs/director/pending/launch-pay/README.md` and replaces `rulings-2026-10-22.md` §1b with its follow-ups (`rulings-2026-10-23.md` §2a, §9, §10b to §10d) where they differ.

## The cause

Encounter's arms show it plainly. **With the string armour (2a), the AI launches 93 times a match, against 39 without it.** Each journey is 6.2 s and 96 damage, against 10.3 s and 270, and brawl damage a minute falls from 1,645 to 972. The match runs long on many weak pops, each of which ends a brawl that was dealing damage. Paying the landing more (9a) can't reach that. The lit-B newcomer wins 99 of 100 with 2a alone, so 2a is the cause there too.

**What is wrong is that a weak launch separates the fighters.** A launch from one or two blows sends him on a journey, and every journey costs the brawl a few seconds. Launch anywhere made those launches easy, and 2a made them land.

## The decisions

| # | The decision | Owner |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | **A weak launch is a pop, and only a full one is a send.** A launcher whose power after freshness is **under 0.8** pops him up instead of sending him. **The pop:** he goes up 1.5 to 3 bh by power and comes back down **in reach,** within 30 ticks. He makes no journey, so there is no separation, no freshness mark, no wary window and no first move, and the brawl's pull holds. The attacker's blows land on him in the air: a juggle. The pop is worth its blow (`anywhere.worth` 5 × power). The popped fighter can tech his landing (a dodge tap), and burst out after his first 12 ticks. **A launcher of power 0.8 or more is the send,** as ruled: the journey, the flight curve, the building ladder, freshness, 9a's paying landing, and 10c's wary window after it | Encounter, Simulation, Animation |
| 2 | **A pop builds the string.** A landed pop counts 2 points toward the next launcher's string within the link. So a combo grows into its send: two lights, a pop, a follow-up blow, then B for a full launch. That is Orb's "chain together attacks dynamically and emergently", in the brawl, not as a storm of journeys | Encounter |
| 3 | **2a goes: no string armour.** Safety is as `launch-anywhere.md` §3 first ruled. ## For Orb: one clear question

**This changes what an early B does, which Orb shaped in homework 23 and 30.** Orb asked that "launchers should have an intuitive way to activate and aim at any point in a combo string", with no cooldowns.
- **What still holds:** B still does something at any point in a string. There is still no cooldown and no cap. Repeats still wreck more, and freshness stays gentle.
- **What changes:** early in a string, B pops him up for a follow-up instead of sending him flying. The full send comes as the string grows, or at once from an opening or a perfect.

**The question for Orb:** "Early in a combo, should B pop him up so you can keep hitting, and send him flying once the combo has built up (recommended), or always send him flying, even from the first hit?" The second keeps today's problem: matches that run long on many short flights, and a newcomer who wins by pressing B.

## The second read (`docs/director/pending/launch-spike/README.md`)

Every AI v AI row is in band: a median of 385.8 s (p10 319, p90 496.5), 8.3 sends a match, 17.6% of civilians lost and 1.88 flips a minute. **But the lit-B newcomer still wins 100 of 100.** He pops the medium AI about 149 times a match. 55% of his damage lands on the popped AI, 42% in the brawl and 3% on journeys, and no spike happened in 1,492 pops.

**The root cause: a pop makes the defender helpless.** The brawl is even because both strike at once. A pop takes the defender out of that exchange and gives the attacker free blows, and a pop can follow any landed blow. So pressing B after every hit turns an even brawl into a one-sided juggle, again and again.

| # | The decision | Owner |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 12 | **A popped fighter is back in the brawl from his pop's apex.** The pop's rise is a reel, about 8 ticks, in which the attacker's blows land. **From the apex (about 12 ticks after the pop) he is free in the air:** he can guard, perfect-block and strike as on the ground, and the brawl's pull brings both down together. So a pop buys one or two blows and a better angle, plus its 2 points toward the next launcher, and never a free juggle. The spike stays (B at him in the air brings him down), and from the apex it can be guarded like any blow. **No other lever moves:** no juggle scaling, no change to the AI's tech or burst shares, no cost on the pop | Encounter, Animation |
| 13 | **L17's band for a player who follows the game's own hint:** against medium he wins **10 to 50%** (sometimes, but medium wins most), and against easy 50 to 95%, as before. The hint should teach a line that works, not one that always wins | QA |

- **The masher's 51%** (band 35 to 50) is a point over, inside the noise. Read it again with item 12, which changes the pops he takes too.
