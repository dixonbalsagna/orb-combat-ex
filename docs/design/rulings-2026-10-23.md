## 9. Launch anywhere's length with the string armour (Encounter's §7a read, 3726e364)

**The read** (AI v AI, launch anywhere on, 40 matches an arm). Control: 560 s, 15.7 full launches, 31.6% civilians lost. With 2a (the string armour): 896.7 s (the 900 cap), 25.3 full launches, 34.1%, with 169 of 191 AI launchers hard to answer. The old in-string shares change nothing, since most AI launches come after an opening. 2a at three blows: 875.6 s.

**Why.** A launch costs a flight and a return but pays little wear, and the match's length comes from wear. So every launch that lands makes a match longer, and 2a lands more of them. The fix is to make a launch pay, not to land fewer.

| # | The decision | Owner |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 9a | **A launch pays.** Arm 1, for Encounter to run: **the landing's wear for a launcher's send is × (1 + power)** in World's contact pay (`sim/world/contact.gd` `_pay`, a grant to World): a full launch's landing pays double, a weak pop ×1.55. **The launching blow is worth 5 × power** brawl lights, where it was 3 × power. 2a stays as ruled, since players need a string to protect its launcher. **Targets:** a median of 420 to 480 s (p90 at most 600), full launches 6 to 12, civilians lost at most 30% | World, Encounter |
| 9b | **Arm 2, if Arm 1's median is still over 480:** Arm 1 with the AI's share after an opening at 0.12, 0.25 and 0.5 by level (from 0.15, 0.35 and 0.6), so it lands fewer launches | Encounter |
| 9c | **Not levers:** 2a at three blows (it still runs 875 s, and puts the string row at 65%), and a weaker AI answer, which would make spam pay | |
| 9d | **Guard and grab's throw is down,** as 1d says: QA's script holds the stick down at the throw, so the bury opens the sure launcher that the row exists to test. ## 10. QA's re-read on 1b66c4bb (`docs/qa/reread-2026-10-24.md`): the lit-B newcomer wins everything

**The read.** With launch anywhere and 2a built, the newcomer who mashes X and presses B when it lights wins 100 of 100 against medium and easy. He launches 144 and 98 times a match, the first at about 0.5 s, and is launched 16 and 6 times. **Why:** a mash lands two blows in a chain at once, so 2a's string armour makes nearly every launcher after a mash hard to answer, and freshness is gentle, so the loop never tires. 9a's paying launches would make it worse.

| # | The decision | Owner |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 10a | **A dominance row for the newcomer.** The lit-B newcomer (mash X, B 0 to 20 ticks after the light) **wins at most 50% against medium,** and against easy at least 50% (a first-timer beats Easy, `modes.md`) and at most 95%. The first-launch rows stay as they are (75% and 90% inside 60 s), and are read together with this one | QA |
| 10b | **A mash builds no string.** The string that makes a launcher hard to answer (2a) counts **tapped blows, perfect follow-ups, mediums and heavies,** never mashed or burst blows. A mash is the mash rule's blows, each within 9 ticks of his last press. **So "mash, then B" is an answerable launcher:** its armour takes one light, and a second within 6 ticks stops it. The defender's own flurry answers it, as it should. A real string, built of placed blows, still protects its launcher. **It follows the nine cells' own line:** a masher has the flurry's pace, and a tapper has what timing earns | Encounter |
| 10c | **The returning fighter is wary.** For 2 s (120 ticks) after he lands from a launch, a launcher at him that isn't sure is answerable, whatever its string. **So each launch in a loop needs a new opening.** It is an answer window for the one launched, not a cooldown on anyone's button: the launcher can still launch him at once off an opening | Encounter |
| 10d | **Checked against 9a.** With 10b and 10c, the loop's launchers are answerable, and the AI answers most of them, so the newcomer's 144 launches should fall to the openings and the strings he builds. Those are the launches 9a makes pay. **Read 10b and 10c with 9a,** on the newcomer's row, the route and AI v AI. If the newcomer still wins over 50% against medium, the next lever is freshness's general term back to ×0.9 (from ×0.97): a repeat loop tires, and a varied fighter doesn't | Encounter, QA |
| 10e | **Guard and grab reads 21% at the brink** (band 30 to 70). **Encounter runs the throw's worth at 8 now,** as its own arm, and the row is read again with 9a when 9a lands. The band stays | Encounter, QA |
| 10f | **The band calls** (catch tells 0.57 a minute against 1 to 3; flips 1.11 against 1.2; the route launched 76 times a match against 10 to 40; the route's first launch inside 60 s at 87% against 90) **all wait for 9a and 10b to 10c,** since each reads the launch storm: a fight that is mostly launches leaves little brawl for catches and knock-backs. **One fallback is named now:** if catch tells are still under 1 a minute after them, the AI's catch shares go from 0.02, 0.04 and 0.06 to 0.04, 0.08 and 0.12 | QA, Encounter |
