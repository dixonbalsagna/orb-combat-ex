## 2. Combat's choreography and `fight-feel.md`, reconciled

Combat's four pieces (recoil exits, ricochets, clash meets, turns) and the rules in `fight-feel.md` describe one system. Combat draws how each looks, and these are the rules they play by. **The build order is one piece at a time:** 1) exits and flips (flips built), 2) ricochets, 3) meets and the clash exchange, 4) turns.

| # | The piece | The decision | Owner |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 2a | **Exits: ticks and distances** (Combat's 10, 18 and 24 ticks; 1, 2 and 3 to 4 bh) | **The exit fits inside today's reel or knock-back, which doesn't change length.** The distance and time are the sim's (the reel, the 1.5 bh heavy recoil of 1a, `knockBack.distBh`). Combat's 10, 18 and 24 ticks are the animation's nominal lengths, scaled to fit. Combat's exits are the look of every separation by weight (skip, handspring, backflip, tuck). **The recoil flip is the player's cut of it:** a dodge tap in the first 10 ticks lands him set 12 ticks later, with the counter-rush | Animation, Encounter |
| 2b | **Ricochets: the speed line, the counter, the count** | **The line is 1,500 units a second into the surface** (the speed along its normal). **Below it, Combat's plant:** he plants on the surface (a 4-tick stop, a tell of at least 6 ticks) and springs back toward the rival. **A press of X, Y or B within 6 ticks of the plant makes the rebound a blow of that strength** (Combat's counter), and a dodge tap aims the spring by the stick instead. **At or above it:** a glancing contact (under 40°) glances off at 0.7 of the speed, and a steeper one is the wall slam, the lodge or the chain, as ruled. There a dodge tap within 4 ticks either side is the wall kick (half the slam's damage, a kick-off at 0.6 of the speed). **At most two ricochets in a row,** of either kind, then the next surface is a plain stop (Combat's count, where `fight-feel.md` had three). **A rebound heavy launches only into an opening,** as any B: otherwise it is a heavy, with 1a's recoil | Simulation, Encounter, World |
| 2c | **Meets: the window, who wins, the escalation** | **The window is 3 ticks.** Contacts within 1 tick of each other meet; 2 or 3 ticks apart, **the earlier breaks through** at half its worth (Combat's "break by timing"). **Equal strengths meet:** X knocks wrists and both bounce 0.5 bh, and pressing again meets again; Y bumps elbows and both slide 1 bh; B is the heavy clash ring and both backflip 3 bh. **Unequal: the heavier goes through** at full worth, and the lighter takes his exit (the triangle, visible). **The third meet in a row escalates** into the clash exchange on the pulse (`fight-feel.md` §4). **A B meet breaks glass near it from tier 2,** and its shockwave damages structures from tier 3, as rule of cool item 15 has it | Encounter, World, VFX |
| 2d | **Turns: the deflect's window and the count** | **The deflect:** a fresh guard press in the last 16 ticks of an incoming blow's wind-up that misses the perfect-block window turns the blow aside, with no damage and no stagger. His next press within 8 ticks is **the return,** a quick blow from his stop and reversal pools. A turn is the deflect plus the return, 12 to 16 ticks. **2 to 6 turns,** ending when one misses a deflect (he takes the blow and his exit), or escalates (a heavy is a meet, a launch is a link). A press inside the deflect window never starts the perfect block's lockout. The perfect block stays the better read: its reversal and stagger | Encounter |
| 2e | **The AI's shares,** by level | Recoil flips 20, 45 and 70%, and their counter-rush 50, 70 and 90% (1e). The plant's counter press 30, 55 and 80%. The wall kick 15, 35 and 55%. Deflects 20, 40 and 60%, and the return after a deflect 60, 75 and 90%. The clash exchange's pulses 40, 65 and 85%, as in every clash. All of them start no sooner than its reaction time after the cue that starts them | Encounter |

**QA's rows, piece by piece:** flips 2 to 6 a minute; plants and glances 2 to 8 a match, wall kicks 1 to 4; meets 3 to 8 a minute, clash exchanges 1 to 4 a match; turns 2 to 8 a minute, and runs of turns 2 to 6 long.

## 4. The glance line (2026-10-22, later)

Simulation built the glance as ruled (`docs/architecture/ricochet-core.md`), and it never fired in AI play. Of 58 ground contacts, none would glance, since the shallow ones are slow into the ground: a level send gains downward speed only from gravity.

| # | The decision | Owner |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 4 | **(a): the line is on his whole speed,** 1,500 units a second, not on the part along the surface's normal. Below it he plants; at or above it, under 40° he glances (0.7 of his speed) and steeper he slams. **It applies to building faces and to ground sloped 30° or more.** On ground under 30° the knocked-about rules stand (skid, bounce, tumble, slam), because the skid is Orb's own picture and a glance shouldn't replace it. QA's row counts plants and glances together, 2 to 8 a match. If it reads under, the lever is the choreographer's taste for sends at walls and slopes, not the line | Simulation, Encounter, QA |

## 6. A mash flows (2026-10-22, later)

Orb: "I want to make sure gameplay feels smooth and responsive." Encounter measured §5b's table at 20.2% of all ticks frozen, against 18%. Mashed and burst lights alone were 7,996 blows at 2 ticks each, 14.8% of all ticks, and the ×0.75 step can't cut a stop already at 2.

| # | The decision | Owner |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 6 | **(b): only a mash's first blow stops, for 2 ticks, and the rest flow with no stop.** A mash is a run of one fighter's mashed or burst blows, each landing within 12 ticks of his last. It starts again after 12 ticks without one. **The later blows still sell the contact without freezing:** the struck body's shake (drawn only), the impact spark and the sound. **Every other blow keeps its stop:** a tapped light, a flurry's close (12, as a close), mediums and up. The budget stays 18% with its second step. A mash of five blows comes to about 2 frozen ticks where it was 10, so the frozen share should fall to about 9%. Encounter measures it in scratch | Encounter, Simulation, Animation, VFX, Audio |

## 7. Responsiveness, the calm turn, the chase and the freeze (2026-10-22, later)

Orb: "I want to make sure gameplay feels smooth and responsive."

| # | The read | The decision | Owner |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 7a | **A player's press after an exchange waits up to 36 ticks** (0.25 to 0.6 s), held by the director's cooldown "as the choreography's beat" | **A player's press never waits more than 6 ticks (100 ms) after an exchange ends,** and **12 ticks after a set piece's end** (a beam struggle, a clash exchange, a throw's landing, a finisher contest), whose outro the camera is still telling. The press is kept and thrown at the cap, never dropped, and its "queued" acknowledgement stays. **The AI keeps the director's beat** (0.25 to 0.6 s): that is its pacing, a human-like breath, and it costs a player nothing. **Positional rules aren't this wait and stay:** the sent fighter's 45-tick first move after a launch or a wall slam is about where he may go, not about a press being held. **QA's rows (hard):** a player's press to his blow's wind-up, at most 6 ticks after an exchange and at most 12 after a set piece | Encounter, QA |
| 7b | **The Whole ring turns 0.6 times a match,** all at form pauses. Landings are lost because World still sees the downed fighter sliding when the 45-tick window lapses | **A landing's moment starts at the end of his journey** (the slide's end, `journey_end`), not at his first contact. The window is 45 ticks from there, with both set (under 60 u/s) and nothing in flight. That is what "landed" meant. `landedTicks` doesn't grow to 90, since a window that long would turn the ring while they are already fighting again. **QA's row:** 1 to 3 turns a typical match. If it is still under, the next lever is the 300-unit ground rule (`rulings-2026-10-19b.md` 1f) going to 450, never a hitch | Encounter, World, QA |
| 7c | **The AI's chase rarely catches:** the chaser tops out at 800 to 1,400 u/s against a glide of about 1,500, and 7 of 11 medium chases run out at 90 ticks, a median 4,000 units behind | **A chase is the zip, and pays its ki** (as `brawl-second-pass.md` §16 has it: "chasing a launch stays the zip of Z2, with its ki"). It flies at 1.25 times the sent body's speed, and never under 1,900 u/s, so it closes on the glide within about 60 ticks. Without the ki there is no chase. If it hasn't caught him by his arrival, it arrives at his landing for the first move. **The same for a player's chase.** QA's row: of the AI's chases, at least 60% catch | Encounter, Simulation |
| 7d | **The mash flow reads 12.4% frozen** (not about 9%): 2,099 first mash blows at 2 ticks, 1,004 tapped lights and 452 bolts at 3, and the heavier stops. 41 of 150 ten-second blocks are over 18% | **Accepted.** The budget is the match's share, at most 18%, and 12.4% passes. A ten-second block over 18% is the trigger for the ×0.75 step, not a failure: local spikes are big moments (launches, clashes, closes). **A new hard row against stutter: no ten-second block over 40% frozen.** If one is, the step cuts deeper in that block, ×0.5 on stops under a decisive blow, still at least 2 | QA, Encounter |
