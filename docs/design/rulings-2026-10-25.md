# Rulings, 2026-10-25: the henchmen's grades

Owner: Game Design. **One decision per item, routed by owner.** Paper only; every number is a first value. It answers `docs/combat/henchmen-grades.md` §6 ("Game Design: every number above"), for the campaign's horde stages (`campaign.md` §2.2).

**The rule over all of it.** A horde is flow and texture: many bodies, each easy alone, read at a glance, cleared with style. It is never a test of how long a player can stand being hit. Its numbers serve that, and its limits exist for reading and frame rate, never as cooldowns.

| # | The question | The decision | Owner |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | **The four grades and their toughness:** fodder 1 light, grunt 4, veteran 8 (the first 2 lights shrugged off), elite 40% of a roster fighter's health with wounds at half rate | **Accepted as a set.** The veteran, Combat's new grade between Research's 1 and 2, is the right idea: a wall to break without the cost of a full fighter | Combat, Encounter |
| 2 | **Their blows on a player:** fodder 1.5%, bruiser 3%, marksman 2%, speedster 1.5%, veteran 6%; the flinches, the skip and the handspring | **Accepted, with one band, so the horde stays pressure and not attrition.** A medium-skill player (the AI as player) loses **10 to 30% of his health over a horde stage in columns 1 and 2,** and 20 to 45% by column 4. If it runs over, the levers go in order: the turn's gap (item 6), then the blow's damage. A fodder's flinch never holds the player's next press, as written (control first) | QA, Encounter |
| 3 | **Lights hit one body** | **Confirmed.** One press, one blow, one body. The string travels instead: the next press with no stick hops 1.5 bh to the next body, so a light string through five fodder reads as one sweep | Encounter |
| 4 | **The crowd caps:** a medium hits 2, a sweep 3, a spin 4, a splash of 1 bh | **Accepted.** They are how many bodies one blow reads through, which keeps a blow legible and the frame rate inside Performance's body cap | Encounter, Performance |
| 5 | **The domino:** a sent body knocks down up to 3 of his grade or lower | **Raised for the heavy blows, by the rule of cool.** A body sent by a medium dominoes up to 3. **A body sent by a heavy, a throw or a launch dominoes up to 5.** A heavy through a packed line should bowl the line. A higher grade still stops him and is staggered | Encounter |
| 6 | **The turns:** 2 a player (1 on easy), a fodder or grunt weighs 1, a veteran or elite 2; contacts never within 8 ticks of each other; the gap 20 ticks (easy 30, hard 12); never from outside his pane; from behind only with a 20-tick wind-up and its own sound | **Accepted as written.** The 8-tick spacing and "never from outside his pane" are the two hard rows. In co-op a henchman holds at most one player's turn, and **striking a henchman who holds your partner's turn frees it:** the help is felt | Encounter, QA |
| 7 | **The return:** at most twice in a row | **Accepted.** It is the ricochet's own limit (`fight-feel.md` §3), so one rule covers both | Encounter |
| 8 | **The co-op relay:** at most twice a body | **No cap.** The body's toughness ends a relay: a grunt falls after a few blows, and a veteran soon after. Two players juggling a grunt between them is the rule of cool, and it pays for itself | Encounter |
| 9 | **An elite has no brink** | **Confirmed.** Finishers and their struggles belong to fighters with names, the lieutenants and bosses. An elite falls at zero to an ordinary blow | Encounter |
| 10 | **The elite's six tricks,** one each, shown by his silhouette | **Accepted.** A trick is a type picked for each spawn, never kept, never named, and with no memory. That keeps an elite inside Legal mc01 (`docs/legal/campaign-screen.md`): no generated individual with traits or a history. Legal sees the trick list once, since it is the nearest thing to a trait | Combat, Legal |
| 11 | **A hit's weight with no sim-wide hit-stop:** the struck henchman alone holds, 3, 5 or 7 ticks, and a sweep holds each body a tick apart | **Accepted.** It is the one-body hold of `energy-overhaul.md` E102, so Simulation builds one mechanism for both. Like E102, it doesn't count toward the freeze budget | Simulation, Encounter |
| 12 | **Research's question: an elite's brawl freezes the horde and the other player** | **Recommended, until Orb says otherwise: in any stage with a second player or a horde, every hit-stop is a one-body hold.** The two bodies in the exchange hold, and everything else runs. The sim never freezes as a whole, so the other player never stops. This keeps co-op's no-break rule (Orb, 2026-10-11), and the mechanism is E102's | Simulation, Encounter |
| 13 | **Research's question 4, which is Orb's: does a downed henchman count toward anything?** | **Recommended for Orb: he pays a little ki and counts toward nothing else.** Fodder 2, grunt 4, veteran 8, elite 15. Clearing a horde feeds his energy, which suits the energy overhaul, and a horde before a duel becomes a choice about ki. **No score, no meter and no reward of any other kind:** henchmen aren't civilians, but menace and anguish stay the civilians' (c08), and henchmen stay anonymous (mc01) | Orb, then Encounter |
| 14 | **The signature beam against an elite:** "guard or dodge only, with no struggle" | **Moot, as written.** Struggles are live now (`energy-overhaul.md` E97) and pause nothing, but an elite's kit has no beam, so none arises. If an elite ever gets one, it struggles like anyone | Combat |
| 15 | **QA's bands** | Combat's three, plus item 2's: a wave of 12 fodder falls in **10 to 20 s;** at least **four kinds of body meeting** happen in a minute of a mixed wave; **no player is hit by two bodies within 8 ticks;** and **no player is struck by more than his turns allow** (the last two are hard tests) | QA |

- **Nothing here waits on Orb except items 12 and 13.** Item 12 is a default until Orb says otherwise. Item 13 is a recommendation for the EP to put to Orb with the campaign's questions.
