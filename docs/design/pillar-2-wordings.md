# Pillar 2: wordings for Orb's pick

Owner: Game Design. 2026-10-30. A paper for Orb's choice; `pillars.md` and CLAUDE.md stay as they are until then. Orb turned down "Intent, not combos" and asked: don't alienate players who like predetermined combos, appeal to platform-brawler fans and to fans of generative content, and "why don't fighting games take this approach to the combos that their fighters use during a match?"

**An answer to Orb's question.** Fighting games don't do it because a combo is something players learn, practise and rely on, and anything that changes under them can't be learned or balanced. But procedural-universe games don't have that problem either: their worlds are generated once from a seed, so every player finds the same planet. **The same trick works here. Generate a fighter's combos from their style and a seed, once, and every player gets the same fighter.** Our generator already has the other half: a generated move may change how a press looks and never what it's worth (`docs/combat/moveset-generator.md`), so no generated move can be the best button.

**So the question that separates the wordings is: do the same presses give the same combo?** Today the answer is half yes. Timing, worth and result repeat, and the director picks the look.

**All five keep:** each press is one blow (Orb's approved press feel); mash, timing and hold as styles; the stances on the left hand; the director owning the staging, the camera, launches and set pieces, and each signature's variant (pillar 7). **The direction-and-charge play that platform-brawler fans expect is already there in Orb's 4a pick,** so no wording has to carry it. **All five also keep no motion inputs,** because of the claw law and accessibility. Orb hasn't been asked that directly, and combo fans may expect them, so it's worth a word from Orb.

**My recommendation: #1, with this body text.** It's the only one that answers Orb's question head-on and also keeps the combo players, and #5's three doors are already in the body:

> **2. Every fighter grows their own combos.** A press is a blow, a direction aims it and a hold charges it, so anyone can fight at once. Each fighter's combos are generated from their style by a fixed seed, the same for every player, so they can be learned, practised and relied on. The director owns the staging: the camera, the launches and the set pieces.

**What #1 still needs before it's a promise.** It's a direction, not a design. Combat and Encounter would have to say how a generated string maps to presses, what happens to a string the player breaks off, and how it sits with the rally's links and freshness. If Orb wants the promise today and that design later, #2 is true now and can be replaced by #1 when it lands.
