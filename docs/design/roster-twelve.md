# A roster of twelve: roles, the first four, and templates for the rest

Owner: Game Design. Version 2, 2026-10-05. This is the mechanical half of the roster document. Narrative's identity half is `docs/narrative/roster-twelve-identity.md`, and the EP merges the two. **Version 2 lines the templates up one to one with Narrative's nine,** adds the juggernaut, and ends with one table of twelve (§8).

**Orb's ask** (`docs/ep/vision.md`, 2026-10-05): "I want to lay the groundwork for a full roster of twelve, so classify the roles and subroles we've already established with our first four in our roster, then give me notes and proposals for fighters who can fill out the rest", with "generic templates to consider for fighter niches we haven't touched yet".

**How to read it.**
- §1 is a set of eight axes for describing any fighter in this game.
- §2 puts the first four on those axes and lists the gaps they leave.
- §3 is nine templates, each paired with one of Narrative's identities. Eight fill the roster and the ninth is the spare. §3b keeps four more as alternatives.
- §4 shows three ways to pick the twelve, with what each covers.
- §5 is the order to build them in on a zero budget.
- §8 is the single view: one table of twelve.

**Everything here is an option for Orb.** The templates are functions. They carry no lore, look or personality: that is Orb's universe and Narrative's half. Every template still needs Legal's screen before it becomes a character.

## 1. The axes

A generic fighting game sorts fighters by range and speed. This game has more to sort by: five stances, a world that breaks, a meter of ego for each fighter, forms, and wounds. So a fighter here is described on eight axes.

| # | Axis | The question | The values so far |
| ---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | **Role and subrole** | How does he win a fight? | Duelist, Commander, Devourer (§2). The templates add eight more, and the spare a ninth |
| 2 | **Range home** | Where does he want the fight? | The brawl (in reach), zip range (the mid band), far (energy), or anywhere |
| 3 | **Stance spine** | Which two of the five stances are his best, and which one is his worst? | Martial arts, defensive, energy arts, charging, manoeuvre |
| 4 | **The world** | What is the landscape to him? | He tends it, ignores it, annexes it or feeds on it (Narrative's words). Still free: builds on it, hides in it, is part of it, plays to it |
| 5 | **His meter** | What fills his ego meter? | The rival's deeds, his own wins, his own losses, or the world. Still free: time, movement, position, contact, a partner, an audience |
| 6 | **His forms** | What shape does his transformation take? | A drain, thresholds with a crash, a long ladder, or molts that can't be undone |
| 7 | **His wounds** | How does he take damage differently? | He spreads it, hides it, repairs it, or regrows it around a weak point |
| 8 | **His part in the story** | What is he to the cast? | Hero, rival, villain, wildcard |

**Why the stance spine has a weak stance.** A fighter with no weak stance has no matchup to fear. Every template names one. The first two fighters are all-rounders on purpose, as Orb wanted, so they lean and don't lack.

**What the story axis is for here.** It only balances the cast. Who each one is belongs to Orb and Narrative.

| Role | He wins by | Who | Subroles seen so far |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Duelist** | Reading the rival, at any range | The Protagonist, the rival | The redirector; the closer |

### The gaps they leave

| Axis | What nobody does yet |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Role** | Nobody wins by taking hold, by lines of fire, by speed alone, by size, by not being seen, by helpers of his own, by a changing kit, or by simply not stopping |
| **Range home** | Nobody lives at zip range. Nobody holds the far band alone, without bodies in front |
| **Stances** | Nobody is at home in the charging stance. Nobody is a manoeuvre fighter from the first second. Nobody is built on the A button (grabs). The three reserved hybrids have no owner |
| **The world** | Nobody builds on it, hides in it, mends it, or plays to the people on it |
| **Meters** | Nothing is filled by time, by movement, by position, by contact, by a partner or by an audience |
| **Forms** | Nothing switches back and forth. Nothing comes on a schedule. Nothing involves a second body |
| **Wounds** | Nobody is armoured. Nobody is fragile. Nobody shares wounds with a partner. Nobody aims at one region on purpose |
| **Bodies** | All four are one human-sized body at the start |
| **Story** | One hero, one rival, and two threats. No second hero, no ally, no neutral |

## 3. Nine templates, paired with Narrative's identities

Each template uses systems that exist or are planned: the five stances and their readings, the zip, juggles, counters, grabs and their lockout, mines, zones, terrain arts, staged buildings, civilians, the fold, wounds by region, the mood and the weather. Costs are rough: **low** is data and tuning on what is built, **medium** is one new system, and **high** is several, or a change to the rig or the camera.

Every fighter costs the same base on top of his template: an identity block for the generator, his moves in each stance, five signatures, three forms, his voice, Legal's screens, and a balance pass against every other fighter.

The names in brackets are Narrative's identity templates. The pairing is where the fit is natural, and Orb can re-pair any of them.

### T1. The grappler (Narrative's Veteran)

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The niche** | He doesn't chase. He waits, takes hold, and the throw is the lesson |
| **Role and subrole** | Grappler. The catcher |
| **Range home** | The brawl |
| **Stances** | Best: martial arts, defensive (the guard throw is his). Worst: energy arts |
| **Signature mechanic** | **A is his main button.** His grab lockout is 60 ticks where everyone else's is 90. Every throw of his is aimed, and the launch planner offers him a target: the ground, a ridge, the sea, a building. **The catch:** his grab beats a zip on its arrival, as a third kind of counter. A hold can be struggled out of on the pulse, so being held is never a cut scene |
| **Meter** | **Grip.** It fills while he is in reach of the rival and drains at range. High Grip lengthens his carry and widens his throws' aim |
| **Forms** | By feats: each form is unlocked by landing a named throw, and not by a threshold |
| **The world** | He tends it, gruffly: his throws aim away from people. The same kit on a villain would aim into them |
| **Wounds** | His arms are his kit: an arm broken halves his holds |
| **Against the four** | The Protagonist turns force aside, and the grappler adds his own. The rival softens from range, and the grappler has no range game. The Empress keeps bodies in front, and he goes through them one at a time. The Cyborg eats the town, and the grappler uses it as an anvil |
| **New tech** | **Medium.** Grabs, the carry, the guard throw and the launch planner exist or are specified. New: paired poses for holds, which the generator doesn't make yet, and a struggle-out on the pulse |
| **Risk** | Being grabbed is the least fun thing in a fighter. Orb has already asked for no grab spam, so his shorter lockout has to be earned by Grip |

### T2. The speedster (Narrative's Prodigy)

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The niche** | He is the quickest fighter in the first two minutes, and he learns what the rival repeats |
| **Role and subrole** | Skirmisher. The quick study |
| **Range home** | Zip range |
| **Stances** | Best: manoeuvre, martial arts in its speed reading. Worst: charging, since he can't stand still |
| **Signature mechanic** | **Acceleration at the base tier.** At his first form his zips cost about 12 and 20 ki where others pay 20 and 30, his tells are the shortest, and a chain of his zips doesn't weaken. **His forms add reads, not speed.** So at the top tier he is no faster than anyone, and he doesn't collide with Orb's note that the top form is almost too fast, or with the Cyborg's last molt. **Learning:** a move the rival repeats goes stale against him sooner, and each repeat widens his slip window against that move, until the rival varies. His blows are light, and he staggers for longer when caught |
| **Meter** | **Pace.** It fills with zips that get out clean and with repeats he has punished. It drains when he stands still or is caught |
| **Forms** | Lessons: each form is something he has worked out, shown as steadier movement and not as more speed |
| **The world** | He doesn't think about it. His collateral is the lowest on the roster |
| **Wounds** | His legs are everything: a broken leg takes his discount away |
| **Against the four** | The Protagonist stands and reads, and the speedster gives him many small reads where others give few big ones. The rival's straight lines are what a zip punishes. The Empress's guard exists to catch him. The Cyborg ends the match faster than he does, so the speedster has to win early |
| **New tech** | **Low.** The zip, its counters and its looks are being built now, and the stale-habit rule exists. He is an identity block and numbers |
| **Risk** | He is the fighter most likely to read as a vanish step (Legal's RL-076 and RL-077). Zip spam is also downtime for the rival. A fighter who peaks early can feel like a loss already decided by minute five |

### T3. The stalker (Narrative's Turncoat)

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The niche** | He wins the first blow of every exchange, from where he wasn't seen |
| **Role and subrole** | Ambusher. The stalker. This is the future stealth fighter that pillar 6 holds the hiding kit for |
| **Range home** | Zip range, out of sight |
| **Stances** | Best: manoeuvre, energy arts (a shot from cover). Worst: defensive |
| **Signature mechanic** | **Cover, as kept in `future-stealth-fighter.md`.** In cover (the sea, a forest canopy, a ridge, the shell of a staged building) his power can't be read and the rival's lock is lost. Leaving cover gives an ambush: his next zip, strike or shot inside 2.5 s is ×1.5 and can't be countered on the arrival |
| **Meter** | **Unseen.** It fills while the rival has no lock on him, and is spent on the ambush |
| **Forms** | An unveiling: each form shows more of him and hits harder, and each makes him easier to find |
| **The world** | He hides in it, so he is the one fighter who wants the town standing. A burned forest or a levelled block is cover he has lost |
| **Wounds** | Light everywhere. He can't afford a brawl |
| **Against the four** | The Protagonist leaves towns, and the stalker needs them. The rival wants to be watched, and the stalker wants the opposite. The Empress watches from behind her guard in plain sight. The Cyborg eats the stalker's cover |
| **New tech** | **High.** See the open question below. His AI must also be built around the ambush: in the prototype the AI ambushed 0.06 times a match |
| **Risk** | Hide and seek is downtime. Narrative names this identity as the natural home of the parked mask, which is Art's and Orb's call |

**Still open: what is hidden, and from whom.** On one shared screen, both players see the same picture. Three answers, to be chosen before he is built:
1. **A screen each.** Online, or in split screen, each pane shows its own truth, and he is simply not drawn in the rival's. The split camera exists. This is the real thing, and it only works in those modes.
2. **Hide the tells, not the body.** On a shared screen he is drawn faded, the rival's lock is denied, and his next attack shows no tell, no streak and no engage ring. The rival has to read the body itself. This works everywhere, and it is the cheapest.
3. **Both:** answer 1 where each player has a screen, and answer 2 on a shared one.

### T4. The giant (Narrative's Colossus)

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The niche** | He doesn't move when hit, and the ground answers where he stands |
| **Role and subrole** | Bulwark. The giant |
| **Range home** | The brawl, and he makes the rival come to him |
| **Stances** | Best: martial arts (the heavies), defensive. Worst: manoeuvre |
| **Signature mechanic** | **Size and weight.** He is several times the others' size. Stream blows (lights and flurry blows) don't reel him. He is knocked back half as far and lifted for half as long. In return he is a bigger target, his zips cost more, and a tech or heavy counter staggers him for longer. His landings and his heavies damage structures at one tier above his own, and the ground under him cracks and tilts |
| **Meter** | **Footing.** It fills while he holds his ground, and empties when he is knocked back or launched |
| **Forms** | Growth, or waking: each form is visibly more of him |
| **The world** | He is part of it. He breaks it by being there, and would rather not |
| **Wounds** | Armoured: less wear from stream blows, full wear from heavies, and his legs are the region to break |
| **Against the four** | The Protagonist redirects, and the giant gives him the most force to redirect. The rival's barrages chip him, so the rival must finish by hand. The Empress has many bodies with one region each, and the giant is one body that has to be worn down. The Cyborg grows by eating, and the giant is big from the first second |
| **New tech** | **High.** The rig, the joint limits, the reach bands and the brawl's magnetism are all measured in body heights, and the camera frames two bodies of one size on a planet that wraps |
| **Risk** | A fighter who ignores mashing is hard on new players, and a slow one is dull to play in a game about flight |

### T5. The zoner (Narrative's Former Villain)

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The niche** | He fights from lines of fire: wide, slow beams that leave the lane hot behind them |
| **Role and subrole** | Zoner. The old gun |
| **Range home** | Far, alone |
| **Stances** | Best: energy arts, charging (his held readings are the widest on the roster). Worst: martial arts |
| **Signature mechanic** | **Hot lanes.** His beams and his charged shots are wider and slower to fire than anyone's, with long tells. Where one has passed, the lane stays hot for a few seconds: a zip or a lunge through it is stopped, and a fighter standing in it takes wear. He can hold two lanes at once. They cool, and they never close a place off, because flight goes over them |
| **Meter** | **Old ground.** It fills with the time the rival spends boxed between his lanes |
| **Forms** | A return: each form gives back something he used to have, one more lane or a wider beam, and none is new |
| **The world** | He remembers it, and scars it. A lane across a town is collateral, under the beam caps for each tier |
| **Wounds** | Fragile in the legs and the back: once caught, he stays caught |
| **Against the four** | The rival barrages and then closes, and the zoner never wants to close. The Empress holds space with bodies, and he holds it with heat that doesn't fight back. The Protagonist deflects single shots well, and a lane isn't a single shot. The Cyborg regrows through chip damage, so lanes alone don't finish him |
| **New tech** | **Low to medium.** The signature beam, the short beam, the charged shot and the beam caps exist. New: the hot lane as a thing in the world, and an AI that respects it |
| **Risk** | He can stall. Pillar 3 says nobody is ever out of range, and the downtime target is 12 s a minute. Lanes must cool, and they count toward the limit of three hazard fronts on screen |

### T6. The puppeteer (Narrative's Legacy)

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The niche** | He fights beside what he inherited, and wins when the rival is between them |
| **Role and subrole** | Summoner. The heir |
| **Range home** | Anywhere: his helpers set the range |
| **Stances** | Best: charging (his three specials each send a helper), defensive (a helper blocks for him). Worst: manoeuvre |
| **Signature mechanic** | **Helpers.** He has up to two simple bodies with one region each, like the Empress's guard. They act for themselves, and the player doesn't steer them: a special sends one to hold a point, to strike, or to stand over a building and take what was coming to it. A blow from him and one from a helper inside a few ticks of each other is a **pincer:** it can't be blocked from both sides. The rival can break a helper, and he re-forms it for ki |
| **Meter** | **Sync.** It fills when he and a helper land in time with each other, and drains while a helper is broken |
| **Forms** | A handing-over: each form passes more of the inherited kit to him, and the last one joins them |
| **The world** | He tends it, nervously. A helper standing over a building is the same idea as Interpose (`economy.md` §4.3) |
| **Wounds** | Shared: some of the wear he takes goes to a helper while one stands |
| **Against the four** | The Empress has three bodies as a front line, and the heir has two as tools. The Protagonist can only redirect one attacker at a time. The rival's barrage hits everything on the field. The Cyborg would eat the helpers |
| **New tech** | **Medium, once the Empress's guard exists.** It reuses her simple bodies and their AI. New: the orders on the charging row, the pincer, and a camera that holds three or four bodies. **High** if the player steers the helper directly, which is the dearer variant |
| **Risk** | Two against one feels unfair unless the helpers are fragile. They have to be separate, physical bodies that the rival can hit. Legal should screen the staging before any art is drawn |

### T7. The wildcard (Narrative's Showman)

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The niche** | His kit changes during the fight, and he is paid for never repeating himself |
| **Role and subrole** | Wildcard. The showman |
| **Range home** | Anywhere: it follows what is on his bill |
| **Stances** | Even across the five, with his best following the bill. Worst: defensive: he bows where others guard |
| **Signature mechanic** | **The bill.** Every other fighter has three fixed specials. He has nine, in three bills of three. After each decisive exchange the bill turns to the next, and both players can see what is coming. The order is seeded, so a replay is identical. Within a bill his cells keep their roles, timings and worth, as the generator's rules require (`melee-press-feel.md` §13) |
| **Meter** | **Applause.** It fills when he lands a move he hasn't used lately, and when people nearby are watching unharmed. It drains on a repeat, and when a civilian near him is hurt |
| **Forms** | Acts: his forms are the three acts of a show, taken in order |
| **The world** | He plays to it. He wants the fight where people can see it, and loses his meter if they are hurt. That pulls him toward towns and makes him careful there |
| **Wounds** | Ordinary |
| **Against the four** | The Protagonist leads the fight away from the people the showman needs. The rival also wants a witness, so the two compete for the same crowd. The Empress brings her own audience, and he plays to her guard. The Cyborg eats his audience |
| **New tech** | **Low to medium.** The special loadout is already planned as a later feature, and he is its first use. New: the bill and its display, and the watching-civilians count |
| **Risk** | Nine specials is three fighters' worth of that content. His balance moves with the bill, so each bill has to be balanced as if it were its own fighter |

### T8. The juggernaut (Narrative's Harbinger)

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The niche** | He names what he will break, and then he breaks it. He doesn't hurry and he doesn't stop |
| **Role and subrole** | Juggernaut. The forecaster |
| **Range home** | The brawl. He walks in |
| **Stances** | Best: charging (his held readings, and his clock), martial arts. Worst: energy arts: he doesn't shoot |
| **Signature mechanic** | **The forecast.** He marks one of the rival's regions, in plain sight. His blows wear that region 25% more and the others 15% less. When it breaks, or after 45 s, the mark moves on, in an order both players can see. **And he is armoured,** at normal size: stream blows don't reel him. His far approach is the slow pursuit, and light shots don't turn it |
| **Meter** | **The clock.** It only rises: with time, and with the wear he takes. It is never spent. Breaking one of his regions sets it back |
| **Forms** | On schedule: a form is ready when the act turns. The rival can't empty the fill, and can only delay it with a break |
| **The world** | He walks through it. He doesn't seek a town and doesn't go round one |
| **Wounds** | Armoured against the stream, and ordinary against heavies. Each break costs him time, not strength |
| **Against the four** | The Protagonist's Respect is filled by a rival who commits fully, and the juggernaut never over-commits. The rival's Pride is fed by humbling people, and armour is hard to humble. The Empress's guards have one region each, so a mark means little against them and everything against her. The Cyborg regrows what the mark wears, so the juggernaut has to mark the hatch he can reach |
| **New tech** | **Low to medium.** Armour is data on reel and knock-back. The mark is the wounds system with a display, and the stick already leans a blow toward a region. New: the mark's order, the clock, and an AI that defends a marked region |
| **Risk** | A fighter who ignores mashing is hard on new players. Inevitability can feel unfair, so the mark has to be visible and the set-back real. He is also the template with no joke in him, which is Narrative's risk to carry |

## 4. Three sets of twelve

### Set A: Narrative's twelve

The first four and T1 to T8. This is the set in §8.

**What it covers:** eleven roles, every range home, every stance as somebody's best and somebody's worst, and twelve different meters and forms. **What it costs:** two high-cost templates, the stalker and the giant.

### Set B: the spare in place of the stalker

| Out | In | Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| T3 Stalker (the Turncoat) | T9 Shaper (the Caretaker) | The hidden-information question goes away. The roster loses its second fighter at zip range and gains one who builds |

### Set C: the spare in place of the giant

| Out | In | Effect |

## 6. A hook for "the fourth season"

This is a mechanic, and not lore. Narrative's half carries the history in voices and in a canon ledger. The mechanical side of it can be **threads:** a pair of fighters who share a past get one thing changed when they meet. `moveset-rules.md` already plans a revealed signature "from a rivalry thread" for the rival.

A thread could change one of these, and only in that pairing:
- the intro, which Encounter's dynamic intros already vary;
- one revealed signature;
- how a struggle between them looks;
- one line of each fighter's voice at the brink.

Two threads for each fighter is 12 threads across a roster of twelve, and each is small. Narrative's "who talks to whom" (its §5.3) says which pairs would have one.
