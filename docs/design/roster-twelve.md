# A roster of twelve: roles, the first four, and templates for the rest

Owner: Game Design. This is the mechanical half of the roster document. Narrative writes the identity half, and the EP merges the two.

**Orb's ask** (`docs/ep/vision.md`, 2026-10-05): "I want to lay the groundwork for a full roster of twelve, so classify the roles and subroles we've already established with our first four in our roster, then give me notes and proposals for fighters who can fill out the rest", with "generic templates to consider for fighter niches we haven't touched yet".

**How to read it.**
- §1 is a set of eight axes for describing any fighter in this game.
- §2 puts the first four on those axes and lists the gaps they leave.
- §3 is ten templates for the gaps. Twelve fighters need eight, so there are two to spare.
- §4 shows three ways to pick eight, with what each covers.
- §5 is the order to build them in on a zero budget.

**Everything here is an option for Orb.** The templates are functions, and their names are placeholders. They carry no lore, look or personality: that is Orb's universe and Narrative's half. Every template still needs Legal's screen before it becomes a character.

## 1. The axes

A generic fighting game sorts fighters by range and speed. This game has more to sort by: five stances, a world that breaks, a meter of ego for each fighter, forms, and wounds. So a fighter here is described on eight axes.

| # | Axis | The question | The values so far |
| ---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | **Role and subrole** | How does he win a fight? | Duelist, Commander, Devourer (§2). The templates add seven more |
| 2 | **Range home** | Where does he want the fight? | The brawl (in reach), zip range (the mid band), far (energy), or anywhere |
| 3 | **Stance spine** | Which two of the five stances are his best, and which one is his worst? | Martial arts, defensive, energy arts, charging, manoeuvre |
| 4 | **The world** | What is the landscape to him? | He protects it, shows off on it, rules it, or eats it. Still free: builds on it, hides in it, borrows it as a weapon, breaks it by his weight |
| 5 | **His meter** | What fills his ego meter? | The rival's deeds, his own wins, his own losses, or the world. Still free: time, movement, position, reads, a partner |
| 6 | **His forms** | What shape does his transformation take? | A drain, thresholds with a crash, a long ladder, or molts that can't be undone |
| 7 | **His wounds** | How does he take damage differently? | He spreads it, hides it, repairs it, or regrows it around a weak point |
| 8 | **His part in the story** | What is he to the cast? | Hero, rival, villain, wildcard |

**Why the stance spine has a weak stance.** A fighter with no weak stance has no matchup to fear. Every template names one. The first two fighters are all-rounders on purpose, as Orb wanted, so they lean and don't lack.

**What the story axis is for here.** It only balances the cast: twelve villains is not a roster. Who each one is belongs to Orb and Narrative.

## 2. The first four

This is read from what is built and written: `launch-pair-plan.md`, `moveset-rules.md` §5 and §10, `spec-wounds.md` §3 and `economy.md` §4. The stance leanings for the Empress and the Cyborg are my reading of their kits, since neither is built yet.

### The roles they establish

| Role | He wins by | Who | Subroles seen so far |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Duelist** | Reading the rival, at any range | The Protagonist, the rival | The redirector; the closer |
| **Commander** | Deciding where and when the fight happens, through bodies and the sky | The Empress | The court |
| **Devourer** | Growing during the fight, at the world's cost | The Cyborg | The scavenger |

### The gaps they leave

| Axis | What nobody does yet |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Role** | Nobody wins by taking hold, by preparing ground, by speed alone, by weight, by not being seen, by a second body, or by changing the arena |
| **Range home** | Nobody lives at zip range. Nobody holds the far band alone, without bodies in front |
| **Stances** | Nobody is at home in the charging stance. Nobody is a manoeuvre fighter from the first second. Nobody is built on the A button (grabs). The three reserved hybrids have no owner |
| **The world** | Nobody builds on it, hides in it, mends it, or uses the weather |
| **Meters** | Nothing is filled by time, by movement, by position, by reads, or by a partner |
| **Forms** | Nothing switches back and forth. Nothing starts strong and declines. Nothing is one late bloom. Nothing involves a second body |
| **Wounds** | Nobody is armoured. Nobody is fragile. Nobody shares wounds with a partner |
| **Bodies** | All four are one human-sized body at the start |
| **Story** | One hero, one rival, and two threats. No second hero, no ally, no neutral |

## 3. Ten templates

Each template uses systems that exist or are planned: the five stances and their readings, the zip, juggles, counters, grabs and their lockout, mines, zones, terrain arts, staged buildings, civilians, the fold, the mood and the weather. Costs are rough: **low** is data and tuning on what is built, **medium** is one new system, and **high** is several, or a change to the rig or the camera.

Every fighter costs the same base on top of his template: an identity block for the generator, his moves in each stance, five signatures, three forms, his voice, Legal's screens, and a balance pass against every other fighter.

### T1. The grappler

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The niche** | He wins by taking hold: every throw puts the rival into the landscape |
| **Role and subrole** | Grappler. The thrower |
| **Range home** | The brawl, and the last step of zip range |
| **Stances** | Best: manoeuvre (the tackle, the zip tackle), martial arts. Worst: energy arts |
| **Signature mechanic** | **A is his main button.** His grab lockout is 60 ticks where everyone else's is 90. Every throw of his is aimed, and the launch planner offers him a target: the ground, a ridge, the sea, a building. A hold can be struggled out of on the pulse, so being held is never a cut scene |
| **Meter** | **Grip.** It fills while he is in reach of the rival and drains at range. High Grip lengthens his carry and widens his throws' aim |
| **Forms** | By feats: each form is unlocked by landing a named throw, and not by a threshold |
| **The world** | He borrows it as a weapon. A hero-side grappler throws away from towns, and a villain-side one into them |
| **Wounds** | His arms are his kit: an arm broken halves his holds |
| **Against the four** | The Protagonist turns force aside, and the grappler adds his own. The rival softens from range, and the grappler has no range game. The Empress keeps bodies in front, and he goes through them one at a time. The Cyborg eats the town, and the grappler uses it as an anvil |
| **New tech** | **Medium.** Grabs, the carry, the tackle and the launch planner exist. New: paired poses for holds, which the generator doesn't make yet, and a struggle-out on the pulse |
| **Risk** | Being grabbed is the least fun thing in a fighter. Orb has already asked for no grab spam, so his shorter lockout has to be earned by Grip |

### T2. The trapper

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The niche** | The fight happens where he has prepared |
| **Role and subrole** | Zoner. The trapper |
| **Range home** | Far, alone |
| **Stances** | Best: energy arts, defensive (the zone, from the reserved LB + RB hybrid). Worst: martial arts |
| **Signature mechanic** | **A field of devices.** He can keep more mines out than anyone (five, say, where others have two), they chain, and his aimed mine reaches further. His zone slows shots and stops a zip on its way in. Devices expire, and a zip strike or a shot clears one |
| **Meter** | **Ground held.** It fills with the time the rival spends inside his devices' reach |
| **Forms** | The kit grows: each form adds a kind of device, worn on his body until it is placed |
| **The world** | He builds on it. Staged buildings and ridges are anchors for his devices |
| **Wounds** | Fragile in the legs: once caught, he stays caught |
| **Against the four** | The rival barrages and then closes, and the trapper never wants to close. The Empress holds space with bodies, and he holds it with objects that don't fight back. The Protagonist deflects single shots well, so the trapper must layer. The Cyborg regrows through chip damage, so mines alone don't finish him |
| **New tech** | **Low to medium.** Mines are built, with their chain and fuse numbers. New: more live devices at once, the zone (which waits for its hybrid), and an AI that respects them |
| **Risk** | He can stall. Pillar 3 says nobody is ever out of range, and the downtime target is 12 s a minute. Devices must expire, and he must still be reachable by a lunge, a zip or a pursuit |

### T3. The stalker

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The niche** | He wins the first blow of every exchange, from where he wasn't seen |
| **Role and subrole** | Ambusher. The stalker. This is the future stealth fighter that pillar 6 holds the hiding kit for |
| **Range home** | Zip range, out of sight |
| **Stances** | Best: manoeuvre, martial arts (the opening strike). Worst: defensive |
| **Signature mechanic** | **Cover, as kept in `future-stealth-fighter.md`.** In cover (the sea, a forest canopy, a ridge, the shell of a staged building) his power can't be read and the rival's lock is lost. Leaving cover gives an ambush: his next zip or strike inside 2.5 s is ×1.5 and can't be countered on the arrival |
| **Meter** | **Unseen.** It fills while the rival has no lock on him, and is spent on the ambush |
| **Forms** | An unveiling: each form shows more of him and hits harder, and each makes him easier to find. He trades stealth for power, and can't trade back |
| **The world** | He hides in it, so he is the one fighter who wants the town standing. A burned forest or a levelled block is cover he has lost |
| **Wounds** | Light everywhere. He can't afford a brawl |
| **Against the four** | The Protagonist leaves towns, and the stalker needs them. The rival wants to be watched, and the stalker wants the opposite. The Empress watches from behind her guard in plain sight. The Cyborg eats the stalker's cover |
| **New tech** | **High.** Hidden information needs a screen for each player. The split camera exists, so each pane could show a different truth, but on one shared screen he is only faded. His AI must be built around the ambush: in the prototype the AI ambushed 0.06 times a match |
| **Risk** | Hide and seek is downtime. The masks that Art parked for a future fighter would suit him, which is Art's and Orb's call |

### T4. The giant

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The niche** | He doesn't move when hit, and the ground moves when he lands |
| **Role and subrole** | Bulwark. The giant, or at normal height, the armoured heavy |
| **Range home** | The brawl, and he makes the rival come to him |
| **Stances** | Best: martial arts (the heavies), defensive. Worst: manoeuvre |
| **Signature mechanic** | **Weight.** Stream blows (lights and flurry blows) don't reel him. He is knocked back half as far and lifted for half as long. In return he is a bigger target, his zips cost more, and a tech or heavy counter staggers him for longer. His landings and his heavies damage structures at one tier above his own |
| **Meter** | **Footing.** It fills while he holds his ground and attacks, and empties when he is knocked back or launched |
| **Forms** | Growth: each form is visibly bigger |
| **The world** | He breaks it by being there. On the hero's side that is a burden like anguish, and on the villain's side it is the point |
| **Wounds** | Armoured: less wear from stream blows, full wear from heavies, and his legs are the region to break |
| **Against the four** | The Protagonist redirects, and the giant gives him the most force to redirect. The rival's barrages chip him, so the rival must finish by hand. The Empress has many bodies with one region each, and the giant is one body that has to be worn down. The Cyborg grows by eating, and the giant is big from the first second |
| **New tech** | **High as a true giant:** the rig, the joint limits, the reach bands and the brawl's magnetism are all measured in body heights, and the camera frames two bodies of one size. **Low to medium as an armoured heavy** of normal height, which is only data on reel, knock-back and lift |
| **Risk** | A fighter who ignores mashing is hard on new players, and a slow one is dull to play in a game about flight |

### T5. The speedster

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The niche** | He lives at zip range and is never where the blow lands |
| **Role and subrole** | Skirmisher. The speedster |
| **Range home** | Zip range |
| **Stances** | Best: manoeuvre, martial arts in its speed reading. Worst: charging, since he can't stand still |
| **Signature mechanic** | **The zip is his walk.** His zips cost about 12 and 20 ki where others pay 20 and 30, and a chain of them doesn't weaken. His blows are light: he has the stream and little else, and his heavy is the weakest on the roster. When he is caught or countered he staggers for longer. Legal's floor on zip travel holds for him as for everyone |
| **Meter** | **Pace.** It fills with zips that get out clean, and drains when he stands still or is caught. High Pace shortens his tells |
| **Forms** | Gears: steps he can shift up and down. A higher gear is faster, and makes each catch cost him more |
| **The world** | He skims it. His collateral is the lowest on the roster, with windows at most from his wake at the top gear |
| **Wounds** | His legs are everything: a broken leg takes a gear away |
| **Against the four** | The Protagonist stands and reads, and the speedster gives him many small reads where others give few big ones. The rival's straight lines are what a zip punishes. The Empress's guard exists to catch him. The Cyborg's final form is the fastest in the game, so the speedster has to be the fastest from the first second and no faster at the last |
| **New tech** | **Low.** The zip, its counters and its looks are being built now. He is an identity block and numbers |
| **Risk** | He is the fighter most likely to read as a vanish step (Legal's RL-076 and RL-077). Zip spam is also downtime for the rival |

### T6. The puppeteer

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The niche** | He fights as two bodies, and wins when the rival is between them |
| **Role and subrole** | Summoner. The puppeteer |
| **Range home** | Anywhere: he sets his own range with the second body |
| **Stances** | Best: charging (his three specials are his partner's three actions), defensive (the partner blocks for him). Worst: martial arts, when he is alone |
| **Signature mechanic** | **A partner body.** It is a simple body with one region, like one of the Empress's guards. It holds a point, strikes on his beat, or returns. A blow from each inside a few ticks of the other is a **pincer:** it can't be blocked from both sides. The rival can break the partner, and the puppeteer re-forms it for ki |
| **Meter** | **Sync.** It fills when both bodies land in time with each other, and drains while the partner is broken |
| **Forms** | A merge: the partner changes, and the last form joins the two |
| **The world** | He builds on it, if the partner is raised from the ground under him, which would reuse the terrain arts |
| **Wounds** | Shared: some of the wear he takes goes to the partner while it stands |
| **Against the four** | The Empress has three bodies that act for themselves, and the puppeteer has one that the player places. The Protagonist can only redirect one attacker at a time. The rival's barrage hits both bodies. The Cyborg would eat the partner |
| **New tech** | **High.** A second body the player directs: its commands (no new input bits are needed if they ride on the charging row), its AI, its poses, and a camera for three bodies. It reuses the Empress's guard once that exists |
| **Risk** | Two against one feels unfair unless the partner is fragile. The partner has to be a separate, physical body that the rival can hit. Legal should screen the staging before any art is drawn |

### T7. The shaper

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The niche** | He changes the arena, and fights on the one he made |
| **Role and subrole** | Support. The terrain shaper |
| **Range home** | Far, behind his works |
| **Stances** | Best: charging (his works are channelled), energy arts (shots that carve or raise). Worst: martial arts |
| **Signature mechanic** | **Works.** He keeps up to three standing: a ridge, a trench, a barrier over a block of a town. They block shots and launches, give cover and change the lanes. This is the reserved LT + LB hybrid, with Orb's two versions: barriers for the hero's side and collapses for the villain's. A hero-side shaper can also mend a staged building by one stage |
| **Meter** | **Standing.** It is the count of his works that still stand, with the civilians and buildings under them |
| **Forms** | He takes the land's shape: each form dresses him in what he has shaped (stone, water, storm) |
| **The world** | He builds on it and mends it. He is the only fighter who can leave a town better than he found it |
| **Wounds** | Ordinary. His works are his armour |
| **Against the four** | The Protagonist protects by leaving, and the shaper protects by walling. The rival levels buildings for Pride, and the shaper puts a wall in front of them. The Empress changes the sky for everyone, and the shaper changes the ground in one place. The Cyborg's floods and his works meet head on |
| **New tech** | **Medium.** The heightfield already deforms, and a ridge is a crater turned over. New: barriers as world objects, mending a building, and a launch planner and AI that read the new ground |
| **Risk** | Pillar 1 says there are no walls and no corners. His works must be cover and launch surfaces, and never a cage: flight goes over anything. They also count toward the limit of three hazard fronts on screen |

### T8. The wildcard

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The niche** | He fights with what the rival brought |
| **Role and subrole** | Wildcard. The mimic |
| **Range home** | Wherever the rival's is |
| **Stances** | Even across all five. Best: whichever stance he has borrowed. Worst: charging, since he has no ultimate of his own |
| **Signature mechanic** | **A borrowed cell.** When he reads the rival (a perfect block, a parry, a counter), he can load one cell of the rival's (one button in one stance) for 20 s. The blow is drawn in his own hands and paths, so it is his reading of the move and not a copy of the pose |
| **Meter** | **Study.** It is filled by reads, and spent to borrow |
| **Forms** | A mirror: his form steps follow the rival's, one behind |
| **The world** | It mirrors the rival's care for it, reversed: against a destroyer he protects, and against a protector he destroys |
| **Wounds** | Ordinary |
| **Against the four** | He needs each of them to be distinct, which the generator's identity blocks already ensure. Against the Protagonist he gets palms and catches, against the rival barrages, against the Empress a guard's volley, and against the Cyborg a bite |
| **New tech** | **Low to medium.** The generator already fills cells from parts under an identity. Borrowing is running the rival's cell through his identity. New: the loading rule, its display, and a check that a borrowed move passes his own "never" rows |
| **Risk** | His balance is every matchup at once, and he blurs the identities the roster is built on. His mirror match needs a rule of its own |

Two other wildcards, if a mimic isn't wanted:
- **The gambler.** His specials are drawn from a deck, and the next three are shown. It reuses the upgrade plan's cards. The draw is seeded, so replays hold.
- **The mood rider.** His kit follows the fight's mood (calm, tense, frenzied) or the weather, both of which exist. He is weak in a calm fight and the strongest fighter in a frenzied one.

### T9. The banker

| :--- | :--- |

| **Role and subrole** | Charger. The banker |
| **Range home** | Far, until he is ready |
| **Stances** | Best: charging, energy arts. Worst: manoeuvre |

| **Meter** | **Reserve.** It is filled by time spent channelling without being hit |
| **Forms** | One late bloom: a single transformation, late in the match, and the biggest change on the roster |

| **Wounds** | His core is his bar: a battered core shrinks the reserve |

## 5. Build order on a zero budget

Three things are built once and shared: **the generator** (it fills a fighter's cells from parts under an identity block), **the rig** (one human skeleton with joint limits and posed key sets), and **the director** (the brawl, the zip, struggles, the launch planner). ## 6. A hook for "the fourth season"

This is a mechanic, and not lore. An earlier-seasons feeling can come from **threads:** a pair of fighters who share a past get one thing changed when they meet. `moveset-rules.md` already plans a revealed signature "from a rivalry thread" for the rival.

A thread could change one of these, and only in that pairing:
- the intro, which Encounter's dynamic intros already vary;
- one revealed signature;
- how a struggle between them looks;
- one line of each fighter's voice at the brink.

Two threads for each fighter is 12 threads across a roster of twelve, and each is small. Orb and Narrative decide who shares a past, and the templates above don't assume any.
