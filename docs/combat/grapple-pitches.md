# Grappling on A: three pitches

Owner: Combat and Choreography. Date: 2026-10-14. Paper only: nothing is built or changed. For Orb's homework, beside Game Design's and Animation's pitches (`docs/ep/vision.md`, last section: "Let the team pitch it").

**Orb's standing rules, which all three keep.**
- A has a press and a hold.
- One press is one attack.
- Grabs cannot be spammed. Every grab here shares the one lockout of 90 ticks, and a thrown fighter can't be grabbed again for 120 (`brawl-second-pass.md` §4).
- A player always feels in control. Each pitch gives the held fighter a way out every time, and the holder a say in where it ends.

**Legal's rows for all three** (`movegen-banned.json` g01 to g05):
- A hold point is the collar, a shoulder, an upper arm, the body, the waist or an ankle: never the throat, the hair or a tail, and never a clawed hand on the face.
- No hands locked or laced between the two, and nothing glowing at the hands in a hold.
- A lift or a carry is drawn whole: the carried body stays in view every tick and is never hidden by a zip or a cut. It lasts at least 4 ticks and moves no more than 3 body heights a tick.
- A throw is a launch through the planner, never a vanish.
- A tackle takes the waist or the shoulders. Where a carry ends, the crater is local: debris falls, with no rising ring of rubble and no light.
- **Inside a hold** (s08), vicious blows run no more than two in a row to the same place.

**Today's ruling,** for comparison: Orb's own sketch (questionnaire 14), built into `brawl-second-pass.md` §4, puts the shove on a tapped A, a clinch into a throw on a mashed A and a tackle on a held A. These are three other ways. The seize on A+B (§6) stays as it is under all three.

## 1. The catch: a quick throw on a tap, a carry on a hold

**Tap.** A reach with a 10-tick tell, at a hand's reach. If it lands, the throw goes at once, and the stick picks it. Toward: a hip throw or a one-arm sling across. No stick: driven down ahead of him. Away: over his shoulder, or a half turn that sends him behind. These are the seven throws Combat specified in wave 4.

**Mash.** Nothing more: the second press meets the lockout, and the game says so with a refused cue rather than a silent button.

**Hold.** Catch and carry. He keeps the grip and drives the rival along the stick's line, up to 1.5 seconds. He lets go into the throw, or at the cap, or at the first surface the carry meets.

**How it ends.** A throw is a launch, so the planner stages it and the ladder of buildings applies. Thrown at a tower that holds him, he takes the wall slam (twice the building's hit, `wall-slam.md`). A carry that meets a wall ends with both in its crater: the rival is pinned there, staggered for 24 ticks.

**The way out.**
- **Against the reach:** any blow that lands first, or a dodge, after which the catcher is open for 20 ticks.
- **Against the grip:** his own tap of A within 8 ticks of the catch breaks it, and both are pushed apart.
- **Against the carry:** each press of A takes a fifth off what is left of it. He keeps half his nudge throughout.

**Counters.** A catch beats a guard and a heavy's armour, since a guard is no answer to a hand on the collar. It loses to a light, a medium or a burst blow that lands first.

**What it needs.**
- **Animation, per fighter:** the reach, the grip, six or seven throws and the carry. About 10 key sets (estimate), all specified in wave 4.
- **The sim:** a grab state, the carry as drawn travel, and a throw handed to the launch planner.

**The Press.** His catch is a clamp of both forearm plates on the body, one either side and never laced. His carry runs further, and his throws are slams down rather than slings across.

## 2. The clinch: a contest for the grip

**Tap.** A tie-up at the collar and the elbow, or the waist (g05). Both are in the clinch, and neither is ahead.

**In the clinch,** for at most 90 ticks:
- **A on the beat** is a pummel: a step ahead for the grip, which runs from two behind to two ahead.
- **X, Y and B** are vicious blows on the region the stick leans to, up to four, and never more than two in a row to the same place (s08).
- **Two ahead,** the next press of A is the throw, and the stick picks its way.

**Mash.** A pressed off the beat gives the grip away: mashing is how the clinch is lost. One press is still one attack: each is one try for the grip.

**Hold.** Brace. He can't be thrown while he holds A, and he throws nothing himself. It is a way to wait out a stronger grip.

**How it ends.** The throw, from two ahead. Or the 90 ticks run out and both are pushed apart. Or a vicious knee that a battered region can't take.

**The way out.** The held fighter has the same buttons. He can win the grip back, so the contest goes both ways. At two behind his tap of A on the beat is a reversal throw.

**Buildings.** The fighter ahead walks the pair along his stick. Against a wall it is a pin: his vicious blows there wear one and a half times as hard, and a throw from the pin is the wall slam at once, with no flight in between. Thrown through the shell of a damaged building, he finishes it (Orb, questionnaire 19).

**Counters.** The tie-up beats a guard. It loses to any blow that lands first and to a dodge.

**What it needs.**
- **Animation, per fighter:** the tie-up, three positions of the grip, the pummel, a vicious blow on each region, and two throws. About 12 key sets (estimate). Some are posed as a pair, both bodies at once.
- **The sim:** the grip as a small contest, read on the beat Game Design is building for the perfect follow-up. It is the largest of the three.

**The Press.** His clamp starts the clinch one ahead, and his brace can't be pummelled out of. The mass of him is a grip advantage.

## 3. The clamp: a sure hold that sets up a blow

**Tap.** A short clamp, quicker than a catch (an 8-tick tell, a shorter reach). It holds the rival where he stands for 24 ticks and throws nothing. It is an opening. In that window:
- **B** is the launcher, which always lands in an opening, by the stick's send as today.
- **Y** is a medium that knocks him back.
- **X** is up to four vicious blows on the stick's region (s08 applies).

**Mash.** Mashing X in the clamp is those blows, and A again is refused (the lockout).

**Hold.** The drag. He keeps the clamp and walks the rival along the stick, up to a second, then lets go into the same window.

**How it ends.** By the blow he chooses, or at the end of the window. Then both part, with the first move his (the rule of 45 ticks that follows a launch).

**The way out.**
- **Against the clamp:** his tap of A inside its 8-tick tell, or a blow or a burst that lands first.
- **Against the drag:** each press of A takes a fifth off it.
- **In the window:** a perfect block of the follow-up is still a perfect block.

**Buildings.** Dragged to a wall and launched from there, he goes into it at point-blank range: the wall slam, without needing a long flight to reach a tower. A clamp in town is how a tier-1 fighter puts a rival into a wall on purpose.

**Counters.** A clamp beats a guard. It loses to any blow that lands first and to a dodge.

**What it needs.**
- **Animation, per fighter:** the clamp and the drag. About 3 or 4 key sets (estimate); every follow-up is a piece that exists.
- **The sim:** a clamped state, which is an opening of the kind the launcher already reads, and the drag as drawn travel. The smallest of the three.

**The Press.** This is his move. Two plates close on the torso, and that is the silhouette's point: catches you between his plates. His window is longer, his drag goes further, and his follow-up heavy is his own falling hammer fist.

## Which, and what it costs

| | The catch | The clinch | The clamp |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| What A becomes | A throw on demand | A fight inside the fight | A way into the heavy |
| New key sets a fighter (estimate) | About 10 | About 12 | 3 or 4 |
| New in the sim | A grab, a carry, a throw to the planner | A grip contest on the beat | An opening and a drag |
| Wall slams it makes | From a throw or a carry | From a pin, at once | From a drag, at point-blank range |

**Combat's view.**
- **The clamp is the cheapest,** and it feeds what is already built: the launcher, the wall slam, the first move. It also gives the Press his identity outright.
- **The catch is the most familiar.**
- **The clinch is the deepest,** and the one Orb's "vicious inside the clinch" asks for. It costs the most.

They combine: a clamp on a tap with the catch's throws on a hold is one A that does both.
