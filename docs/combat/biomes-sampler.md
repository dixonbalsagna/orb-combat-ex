# How a fight reads in each biome

A sampler for Orb. Combat and Choreography, 2026-10-23.

One short scene per biome, describing how a typical exchange plays there once the parked biome layer is built (`biomes.md`).
- Nothing here is built yet.
- Game Design has ruled the numbers and the open questions (`docs/design/rulings-2026-10-23b.md`); the scenes follow its rulings.
- The variant names are working labels waiting on Legal, and the new biomes' own names wait on World.
- The village uses LANE SWEEP, which Legal has cleared and which waits on Orb's yes (`biomes.md` §2b).

**Ocean.** The fight hangs low over open water.
- A dash kicks up a wake. A knock-back skims a fighter across the surface before he sinks to the waist.
- Instead of losing him far out to sea, the director can skim him back toward land in a few long bounces.
- The signature, HORIZON CLEAVE (TIDE CLEAVE, if Orb takes the new name), parts the sea. For a second and a half the beam's path is an open trench between two walls of water. Then the sea rushes back as a wave that shoves anyone at the surface along the beam's line and, near land, runs up the beach. A defender caught by the hit is driven under and comes back up a moment later, ready to fight.

**Plains.** Open ground and soft soil: the clean stage the others are measured against.
- Every knock-back ploughs a furrow, with a berm of earth heaped in front of the feet. A slam leaves a plain round bowl.
- Long launches carry the fight across empty country.
- FIELD SCAR cuts one long, straight furrow, and a clash flattens a wide ring under the meeting point.

**City.** Paving and towers.
- Heels dig in and throw sparks when someone is driven back down a street.
- A slam lifts the paving into a ring of tilted slabs.
- Launches look for a building to send him through.
- BOULEVARD RAZE tears up through the towers behind the defender, and a clash blows out the windows below. The Protagonist pays for every bit of it in anguish.

**Village.** Low houses on lanes. The same sparks fly on the paving, but the danger sits low.
- LANE SWEEP grazes along the lane instead of up, partly flattening a whole row of houses.
- A clash lifts every roof along the lane both ways, and a hit sweeps the defender through a row of sheds.
- It costs fewer people than a tower, but the damage is spread over many homes.
- The Protagonist's sweep narrows near occupied houses and spares more of the row. Every life it takes still costs him, and a row the fight's budget can't afford is evacuated instead.

**Forest.** Trunks everywhere.
- The director likes to drive a fighter down into the forest floor.
- It can also send him low through a stand of trees, snapping trunk after trunk, each one slowing him, the way a building smash works in the city.
- FIRESTORM sets the canopy alight where the beam passes low.
- Someone driven back on guard snaps saplings as he goes.

**Desert.** The sand gives.
- A fighter driven back sinks and slides, throwing up a fan of sand.
- A slam leaves a wide, shallow bowl that slumps back in on itself, and landings are a little softer.
- GLASS TRENCH fuses the sand into a trench of glass. A clash leaves a glass disc, and a defender hit in the trench cracks the glass under him.
- The new crater and the softer landing change today's desert, so they wait behind their own switch.

**Mountains.** Bare rock.
- A fighter driven back grinds across it with chips flying.
- Slams leave steep bowls cracked in a star.
- The director throws people into the mountainside.
- RIDGE BORE drills a deep groove into the rock, and a hit drives the defender into the face under a rain of chips.

**Ice sheet.** Deep snow over ice.
- A fighter knocked back ploughs a channel through the snow and ends up shin-deep.
- Slams sink deep, clean holes that slowly drift back in.
- ICEFIELD THAW melts a channel that refreezes into glass-smooth ice for the rest of the fight. That strip is now a slide: knock someone onto it and he skids its whole length. He gets his hands back as early as he would on rock, and rides out the rest of the slide as a skate he can attack out of.

**Sea ice.** Flat floes over black water.
- Nobody stops cleanly on ice. A fighter driven back spins out, a hand braking him, and comes round to face his opponent.
- The director can send him skidding across a floe into open water, or against an iceberg.
- A slam punches through the floe into the sea.
- FLOE SPLIT opens a lead of water along the beam. A defender hit as it opens drops in and comes back up a moment later.

**Jungle.** Dense, wet and tangled, and the director prefers short launches here.
- Driven back fast, a fighter catches a foot on a root and goes over into a roll.
- The director can send him through a line of trunks.
- A slam bares the roots and leans the trees outward.
- VINE SHEAR fells a clearing. The trunks come down across the cut, the canopy cover along it is gone, and the fallen trunks are something a fighter sent into them can rebound off.

**Savanna.** Wide-open grass and lone trees. The director favours long launches here more than anywhere.
- A fighter driven back peels up a strip of turf with his heels.
- The director can smash him into a lone tree.
- GRASSLAND BLAZE lights the grass. A fire line spreads out from the cut for a few seconds, and the next exchange happens across it or through the smoke.
- Anyone standing in the flames takes a light singe. Flying over them costs nothing.

**Tundra.** Frozen ground that cracks.
- Footing skitters as a fighter is driven back, the crust cracking under him.
- Slams throw up rims of frozen plates.
- FROST HEAVE heaves a line of plates along the cut, and they stay for the whole fight:
  - a later knock-back stops dead against them;
  - a fighter sent into them can rebound off them;
  - plates tall enough become a cliff to throw someone into.

**Taiga.** Snow-laden pines.
- Knock-backs plough through deep snow.
- The director can send a fighter through a line of pines, snapping them as he goes.
- PINE BREAK snaps the trunks along the beam, and every tree around sheds its snow at once. The crowns and their snow come down on a defender who's hit, and the scar partly fills with snow.

**Wetland.** Mud and standing water.
- A fighter skidding to a stop in mud sinks and is dragged. He takes a brief beat to pull his feet free before he can move, though he can still strike.
- Skids end short.
- The director likes to slam people straight down into the marsh, which swallows them and closes over the hole. The Protagonist favours it because almost nothing is destroyed.
- MIRE BOIL boils the marsh and floods the cut, so a defender hit there lands in water.

**Beach.** Shingle, surf and sea cliffs.
- Pebbles roll underfoot as a fighter is driven back.
- A fight drifting out over the sea gets skimmed back onto the beach, and the cliffs are there to throw people into.
- Craters near the water fill with seawater.
- SHORE SCOUR cuts a channel, and the sea floods it behind the defender.

**Highland.** Turf over rock, steep slopes and lone outcrops.
- Knock-backs peel up turf.
- The director sends fighters tumbling down slopes, into crags, or into a single outcrop that breaks.
- SCREE RUN loosens the slope. Rubble pours downhill from the cut, and anyone sent down it tumbles.

**Volcanic.** Black crust over heat, ash fields and vents.
- Crust breaks under the heels of a fighter driven back, leaving glowing cracks, and slams crack in a glowing star.
- An uppercut over a vent gets an extra blast of lift.
- The cones are cliffs to throw people into.
- CRUST SEAR leaves a glowing vein for ten seconds. It lights anything burnable, and it singes a fighter who stands on it while it glows.
