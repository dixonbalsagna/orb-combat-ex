# Performance audit of main, 2026-10-16

Owner: Performance and Platform, for the EP. Measured tree: **HEAD e6fa72ff**, and main as it stood later that day, **8ccf9a19** (sections 3.5 and 4.0). Every figure below is from a **scratch export** made from a clean `git archive` of that commit; nothing in the working tree was touched, no Godot window was opened, and every browser was started through `tools/machine/browser.mjs` (session `meridian-performance-platform`, below-normal priority, its 4-core mask, a time limit, one at a time).

## 1. The answer

**Where main stands against the budgets** (the web build on the dev PC, three of Tools' scenes, Chrome's CPU throttle standing in for slower machines; every figure from a run the load rule passed):

|---|---|---|
| **This PC, x1** (60 fps) | yes: p50 5.5 to 7.3 ms | yes: p95 7.9 to 11.5 ms |
| **T1, a 2019 desktop, x2** (60 fps) | yes: 9.9 to 11.8 ms | city brawl yes (14.6), ring on the line (16.9), **beam in the city 27% over (21.2)** |

| **T3, a mid-range phone, x6** (30 fps) | **no: 37 to 46 ms** | **no: 61 to 71 ms; reduced effects do not fix it (52 to 64 ms)** |

**What the parked work costs:** VFX's beam look (now on main), Animation's five patches, and all eight of them on today's main, together with the lobby, cost **0.0 to 0.5 ms a frame at the median at any tier (inside the noise), no draw calls, no primitives, no memory, and leave the sim hash unchanged.** The lobby (depth on) costs +0.4 ms; its own opening is not exercised by these scenes.

**The planet slice's read** (World's 0.9 s on a busy PC) is **0.7 s along the equator and 1.4 s through the poles in the browser on this PC, 3.1 and 6.2 s at x4, 4.9 and 9.8 s at x6, 6.9 and 13.5 s at x8 (a slow phone).** It is a single call that freezes the page. **Acceptable on a fast desktop only hidden behind the intro; not acceptable as one call on an old laptop or a phone.** World's own coarse variant (the planet function every 8th column) takes **0.085 and 0.17 s here and 0.6 and 1.2 s at x6**. Budgets and the fix are in sections 4.1 and 6.

**The top three hot spots**, each with its owner, evidence and fix in section 5:
1. **The HUD is 94% of the frame's draw calls (344 of 364) and 1.3 ms of the 2.4 ms a frame takes to draw** (UI; `render_view` also redraws both HUDs every frame).
2. **First-use hitches of 150 to 510 ms** at the same ticks of every match (a third of a second at the first beam, guard launch and transformation): invisible in averages, they are the p99 and the maximum (VFX, Rendering: a warm-up pass).
3. **Geometry the picture does not need**: the water mesh is the terrain's grid (171 k of 624 k primitives for nothing in a city), the whole crowd is drawn every frame (259 k), and Camera's inset pane draws a second world while it is up (770 k of 1.39 M) (World, Rendering, Camera).

**Also for phones:** the page holds **about 500 MB** and the browser about 1 GB; the first load is 14.4 MB and a cold start 20 to 26 s on this PC.

**What I could not do and why:** native frame times with a GPU (needs a window), the integrated GPU's headroom (a web run is locked to the display's refresh), a real old laptop, phone or Steam Deck (none on the bench), and GPU time (the web reports none). Section 8 lists what would measure each.

**New files:** `docs/perf/budgets.md` (the budget table, the three tiers, the proposed gate, how to read other people's numbers), this audit, and the tools in `docs/perf/tools/` that rerun all of it with one command each (section 9). **A correction to my own first runs** is in section 2.2: they timed every tick as two animation frames; I found it and redid everything.

## 2. Method, and what the numbers can and cannot say

### 2.1 What was built and measured

| Variant | What it is |
|---|---|

| `vfx` | `base` plus VFX's `docs/vfx/pending/energy-and-choreo/apply.patch` (the beam look and the explosions) |

All patches applied cleanly with `git apply --ignore-whitespace`. **The sim hash is identical in all of them** (`--headless --bench --seed=4`: tick 1800 `5c8a828d4ef852c2` in `base`, `vfx`, `anim` and `both`, three rounds each; tick 2400 `8f2e64728709d8d9` in `base`, `both`, `baseprof` and `bothprof`), so the parked work is render-side only, as its notes say. The measuring code is in `docs/perf/tools/scratch-hooks/` and is never applied to the repo; `docs/perf/tools/make-audit-sites.mjs` rebuilds every variant from HEAD.

### 2.2 How a frame is timed

- The page is Tools' capture hook (`/play/?flashcap=1`), which stages Tools' scenes (`mashcity`, `signaturecity`, `ring`) with a seed and scripted pad input, as in the flash set. **The scene names in the tables map to the brief like this:** *city brawl* = `mashcity` (two human fighters mashing beside the tallest tower), *a signature beam in the city* = `signaturecity` (player one fires the signature every 90 ticks at the AI), *a 3-body ring* = `ring` with `PROTAGONIST, RIVAL, RIVAL` (the pair plus one guard, who fights). They are Tools' staging, not play.
- **A frame is the interval between two animation frames, with the game running one tick per frame** (the hook's auto mode, which I added in the scratch export), vsync and the frame limit off, 1280 by 720 at a device scale of 1, 120 frames of warm-up then 600 measured, in headless Chrome on the RTX 5070 Ti through ANGLE and D3D11. The game's own per-frame timers give the sim tick, the FX and camera time, and the view update. Draw calls, primitives and objects come from `RenderingServer.get_rendering_info`; they are exact and do not depend on the machine.
- **A correction to my first runs.** My first version called the hook's `step()` in a loop and timed it. `step()` runs a tick on every *other* animation frame (its promise resolves after `poll()` has already looked for the next request), so a step took 2.00 animation frames and the second only repeated the picture. I found that by counting animation frames per step, discarded every run made that way and reran everything. The numbers here are the corrected ones.
- **CPU time per frame** is the sum of the user and kernel time of the browser's processes (renderer, GPU process, browser, utility) over the measured frames, divided by the frame count. It is the figure that survives a busy machine, and it shows where the work is: the **renderer process is the page's main thread** (the wasm game and the engine's draw submission), the GPU process is the driver side.
- **Old laptop and phone: Chrome's CPU throttle** (`Emulation.setCPUThrottlingRate`, x2, x4, x6, x8) makes the page's main thread that many times slower. It does not slow the GPU, the memory or a hot chip, so a throttled run is a **lower bound** on a slower machine, never a prediction. The tiers are in `docs/perf/budgets.md`.
- **Natively.** A native Godot with a GPU needs a window, which the no-windows rule forbids. So "native" in this note means **headless Godot (`--headless --bench`)**: it measures the sim tick, the FX and camera time and the GDScript view update, and cannot measure draw calls, GPU time or VRAM. For those, the web build on the RTX and on the integrated Radeon is the measurement.
- **Validity.** Before and after each run the driver reads the CPU use of every logical processor. A run is **valid** when the mean use of all processors was at most 60% and of the capture's four cores at most 70%, before and after; otherwise it is marked invalid and its numbers are an upper bound. About 20 sessions share this PC and Orb plays on it: between 18:00 and 19:30 every core read 95 to 100% and nothing taken then is used here. A quiet stretch opened at about 19:35 and held, with gaps, until the end; **every figure in this note comes from a run the rule passed**, unless a table says "inv". The quiet-aware runner (`audit-run.mjs --wait-quiet`, `quiet.cjs`) waited for it.
- **Repeats and order.** The A/B runs rotate the order of the sites from scene to scene, so a drift in load is not charged to the site that ran first. Noise is in section 7.

### 2.3 What this cannot show

No real old laptop, phone or Steam Deck was measured; no GPU time (the web build reports none); no native GPU; no audio cost (Tools' hook runs without real audio output); no human input latency; no memory on a phone; nothing about thermal throttling. Stepped scenes are one-tick-per-frame, not real pacing.

### 3.4 The integrated Radeon and the draw list

On the 9800X3D's integrated Radeon (2 compute units; forced with Chrome's `--force_low_power_gpu` and checked against the page's GPU string) all three scenes ran at **p50 15.4 to 16.8 ms and p95 33.7 to 34.0 ms in every variant**: the frame is locked to the 60 Hz present (a frame is 16.7 ms or twice that), so the run **cannot show the iGPU's headroom**. What it does show is that **at least one frame in twenty took two refreshes** even in the city brawl, so the integrated GPU is at or near its limit at 1280 by 720 with the draw list below, while the CPU cost is unchanged (page main thread 5.0 to 6.5 ms). No GPU timer is available on the web, so I cannot say by how much.

The draw list per frame (exact, the same in every run of a scene and every variant): **305 draw calls and 623 k primitives in the city brawl, 337 and 624 k in the beam scene, 346 and 523 k in the ring**, with peaks of 377 to 452 draw calls and **1.39 M primitives (beam) and 1.79 M (ring)** in single frames. ### 3.6 The first-use hitches

Single frames of **150 to 510 ms** happen at the same ticks in every run of a scene, in every variant and at every throttle: in the beam scene at **tick 427 (155 to 230 ms)**; in the ring at **tick 154 (300 to 396 ms), tick 211 (216 to 307 ms) and tick 279 (363 to 484 ms in some runs)**; the ring of five at tick 250 (513 ms). They do **not** scale with the CPU throttle (209 ms at x4 against 171 ms at x1 at tick 427), so they are not the page's compute: that points at the graphics driver compiling a shader or pipeline the first time an effect is drawn, or at an allocation the first time a pool grows. They are invisible in an average and are a freeze of a third of a second at the first beam, first guard launch and first transformation of a match. The cause is a hypothesis; the test is cheap (draw each effect once, hidden, behind the notice and see whether the hitch moves).

### 4.1 The planet slice's read (World's prototype, `docs/world/scratch-build/planet-slice/`)

World measured about **0.9 s in GDScript on a busy PC** for the 4,800-column strip (`strip_time2.gd`), picking 70 to 110 ms and placing the places 0.1 to 0.4 s, and wrote that the web build and an old laptop were not measured. They are now. The same script (World's `planet2_lib.gd`, the same 4,800 columns with the 3-octave detail, best of three) in the web build, and in headless native Godot:

| Strip read | Native, headless | Web x1 (this PC) | Web x4 (T2) | Web x6 (T3) | Web x8 (a slow phone) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Along the equator (no ice) | 0.60 s | 0.69 s | 3.1 s | 4.9 s | 6.9 s |
| Through both poles (the worst case: ice floes and bergs, two cellular noises per column) | 1.20 s | 1.36 s | 6.2 s | 9.8 s | 13.5 s |
| Coarse: the planet function every 8th column, heights between interpolated (World's own idea, not built; my scratch) — equator | 75 ms | 85 ms | | 0.61 s | |
| Coarse — poles | 149 ms | 169 ms | | 1.22 s | |

- **The web is no slower than native here** (1.15 times on a quiet PC), so World's 0.9 s carries over to the browser on a fast machine. The throttle scales it as expected (x4 gives 4.5 times, x8 gives 10 times).
- **Is 0.9 s acceptable at touch-down? On a fast machine only behind something.** It is a single call that freezes the page: 0.7 to 1.4 s is a noticeable hang. The intro's fall lasts 5 to 7.6 s (`docs/ep/vision.md`), which can hide it if the work is spread over it (the intros are skippable, so a skip must not skip the generation). **On T2 it is 3 to 6 s, and on a phone 5 to 14 s of a frozen tab**: browsers show a "page unresponsive" prompt for a long main-thread task (not tested here), and a mobile browser may reload the tab. **It is not acceptable as one call on T2 or T3.**
- **The worst case on a phone** (x8, through the poles) is **13.5 s for the strip alone**; adding World's picking (70 to 110 ms) and placing (0.1 to 0.4 s), scaled by the same ratios, is an estimate of 15 to 18 s (not measured).
- **The coarse read removes 8 times the work** (the planet function every 8th column; detail and biome from the nearest sample): 0.085 to 0.17 s on this PC and **0.6 to 1.2 s at x6**. Its limit is fidelity: the heights between samples are a straight line, which is fine for the continent and relief octaves (wavelengths of 70 columns and up) and not for the polar floes and bergs (cells of 34 to 69 columns, bergs 9 columns wide), which need the full read near the poles. World decides how much that matters.
- **Recommended, and a budget (section 6):** read the strip in time-sliced chunks of at most 4 ms a frame, nearest the touch-down point first, over the intro's fall; use the coarse read for the far parts; never block a frame for more than 8 ms.

### 4.2 Rendering's lobby and the facade work to come (`homework4-pitch.md`)

The lobby is in the table above. The facade steps A and B (glass that takes the sky, a tint per tower) are one `mix` a pixel and no geometry. Fragment cost depends on pixels covered, not on primitives (the buildings are only 3.4 k of the 624 k primitives, but towers cover much of the picture): it is not measurable on the RTX and not resolvable on the integrated GPU in my runs, which are locked to the display's refresh. **Step C (ribbon windows, fins, a crown that differs from the shaft) adds branches to the window shader.** Nothing here is built, so nothing is measured; re-run the layer cut and the integrated-GPU runs after Rendering builds it (section 9).

### The three hot spots, in the order I would fix them

**1. The HUD is 94% of the frame's draw calls and over half of its drawing time (owner: UI).**
- Evidence: 344 of 364 draws and 1.3 of 2.4 ms in the city brawl; 330 of 382 and 1.1 of 2.5 ms in the beam scene. The whole 3D world is **23 draw calls**. In the web build the main thread pays for each one; with the HUD's script (`hud_advance`, 0.3 to 0.4 ms) it is about **1.6 to 1.7 ms of a 6.5 ms frame: a quarter at x1, 6.5 ms of the 33.3 ms budget at x4.**
- A concrete lever: `render/core/main.gd`, `render_view()` calls `ui_hud.queue_redraw()` and `hud.queue_redraw()` **every frame**, so both HUDs rebuild their whole draw list every frame whether or not a thing changed (Rendering's file; UI's `_draw()`).
- Proposed fix: UI caches the HUD's static plates and frames into textures and redraws a part only when its state changes; merges text and shapes drawn with the same material into one canvas item; skips parts that are hidden (the touch layer on a desktop, the legend after the first matches). Budget: HUD at most 60 draw calls and 0.5 ms of drawing, so a frame holds at most 100 draw calls. Re-run section 9's layer cut to check. Saving: 1.0 ms at x1, 4 ms at T2.

**2. First-use hitches of 150 to 510 ms at the same ticks every match (owners: VFX and Rendering).**
- Evidence: section 3.6; invisible in averages, they are the p99 and the maximum in every table, and they do not scale with the CPU throttle, which points at the driver rather than the page.
- Proposed fix: **a warm-up pass**: during the flashing-effects notice or the loading card, draw every effect's material once, hidden (a beam, a blast, a transformation, the guards' bolts, a launch), so no first use happens in a fight. Test the hypothesis first: draw one beam at the notice and see whether tick 427's 160 to 230 ms goes away. Saving: removes the 0.2 to 0.5 s freezes; no change to the median.

**3. Geometry the picture does not need (owners: World and Rendering; Camera for the inset).**
- Evidence: the water mesh is **the same grid as the terrain** (hiding either removes exactly 171.4 k primitives, so water is drawn in full under a city with no sea in view); the whole crowd (1,800 people, **259 k primitives**) is drawn every frame whether or not it is on screen (a MultiMesh is one object that is drawn whole); and the inset pane draws the world a second time. Together they are **96% of the city brawl's primitives (602 k of 624 k) and 98% of the beam scene's (1.35 M of 1.39 M)**.
- Why it matters: on the RTX it costs nothing visible; on the integrated Radeon the frame sits at the 60 Hz limit with a miss in every twenty frames, and a phone has a fraction of that GPU. It is the first thing to go for T3.
- Proposed fix: (a) **draw a water chunk only where the chunk holds water** (World/Rendering: about 171 k less in the city, more in the beam scene); (b) **split the crowd into spatial chunks** and draw only those within the camera's window (about 250 k less); (c) **render the inset pane at a lower detail tier** with no crowd and the far-tier terrain only (Camera/Rendering: about 400 k less while it is up). Budget: 300 k primitives typical, 450 k at most.

### Two more for phones (not a hot spot of the frame, a limit of the page)

- **Memory: the page's process holds 495 to 537 MB of private memory and the browser about 1 GB.** Godot's own issue tracker lists about 450 MB as a default web build's size and links it to iOS losing the context, and an iPhone's tab limit is set by the system and varies with the device and load (one forum report puts it at roughly 1.5 GB on an iPhone 12 Pro and 3 GB on an iPhone 15 Pro, and much less on old ones: unverified). We are at the size where a phone tab is closed. Owner: Rendering and Tools: find what the wasm heap holds (the engine's static memory counter reads 0 in the web build; a debug export with the memory monitor will say).
- **Cold start: 20 to 26 s from navigation to the first frame** on this PC through a local server (no network, no browser cache, below-normal priority and four cores) for **14.4 MB** of download (the wasm 9.7 MB, the pack 4.6 MB, gzip level 6; 45.9 MB raw). Owner: Tools. The player's real number adds the download.

## 7. Noise and validity

- **Every figure is from a run the load rule passed.** I took about 200 runs and use **117**. The rest were made with my first timing method (29, section 2.2) or were invalid by the load rule (about 55: the ones taken while every core read 95 to 100% from 18:00 to 19:30, and a few later when another session started a heavy job); each was repeated in a quiet stretch. A quiet stretch began at about 19:35 and held with gaps; the quiet-aware runner waited for them.
- **Exact numbers are exact.** Draw calls and primitives did not vary by a single call or triangle across runs of the same scene, in any variant: 305 / 623 k, 337 / 624 k, 346 / 523 k (peaks 425 / 626 k, 377 / 1,391 k, 432 / 1,792 k). **They are the numbers to gate strictly.**
- **Timing noise.** The same scene (base, city brawl, x1) measured four times in the quiet stretch read p50 6.5, 8.6, 6.9 and 7.4 ms (**a 28% spread**) at 19 to 54% load, and page main-thread CPU 5.9 to 7.4 ms (22%). With the machine quieter (8 to 36% load) the ring read p50 6.4 to 6.9 ms over twelve runs (**about 8%**), and the main-thread CPU time 5.5 to 6.6 ms across variants. The earlier readings of 9.8 to 15.7 ms for the same code, at 90 to 100% load, are why the discarded runs are discarded.
- **Two kinds of valid.** A pass of the rule at 50% load is noisier than at 10%. The A/B tables compare variants run at the same time, in rotating order, and the parked-item deltas are all inside the spread; I would not act on a delta under 0.5 ms at x1.
- **What the throttle does.** Chrome's x4 made the frame 3.3 to 4.0 times longer at p50 (city brawl 6.5 to 22.3 ms, beam 7.3 to 23.8, ring 6.6 to 26.3) and the page's CPU time per frame 5 to 6 times larger (city brawl 5.9 to 30.2 ms). Use the throttle to compare builds and to find which tier a build clears, not to forecast a particular laptop.

## 9. Reproduce

All of it is in `docs/perf/tools/`; nothing writes to the repo.

```bash
# 1. the scratch exports (from a clean git archive of HEAD; about a minute each; no window)
node docs/perf/tools/make-audit-sites.mjs --out <scratch folder> [--only base,vfx,anim,both,baseprof,bothprof,depthprof,layerprof,lowprof]

# 2. the matrix, one browser at a time through tools/machine/browser.mjs, waiting for a quiet machine
MERIDIAN_SESSION=meridian-performance-platform node docs/perf/tools/audit-run.mjs --sites base=<scratch>/base/build/site,vfx=<scratch>/vfx/build/site,anim=<scratch>/anim/build/site,both=<scratch>/both/build/site --out <runs folder> --plan ab --wait-quiet 1500

# one run
node docs/perf/tools/frame-bench.mjs --site <scratch>/base/build/site --scene mashcity|signaturecity|ring [--slots PROTAGONIST,RIVAL,RIVAL] [--throttle 4] [--igpu] [--page-args depth=1|lowq=1] [--layers --freeze 104] [--slice]

# 3. the tables
node docs/perf/tools/audit-summary.mjs <runs folder>... --only-valid

# native, headless (sim tick, FX, view update; no GPU)
powershell -File docs/perf/tools/native-cpu-bench.ps1 -Project <scratch>/base -Seed 4 -Frames 2400 -Out <log>
```

`docs/perf/budgets.md` has the budget table and the proposed gate; the gate would run `audit-run.mjs --plan ab` on a commit and its parent on a quiet machine.
