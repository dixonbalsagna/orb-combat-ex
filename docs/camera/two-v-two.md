# 2v2 over LAN: what the camera needs (Camera, 2026-11-01)

For Netcode's feasibility check on Orb's question (`docs/ep/vision.md`, end). Paper, read from the rig's code. Nothing's built or run.

**Yes, the one-pair camera is reusable per player, with one small change and no second rig design: on each PC, run one `SplitRig` and feed it a pair, the player and whoever they've focused.** The rig reads `S.fighters[0]` and `[1]` directly at 109 places, so it wants a thin pair view in place of `S.fighters`, about half a window by my estimate: mechanical, and byte-identical in 1v1 if the pair is just the two. The camera is view only, so two players' screens can differ with no netcode cost; each PC steps the same sim and its own rig. The split still works: one pane for you, one for your focus when they're apart. **It needs `CamPlaces` and swing reads unchanged, since the line and the swing are the fight's. That's the open question, and it isn't mine: one planet's frame can't turn for one pair and not the other, so a swing is for all four or none. Netcode and Simulation say which.**

| Need | Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A focused opponent** | The pair's second body. Framing, split, lead, anticipation and the launch shot all follow it with no new logic. **Anticipation and blow push must filter to events involving your pair,** so a partner's blow doesn't tighten your view: about a dozen event reads, one rule |
| **A switch of focus, no jolts** | Ease it, as the rig's other moves. The pair's second body changes on one tick, so `u`, `sep` and the targets step; **a new blend of about 0.5 s across the old and new focus points hides that, and `launch_shots_check`'s jolt test can stage it.** If the new focus is more than a split away, the split opens by its own eased rule. No cut. Under the motion setting, a plain cut with its named reason. Holding off during a hit-stop or an exchange's beat stops a stray D-pad press from moving the frame mid-blow |
| **The teammate, without covering fighters** | Drawn in the world as they are, at their real place, when they're in frame. **Off frame: the edge HUD's existing distance chip** (the rival pointer's own kind, UI's), which sits at the edge rather than over anyone. No pane or strip of their own by default. `one-v-many.md`'s rule holds: a pane belongs to a player, and a third body never shrinks your frame. A body in your frame by chance doesn't change your framing |
| **A partner's meteor launch, far away** | **Your camera doesn't take it.** You're fighting, and the peak shot on your screen would leave your own fight unframed. Three things tell you: the **world shows it** where it can (the trail across your sky, on the true globe, and its landing shaking your ground by distance: VFX, Audio), a **short UI line**, and the **damage inset's** kind of small picture in a corner, away from the fighters, if Orb wants to see it, and only in a calm moment. **When you and your focus are both free,** a brief wide could take it in. That's a design call for Orb's eye. **A peak shot your own pair is in runs as now,** with `peak_launch` keyed to your pair |

**Worth knowing.** Two rigs on one sim already run in scratch (I used it to compare rules on the inset), so the rig holds no global camera state. The older PC's frame cost is one rig plus its view, so it's the same as 1v1 for the camera, though four fighters and the view cost more (Performance). Two rigs on one screen, couch 2v2, is a different question and isn't asked here.
