Brief: `docs/ep/cloud-briefs/encounter-tick-skip.md`. Branch: `cloud/encounter-systems/tick-skip`.

## SUMMARY
Simulation's parked accessor patch applied cleanly, as its own commit. On top of it, every change is a provably equal
rewrite: the same draws, in the same order, to the same state. The p50 tick, main against the branch:
- AI match: 680 to 519 µs, -24%;
- gen 2: 684 to 557 µs, -19%;
- net's scripted players: 393 to 347 µs, -12%.

These are the medians of three runs back to back on this machine, which is about 1.4x slower than the EP's.

**It is not 0.3 ms.** The tick's cost is no longer idle sub-ticks. It is call count in real work (the brawl's move and
AI, each press's log read), and two of Controls' helpers that rebuild a dictionary on every call (NEEDS 1). The golden
is byte-identical, and the state hash and every tick's feed and cues match main's on 43,200 ticks of seeded matches.

## DECISIONS
- **Step 2's commit message overstates the cut.** It says "-30% (0.90 to 0.63 ms)", from a first pair of runs taken
  while the machine was busy. The quiet re-measure above (-24% p50 on the AI match) is the result.
- **Equivalence was proven by comparison, not only argued.** `tick_hash` on main and on the branch gives byte-identical
  files: 43,200 ticks, the state hash and the tick's feed and cues. Each rewrite also has its argument in a comment:
  - a pure read was moved or reordered;
  - a write was skipped only where it would have written the value already there, to a record already sized;
  - a cache holds only constants, or a pure function's answer for its exact inputs.
- **Some sub-ticks were left alone.** Their remaining cost is the call to them, and no cheaper guard exists that reads
  less than they do: Bands, Blast, Zip (its read sizes the record), Recoil, WallKick, Chase, Calm, Rally, sentTick and
  the queues. Nothing was found that could not be made equivalent. I did not chase these further.
- **No direct indexing of the brawl's integers.** Reading `f.act.dirI[K]` in place of `_g(f, K)` saves about 0.4 µs a
  read, about 15 µs a brawl tick. It would make `brawl.gd` harder to read than the gain is worth.
- **The memo in `read` is not state.** It is keyed on the sim object and the tick. If Controls' input data were changed
  inside a step, a second read at that tick could see the old answer. Nothing does that.

## NEEDS FROM EP
1. **Controls: cheap `params()`.** `SimPressRead.params()` costs about 60 µs and `SimAim.params()` about 8 µs: each
   rebuilds a dictionary from about 35 data lookups. `classify` and its helpers take `opts.get("p", params())`, which
   builds it even when `p` is passed. Two things would cut it:
   - a `params()` that caches until `SimInputData` reloads;
   - `opts["p"] if opts.has("p") else params()`.

   That, with a cheaper `logOf` input, would take most of the press cost (about 600 µs a press, about 60 µs a tick).
2. **Simulation: `tick_prof.gd --gen2` passes `"arena": 2400`.** `newMatch` now wants `{"cols": 2400}`, so the run
   errors and plays the planet's full arena. `dir_prof.gd` passes the dictionary.
3. **Simulation, a note:** the first `DirInterrupt.on()` of a process parses the director's data, about 180 ms here. It
   lands on a match's first live tick: a one-off hitch, and it inflates tick_prof's mean (the 300 ms max line). A
   warm-up in `SimCore.newMatch` or the loading screen would hide it.

## RISKS
- **A memo must not hold a sim.** The first version of `read`'s memo kept the last `SimState` in a static, and the
  rollback check's leak count failed it ("sessions leak objects (402 more)"; every hash comparison passed). It now
  keeps the sim's instance id, and the check passes.
- The `_base()` caches assume the layout constants never change while a process runs. They are `const`s, so they can't.
- A tool that edits Controls' input data inside a step would get a stale `read()` at that tick. None does.
- These timings are from one noisy cloud machine. The ratios between main and the branch, measured back to back, are
  the result, not the absolute numbers.

## NEXT
1. Controls' `params()` (NEEDS 1), then re-measure the press.
2. **0.3 ms needs a structural pass, not guards.** The brawl's per-tick move and AI could keep their fighter's
   integers in a typed local record for the tick and write it back once. A data-oriented press log, one packed array
   read by Controls without building 20 dictionaries, is the other. This is a design brief of its own, with Simulation
   on the tick contract.
